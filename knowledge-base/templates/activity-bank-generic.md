# Generic Activity & Game Bank (topic-agnostic)

40+ reusable activity **frames**. Each works one-to-one at home and drops onto any subject — swap the content into the slots, skin it in the child's interests. See `activity-bank-README.md` for how to pick and adapt.

Each entry: **Name** · **Goal / when** · **How to run** · **Adapt to any topic** · **Classai block(s)** · **Primary example**.

Throughout: *display blocks are look-only; the interactive block carries the answer.*

---

## A. Sorting, matching & comparing

### 1. Matching Pairs
- **Goal / when:** Check / practice / retrieval. Best after vocabulary or relationships have been taught.
- **How to run:** (1) Make a set of pairs — term↔definition, picture↔word, problem↔answer, cause↔effect. (2) Child connects each left item to its right partner. (3) For a memory version (pelmanism), turn all cards face-down and find pairs by remembering positions.
- **Adapt to any topic:** the slot is *pairs of things that belong together*. Spanish word↔English, fraction↔picture, country↔capital, instrument↔family, command↔output.
- **Classai block(s):** `matchPairs` (connect version); `flashcards` for the face-down memory version.
- **Primary example:** **Science** — match each animal to its group: frog↔amphibian, robin↔bird, salmon↔fish, snake↔reptile, dog↔mammal.

### 2. Odd One Out
- **Goal / when:** Hook or check. Forces the child to find the *rule* that connects three of four.
- **How to run:** (1) Show 4 items, 3 share a property, 1 doesn't. (2) Child picks the odd one **and says why**. (3) Push further: "is there a different answer?" — good sets have more than one defensible odd-one-out.
- **Adapt to any topic:** slot = *4 items + a shared property*. Numbers (one isn't even), words (one isn't a noun), shapes, historical figures, materials.
- **Classai block(s):** `multipleChoice` (pick the odd one); follow with `speak`/`shortText` for the reason.
- **Primary example:** **Maths** — 6, 8, 9, 12. Odd one out is 9 (the only odd number) *or* arguably 9 (not in the 2× table) — both reveal thinking.

### 3. Which One Doesn't Belong (WODB)
- **Goal / when:** Hook / discussion. Like odd-one-out but *every* item can be justified as the odd one — designed to provoke reasoning, no single right answer.
- **How to run:** (1) Show a 2×2 grid of 4 items. (2) Child argues which one doesn't belong and why; then find a reason for *each* of the other three. (3) Celebrate multiple valid answers.
- **Adapt to any topic:** slot = *4 items chosen so each is uniquely different in some way*. Numbers, shapes, words, animals, paintings.
- **Classai block(s):** `image`/`emojiViz` to display the four; `speak`/`shortText` to capture reasoning (open-judged).
- **Primary example:** **Maths** — 9, 16, 25, 43. (16 is even; 43 isn't square; 9 is one digit; 25 ends in 5…)

### 4. Sorting / Categorising
- **Goal / when:** Check / practice. Reveals the criteria a child is using.
- **How to run:** (1) Give a pile of items and 2–3 labelled boxes. (2) Child drags each item into a box. (3) Discuss the tricky/boundary cases.
- **Adapt to any topic:** slot = *items + categories*. Living/non-living, vowel/consonant, push/pull forces, fact/opinion, prime/composite.
- **Classai block(s):** `categorize`.
- **Primary example:** **Science** — sort into Living / Once-living / Never-living: oak tree, rock, fossil, ladybird, plastic spoon, apple.

### 5. Concept Sort (open sort)
- **Goal / when:** Explain / consolidation. Child invents the categories (open) or uses given ones (closed).
- **How to run:** (1) Give 8–12 items, no labels. (2) Child groups them their own way and **names each group**. (3) Tutor probes the rule; re-sort by a new rule to show the same things can be classified many ways.
- **Adapt to any topic:** slot = *a rich set of items that can be grouped on several dimensions*. Materials (by hardness / by transparency), words (by sound / by meaning), shapes.
- **Classai block(s):** `categorize` (closed version); `speak`/`shortText` for the open version (child names groups).
- **Primary example:** **English** — sort 10 words; child might group by syllables, then re-sort by first sound, then by part of speech.

### 6. Venn / Two-Box Compare
- **Goal / when:** Check / consolidation. Surfaces similarities *and* differences.
- **How to run:** (1) Two overlapping regions (A only / both / B only). (2) Child places each feature where it belongs. (3) The overlap is the rich bit — push for it.
- **Adapt to any topic:** slot = *two things to compare + features*. Multiples of 2 vs 3, frogs vs toads, Stone Age vs Bronze Age, Spain vs UK.
- **Classai block(s):** `categorize` with three buckets (A / both / B).
- **Primary example:** **History** — Then vs Now: candles, electric light, horse, car, quill, both-have-houses.

## B. Sequencing & ordering

### 7. Ordering / Sequencing
- **Goal / when:** Check / practice. Tests understanding of order, size, time, or process.
- **How to run:** (1) Give items out of order. (2) Child arranges them. (3) Ask them to justify two adjacent items ("why does this come before that?").
- **Adapt to any topic:** slot = *anything with a correct order*. Numbers smallest→largest, life-cycle stages, story events, steps of long division, historical dates.
- **Classai block(s):** `ordering`.
- **Primary example:** **Science** — order the butterfly life cycle: egg → caterpillar → chrysalis → butterfly.

### 8. Timeline Build
- **Goal / when:** Practice / consolidation, especially history & process topics.
- **How to run:** (1) Give events/dates. (2) Child places them on a line. (3) Add "what came between?" and "what happened because of X?" to build causal sense, not just order.
- **Adapt to any topic:** slot = *dated or sequenced events*. Reigns, inventions, a story's plot, stages of plant growth.
- **Classai block(s):** `ordering`; `numberLine` to display the timeline scaffold.
- **Primary example:** **History** — order: Stone Age, Bronze Age, Iron Age, Roman Britain.

### 9. Story / Sentence Chain
- **Goal / when:** Practice / consolidation. Collaborative building; great for language and creativity.
- **How to run:** (1) Tutor says one sentence; child adds the next; alternate. (2) Each turn must connect logically. (3) Read the whole chain back at the end.
- **Adapt to any topic:** slot = *a sequence the pair co-builds*. A story, a maths word-problem ("…and then she bought 3 more…"), a chain of historical consequences, a recipe.
- **Classai block(s):** `speak` (spoken chain) or `shortText`/`fillBlank` (written, Mad-libs style with gaps).
- **Primary example:** **English** — build a story; tutor "The dragon woke up hungry." Child "So it flew to the village…" Practise connectives (so, but, then, suddenly).

## C. True/false, gaps & error-spotting

### 10. True/False Swat
- **Goal / when:** Check / retrieval. Fast, physical, fun.
- **How to run:** (1) Lay two cards: TRUE and FALSE (or child uses two hands / thumbs). (2) Tutor reads a statement; child "swats" the right card fast. (3) For every FALSE, child *fixes it* to make it true.
- **Adapt to any topic:** slot = *a true/false claim*. "All spiders are insects" (false), "7 is prime" (true), "Paris is the capital of Spain" (false).
- **Classai block(s):** `trueFalse`; follow with `shortText`/`speak` for the correction.
- **Primary example:** **Science** — "The Sun goes round the Earth." (False → child corrects: the Earth goes round the Sun.)

### 11. Fill the Gap (cloze)
- **Goal / when:** Check / practice. Tests recall in context.
- **How to run:** (1) Present a sentence/equation with a blank. (2) Child supplies the missing word/number. (3) Offer a word bank for support; remove it to fade scaffolding.
- **Adapt to any topic:** slot = *a sentence or equation with one key element removed*. "A baby cat is a ____." "6 × ___ = 42." "Henry VIII had ___ wives."
- **Classai block(s):** `fillBlank` (with optional `wordBank`); `numberEntry` for number gaps.
- **Primary example:** **Maths** — "____ + 8 = 15" (child types 7).

### 12. Spot My Mistake (error-spotting)
- **Goal / when:** Check / consolidation. Finding errors needs *deeper* understanding than producing answers.
- **How to run:** (1) Tutor shows a worked example or claim that's **wrong** (plant a realistic error). (2) Child finds the mistake, explains *why* it's wrong, fixes it. (3) Make the error a common misconception, not a silly slip.
- **Adapt to any topic:** slot = *a deliberately wrong answer/method*. A mis-added column, a sentence with a punctuation error, a mislabeled diagram, "½ + ½ = 2/4".
- **Classai block(s):** `trueFalse` (is this right?) then `shortText`; or `multiSelect` (find all the mistakes); or `whiteboard` to show the flawed work.
- **Primary example:** **Maths** — "23 + 9 = 32… no wait, 23 + 9 = 23 + 10 − 1 = 32." Show "= 33" and have child catch and fix it.

### 13. Myth-Buster
- **Goal / when:** Hook or check. Targets a known misconception head-on.
- **How to run:** (1) State a popular wrong belief. (2) Child votes true/false, then tutor reveals + explains. (3) Child restates the correct idea in their own words.
- **Adapt to any topic:** slot = *a common misconception from the topic's misconceptions list*. "Bigger numbers always have more digits." "Spiders are insects." "We use 10% of our brain."
- **Classai block(s):** `trueFalse` then `shortText`/`speak`.
- **Primary example:** **Maths** — "A longer decimal is always bigger: 0.45 > 0.5?" (False — bust the "more digits = bigger" myth.)

## D. Estimation, number & probability games

### 14. Estimation Station
- **Goal / when:** Hook / number sense. Builds reasoning about size and reasonableness.
- **How to run:** (1) Show a quantity/length/jar. (2) Child gives a *too-low* and *too-high* bound, then a best estimate, and **reasons aloud**. (3) Reveal the actual; discuss how close and why.
- **Adapt to any topic:** slot = *a quantity to estimate*. Sweets in a jar, length of the table in hand-spans, how many words on a page, how long ago the Romans were.
- **Classai block(s):** `numberEntry`; `image` to show the thing.
- **Primary example:** **Maths** — "About how many paperclips long is this pencil?" Bound it, estimate, then measure.

### 15. Dice Games
- **Goal / when:** Practice / fluency. Random numbers = endless fresh practice; cheap and physical.
- **How to run:** (1) Roll 1–2 dice. (2) Apply the rule (add, multiply, find difference, make the biggest 2-digit number). (3) Score over several rounds; beat your best.
- **Adapt to any topic:** slot = *an operation/rule applied to rolled numbers*. Number bonds, times tables, place value, "roll a word type and use it in a sentence" (label die faces).
- **Classai block(s):** `numberEntry`; `custom` for a bespoke board; `whiteboard` to show the roll.
- **Primary example:** **Maths** — roll two dice, multiply, say the product. Race the clock for 10 rolls.

### 16. Card Games (number/word decks)
- **Goal / when:** Practice / retrieval. A deck of cards is a flexible engine.
- **How to run:** (1) Use a deck (number cards 1–10, or topic word cards). (2) Play a frame: War/Top-Trumps (higher value wins), Snap (match a property), Make-the-target (combine cards to hit a number). (3) Keep rounds short.
- **Adapt to any topic:** slot = *a deck where cards have a comparable value or matchable property*. Fractions (which is bigger?), animal stats, vocabulary, element cards.
- **Classai block(s):** `multipleChoice`/`numberEntry` for the decision; `flashcards`/`custom` for the deck.
- **Primary example:** **Maths** — Fraction War: each flips a fraction card; bigger fraction wins the pair. Forces comparison.

### 17. Target Number / Make 10
- **Goal / when:** Practice / fluency, number bonds and mental calculation.
- **How to run:** (1) Give a target (10, 20, 100) and some numbers/operations. (2) Child combines them to hit the target, many ways. (3) Reward *multiple* solutions.
- **Adapt to any topic:** slot = *a target + pieces that combine to reach it*. Could be money (make 50p), or "make a sentence of exactly 7 words."
- **Classai block(s):** `numberEntry`; `shortText` for word versions.
- **Primary example:** **Maths** — "Numbers 2, 5, 6, 3 and + − ×. Make 20." (e.g. 5×3+6−... ) find as many as possible.

## E. Speaking, describing & guessing games

### 18. 20 Questions
- **Goal / when:** Hook / retrieval. Trains questioning and category thinking.
- **How to run:** (1) Tutor (or child) thinks of a topic item. (2) Other asks yes/no questions to narrow it down. (3) Cap at 20; discuss which questions were most powerful (splitting the field in half).
- **Adapt to any topic:** slot = *a set of items to guess from*. Animals, numbers (1–100), historical figures, shapes, countries.
- **Classai block(s):** `speak` (spoken Q&A), AI judges; `trueFalse` for each yes/no.
- **Primary example:** **Maths** — "I'm thinking of a number 1–100." Child asks "Is it even? Bigger than 50?" Practises bisection + number properties.

### 19. Taboo / Describe-It
- **Goal / when:** Check / consolidation, vocabulary depth.
- **How to run:** (1) Child must get the tutor to guess a target word **without saying it** (and without 2–3 banned words). (2) Swap roles. (3) Forces use of definitions, examples, synonyms.
- **Adapt to any topic:** slot = *a target term + banned words*. "Perimeter" (banned: around, edge), "evaporation," "adjective," a country.
- **Classai block(s):** `speak` (AI judges whether the description is accurate and avoids the word).
- **Primary example:** **Science** — describe "habitat" without saying *home* or *live*. ("It's the place an animal naturally finds its food and shelter…")

### 20. Charades (act it out)
- **Goal / when:** Hook / consolidation. Movement; great for verbs, processes, vocabulary.
- **How to run:** (1) Child acts out a word/concept silently; tutor guesses (or vice-versa). (2) Swap. (3) Good for kinaesthetic learners and re-energising.
- **Adapt to any topic:** slot = *an actable word/process*. Verbs, animals, water-cycle stages, an emotion, a historical job.
- **Classai block(s):** `speak` (child narrates/reports; the AI can't see, so child says what they're acting and tutor guesses, or describes); `custom`.
- **Primary example:** **English** — act out verbs: *stomp, whisper, sprint, tremble.* Builds "show don't tell" vocabulary for writing.

### 21. Pictionary / Draw-It-Guess-It
- **Goal / when:** Hook / check. Drawing forces a mental model.
- **How to run:** (1) Child draws a concept (no words/numbers); tutor guesses. (2) Swap. (3) The *attempt* to draw reveals understanding (e.g. drawing "evaporation").
- **Adapt to any topic:** slot = *a drawable concept*. A fraction, an animal, a setting from a book, a shape, a historical scene.
- **Classai block(s):** `whiteboard` (child draws), then `speak`/`shortText` to label/explain.
- **Primary example:** **Maths** — draw "three quarters." Watch whether they shade 3 of 4 *equal* parts.

### 22. Hot Seat
- **Goal / when:** Check / consolidation. Rapid Q&A on one role/topic.
- **How to run:** (1) Child becomes an expert/character "in the hot seat." (2) Tutor fires questions; child answers in role. (3) Great for empathy + recall (history, science, books).
- **Adapt to any topic:** slot = *a role or concept to embody*. A Roman soldier, a fraction explaining itself, an explorer, a character.
- **Classai block(s):** `speak`; `shortText` for written answers.
- **Primary example:** **History** — child is a Victorian child; tutor asks "What's your school like? What do you eat?" (recall of taught facts).

### 23. Would You Rather
- **Goal / when:** Hook. Instant engagement and reasoning; no wrong answer, but must justify.
- **How to run:** (1) Pose a two-option dilemma tied to the topic. (2) Child picks **and explains why** with topic reasoning. (3) Sneak the concept into the justification.
- **Adapt to any topic:** slot = *two options that force a topic-based comparison*. "Would you rather have ½ or ⅓ of the cake?" "…live in the Stone Age or Iron Age?"
- **Classai block(s):** `multipleChoice` (the choice) + `speak`/`shortText` (the why).
- **Primary example:** **Maths** — "Would you rather get 25% off or ¼ off?" (reveals they're equal — great discussion).

## F. Quiz, retrieval & flashcard games

### 24. Quiz-Quiz-Trade (1:1 version)
- **Goal / when:** Retrieval / practice. The classic peer game, adapted for a pair.
- **How to run:** (1) Each holds a question card. (2) Tutor quizzes child; child quizzes tutor; then **trade cards** and find new partners (in 1:1, just shuffle the deck). (3) Keep cards circulating so questions stay fresh.
- **Adapt to any topic:** slot = *a deck of Q&A cards*. Times tables, spellings, capitals, definitions.
- **Classai block(s):** `flashcards` for the deck; `multipleChoice`/`shortText` to answer.
- **Primary example:** **Geography** — capital cards: child asks "Capital of France?" tutor asks back "…of Italy?" trade and continue.

### 25. I Have… Who Has…
- **Goal / when:** Retrieval / practice. A self-checking chain.
- **How to run:** (1) Make a loop of cards: each says "I have X. Who has [clue for Y]?" (2) In 1:1, deal half each; pair reads alternately, racing to complete the loop. (3) The loop only closes if every link is right — self-correcting.
- **Adapt to any topic:** slot = *a closed loop of linked items*. "I have 12. Who has 6×2?"; word↔definition; country↔capital.
- **Classai block(s):** `matchPairs`/`flashcards`; `multipleChoice` for each "who has" link.
- **Primary example:** **Maths** — times-table loop: "I have 24. Who has 7×3?" → next card "I have 21…"

### 26. Bingo
- **Goal / when:** Practice / retrieval. Listening + recognition; very young-friendly.
- **How to run:** (1) Child fills a 3×3 grid with answers from a pool. (2) Tutor calls clues; child marks the matching answer. (3) First full line/grid wins — race the clock in 1:1.
- **Adapt to any topic:** slot = *answers + clues that point to them*. Numbers (tutor calls "6×4", child marks 24), vocabulary (call definition, mark word), shapes.
- **Classai block(s):** `categorize`/`multiSelect` to mark the grid; `image`/`table` to display it.
- **Primary example:** **Maths** — answer grid of products; tutor calls multiplication facts; child marks them. Reinforces tables.

### 27. Flashcard Games
- **Goal / when:** Retrieval / fluency. The workhorse of spaced practice.
- **How to run:** (1) Use a deck (term→answer). (2) Play a mode: *self-test* (recall before flip), *speed round* (how many in 60s), *Leitner sort* (cards you got right move to a "review later" pile, wrong ones come back soon). (3) Space sessions over days.
- **Adapt to any topic:** slot = *prompt→answer cards*. Spellings, facts, vocab, definitions, dates.
- **Classai block(s):** `flashcards` (display) + `multipleChoice`/`shortText`/`speak` to answer; ties to the app's spaced-review system (`server/src/memory/`).
- **Primary example:** **Spanish** — vocab deck; child sees "perro," says "dog"; correct cards spaced further out.

### 28. Brain-Dump (free recall)
- **Goal / when:** Retrieval / consolidation. Powerful, low-prep retrieval practice.
- **How to run:** (1) "Write/say everything you can remember about [topic]" for 60–90s, no looking. (2) Then check against notes and **add** what was missed in a different colour. (3) The gaps show what to revisit.
- **Adapt to any topic:** slot = *any taught topic*. Truly universal.
- **Classai block(s):** `shortText` or `speak`; AI compares to the topic's key concepts.
- **Primary example:** **Science** — "Tell me everything you know about the water cycle." Reveals which stages stuck.

## G. Ranking, prioritising & opinion

### 29. Ranking / Diamond-9
- **Goal / when:** Consolidation / discussion. Forces evaluation and justification.
- **How to run:** (1) Give 9 items (Diamond-9) or a list to rank. (2) Child arranges in a diamond (1 most important at top, 2, 3, 2, 1) or a line. (3) The *reasoning* is the learning — push "why is this above that?"
- **Adapt to any topic:** slot = *items to prioritise/order by a criterion*. Causes of an event, healthiest foods, most important inventions, hardest words.
- **Classai block(s):** `ordering`; `categorize` for tiered importance.
- **Primary example:** **History** — rank the causes of the Great Fire of London spreading (wooden houses, wind, close streets, slow response…).

### 30. Continuum / Human Bar Chart
- **Goal / when:** Hook / discussion. Position on a line = strength of agreement or a value.
- **How to run:** (1) Draw a line (agree↔disagree, or small↔big). (2) Child places items/themselves along it and explains. (3) Move things to compare.
- **Adapt to any topic:** slot = *items to place on a spectrum*. Number line placement, "how alive is it?", fact↔opinion strength, probability (impossible↔certain).
- **Classai block(s):** `numberLine`; `ordering`.
- **Primary example:** **Maths** — probability line: place "it will rain," "a coin lands heads," "the sun rises tomorrow" from impossible to certain.

## H. Mystery, puzzle & investigation

### 31. Mystery / Whodunit
- **Goal / when:** Hook / practice. Clues that require topic knowledge to solve.
- **How to run:** (1) Present a puzzle with a hidden answer and a set of clues. (2) Child uses each clue to eliminate options until one remains. (3) Reveal; child explains the deduction.
- **Adapt to any topic:** slot = *a hidden answer + clues that test the concept*. "My number is even, > 20, a multiple of 5, < 40." A mystery material from its properties; a missing word from sentence clues.
- **Classai block(s):** `numberEntry`/`multipleChoice` for the solution; `steps`/`richText` to reveal clues one at a time.
- **Primary example:** **Maths** — "I'm even, between 30 and 50, and in the 3× table. Who am I?" (36 or 42 — find both, then narrow with one more clue.)

### 32. Scavenger Hunt
- **Goal / when:** Practice / consolidation. Active, around-the-house search.
- **How to run:** (1) Give a list of things to find that match a property. (2) Child hunts the room/house and reports/photographs each. (3) Count and classify finds.
- **Adapt to any topic:** slot = *a property to find examples of in the environment*. Shapes, materials (something metal/wooden/transparent), 2D vs 3D, things that use electricity, items starting with a sound.
- **Classai block(s):** `speak`/`shortText` to report finds; `numberEntry` for counts; `categorize` to sort what they found.
- **Primary example:** **Maths** — "Find 5 things shaped like a cylinder." Child reports a tin, a roll, a cup… then explains what makes a cylinder.

### 33. Code / Cipher Crack
- **Goal / when:** Hook / practice. Puzzle-solving with a hidden rule.
- **How to run:** (1) Give an encoded message or pattern. (2) Child works out the rule and decodes. (3) Then *encode* one back — making is harder than breaking.
- **Adapt to any topic:** slot = *a rule-based transformation*. A=1 B=2 cipher (maths + spelling), a +3 number pattern, a sound-symbol code, an algorithm to follow.
- **Classai block(s):** `fillBlank`/`shortText` for the decoded message; `numberEntry` for number patterns.
- **Primary example:** **Computing** — decode "1-16-16-12-5" using A=1…Z=26 (→ APPLE). Bridges to algorithms.

### 34. Notice & Wonder
- **Goal / when:** Hook. Opens curiosity with no pressure; great lesson starter.
- **How to run:** (1) Show an intriguing image/object/number. (2) Child lists what they **notice** and what they **wonder**. (3) Use their wonderings to launch the lesson.
- **Adapt to any topic:** slot = *anything intriguing related to today's idea*. A weird graph, a fossil, an old photo, a pattern of numbers.
- **Classai block(s):** `image`/`emojiViz` to display; `speak`/`shortText` to capture notice/wonder.
- **Primary example:** **Science** — show a photo of a Venus flytrap. "What do you notice? What do you wonder?" → launch into how it eats.

## I. Producing — write, draw, build

### 35. Quick-Write (one-minute paper)
- **Goal / when:** Consolidation / check / exit. Fast written processing.
- **How to run:** (1) Pose one prompt. (2) Child writes for 60–90s without stopping. (3) Tutor reads and responds to the thinking.
- **Adapt to any topic:** slot = *one focusing prompt*. "Explain X to a younger child." "What's still confusing?" "Why does this work?"
- **Classai block(s):** `shortText`; `speak` for a spoken version.
- **Primary example:** **English** — "In one minute, explain what a fronted adverbial is, with an example."

### 36. Draw-It (concept sketch)
- **Goal / when:** Explain / check / consolidation. A drawing externalises the mental model.
- **How to run:** (1) Ask for a labelled sketch of a concept. (2) Child draws and annotates. (3) Tutor reads the drawing for misconceptions (a common diagnostic move).
- **Adapt to any topic:** slot = *a concept that has a visual form*. A bar model, a food chain, the water cycle, a story map, a circuit.
- **Classai block(s):** `whiteboard` (`animate` for the tutor's model); `shortText` to label.
- **Primary example:** **Science** — "Draw and label a food chain with a plant, a rabbit and a fox." Arrows reveal whether they get energy flow.

### 37. Build-It (model / manipulatives)
- **Goal / when:** Explain / example / practice. Concrete-first learning (CPA).
- **How to run:** (1) Use physical objects (counters, blocks, Lego, coins, household items). (2) Child builds the concept. (3) Connect the model to the picture and then the symbol.
- **Adapt to any topic:** slot = *a concept you can represent with objects*. Place value with bundles of 10, fractions with folded paper, a sentence with word cards, a circuit, a map with blocks.
- **Classai block(s):** `whiteboard`/`image` to show the build; `numberEntry`/`speak` to report; `custom`.
- **Primary example:** **Maths** — build 34 with ten-bundles and ones (3 tens + 4 ones); then trade for subtraction.

### 38. Role-Play / Simulation
- **Goal / when:** Example / consolidation. Living the concept builds memory and empathy.
- **How to run:** (1) Set a scenario and roles. (2) Pair acts it out (shopkeeper + customer; reporter + witness; two atoms). (3) Debrief what the role revealed.
- **Adapt to any topic:** slot = *a scenario that enacts the concept*. A shop (money/change), a court (persuasion), an interview (history), a dialogue (language).
- **Classai block(s):** `speak`; `numberEntry` for money totals; `custom`.
- **Primary example:** **Maths** — play shop: tutor "This costs 65p, here's £1." Child gives correct change and counts it out.

## J. Frames, boards & exit games

### 39. Board-Game Frame
- **Goal / when:** Practice / fluency. A reusable board turns any question set into a game.
- **How to run:** (1) Use a simple track (snakes-and-ladders style). (2) To move, answer a question from the deck. (3) Right = move/roll; wrong = stay. Race to the end or beat-your-time.
- **Adapt to any topic:** slot = *a question deck*. Any subject's facts, problems, or vocabulary become the "to-move" questions.
- **Classai block(s):** `custom` for the board; `multipleChoice`/`numberEntry`/`shortText` for the questions; `whiteboard` to show the board.
- **Primary example:** **Times tables** — answer a fact to roll; first to the finish square. Endless reuse with any deck.

### 40. Exit-Ticket Games (3-2-1, traffic light)
- **Goal / when:** Recap / check at the door. A structured close that doubles as assessment.
- **How to run:** (1) End with a tiny structured recall: **3-2-1** (3 things I learned, 2 I found interesting, 1 question I still have) or **traffic light** (green = got it, amber = mostly, red = lost — and *why*). (2) Tutor uses it to plan next time (feeds spaced review).
- **Adapt to any topic:** slot = *any lesson's content*. Universal closing frame.
- **Classai block(s):** `shortText`; `multipleChoice` for the traffic-light self-rating; sets up the app's next-lesson recommendation.
- **Primary example:** **Any** — "3 things about fractions you learned, 2 you'd tell a friend, 1 thing still fuzzy."

### 41. Splat / Quick-Tap
- **Goal / when:** Retrieval / warm-up. Fast recognition under mild time pressure.
- **How to run:** (1) Display a grid of answers. (2) Tutor calls a clue; child "splats" (taps/points to) the matching answer as fast as possible. (3) Speed round; beat your best time.
- **Adapt to any topic:** slot = *a grid of answers + clues*. Numbers, words, capitals, shapes, symbols.
- **Classai block(s):** `multipleChoice`/`multiSelect` over a displayed `table`/`image` grid.
- **Primary example:** **Maths** — grid of numbers; tutor calls "the number that's 3 more than 9" → child taps 12.

### 42. Think-Aloud (modelled metacognition)
- **Goal / when:** Explain. The tutor models *how an expert thinks*, then hands it over.
- **How to run:** (1) Tutor works a problem out loud, narrating every decision and even false starts. (2) Then child thinks aloud on the next one. (3) Makes invisible reasoning visible.
- **Adapt to any topic:** slot = *any problem with a reasoning process*. A maths method, planning a sentence, deducing from a source.
- **Classai block(s):** `steps`/`richText` (display the modelled thinking); then `speak`/`shortText` for the child's think-aloud.
- **Primary example:** **Maths** — "I see 38 + 27. I'll add the tens first: 30 + 20 = 50. Then 8 + 7 = 15. 50 + 15 = 65." Then child does 46 + 35 aloud.

### 43. Beat-the-Clock / Personal Best
- **Goal / when:** Practice / fluency. Solo competition that motivates without a peer.
- **How to run:** (1) Set a timed challenge (how many facts in 60s; sort the pile before the sand runs out). (2) Record the score. (3) Next time, **beat your best** — progress is visible and motivating.
- **Adapt to any topic:** slot = *any quick, repeatable task*. Fact recall, sorting, spelling, reading words.
- **Classai block(s):** `numberEntry`/`multipleChoice` rapid items; the app logs the score.
- **Primary example:** **Spanish** — "How many colours can you say in 60 seconds?" Track and beat it weekly.

### 44. Two Truths and a Lie
- **Goal / when:** Check / consolidation. Disguised error-detection.
- **How to run:** (1) Give three statements about the topic; two true, one false. (2) Child spots the lie and corrects it. (3) Then child makes their own set for the tutor.
- **Adapt to any topic:** slot = *three statements, one wrong*. Facts about a number, an animal, a country, a character.
- **Classai block(s):** `multipleChoice` (which is the lie?) + `shortText` (fix it).
- **Primary example:** **Geography** — "France is in Europe. Its capital is Madrid. People speak French." (Lie: capital is Paris.)

## How to combine these

A whole lesson can be stitched from frames: **Notice & wonder** (hook) → **Think-aloud** (explain) → **Build-it** together (example) → **Spot-my-mistake** (check) → **Board-game** or **Bingo** (practice) → **3-2-1** (recap). Mix modalities so the child reads, says, sorts, and makes within one session. See `activity-bank-by-subject.md` for subject-signature games, and `teaching/lesson-anatomy.md` for how beats fit together.
