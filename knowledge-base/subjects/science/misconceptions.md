# Science — Consolidated misconceptions (KS1–KS2)

Children arrive at science with **strong, sensible-feeling wrong ideas** built from everyday experience. They are not careless errors — they are coherent little theories that explain what the child sees ("heavy things obviously fall faster", "the Sun obviously moves across the sky"). Because they make sense to the child, you cannot fix them by simply stating the right answer; the wrong idea has to be **replaced** with a better explanation through an experience or argument it can't account for.

**How to use this file.** Each entry gives the **wrong idea**, **why** children hold it (the faulty model), and a concrete **fix** — usually a predict→check demonstration. The single most valuable move in primary science is to **surface the misconception, confront it with evidence, and offer the correct idea as the better explanation.** Always start with the child's prediction; the moment a confident prediction fails is when the mental model updates. These entries also appear in the relevant year files; this is the consolidated quick-reference.

## Physics & forces

### "Heavier objects fall faster than lighter ones." (Y5 Forces)
- **Why:** everyday experience seems to prove it — a stone hits the ground before a feather, a brick before a leaf. The child concludes weight controls falling speed.
- **The hidden factor:** the difference is **air resistance**, not weight. Air slows light, spread-out things (feather, leaf) much more than dense, compact ones.
- **Fix:** drop two **identical** sheets of paper, one **scrunched into a tight ball**. Same paper, same weight — yet the ball falls fast and the flat sheet floats down. The only difference is air resistance. (Famously, with the air removed, a feather and a hammer fall together.) Gravity pulls everything toward Earth equally; air resistance is what makes light, flat things drift.

### "There's no gravity in space / gravity needs air."
- **Why:** astronauts are seen floating, so children conclude gravity is absent in space, and link gravity to air.
- **Fix:** gravity acts through empty space and is what keeps the Moon orbiting Earth and Earth orbiting the Sun. Astronauts float because they are **continuously falling around** the Earth (in orbit), not because gravity has switched off.

### "Things stop moving because they run out of force or energy." (Y3 Forces)
- **Why:** every moving thing the child pushes slows down and stops on its own, so stopping feels natural and force feels like a fuel that runs out.
- **Fix:** things stop because **friction** (and air/water resistance) acts against them. On a very low-friction surface (ice, a smooth floor) a push keeps something sliding far longer — remove the friction and it would never stop.

### "Sound can travel through empty space." (Y4 Sound)
- **Why:** films play explosions and engines in space, so children assume sound travels there.
- **Fix:** sound is vibrations passing through a **medium** (air, water, solids). In a vacuum there's nothing to vibrate, so no sound travels. (A ringing alarm in a jar goes silent as the air is pumped out.)

### "Loud and high mean the same thing." (Y4 Sound)
- **Why:** both are "more" of a sound to a young child.
- **Fix:** **volume** (loud/quiet) depends on the **size** of the vibration; **pitch** (high/low) depends on the **speed** of the vibration. A guitar string plucked harder = louder; tightened = higher. They change independently.

### "Electricity only needs to reach the bulb (one wire is enough)." (Y4 Electricity)
- **Why:** children draw a single wire from battery to bulb, imagining electricity is "used up" at the bulb.
- **Fix:** a circuit must be a **complete loop** — out of the cell, through the bulb, and **back** to the cell. Break the loop anywhere and the bulb goes out. Build it and snip a gap to prove it.

### "Adding more bulbs makes them all brighter." (Y6 Electricity)
- **Why:** more bulbs sounds like more light.
- **Fix:** in a **series** loop the bulbs **share** the voltage, so each one gets **dimmer**. Adding more **cells** is what makes a bulb brighter.

## Light & seeing

### "We see because light (or rays) comes out of our eyes." (Y6 Light)
- **Why:** looking feels active, like the eyes reach out and touch what we see ("I look at it"). This is one of the most persistent science misconceptions.
- **Fix:** seeing is the eye **receiving** light. Light travels from a **source**, **bounces off** an object, and goes **into** the eye. The proof: in a completely dark room you cannot see — however hard you stare — because there is **no light** to bounce into your eyes. If sight came from the eyes, the dark wouldn't matter.

### "Dark is a thing that fills the room / comes in." (Y3 Light)
- **Why:** when the light goes off, darkness seems to flood in, so it feels like a substance.
- **Fix:** dark is simply the **absence of light** — nothing comes in; light just leaves. Turn a torch on and the dark "disappears" because light fills the space.

### "A shadow is a reflection / a copy of the object." (Y3 Light)
- **Why:** shadows are the same shape as the object, so they feel like an image of it.
- **Fix:** a shadow is where an **opaque object blocks the light**, leaving a dark gap with no light — it is the *absence* of light, not a bounced image. (Reflection is light bouncing; a shadow is light blocked.)

### "Sunglasses make it safe to look at the Sun." (Y3 Light – safety)
- **Why:** sunglasses cut brightness, so they seem to make it safe.
- **Fix:** **never** look directly at the Sun, even through sunglasses — it can permanently damage the eyes. Always teach Sun safety explicitly.

## Earth & space

### "The Sun moves across the sky / around the Earth." (Y5 Earth & space)
- **Why:** it genuinely looks that way — the Sun rises, arcs across the sky and sets, while the ground feels perfectly still.
- **Fix:** the **Earth spins (rotates)** once every 24 hours; the Sun stays put and only *appears* to move. Model it with a torch (Sun) and a globe: spin the globe and a town moves from day into night while the torch never moves. "Sunset" really means "my part of the Earth has turned away from the Sun."

### "Night happens because the Sun goes away / switches off." (Y5 Earth & space)
- **Why:** the Sun disappears at sunset.
- **Fix:** the Sun shines constantly; your side of the Earth has simply **rotated away** from it. On the other side of the world it's daytime at that very moment.

### "It's summer because Earth is closer to the Sun." (Y1/Y5 seasons)
- **Why:** closer should mean warmer — a reasonable guess.
- **Fix:** seasons are caused by the **tilt** of the Earth, which changes how directly sunlight hits us and how long the days are — not by distance. (When it's summer in the UK it's winter in Australia at the same distance, which distance alone can't explain.)

## Living things, plants & the body

### "Plants get their food from the soil." (Y3 Plants)
- **Why:** we "feed" plants by potting them in soil and adding fertiliser, so soil looks like plant food.
- **Fix:** plants **make their own food** in their leaves using **light** (photosynthesis). From the soil they take **water and minerals**, not food. Evidence: a plant grown in plain water (hydroponics) with light still grows; a plant in soil but kept in the dark goes pale and dies.

### "A whale (or dolphin) is a fish because it lives in the sea." (Y1 Animals)
- **Why:** children group animals by **where they live** rather than by body features.
- **Fix:** group by features — a whale **breathes air**, has **no scales or gills**, and **feeds its young milk**, so it's a **mammal** that happens to live in water.

### "A caterpillar and a butterfly are different animals." (Y5 Life cycles)
- **Why:** they look completely different.
- **Fix:** it's the **same animal** at different stages of its life cycle. **Metamorphosis** transforms the body: egg → caterpillar → chrysalis → butterfly.

### "All bugs (spiders, woodlice) are insects." (Y6 Classification)
- **Why:** "bug" means any small crawling creature in everyday speech.
- **Fix:** **insects** have **6 legs and 3 body parts**; **spiders** have **8 legs** (arachnids); woodlice have many legs (crustaceans). All invertebrates, but not all insects — count the legs.

### "An animal changes its own body in its lifetime to suit its environment (e.g. a giraffe stretched its neck)." (Y6 Evolution)
- **Why:** "adaptation" sounds like an individual making an effort.
- **Fix:** individuals don't redesign themselves. Those **born** with helpful variations survive better and **pass them on**; over **many generations** the species changes. Evolution acts across generations, not within one lifetime.

### "Offspring are exact copies of their parents." (Y6 Inheritance)
- **Why:** children resemble their parents.
- **Fix:** offspring inherit a **mix** of features and show **variation** — similar but never identical (except in asexual reproduction). This variation is the raw material for evolution.

### "Muscles push the bone to move it." (Y3 Animals)
- **Why:** movement looks like pushing.
- **Fix:** muscles can **only pull** (by shortening). That's why they work in **pairs** — one pulls the bone one way, its partner pulls it back. Feel the biceps and triceps take turns as you bend your arm.

### "A car/anything that moves is alive." (Y2 Living things)
- **Why:** children use **movement** as the test for life.
- **Fix:** movement isn't enough — living things also **grow, feed, and reproduce**. A car does none of these. (And plants are alive but don't run around.)

## Materials & changes

### "Condensation is air turning into water." (Y4 States of matter)
- **Why:** water droplets appear on a cold glass "out of nowhere," so the air itself seems to become water.
- **Fix:** the air already contains invisible **water vapour** (a gas). When it touches a **cold** surface it **cools and condenses** into liquid droplets. It's vapour becoming liquid — not air becoming water. (A glass of iced drink "sweats" because of vapour from the room, not the drink leaking.)

### "When a puddle dries, the water disappears / is destroyed." (Y4 States of matter)
- **Why:** the water can't be seen any more.
- **Fix:** the water **evaporated** into invisible **water vapour** in the air. It still exists — it's just spread out as a gas (and will fall again as rain in the water cycle).

### "When sugar/salt dissolves, it disappears." (Y5 Materials)
- **Why:** the solid can no longer be seen.
- **Fix:** it's still there in the **solution** — you can taste it, and **evaporating** the water leaves the solid behind. Dissolving hides it; it doesn't destroy it.

### "All changes can be undone." (Y5 Reversible/irreversible changes)
- **Why:** melting↔freezing and dissolving↔evaporating undo easily, so children over-generalise.
- **Fix:** **irreversible** changes (burning, cooking, rusting, some chemical reactions) make a **new material** that **can't** be turned back. Clues a new material formed: gas/bubbles, heat, light, or a colour change.

### "Cold 'comes in' / cold is a thing that travels." (general – heat)
- **Why:** opening a freezer or a door, we feel cold rush in, so cold seems to be a substance that moves.
- **Fix:** there is no such thing as "cold" travelling — only **heat** moves, always from warmer to cooler. We feel "cold" when **heat leaves** our body (or the room) and moves to the colder thing. A coat doesn't "keep the cold out"; it **slows heat escaping** from you.
