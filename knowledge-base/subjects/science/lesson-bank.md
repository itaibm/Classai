# Science — Lesson Bank (ready-to-run mini lessons, question bank & activities)

Six mini lesson outlines mapped to the app's beats (**hook → explain → example → check → practice → recap**), each on a high-value science topic, then a question bank (~12 with answers + a likely wrong answer each) and the highest-value practice activities. British spelling. Safety notes explicit (adults handle hot/sharp/mains; never look at the Sun; never taste unknown substances).

---

## Lesson 1 — Fair testing and variables

- **Hook:** "A toy car rolled further on wood than carpet — but I also pushed it harder. What went wrong?"
- **Explain:** change **one** thing (the variable), keep the rest the **same**, measure the result; name the three variable jobs.
- **Example:** "Does ramp height affect rolling distance?" — change height; keep car, surface and push the same; measure distance.
- **Check:** "Testing if water amount affects plant growth — what must stay the same?" (light, soil, pot, temperature, plant). Watch for changing two things at once.
- **Practice:** categorize items into change / keep the same / measure for a given question.
- **Recap:** "How many things do you change in a fair test?" (one).

## Lesson 2 — Food chains and food webs

- **Hook:** "What would happen if all the rabbits disappeared from a field?"
- **Explain:** energy flows Sun → producer → consumers; the **arrow points to the eater**; webs link many chains.
- **Example:** grass → grasshopper → frog → snake → hawk; name producer, herbivore, predators, top predator.
- **Check:** "Which way does a food-chain arrow point?" (towards the animal that eats). Watch for fox → rabbit.
- **Practice:** ordering organisms into a correct chain; build a web and predict the effect of removing one species.
- **Recap:** "What is a producer, and where does the energy start?" (a plant; the Sun).

## Lesson 3 — The water cycle

- **Hook:** "A puddle disappears on a sunny day — where did the water go?"
- **Explain:** evaporation → condensation → precipitation → collection, driven by the Sun; the **same** water recycled.
- **Example:** the puddle evaporated to vapour; a mirror fogs because vapour condenses on cool glass. (Adult handles any kettle/steam.)
- **Check:** "What makes clouds form?" (vapour cooling and condensing). Watch for "the water is gone".
- **Practice:** ordering the cycle stages; fillBlank label a water-cycle diagram.
- **Recap:** "Is new water made?" (no — recycled).

## Lesson 4 — Reversible vs irreversible change

- **Hook:** "Can you un-bake a cake? Can you un-melt chocolate?"
- **Explain:** ask **is a new material made?** No → reversible/physical; yes (gas, new colour, heat, smell) → irreversible/chemical.
- **Example:** melt/freeze ice = reversible; vinegar + bicarb fizzes (new gas) = irreversible. (Adult supervises; never taste.)
- **Check:** "Is burning toast reversible?" (no — new material/ash). Watch for "all changes can be undone".
- **Practice:** categorize changes reversible/irreversible and tick the "new material" clues.
- **Recap:** "What's the test for a chemical change?" (a new material is made).

## Lesson 5 — Deeper circuits: bulb brightness

- **Hook:** "Why is a torch with two batteries brighter than one?"
- **Explain:** needs a complete loop; **more cells = brighter** (more voltage); **more bulbs in one loop = dimmer** (shared). Batteries only — never mains.
- **Example:** 1 cell+1 bulb (normal) → add a cell (brighter) → instead add a second bulb (both dimmer).
- **Check:** "Add a second bulb to the same loop — brighter or dimmer?" (dimmer). Watch for "electricity gets used up".
- **Practice:** multipleChoice predict brightness for cell/bulb combos; matchPairs components to circuit symbols.
- **Recap:** "Why won't a bulb light in a broken circuit?" (no complete loop).

## Lesson 6 — Shadows and how we see

- **Hook:** make a shadow puppet — "how do I make the shadow bigger?"
- **Explain:** light travels in straight lines; opaque objects block it → shadow; we see by light reflecting **into** our eyes (not out of them). Never look at the Sun.
- **Example:** hand between torch and wall; move towards the torch → shadow grows; dark-room test → no light, no sight.
- **Check:** "Why can't you see in a totally dark room?" (no light entering your eyes). Watch for "light comes out of our eyes".
- **Practice:** predict-and-check shadow size as the object moves; draw light rays source → object → eye.
- **Recap:** "Why does a shadow form?" (opaque object blocks straight-line light).

## Question bank (with answer + a likely wrong answer)

1. **In a fair test, how many variables do you change?** A: One. Wrong: "as many as you like."
2. **Testing if light affects plant growth — name one control variable.** A: Water (or soil/pot/temperature). Wrong: "the amount of light" (that's the one you change).
3. **Which way does a food-chain arrow point?** A: Towards the eater (direction energy flows). Wrong: from eater to food.
4. **What is a producer?** A: A plant that makes its own food using light. Wrong: "an animal that eats plants."
5. **What is evaporation?** A: Liquid water heating into water vapour (gas). Wrong: "water disappearing forever."
6. **What forms clouds?** A: Water vapour cooling and condensing into droplets. Wrong: "smoke rising."
7. **Reversible or irreversible: frying an egg?** A: Irreversible (new material). Wrong: Reversible.
8. **A clue that a chemical change happened?** A: A new material — fizz/gas, new colour, heat or smell. Wrong: "it got smaller."
9. **Add a second cell to a circuit — what happens to the bulb?** A: Brighter (more voltage). Wrong: "it stays the same."
10. **Why can't you see in a completely dark room?** A: No light enters your eyes (we see reflected light). Wrong: "your eyes need to send out light."
11. **Move an object closer to the torch — its shadow…?** A: Gets bigger. Wrong: gets smaller.
12. **Can sound travel through empty space?** A: No — it needs a medium. Wrong: Yes (like in films).
13. **What makes a sound higher in pitch?** A: Faster vibrations. Wrong: "making it louder."
14. **What enquiry suits "how does the Moon's shape change over a month?"** A: Observing over time. Wrong: "a fair test."

## Highest-value practice activities (science)

- **Predict → Explain → Check** — always ask "what do you think will happen and why?" before any demo; the surprise updates the mental model.
- **Hands-on home investigations** — ramps, dissolving, melting, growing seeds, shadows, string telephones, vinegar + bicarb (with the stated safety notes).
- **Design a fair test** — for a real question, name the variable to change, the controls to keep the same, and what to measure.
- **Record then graph** — put results in a labelled table, choose bar vs line, and read values back off the chart.
- **Confront a misconception head-on** — surface the wrong idea, give an experience it can't explain, offer the correct idea (e.g. the dark-room "we see with our eyes" test).
- **Sort and classify** — use and build yes/no classification keys for minibeasts, leaves or materials.
- **Observe over time** — keep a log of a germinating seed, melting ice, or the Moon's phases over days.
- **Secondary research** — look up what can't be observed (inside the Earth, dinosaurs, the heart) using trusted sites, then explain it back.
