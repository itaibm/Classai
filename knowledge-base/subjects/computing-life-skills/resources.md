# Computing & Life Skills — Resources

Curated, **real, free** resources for ages 5–11, aligned to the UK National Curriculum (Computing) and PSHE/life skills. Each entry names a **stable platform + the exact topic/section** so the tutor can resolve a real page — **no invented video IDs.** Prefer linking at the platform/topic level; if a precise URL can't be confirmed, the tutor should build its own whiteboard/slideshow rather than guess.

## Coding & computational thinking

- **Scratch — scratch.mit.edu** (MIT, free). The block-coding platform itself, plus the built-in **"Tutorials"** (Getting Started, Animate a Name, Make it Fly, Pong Game, Make a Chase Game, Create a Story). Best for: sequence, loops, variables, conditionals, creating content. No account needed to try; free account to save.
- **ScratchJr — free tablet app (iPad/Android), scratchjr.org.** Block coding for ages 5–7. Built-in starter activities and a free curriculum/activity guide on the site. Best for: first programs, sequence, events, simple loops.
- **code.org — code.org** (free). **"CS Fundamentals" Courses A–F** (mapped by age) and the **Hour of Code** activities (e.g. *Minecraft*, *Dance Party*, *Flappy*). Best for: algorithms, loops, conditionals, variables, debugging — self-paced, blocks-based, no login needed for Hour of Code.
- **Hour of Code — hourofcode.com/learn** (free). One-hour, self-contained coding activities for all ages and reading levels. Best for: a quick, motivating first taste of programming.
- **Barefoot Computing — barefootcomputing.org** (free, BCS/National Centre for Computing Education; free account). Excellent **unplugged** activities and concept pages: *Algorithms, Decomposition, Patterns, Abstraction, Logical reasoning, Debugging*, plus "Crazy Character Algorithms." Best for: computational-thinking-without-a-computer and teacher guidance.

## Computing curriculum (BBC Bitesize)

- **BBC Bitesize KS1 Computing — bbc.co.uk/bitesize (KS1 Computing).** Topics include *What is an algorithm?, Following and writing instructions, Using technology safely, Creating digital content.* Best for: ages 5–7 introductions with short articles and activities.
- **BBC Bitesize KS2 Computing — bbc.co.uk/bitesize (KS2 Computing).** Topics include *Algorithms & debugging, What is the internet?, Computer networks, Using search engines, Reliable information online, Spreadsheets, Branching databases, Creating digital content.* Best for: ages 7–11.
- **BBC Bitesize KS1/KS2 Maths — "Pictograms," "Bar charts," "Handling data."** Best for: the data & information topic (links to maths statistics).

## Online safety

- **ThinkUKnow (CEOP, UK National Crime Agency) — thinkuknow.co.uk** (free). Age-banded online-safety resources and animations: **Jessie & Friends** (ages 4–7) and **Play Like Share** (ages 8–10), plus the **CEOP report** tool for older children. Best for: personal information, kind behaviour, telling a trusted adult.
- **Internet Matters — internetmatters.org** (free). Age-specific advice and activities on **privacy & identity, screen time, age ratings/PEGI, critical thinking & fake news, cyberbullying.** Best for: parent guidance plus child-friendly activities.
- **Childline — childline.org.uk** (free, NSPCC). Calming activities, "toolbox," and friendship/feelings advice, plus a **free confidential helpline (0800 1111)** for children (mention to KS2). Best for: getting help, feelings, friendship worries.
- **BBC Bitesize KS2 — "Online safety," "Being kind online / cyberbullying," "Fake news / reliable information."** Best for: short, age-appropriate lessons.
- **PEGI — pegi.info** (free, official game age-ratings body). Explains what PEGI age labels and content descriptors mean. Best for: age-appropriate choices.

## Life skills — money & critical thinking

- **BBC Bitesize KS1/KS2 — "What is money?", "Needs and wants", "Saving and spending", "Budgeting".** Best for: financial literacy across the age range.
- **BBC Bitesize KS2 / BBC Newsround — "Fact and opinion", "Spotting fake news", "Asking questions / thinking critically".** Best for: critical thinking, facts vs opinions, evaluating information.

## Life skills — health, wellbeing & social skills

- **BBC Bitesize KS1/KS2 PSHE — "Healthy eating / Eatwell Guide", "Sleep", "Exercise", "Feelings and emotions", "Growth mindset", "Friendships and resolving conflict".** Best for: the health & wellbeing and social-skills topics.
- **NHS — nhs.uk** (free, official). *"Healthier families" (Change4Life) food & recipes; "How much sleep do children need?"; "Physical activity guidelines for children"; "Children's emotional wellbeing".* Best for: accurate, trustworthy health guidance.
- **ClassDojo — "Big Ideas: Growth Mindset" (with Mojo)** — free animated video series on perseverance and "the power of yet." Best for: growth mindset & resilience.

## Life skills — practical (time & safety)

- **BBC Bitesize KS1/KS2 — "Telling the time", "Days, months and the calendar".** Best for: the time/calendar topic (links to maths).
- **Topmarks — topmarks.co.uk** (free). Interactive **"Telling the Time"** clock games (e.g. *Teaching Clock*) and other maths games. Best for: hands-on time practice.
- **THINK! — think.gov.uk** (free, UK Department for Transport). Children's **road safety** resources (incl. *Tales of the Road*) and the *Stop, Look, Listen, Think* message. Best for: road safety.
- **St John Ambulance — sja.org.uk** (free). **"First aid for kids" / "First aid lessons for young people"** and simple emergency guidance (including calling 999). Best for: first-aid awareness.

## Notes for the tutor

- All platforms above are well-known, stable and free to access for the topics named. When a `video` block is wanted, prefer these named sources at the **channel/topic/playlist** level.
- For PSHE and online-safety topics, always pair a resource with the warm message: *if anything worries you, tell a trusted adult — it's never your fault.*
- If a precise URL can't be resolved, build a whiteboard/slideshow from this knowledge base instead of guessing a link.
