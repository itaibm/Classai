# Israel History & Heritage — Curated Free Resources

Real, stable, free resources, named by **platform + exact topic/section title** (never invented video IDs). The tutor should resolve these by searching the named platform for the named title, or point the parent there. When a `video` block can't be resolved to a real URL, fall back to a self-built `slideshow`/`whiteboard` rather than guessing a URL (see `INGESTION.md` §5).

These are chosen because they're **well known, free, child-appropriate and stable**, and they cover **heritage, ancient history, the festivals, and a light, balanced modern strand**. Maps to **subjectKey: `history`**.

> **Even-handedness note.** For the **modern/contested** topics (Zionism, 1948, present-day Israel), prefer **neutral reference and country-profile** sources, and parent-guided reading; avoid any single advocacy source. The festivals and ancient-heritage resources below are mostly explanatory and gentle.

---

## Whole-subject hubs

- **BimBam** (`bimbam.com`, archive on `reformjudaism.org`): award-winning **animated Jewish videos for children** — Bible stories (Abraham, the Exodus, King David), the festivals (Passover, Hanukkah, Sukkot, Purim, the High Holy Days), and Israel. Free; over 400 videos. **The first stop for animated heritage stories.**
- **PJ Library** (`pjlibrary.org`): free, high-quality **Jewish children's books and stories** (ages 0–12) covering the festivals, Bible heroes, Israel and immigration — read-aloud heritage. Has an **Israel Hub** (`pjlibrary.org/israel`). Excellent for story-first teaching.
- **My Jewish Learning** (`myjewishlearning.com`): clear **"101" explainer articles** on every festival, period and idea (e.g. "Passover 101," "Shavuot 101," "Eliezer Ben-Yehuda") — best as a **parent/tutor reference**.
- **Sefaria** (`sefaria.org`): free library of Jewish texts in **plain English** (Tanakh, the Books of the Maccabees, the Book of Esther) — look up the actual story passages; parent-guided.
- **The Israel Museum, Jerusalem** (`imj.org.il`): one of the world's leading **art and archaeology** museums; online collections and the famous **"Model of Jerusalem in the Second Temple period."** Great for **artefacts and "how do we know?"**; parent-guided.
- **National Geographic Kids** (`natgeokids.com`): kid-level **country profile and facts** for modern Israel (map, people, the Dead Sea) — neutral and engaging.
- **BBC Bitesize — "Judaism"** (`bbc.co.uk/bitesize`): UK-curriculum **Religious Education** pages on Jewish beliefs, the Tanakh, the Temple, Jerusalem and the festivals — clear and balanced, written for children.

## Ancient Israel (Patriarchs → Hasmoneans)

- **BimBam — Torah/Bible story animations** — Abraham and the Patriarchs, the Exodus and Moses, King David and Goliath (heritage narratives, animated).
- **PJ Library — Bible-hero and Patriarch story books** — Abraham, Moses, David, Esther (read-aloud).
- **BBC Bitesize — "Judaism: the Tanakh"** — the Hebrew Bible, the prophets (Nevi'im), and how Jews understand these texts.
- **BBC Bitesize — "Judaism: the Temple and Jerusalem"** — the First and Second Temples and the place of Jerusalem in Jewish life.
- **The Israel Museum — "Model of Jerusalem in the Second Temple period"** — online images of the great outdoor model; for picturing the Second Temple (parent-guided).
- **Sefaria — Tanakh in English / "The Books of the Maccabees"** — read the actual story passages for the kings, the Temple and the Maccabees (parent-guided).
- **BimBam — "What Is Hanukkah?" / Maccabees** — the Maccabean revolt and the rededication of the Temple, for children.

## Festivals & heritage

- **BimBam — festival videos** — "Passover," "Shavuot," "Sukkot," "Rosh Hashanah," "Yom Kippur," "What Is Hanukkah?," "Purim," "Tu B'Shvat," "Yom HaAtzmaut" (animated, child-friendly; the **best single source** for festivals).
- **PJ Library — festival story books** — a story book for each major festival (Passover, Hanukkah, Sukkot, Purim, Rosh Hashanah and more).
- **My Jewish Learning — festival "101" articles** — "Passover 101," "Sukkot 101," "Shavuot 101," "Hanukkah 101," "Purim 101," "Rosh Hashanah 101," "Yom Kippur 101," "Tu Bishvat 101" (parent/tutor reference for accurate detail).
- **BBC Bitesize — "Jewish festivals" (RE)** — Pesach, Shavuot, Sukkot, Rosh Hashanah, Yom Kippur, Hanukkah explained for the UK curriculum.
- **Keren Kayemeth LeIsrael–JNF (`kkl-jnf.org`)** — background on **Tu BiShvat tree-planting** in Israel (the modern custom; parent reference).

## Exile, revival & the modern state (light, balanced)

- **My Jewish Learning — "Eliezer Ben-Yehuda" and "The Revival of Hebrew"** — accurate, readable background for the Hebrew-revival topic (parent/tutor reference).
- **PJ Library — immigration / aliyah and "coming to Israel" story books** — child-level stories of Jews moving to Israel from many countries.
- **National Geographic Kids — "Israel country profile"** — neutral facts on the modern country: map, capital, people, languages, the Dead Sea, flag — good for **national symbols** and **modern diverse society**.
- **BBC Bitesize — Israel / Middle East geography & society pages** — parent-guided, balanced background on the modern country.
- **Encyclopaedia entries (Britannica Kids / reputable reference) — "Zionism," "Theodor Herzl," "Founding of Israel 1948"** — for older children, **parent-guided**, choosing balanced, age-appropriate, neutral sources for the contested modern topics.

## Usage notes for the tutor

- **Story first, then meaning.** For heritage narratives (Patriarchs, Exodus, festivals), lead with **BimBam** (animated) or **PJ Library** (books); draw out the meaning afterwards.
- **"How do we know?"** Use the **Israel Museum** model and artefacts, and **Sefaria** texts, to separate **tradition** from **documented evidence** honestly — never present "tradition says" as "scientists proved."
- **Keep the modern era light and even-handed.** For Zionism, 1948 and present-day Israel, prefer **neutral country-profile and reference** sources, parent-guided; avoid advocacy from any side, and voice that the land is home to **more than one people**.
- Always name the **platform + exact title** when suggesting a resource; **never fabricate a specific video URL or ID**. If a real link can't be resolved, build the visual in-app (`slideshow`/`whiteboard`) instead.
- See `videos.md` for the recommended video channels and search terms.
