# World Language (Spanish) — Subject Overview (UK National Curriculum, KS2, Years 3–6)

This subject covers **primary Spanish** for ages 7–11, aligned to the **UK National Curriculum for Languages at Key Stage 2** (Years 3–6). Maps to Classai `subjectKey` **`world_language`**. The single content file is `spanish-ks2.md`, holding 14 topics in the per-topic contract (`_TEMPLATE.md`). Read this overview to understand *how* to teach a primary world language well, then teach from the topic file.

> KS2 languages is statutory in England; the curriculum says children should "make substantial progress in one language" and lists oracy (listen, speak), literacy (read, write) and intercultural understanding. Schools may choose any language — this knowledge base chooses **Spanish** because it is phonetically regular and motivating for young learners.

## What primary language learning is for

The KS2 aim is **not fluency** — it is to lay foundations and light a spark: a child who can understand and join in simple spoken Spanish, read and copy familiar words, and is curious about another culture. The National Curriculum names these goals, and every lesson should touch them:

1. **Listen** attentively to spoken language and show understanding.
2. **Speak** in sentences, using familiar vocabulary and basic structures, with increasingly accurate pronunciation.
3. **Read** carefully and show understanding of words, phrases and simple writing.
4. **Write** phrases from memory and adapt them to create new sentences.
5. **Grammar**: understand basic structures — gender of nouns, simple agreement of adjectives, key features of verb conjugation.
6. **Intercultural understanding**: appreciate stories, songs, poems and rhymes; learn about life in Spanish-speaking countries.

Classai has a **`speak` block**, so this subject leans hardest into **listening and speaking**. Reading and writing support speaking; they are not the main event at KS2.

## How to teach it: comprehensible input + lots of speaking

The most important principle is **comprehensible input**: children acquire a language by understanding messages slightly above their current level (often written "i+1"). You make input understandable with pictures, gesture, tone, cognates and context — *not* by translating every word. The job of the tutor is to be understood while staying in Spanish as much as is sensible.

Practical rules for the Classai tutor:

- **Keep instructions and explanations in English; keep the practice in Spanish.** A 7-year-old should not have to decode "now repeat after me" in Spanish — that wastes effort. So: explain the task in English ("Listen, then say it back"), then run the actual content (*Hola, ¿cómo estás?*) in Spanish. This is the standard primary approach and the one this file follows.
- **Speak it before you read it.** Always present new language by ear first (the tutor says it, the child repeats), *then* show the spelling. Spanish spelling is regular, but the sound must come first.
- **Model → choral repeat → individual.** Say the word; child echoes; child says it alone; child uses it in a tiny exchange.
- **Gesture and pictures carry meaning.** Pair every noun with an image or action. "*el perro* 🐕" is understood; "*el perro* = the dog" is merely translated.
- **Comprehensible, not perfect.** Praise communication. A child who says "*yo gusta el rojo*" has communicated — celebrate it, then gently model the correct "*me gusta el rojo*." Do not red-pen primary speakers.

## Pronunciation: Spanish is regular — teach the rules early

Spanish is wonderfully consistent: letters almost always make the same sound. Teach these from the start and refer back constantly. Pronunciation hints in this knowledge base use plain English approximations (children, not phoneticians, are the audience).

| Spanish letter | Sound | Example | Hint |
|---|---|---|---|
| **a e i o u** | ah · eh · ee · oh · oo | *casa, mesa, sí, no, uno* | Pure, short, never gliding |
| **j** / **g** (before e,i) | throaty "h" | *naranja, gente* | Like clearing your throat softly |
| **h** | silent | *hola, hermano* | Never pronounced |
| **ll** | "y" | *llama, amarillo* | "ya-ma", "ama-ree-yo" |
| **ñ** | "ny" | *España, mañana* | "ny" as in ca**ny**on |
| **v** | soft "b" | *vaca, verde* | Between b and v |
| **z** / **c** (before e,i) | "s" (Latin America) / "th" (Spain) | *azul, cinco* | Either is fine for KS2 |
| **r / rr** | tapped / rolled | *pero / perro* | Encourage a flick of the tongue; don't stress over a full roll |
| **qu** | "k" | *qué, quince* | The u is silent |

Most words stress the **second-to-last syllable** unless there is a written accent (´), which tells you exactly where to stress: *plá-ta-no*, *a-diós*. The accent is a pronunciation signpost, not decoration.

## Spaced vocabulary recycling (the engine of retention)

Children forget new words fast unless they meet them again. Build **spaced recycling** into every lesson — this is exactly what Classai's spaced-review system supports:

- **Recap starter (3–4 min):** quickly revisit words from past topics before new content. Numbers and colours come back again and again because they reappear inside later topics (age, prices, descriptions).
- **Recycle inside new topics:** new topics deliberately reuse old vocabulary — colours reappear in "describe an animal", numbers reappear in "age" and "prices", verbs reappear everywhere. The scope below is built so each topic feeds the next.
- **Little and often beats long and rare.** Five minutes of Spanish most days beats one long weekly session. Songs, counting routines and greetings are perfect daily micro-practice.
- **Interleave, don't block.** Mix a new colour question with an old number question with a greeting. Retrieval from a mixed set is what makes memory stick.

## How to run a Spanish lesson (quick playbook)

1. **Greeting routine in Spanish** — start every lesson with *¡Hola! ¿Cómo estás?* The child answers. This is free daily speaking practice.
2. **Retrieval starter** — 3–4 quick recall items from past topics (a colour, two numbers, a greeting).
3. **Hook** — connect to the child's world (a pet, a favourite food, a football score in Spanish).
4. **Input** — the tutor models new language with pictures/gesture; child *listens* first.
5. **Choral & individual repetition** — child echoes, then says it alone. Pronunciation focus.
6. **Speaking task** — a tiny real exchange (question → answer), a `speak` block, a role-play.
7. **Anchor in writing (light)** — show the spelling, child reads/copies a couple of words.
8. **Close in Spanish** — *¡Adiós! ¡Hasta luego!*

## KS2 Spanish scope & sequence (Years 3–6)

The curriculum spirals: each year revisits speaking, listening, phonics and core vocabulary, adding more structure. A sensible KS2 progression:

| Year | Listening / speaking focus | Vocabulary added | Grammar introduced |
|------|---------------------------|------------------|--------------------|
| **3** (7–8) | Greetings, simple Q&A, repeating & joining in songs/rhymes; clear single-sound phonics | Greetings & names, numbers 0–10 (→20), colours, classroom objects | Nouns have gender: *el / la*; *un / una* |
| **4** (8–9) | Short spoken sentences; answering & asking simple questions; following instructions | Numbers to 31, family, animals/pets, days, months, birthdays, weather | Plurals (+s/+es); *los/las*; possessive *mi/mis* |
| **5** (9–10) | Conversations of several exchanges; expressing likes/dislikes; describing | Food & drink, hobbies, the body, telling the time | Verbs: *me gusta(n)*; key present-tense verbs (*ser, tener, gustar*); adjective **agreement** & position |
| **6** (10–11) | Sustained simple conversation; short descriptions of self/others; reading & adapting short texts | Consolidation + descriptions of people, opinions with reasons | Regular **-ar** present tense (yo/tú/él); joining ideas with *y, pero, porque*; combining gender + number agreement |

By the end of KS2 a child should hold a simple spoken conversation (greet, give name/age, say where they live, describe likes and a pet/person), understand familiar spoken Spanish, read and copy familiar words, and know that nouns have gender and adjectives must agree.
