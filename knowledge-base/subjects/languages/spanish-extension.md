# Spanish — Extension Topics (KS2, Years 4–6)

Ten **additional** Spanish topics that go beyond the fourteen basics already in `spanish-ks2.md` (greetings, numbers, colours, classroom, family, animals, food, days/months, weather, hobbies, body, time, regular **-ar** present, adjective agreement). These extend into **more verbs, simple past & near future, opinions & connectives, classroom questions, directions, shopping/café role-plays, story retelling, and culture** — plus a **speaking-practice activity bank** at the end. Same conventions as the core file: **instructions to the child in English, the language to practise in Spanish; pronunciation hints in plain English with the STRESSED syllable in CAPITALS; always say new language by ear first, then show the spelling.** Spanish leans on listening and speaking, so almost every Activity uses the Classai `speak` block.

## 1. More verbs — regular -er and -ir present tense (comer, vivir)

**Year / Key Stage:** Year 6 (ages 10–11) · **Est. minutes:** 35 · **Subject strand:** Grammar – present tense (-er / -ir verbs)

**Prerequisites:** Simple present-tense -ar verbs (core Topic 13)

### Key concepts
- Besides **-ar** verbs, Spanish has **-er** and **-ir** verbs (*comer* to eat, *vivir* to live); you swap the ending the same way.
- For *yo* the ending is **-o** for all three families; *tú* and *él/ella* differ slightly: **-es / -e**.
- You usually drop *yo/tú* because the ending already shows who.

### Prior knowledge assumed
- The child can conjugate regular -ar verbs (*hablo, hablas, habla*).

### Teach it (the explanation)
You know -ar verbs swap their tail. Two more families do the same: **-er** (like **comer**, to eat) and **-ir** (like **vivir**, to live). Chop the ending, add the new one:
- **comer** → yo **como** (I eat), tú **comes**, él/ella **come**.
- **vivir** → yo **vivo** (I live), tú **vives**, él/ella **vive**.

Notice the pattern: **yo always ends in -o** (just like -ar: *hablo, como, vivo*). For *tú* and *él/ella*, -er and -ir share **-es / -e**. Useful verbs: *comer* (eat), *beber* (drink), *leer* (read), *correr* (run), *vivir* (live), *escribir* (write).

**Worked example.** "Where do you live?" → *¿Dónde vives?* → answer *Vivo en…* + your town. Conjugate *comer* for "she eats": stem *com-* + *-e* → **come** → *Ella come* (or just *come*).

### Hooks (real-world angles)
- Say where you live: *Vivo en Londres.*
- Describe meals: *Como pan, bebo leche.*
- Report on a friend: *Mi hermano lee mucho.*

### Misconceptions (wrong answer → why → fix)
- **Wrong:** *yo come* / *yo vive* (using the él form for "I"). **Why:** -er/-ir endings new. **Fix:** **yo always ends in -o**: *como, vivo*.
- **Wrong:** using -ar endings (*comas, coma*) on -er/-ir verbs. **Why:** over-applying one pattern. **Fix:** -er/-ir use *-es/-e* for tú/él, not *-as/-a*.
- **Wrong:** "¿Dónde vivas?" **Fix:** *vivir* is -ir → *vives*; "where do you live?" = *¿Dónde vives?*

### Checks (ready-to-use)
- **Q:** Say "I eat" and "I live." **A:** *como* / *vivo*.
- **Q:** Ask someone where they live. **A:** *¿Dónde vives?*
- **Q:** "She writes." **A:** *(ella) escribe.*

### Activity / practice
**Speak:** verb chain — tutor gives a verb + person (*comer* — yo); child says the form in a full sentence (*Yo como*). Cycle yo/tú/él through *comer, beber, vivir, escribir*. Finish with the child answering *¿Dónde vives?* (`speak`; optional `fillBlank`: *Yo ___ (vivir) en…*).

## 2. Talking about the past — the simple past (el pretérito), gently

**Year / Key Stage:** Year 6 (ages 10–11) · **Est. minutes:** 35 · **Subject strand:** Grammar – simple past (introduction)

**Prerequisites:** -ar present tense; -er/-ir present tense

### Key concepts
- To say what you **did** (finished past), Spanish uses the **pretérito**; for *yo* (-ar verbs) the ending is **-é**, for él/ella **-ó**.
- A few super-useful past forms to learn as **whole chunks**: *fui* (I went/was), *comí* (I ate), *jugué* (I played).
- Keep it light at KS2 — learn a handful of high-frequency past phrases, not full tables.

### Prior knowledge assumed
- The child can use the present tense and knows some verbs (*jugar, comer, ir*).

### Teach it (the explanation)
To talk about what you **did** yesterday — finished, in the past — Spanish changes the verb ending. This is the **pretérito** (simple past). At your level, learn just a few really useful ones as **chunks** you can drop into a sentence:
- **fui** (fwee) — I went / I was → *Fui al parque* (I went to the park).
- **comí** (ko-MEE) — I ate → *Comí pizza.*
- **jugué** (hoo-GEH) — I played → *Jugué al fútbol.*
- **vi** (vee) — I saw → *Vi una película* (I saw a film).

The pattern hint for regular **-ar** verbs: *yo* ends **-é** (*hablé* = I spoke), *él/ella* ends **-ó** (*habló* = he/she spoke). But don't drill tables — collect handy past phrases and use them to say what you did at the weekend.

**Worked example.** "Yesterday I played football and ate pizza": *Ayer jugué al fútbol y comí pizza.* (*Ayer* = yesterday; join with *y* = and.) Notice *jugué* and *comí* are past chunks.

### Hooks (real-world angles)
- Tell what you did at the weekend: *Fui al parque, jugué…*
- A "yesterday" diary line each lesson.
- Compare now vs then: *Hoy juego… ayer jugué…*

### Misconceptions (wrong answer → why → fix)
- **Wrong:** using the present for "I did" (*juego* for "I played"). **Why:** only present known. **Fix:** past needs the past form — *jugué*, *comí*, *fui*.
- **Wrong:** "*Yo fui jugué*" (two verbs piled up). **Why:** over-marking. **Fix:** one past verb per action: *Fui al parque. Jugué al fútbol.*
- **Wrong:** *comí* stressed "CO-mee." **Fix:** the accent shows the stress → ko-**MEE**.

### Checks (ready-to-use)
- **Q:** Say "I went to the park." **A:** *Fui al parque.*
- **Q:** Say "Yesterday I ate pizza." **A:** *Ayer comí pizza.*
- **Q:** Say "I played tennis." **A:** *Jugué al tenis.*

### Activity / practice
**Speak:** "What did you do yesterday?" Tutor asks *¿Qué hiciste ayer?*; child answers with two past chunks joined by *y* (*Fui al parque y jugué al fútbol*). (`speak`; optional `matchPairs`: present → past chunk, *juego→jugué*.)

## 3. The near future — voy a + verb (what I'm going to do)

**Year / Key Stage:** Year 6 (ages 10–11) · **Est. minutes:** 30 · **Subject strand:** Grammar – near future (futuro próximo)

**Prerequisites:** -ar present tense; hobbies (plain verb after a helper)

### Key concepts
- To say what you're **going to do**, use **voy a + the plain verb**: *Voy a jugar* (I'm going to play).
- It's the same easy pattern as English "going to" — the second verb stays in its plain (-ar/-er/-ir) form.
- *Vas a…* = you're going to…; *va a…* = he/she's going to…

### Prior knowledge assumed
- The child knows plain verbs (*jugar, comer, nadar*) and *me gusta + plain verb*.

### Teach it (the explanation)
Spanish has a lovely easy way to talk about the **near future** — exactly like English "I'm **going to**…". Use **voy a** (BOY ah) + the **plain verb**:
- **Voy a jugar** — I'm going to play.
- **Voy a comer** — I'm going to eat.
- **Voy a nadar** — I'm going to swim.

The second verb stays plain (don't change it) — just like after *me gusta*. For other people: **vas a** (you're going to), **va a** (he/she's going to). Add *mañana* (tomorrow) or *este fin de semana* (this weekend) for when.

**Worked example.** "Tomorrow I'm going to play football": *Mañana voy a jugar al fútbol.* Build it: time (*mañana*) + *voy a* + plain verb (*jugar*) + rest (*al fútbol*).

### Hooks (real-world angles)
- Plan your weekend: *Voy a nadar y voy a dibujar.*
- Say what you'll do after the lesson.
- Ask a friend: *¿Qué vas a hacer mañana?*

### Misconceptions (wrong answer → why → fix)
- **Wrong:** *Voy a juego* (conjugating the second verb). **Why:** over-conjugating. **Fix:** after *voy a*, keep the verb plain: *voy a **jugar***.
- **Wrong:** dropping the *a* (*voy jugar*). **Why:** English has no extra word. **Fix:** you need *voy **a** + verb*.
- **Wrong:** using *voy a* for "I want" (it means "going to"). **Fix:** want = *quiero*; going to = *voy a*.

### Checks (ready-to-use)
- **Q:** Say "I'm going to swim." **A:** *Voy a nadar.*
- **Q:** Say "Tomorrow I'm going to play tennis." **A:** *Mañana voy a jugar al tenis.*
- **Q:** Ask "What are you going to do tomorrow?" **A:** *¿Qué vas a hacer mañana?*

### Activity / practice
**Speak:** weekend planner. Tutor asks *¿Qué vas a hacer este fin de semana?*; child gives two plans with *voy a* joined by *y*. Then the child asks the tutor back. (`speak`.)

### Resources
- **BBC Bitesize KS2 Spanish — *Talking about the future / plans***.
- **Light Speed Spanish — *Near future (ir a + infinitive)* lesson**.

## 4. Opinions, reasons & connectives (opiniones y conectores)

**Year / Key Stage:** Year 5–6 (ages 9–11) · **Est. minutes:** 35 · **Subject strand:** Speaking – opinions & joining ideas

**Prerequisites:** Hobbies & free time (*me gusta… porque*); adjectives

### Key concepts
- Give richer opinions: *me encanta* (I love), *no me gusta* (I don't like), *odio* (I hate), *prefiero* (I prefer).
- **Connectives** make longer, more interesting sentences: *y* (and), *pero* (but), *porque* (because), *también* (also), *o* (or).
- Back an opinion with a **reason** using *porque* + a describing word.

### Prior knowledge assumed
- The child can say *me gusta / no me gusta* and give a simple *porque* reason.

### Teach it (the explanation)
You can already say *me gusta*. Now make your opinions richer and your sentences longer.
Opinion words (strongest to weakest): **me encanta** (I love) · **me gusta** (I like) · **no me gusta** (I don't like) · **odio** (OH-dee-o, I hate) · **prefiero** (I prefer).
**Connectives** glue ideas together so you sound fluent: **y** (and) · **pero** (but) · **porque** (because) · **también** (also/too) · **o** (or).

Combine them: *Me encanta el fútbol **porque** es divertido, **pero** no me gusta el tenis.* One opinion, a reason, and a contrast — that's a great KS2 sentence.

**Worked example.** Build an opinion sentence: *Me gusta dibujar **y también** me gusta leer, **pero** prefiero nadar **porque** es divertido.* Notice four connectives stitching the ideas together.

### Hooks (real-world angles)
- Rate your hobbies, foods or subjects with reasons.
- "Agree or disagree" — react to the tutor's opinions with *pero* or *también*.
- Mini-debate: *Prefiero… porque…*

### Misconceptions (wrong answer → why → fix)
- **Wrong:** *me encanto* / *prefiero* mis-stressed. **Fix:** *me encanta* (fixed chunk for one thing); *prefiero* = pre-fee-EH-ro.
- **Wrong:** listing ideas with no connectives ("me gusta fútbol me gusta tenis"). **Why:** speaking in fragments. **Fix:** join with *y, pero, también* for flow.
- **Wrong:** *porque* (because) vs *¿por qué?* (why?). **Fix:** *porque* (one word) gives a reason; *¿por qué?* (two words) asks a question.

### Checks (ready-to-use)
- **Q:** Say "I love football because it's fun." **A:** *Me encanta el fútbol porque es divertido.*
- **Q:** Give the Spanish for: and, but, because. **A:** *y, pero, porque.*
- **Q:** Say "I like to draw but I prefer to swim." **A:** *Me gusta dibujar pero prefiero nadar.*

### Activity / practice
**Speak:** opinion challenge — child gives an opinion on three things (a sport, a food, a subject), each with a *porque* reason, using at least three different connectives. (`speak`.)

### Resources
- **BBC Bitesize KS2 Spanish — *Giving opinions / connectives***.
- **Light Speed Spanish — *Connectors and opinions* lesson**.

## 5. Classroom questions & survival language (¿puedo…?, no entiendo)

**Year / Key Stage:** Year 4–5 (ages 8–10) · **Est. minutes:** 25 · **Subject strand:** Speaking – classroom interaction

**Prerequisites:** Greetings; classroom objects

### Key concepts
- Useful **classroom phrases** let you stay in Spanish during a lesson: *¿Puedo…?* (May I…?), *No entiendo* (I don't understand), *¿Cómo se dice…?* (How do you say…?).
- These are **fixed chunks** — learn and reuse them, don't translate word by word.
- Asking in Spanish *is* real communication and great practice.

### Prior knowledge assumed
- The child knows greetings and a few classroom nouns.

### Teach it (the explanation)
To have a real Spanish lesson, you need phrases to **ask for help and join in** — "survival language." Learn these as whole chunks:
- **¿Puedo ir al baño?** (PWEH-do) — May I go to the toilet?
- **No entiendo** (no en-tee-EN-do) — I don't understand.
- **¿Cómo se dice "dog"?** (KOH-mo se DEE-the) — How do you say "dog"?
- **¿Qué significa…?** — What does … mean?
- **¿Puedes repetir, por favor?** — Can you repeat, please?
- **Otra vez, por favor** — Again, please. · **No sé** — I don't know.

These keep the conversation going *in Spanish* even when you're stuck — which is exactly what good language learners do.

**Worked example.** You hear a new word and don't get it. Instead of switching to English, say: *No entiendo. ¿Qué significa "perro"?* The tutor answers *"dog"*, and you reply *¡Gracias!* You stayed in Spanish and learned a word.

### Hooks (real-world angles)
- Use one survival phrase in every lesson until it's automatic.
- Role-play being the teacher and the pupil.
- "Stuck? Say it in Spanish" challenge.

### Misconceptions (wrong answer → why → fix)
- **Wrong:** translating "Can I…" literally and getting tangled. **Why:** word-by-word. **Fix:** learn *¿Puedo…?* as a ready-made chunk.
- **Wrong:** switching to English the moment it's hard. **Why:** easier. **Fix:** use *No entiendo / ¿Puedes repetir?* to stay in Spanish.
- **Wrong:** *¿Cómo se dice?* mispronounced. **Fix:** KOH-mo se DEE-the (Spain) / DEE-se (Latin America).

### Checks (ready-to-use)
- **Q:** Say "I don't understand." **A:** *No entiendo.*
- **Q:** Ask "How do you say 'cat'?" **A:** *¿Cómo se dice "cat"?*
- **Q:** Ask "Can you repeat, please?" **A:** *¿Puedes repetir, por favor?*

### Activity / practice
**Speak:** survival-phrase drill — tutor describes a situation in English ("you didn't hear the word"), child responds with the right Spanish chunk (*¿Puedes repetir?*). Cycle through 5–6 situations. (`speak`; optional `matchPairs`: situation → phrase.)

### Resources
- **BBC Bitesize KS2 Spanish — *Classroom language / useful phrases***.
- **Oak National Academy — *Spanish: classroom instructions* unit**.

## 6. Directions & places in town (¿dónde está…? / direcciones)

**Year / Key Stage:** Year 5 (ages 9–10) · **Est. minutes:** 30 · **Subject strand:** Speaking – directions & locations

**Prerequisites:** Classroom objects (nouns/gender); numbers

### Key concepts
- Ask and give simple **directions**: *¿Dónde está…?* (Where is…?), *a la derecha/izquierda* (right/left), *todo recto* (straight on).
- Name **places in town**: *el parque, la escuela, la tienda, el museo*.
- Say where something is: *está cerca/lejos* (it's near/far).

### Prior knowledge assumed
- The child knows *un/una* and a few place nouns; can count.

### Teach it (the explanation)
Imagine you're in a Spanish town and need to find your way. Ask: **¿Dónde está…?** (DON-de es-TA) — "Where is…?" Then learn the direction words:
- **a la derecha** (de-RE-cha) — to the right · **a la izquierda** (eeth-kee-ER-da) — to the left · **todo recto** (REK-to) — straight on · **gira** — turn.

Places in town: **el parque** (park) · **la escuela** (school) · **la tienda** (shop) · **el museo** (museum) · **el restaurante** · **la estación** (station) · **el baño** (toilet). Say how far: **está cerca** (it's near) · **está lejos** (it's far).

**Worked example.** "Where is the park?" → *¿Dónde está el parque?* → answer: *Todo recto, y después a la derecha. Está cerca.* (Straight on, then right. It's near.)

### Hooks (real-world angles)
- Direct the tutor around the room or a toy town in Spanish.
- A treasure hunt with Spanish direction clues.
- Map a Spanish-speaking city and ask *¿Dónde está…?*

### Misconceptions (wrong answer → why → fix)
- **Wrong:** mixing up *derecha* (right) and *izquierda* (left). **Fix:** *derecha* ≈ "direct/right hand"; drill with hand gestures.
- **Wrong:** *¿Dónde es…?* for "where is it." **Why:** wrong verb. **Fix:** location uses *está* → *¿Dónde **está**…?*
- **Wrong:** *izquierda* mispronounced. **Fix:** eeth-kee-ER-da; say it slowly in parts.

### Checks (ready-to-use)
- **Q:** Ask "Where is the school?" **A:** *¿Dónde está la escuela?*
- **Q:** Say "to the right" and "straight on." **A:** *a la derecha* / *todo recto.*
- **Q:** Say "it's near." **A:** *está cerca.*

### Activity / practice
**Speak:** direction role-play — tutor asks *¿Dónde está el/la…?*; child answers with two direction words and *cerca/lejos*. Swap roles. (`speak`; optional `matchPairs`: place → picture.)

### Resources
- **BBC Bitesize KS2 Spanish — *Directions / Places in town***.
- **Rockalingua — *Directions / Places* song and game**.

## 7. Shopping & café role-plays (de compras / en el café)

**Year / Key Stage:** Year 5–6 (ages 9–11) · **Est. minutes:** 35 · **Subject strand:** Speaking – transactional language

**Prerequisites:** Numbers 0–31 (prices); food & drink; *quiero*

### Key concepts
- Run a real **transaction**: ask for things, ask the price, pay and thank.
- Key phrases: *¿Cuánto cuesta?* (How much?), *Quiero…* (I want…), *¿Algo más?* (Anything else?), *Aquí tiene* (Here you are).
- Prices use numbers + *euros*; politeness (*por favor, gracias*) matters.

### Prior knowledge assumed
- The child can count, name foods, and use *quiero… por favor*.

### Teach it (the explanation)
Let's "buy" things in Spanish — a really satisfying conversation. The shopkeeper or waiter and the customer take turns:
- Customer: **Hola, quiero un zumo, por favor.** (I want a juice, please.)
- Ask the price: **¿Cuánto cuesta?** (KWAN-to KWES-ta) — How much is it? → **Cuesta dos euros.** (It costs 2 euros.)
- Server: **¿Algo más?** (Anything else?) → **No, gracias / Sí, también quiero…**
- Pay & close: **Aquí tiene.** (Here you are.) — **Gracias. Adiós.**

For a café, add: **una mesa para dos** (a table for two), **la cuenta, por favor** (the bill, please). Prices reuse your numbers — great recycling.

**Worked example.** Café order, step by step:
1. Waiter: *¡Hola! ¿Qué quieres?*
2. Child: *Quiero un chocolate caliente, por favor.*
3. Child: *¿Cuánto cuesta?* → Waiter: *Dos euros.*
4. Waiter: *¿Algo más?* → Child: *No, gracias.*
5. Both: *¡Adiós!*

### Hooks (real-world angles)
- Set up a pretend Spanish café or market stall with play money.
- Order your real favourite snack in Spanish.
- A "menu" of Spanish foods (*churros, tortilla, zumo*).

### Misconceptions (wrong answer → why → fix)
- **Wrong:** *¿Cuánto es?* vs *¿Cuánto cuesta?* — both are fine; teach *cuesta* as the standard "how much does it cost?".
- **Wrong:** forgetting *por favor / gracias*. **Why:** focusing on the request. **Fix:** politeness is part of the phrase — always tag it on.
- **Wrong:** *quiero* over-changed (*quero/quieron*). **Fix:** keep *quiero* as a fixed chunk for "I want."

### Checks (ready-to-use)
- **Q:** Ask "How much is it?" **A:** *¿Cuánto cuesta?*
- **Q:** Order a juice politely. **A:** *Quiero un zumo, por favor.*
- **Q:** What does *¿Algo más?* mean? **A:** "Anything else?"

### Activity / practice
**Speak:** full café/shop role-play — child plays the customer through all five steps (greet, order, ask price, "no thanks," goodbye), then swaps to be the server. Use real prices with Spanish numbers. (`speak`; optional `numberEntry` for totalling prices.)

### Resources
- **BBC Bitesize KS2 Spanish — *Shopping / In a café / Prices***.
- **Light Speed Spanish — *At the café / shopping role-plays***.

## 8. Retelling a simple story (cuentos sencillos)

**Year / Key Stage:** Year 6 (ages 10–11) · **Est. minutes:** 35 · **Subject strand:** Speaking & listening – narrative

**Prerequisites:** -ar/-er/-ir present; simple past chunks; connectives

### Key concepts
- Retell a short, familiar story in Spanish using **sequencing words**: *primero* (first), *después* (then), *al final* (in the end).
- Use simple **present or past chunks** plus connectives to string events together.
- Repetitive, predictable stories (*La oruga muy hambrienta* — The Very Hungry Caterpillar) are perfect for retelling.

### Prior knowledge assumed
- The child can make simple sentences, join with connectives, and use a few past chunks.

### Teach it (the explanation)
A great way to use *lots* of Spanish at once is to **retell a simple story**. Pick a familiar, repetitive one (a caterpillar that eats through foods; *Los tres cerditos* — the three little pigs). You don't need fancy grammar — chain short sentences with **sequencing words**:
- **Primero** (pree-MEH-ro) — first · **después / luego** — then/next · **y entonces** — and then · **al final** — in the end.

Use simple present (*come, va*) or past chunks (*comió* — ate). Repetition helps: many children's stories repeat a line ("…y todavía tenía hambre" — and was still hungry), so you reuse the same Spanish again and again.

**Worked example.** Retell part of the caterpillar story: *Primero, la oruga come una manzana. Después come dos peras. Y al final, ¡es una mariposa!* (First the caterpillar eats an apple. Then two pears. And in the end, it's a butterfly!) Sequencing words carry the story.

### Hooks (real-world angles)
- Retell a story you know well, with the pictures as prompts.
- Act out the story with toys, narrating in Spanish.
- Make your own 3-sentence story (*primero… después… al final…*).

### Misconceptions (wrong answer → why → fix)
- **Wrong:** trying to translate every English word of the original. **Why:** aiming for perfection. **Fix:** retell the *gist* in simple Spanish — communication, not translation.
- **Wrong:** no sequencing words, so events blur. **Fix:** anchor each step with *primero / después / al final*.
- **Wrong:** switching to English mid-story when stuck. **Fix:** use a survival phrase (*¿Cómo se dice…?*) and carry on.

### Checks (ready-to-use)
- **Q:** Give the Spanish for: first, then, in the end. **A:** *primero, después, al final.*
- **Q:** Retell two events of any story using *primero* and *después*. **A:** e.g. *Primero… Después…* (any sensible two steps).
- **Q:** Say "and in the end, it's a butterfly." **A:** *Y al final, es una mariposa.*

### Activity / practice
**Speak:** story retell — child retells a short, familiar story in 4–6 simple Spanish sentences, using at least three sequencing words and one connective. Tutor prompts with pictures. (`speak`; optional `ordering`: put scrambled story sentences in order.)

### Resources
- **BBC Bitesize KS2 Spanish — *Stories / rhymes in Spanish***.
- **Rockalingua / YouTube — *cuentos infantiles en español* (simple animated children's stories)** (parent-guided).

## 9. Festivals & celebrations in the Spanish-speaking world

**Year / Key Stage:** Year 4–6 (ages 8–11) · **Est. minutes:** 30 · **Subject strand:** Intercultural understanding – culture & festivals

**Prerequisites:** Days, months & birthdays (for dates); greetings

### Key concepts
- Spanish-speaking countries have lively **festivals**: *Navidad* (Christmas), *Reyes Magos* (Three Kings, 6 Jan), *La Tomatina*, *Día de los Muertos* (Mexico).
- Learning about festivals builds **intercultural understanding** — appreciating other cultures, not just words.
- Some festivals are shared, some are special to one country — and dates link to the months topic.

### Prior knowledge assumed
- The child can say dates and months in Spanish and greet people.

### Teach it (the explanation)
Language comes with **culture**. Spanish-speaking countries celebrate brilliant **festivals** (*las fiestas*):
- **Navidad** (Christmas) and, on **el 6 de enero**, **los Reyes Magos** (the Three Kings) — in Spain, children often get presents from the Kings on this day, not just on Christmas Day.
- **La Tomatina** (Spain, August) — a huge, friendly **tomato fight** in the town of Buñol.
- **El Día de los Muertos** (the Day of the Dead, Mexico, early November) — a colourful, *joyful* festival **remembering loved ones** who have died, with marigolds, sugar skulls and music. (Important: it's happy and respectful, not scary.)

Learning these helps you understand the **people** who speak Spanish, in Spain *and* across Latin America — Mexico, Argentina, Colombia and more.

**Worked example.** Match a festival to its date and place: *los Reyes Magos* → **el 6 de enero**, Spain (children get gifts); *el Día de los Muertos* → early November, Mexico (remembering loved ones, joyful). Saying the date in Spanish recycles your months.

### Hooks (real-world angles)
- Make a marigold or sugar-skull craft for *Día de los Muertos* (explaining it's a celebration of memory).
- Compare a Spanish festival with one your family celebrates.
- Find Spain and Mexico on a map — same language, different fiestas.

### Misconceptions (wrong answer → why → fix)
- **Wrong:** "Día de los Muertos is like Halloween / is scary." **Why:** skulls look spooky to outsiders. **Fix:** it's a **joyful, respectful** festival remembering loved ones — celebration, not fright.
- **Wrong:** "Everyone who speaks Spanish lives in Spain." **Why:** the language is named after Spain. **Fix:** most Spanish speakers live in **Latin America** (Mexico has the most).
- **Wrong:** thinking all Spanish-speaking countries celebrate identically. **Fix:** some festivals are shared, others are special to one country.

### Checks (ready-to-use)
- **Q:** What happens on *el 6 de enero* in Spain? **A:** *Los Reyes Magos* (Three Kings) — children often receive presents.
- **Q:** What is *el Día de los Muertos*, and is it scary? **A:** A joyful Mexican festival remembering loved ones — not scary.
- **Q:** Name a country other than Spain where Spanish is spoken. **A:** e.g. Mexico, Argentina, Colombia, Peru.

### Activity / practice
**Speak:** festival fact-share — child names a Spanish-speaking festival, says its date in Spanish (recycling months), and explains one thing about it in English. (`speak`; optional `matchPairs`: festival → country/date.)

- **BBC Bitesize KS2 Spanish — *Spanish festivals / culture***.
- **National Geographic Kids — *Day of the Dead / Spanish festivals***.

## 10. The Spanish-speaking world — countries, capitals & where Spanish is spoken

**Year / Key Stage:** Year 5–6 (ages 9–11) · **Est. minutes:** 30 · **Subject strand:** Intercultural understanding – geography of the language

**Prerequisites:** Countries/continents (geography); greetings; numbers

### Key concepts
- Spanish is spoken by hundreds of millions of people across **about 20 countries**, mostly in **Latin America** plus Spain.
- Name a few: **España** (Madrid), **México** (Ciudad de México), **Argentina** (Buenos Aires), **Colombia** (Bogotá), **Perú** (Lima).
- Accents and a few words **vary by country** (e.g. *vosotros* in Spain), but speakers understand each other — like UK vs US English.

### Prior knowledge assumed
- The child can find continents on a map and knows Spain speaks Spanish.

### Teach it (the explanation)
Where in the world is Spanish spoken? Almost **everywhere across the Americas south of the USA**, plus Spain — about **20 countries** and hundreds of millions of people. That makes it one of the most spoken languages on Earth.

A few to know (country → capital, in Spanish):
- **España** → **Madrid** (Europe)
- **México** → **Ciudad de México** (the most Spanish speakers of any country!)
- **Argentina** → **Buenos Aires**
- **Colombia** → **Bogotá** · **Perú** → **Lima** · **Chile** → **Santiago**

Spanish **varies** a little by place — different accents, some different words, and Spain uses *vosotros* (you, plural) where Latin America uses *ustedes*. But it's all the **same language**: a child in Madrid and a child in Lima understand each other, just as British and American English speakers do.

**Worked example.** Match country → capital → continent: *México* → *Ciudad de México* → North America; *Argentina* → *Buenos Aires* → South America; *España* → *Madrid* → Europe. Notice Spanish bridges three continents.

### Hooks (real-world angles)
- Put pins in a world map for every Spanish-speaking country you can name.
- Footballers' home countries (Messi—Argentina, etc.) — all Spanish-speaking.
- Spot a few word differences (e.g. *coche* vs *carro* for "car").

### Misconceptions (wrong answer → why → fix)
- **Wrong:** "Spanish is only spoken in Spain." **Why:** named after Spain. **Fix:** most speakers are in **Latin America**; Mexico has the most.
- **Wrong:** "Different accents mean different languages." **Why:** accents sound different. **Fix:** it's one language with regional variety — speakers understand each other.
- **Wrong:** "South America all speaks Spanish." **Why:** over-generalising. **Fix:** most does, but **Brazil speaks Portuguese** — a useful exception.

### Checks (ready-to-use)
- **Q:** Roughly how many countries speak Spanish? **A:** About 20.
- **Q:** Which country has the most Spanish speakers? **A:** Mexico.
- **Q:** Name a Spanish-speaking country and its capital. **A:** e.g. España → Madrid; Argentina → Buenos Aires.

### Activity / practice
**Speak + map:** country challenge — child names three Spanish-speaking countries with capitals (in Spanish) and locates each on a world map by continent. (`speak`; optional `matchPairs`: country → capital.)

### Resources
- **BBC Bitesize KS2 Spanish — *The Spanish-speaking world***.
- **National Geographic Kids — country profiles (Mexico, Argentina, Spain, Peru)**.

## Speaking-practice activity bank (use across any topic)

Short, ready-to-run **speaking drills** the tutor can drop into any lesson for extra oral practice. All use the Classai `speak` block. Keep them fast, friendly and forgiving — praise communication first, then gently model the correct form.

1. **Greeting warm-up (daily).** Always open in Spanish: *¡Hola! ¿Cómo estás? ¿Qué tiempo hace hoy?* — child answers each. Free, daily fluency practice.
2. **Number tennis / price tennis.** Tutor says a number; child says the next (or doubles it, or gives it as a price *…euros*). Recycles numbers fast.
3. **Quick-fire ¿Qué es esto?** Tutor names/points to an object, food or animal; child answers *Es un/una…* and adds a colour or adjective (agreement practice).
4. **Opinion ping-pong.** Tutor states an opinion (*Me gusta el fútbol*); child responds with *A mí también* / *A mí no* / *Pero prefiero…* — connectives and opinions.
5. **Verb chain.** Tutor gives a verb + person (*comer — tú*); child says the conjugated form in a full sentence. Mix -ar/-er/-ir.
6. **Weekend report.** Child says one thing they **did** (past chunk: *Fui…, jugué…*) and one thing they're **going to do** (*Voy a…*). Past + near future in one go.
7. **Café / shop role-play.** Run the five-step transaction (greet → order → ask price → "no thanks" → goodbye), then swap roles.
8. **Direction relay.** Tutor asks *¿Dónde está…?*; child gives two direction words and *cerca/lejos*. Then child asks the tutor.
9. **Describe-and-guess.** Child describes a family member, animal or character with two agreeing adjectives joined by *y*; tutor guesses who.
10. **Story relay.** Tutor starts a sentence (*Primero, la oruga…*); child adds the next with *después…*; build a 4–6 line story together.
11. **Survival-phrase reflex.** Tutor gives an English situation ("you didn't hear the word"); child fires back the right Spanish chunk (*¿Puedes repetir?*). Builds automatic classroom Spanish.
12. **Culture minute.** Child names a Spanish-speaking country (with capital) or a festival and says its date in Spanish — recycles geography, months and numbers.

> Extension note: these topics push KS2 Spanish toward **sustained, connected speech** — more verbs across all three families, simple past and near future as usable chunks, opinions stitched with connectives, real transactions and story retelling, plus genuine intercultural knowledge. Keep instructions in English and practice in Spanish; present new language **by ear first**, recycle numbers/colours/verbs relentlessly, and reward communication over perfection.
