# Spanish — KS2 (Years 3–6), UK National Curriculum

Fourteen topics in the per-topic contract (`_TEMPLATE.md`). Spanish leans on **listening and speaking** — every Activity uses the Classai `speak` block where possible. Convention in this file: **instructions to the child are in English; the language to practise is in Spanish.** Pronunciation hints are plain-English approximations; **STRESSED** syllable in capitals. Always say new words by ear first, *then* show the spelling.

## 1. Greetings & introductions

**Year / Key Stage:** Year 3 (ages 7–8) · **Est. minutes:** 25 · **Subject strand:** Speaking & listening – social language

**Prerequisites:** none

### Key concepts
- You can greet, ask how someone is, give your name and say goodbye — a whole tiny conversation.
- Spanish has an upside-down `¿` at the start of questions and `¡` at the start of exclamations.
- *Hola* and *adiós* are friendly with everyone; this is your daily routine language.

### Prior knowledge assumed
- The child can have a simple conversation in English (greet, say their name).

### Teach it (the explanation)
Spanish is full of friendly greetings. Listen, then say each one back to me:
- **Hola** (OH-lah) — hello. The *h* is silent!
- **Buenos días** (BWEH-nos DEE-as) — good morning.
- **Buenas tardes** (BWEH-nas TAR-des) — good afternoon.
- **Buenas noches** (BWEH-nas NO-ches) — good night.
- **¿Cómo estás?** (KOH-mo es-TAS) — how are you?
- **Bien, gracias** (bee-EN GRA-thee-as) — fine, thanks.
- **¿Cómo te llamas?** (KOH-mo te YA-mas) — what's your name? (*ll* sounds like "y")
- **Me llamo…** (me YA-mo) — my name is…
- **Adiós** (ah-dee-OS) — goodbye. **Hasta luego** (AS-ta LWEH-go) — see you later.

**Worked example.** A whole conversation, step by step:
1. Tutor: *¡Hola! ¿Cómo estás?*
2. Child: *Bien, gracias. ¿Y tú?* ("And you?")
3. Tutor: *¡Muy bien! ¿Cómo te llamas?*
4. Child: *Me llamo Alex.*
5. Both: *¡Adiós! ¡Hasta luego!*

### Hooks (real-world angles)
- Greet your toys/pets in Spanish each morning — *¡Hola, perro!*
- Footballers from Spain, Mexico and Argentina greet teammates exactly like this.
- A video call to a Spanish-speaking friend always starts *¡Hola! ¿Cómo estás?*

### Misconceptions (wrong answer → why → fix)
- **Wrong:** pronouncing the *h* in *hola* ("Ho-la" with a breath). **Why:** in English *h* is sounded. **Fix:** Spanish *h* is always silent — say "OH-la"; practise *hola, hermano, hospital*.
- **Wrong:** saying *Me llamo* as "me LAM-o". **Why:** treating *ll* like English "l". **Fix:** *ll* = "y"; "me YA-mo". Pair with *llama* the animal — "YA-ma".
- **Wrong:** answering *¿Cómo estás?* with a name. **Why:** confusing "how are you?" with "what's your name?". **Fix:** *estás* = how you feel (*bien*); *te llamas* = your name.

### Checks (ready-to-use)
- **Q:** How do you say "hello" and "goodbye" in Spanish? **A:** *Hola* / *Adiós* (or *Hasta luego*).
- **Q:** I say *¿Cómo te llamas?* — what am I asking, and how do you answer? **A:** "What's your name?" → *Me llamo…* + your name.
- **Q:** Reply to *¿Cómo estás?* **A:** *Bien, gracias* (fine, thanks).

### Activity / practice
**Speak:** role-play the full greeting conversation. Tutor says *¡Hola! ¿Cómo te llamas?*; the child answers *Me llamo [name]* and asks back *¿Y tú?*. Then *¿Cómo estás?* → *Bien, gracias*. Close with *¡Adiós!* (`speak` block — child speaks each turn).

- **BBC Bitesize KS2 Spanish — *Greetings* (Saludos)** (hello, goodbye, how are you).
- **Rockalingua — *Greetings* song & game** (catchy *hola/adiós* video).

## 2. Numbers 0–31

**Year / Key Stage:** Year 3–4 (ages 7–9) · **Est. minutes:** 30 · **Subject strand:** Speaking & listening – numbers

**Prerequisites:** Greetings & introductions

### Key concepts
- Count 0–31 in Spanish; numbers power age, prices, dates and scores.
- 16–29 are made by joining: *diez y seis → dieciséis* ("ten-and-six").
- Numbers come back in almost every later topic — learn them well.

### Prior knowledge assumed
- The child can count to 31 in English and recognise the digits.

### Teach it (the explanation)
Listen and repeat, in groups:
- **0–10:** cero, uno (OO-no), dos, tres, cuatro (KWA-tro), cinco (THEEN-ko/SEEN-ko), seis (saze), siete (see-EH-te), ocho (OH-cho), nueve (NWEH-ve), diez (dee-ETH).
- **11–15** are special words: once (ON-the), doce, trece, catorce, quince (KEEN-the).
- **16–19** join "ten and…": dieciséis, diecisiete, dieciocho, diecinueve.
- **20:** veinte (VAYN-te). **21–29:** veintiuno, veintidós… veintinueve.
- **30:** treinta. **31:** treinta y uno (with a separate *y* = "and").

**Worked example.** Build 24 step by step: twenty = *veinte*, twenty-something joins as one word → *veinti…* + *cuatro* → **veinticuatro** (vayn-tee-KWA-tro). For 31 the join opens back up: *treinta* **y** *uno*.

### Hooks (real-world angles)
- Say your age: *Tengo nueve años* ("I have nine years").
- Count football shirt numbers or goals in Spanish.
- Count steps, claps or jumps up to 20 as a warm-up.

### Misconceptions (wrong answer → why → fix)
- **Wrong:** "veinte y cuatro" for 24. **Why:** copying the 31 pattern. **Fix:** 16–29 are joined into one word (*veinticuatro*); only from 31 up do you say *y*.
- **Wrong:** confusing *seis* (6) and *siete* (7). **Why:** both start "s". **Fix:** *seis* = "saze" (one beat); *siete* = "see-EH-te" (two beats).
- **Wrong:** "cinco" pronounced "sin-ko/king-ko". **Fix:** THEEN-ko (Spain) or SEEN-ko (Latin America) — both fine.

### Checks (ready-to-use)
- **Q:** Count from 1 to 10 in Spanish. **A:** uno, dos, tres, cuatro, cinco, seis, siete, ocho, nueve, diez.
- **Q:** What is *quince* + *uno*? Say the answer in Spanish. **A:** *dieciséis* (16).
- **Q:** Say 21 and 30. **A:** *veintiuno*, *treinta*.

### Activity / practice
**Speak:** "Number tennis." Tutor says a number in Spanish; child says the next one. Then reverse: tutor shows/says a digit, child says it aloud in Spanish. Finish with the child saying their own age: *Tengo ___ años*. (`speak` / `numberEntry` hybrid — child speaks the Spanish number.)

## 3. Colours (los colores)

**Year / Key Stage:** Year 3 (ages 7–8) · **Est. minutes:** 25 · **Subject strand:** Speaking & listening – descriptive vocabulary

**Prerequisites:** Greetings & introductions

### Key concepts
- Name common colours and say *me gusta el…* (I like the…).
- A colour can change its ending to match the thing it describes (*rojo / roja*) — first taste of agreement.
- Colours reappear when we describe animals, food and objects.

### Prior knowledge assumed
- The child knows colours in English and the greeting routine.

### Teach it (the explanation)
Listen and repeat:
- **rojo** (RO-ho) red · **azul** (ah-THOOL) blue · **amarillo** (ama-REE-yo) yellow · **verde** (VER-de) green · **naranja** (na-RAN-ha) orange · **rosa** (RO-sa) pink · **morado** (mo-RA-do) purple · **negro** (NEH-gro) black · **blanco** (BLAN-ko) white · **marrón** (ma-RON) brown · **gris** (grees) grey.

To say you like one: **me gusta el rojo** (me GOOS-ta el RO-ho) — "I like red."

**Worked example.** Pick blue: *Me gusta el azul.* Now ask the tutor back: *¿Te gusta el verde?* ("Do you like green?") → answer *Sí, me gusta* or *No, no me gusta*.

### Hooks (real-world angles)
- The Spanish flag: *rojo y amarillo*. Argentina: *azul y blanco*.
- Colours of your favourite team's kit, said in Spanish.
- Colour-hunt around the room: point and name in Spanish.

### Misconceptions (wrong answer → why → fix)
- **Wrong:** "amarillo" said with an English "l" ("ama-RIL-lo"). **Why:** *ll* misread. **Fix:** *ll* = "y" → "ama-REE-yo".
- **Wrong:** *me gusto* instead of *me gusta*. **Why:** mixing up endings. **Fix:** for liking one thing it's always *me gusta* — practise as a fixed chunk.
- **Wrong:** thinking colour words never change. **Why:** English colours don't. **Fix:** preview that some change (*rojo → roja*) to match the noun — full agreement comes later (Topic 14).

### Checks (ready-to-use)
- **Q:** Name three colours in Spanish. **A:** any three, e.g. *rojo, azul, verde*.
- **Q:** Say "I like green." **A:** *Me gusta el verde.*
- **Q:** What colours are on the Spanish flag? **A:** *rojo y amarillo*.

### Activity / practice
**Speak:** "Colour I-spy." Tutor names a colour in Spanish; child points to something that colour. Then swap. Finish with the child saying a favourite: *Me gusta el ___* and asking *¿Y tú?*. (`speak` block.)

### Resources
- **BBC Bitesize KS2 Spanish — *Colours* (Los colores)**.
- **Rockalingua — *Colours* song**.

## 4. Classroom objects (en la clase)

**Year / Key Stage:** Year 3 (ages 7–8) · **Est. minutes:** 25 · **Subject strand:** Speaking & listening – nouns & gender

**Prerequisites:** Colours, Numbers 0–31

### Key concepts
- Name everyday classroom things and say *¿Qué es esto?* / *Es un…*.
- Every Spanish noun is **masculine** (*el / un*) or **feminine** (*la / una*).
- Combine with colours: *un lápiz rojo*.

### Prior knowledge assumed
- The child knows some colours and a few numbers.

### Teach it (the explanation)
Spanish nouns have a "gender." Words with *el/un* are masculine; with *la/una* feminine. There's no deep reason — you learn each noun *with* its word. Listen and repeat:
- **un lápiz** (oon LA-pith) a pencil · **un libro** a book · **un bolígrafo** a pen · **una mesa** a table · **una silla** a chair · **una mochila** a backpack · **una regla** a ruler · **una goma** a rubber/eraser · **una pizarra** a whiteboard · **un estuche** a pencil case.

Ask and answer: **¿Qué es esto?** (keh es ES-to) "What's this?" → **Es una silla.**

**Worked example.** Point to a pencil: *¿Qué es esto?* → *Es un lápiz.* Add a colour (the colour goes *after* the noun): *Es un lápiz rojo.*

### Hooks (real-world angles)
- Label your own desk objects with Spanish sticky notes.
- Pack a pretend backpack, naming each item in Spanish.
- "Show me…" game with real classroom things.

### Misconceptions (wrong answer → why → fix)
- **Wrong:** *un mesa* / *una libro*. **Why:** guessing the article. **Fix:** learn the noun together with its article — say "*una* mesa" as one chunk, every time.
- **Wrong:** "un rojo lápiz" (colour before noun). **Why:** English word order. **Fix:** in Spanish the colour comes *after*: *un lápiz rojo*.
- **Wrong:** assuming *-a* always = feminine, *-o* = masculine. **Why:** over-applying a pattern. **Fix:** it's usually true (*mesa, libro*) but not always (*el mapa*) — learn exceptions as you meet them.

### Checks (ready-to-use)
- **Q:** Say "a book" and "a chair" in Spanish (with *un/una*). **A:** *un libro*, *una silla*.
- **Q:** Someone asks *¿Qué es esto?* about a pen. Answer. **A:** *Es un bolígrafo.*
- **Q:** Say "a green book." **A:** *un libro verde.*

### Activity / practice
**Speak:** the tutor holds up (or names) objects and asks *¿Qué es esto?*; the child answers *Es un/una…*, then adds a colour. (`speak` block; optional `matchPairs` linking Spanish word → picture.)

### Resources
- **BBC Bitesize KS2 Spanish — *In the classroom* (En la clase)**.
- **Oak National Academy — *Spanish: classroom objects* unit**.

## 5. Family (la familia)

**Year / Key Stage:** Year 4 (ages 8–9) · **Est. minutes:** 30 · **Subject strand:** Speaking & listening – people & possessives

**Prerequisites:** Greetings & introductions, Numbers 0–31

### Key concepts
- Name family members and introduce them: *Este es mi hermano.*
- Say "my" with *mi* (one) and *mis* (more than one).
- Combine with age: *Mi hermana tiene siete años.*

### Prior knowledge assumed
- The child can count and use the greeting routine; knows *me llamo*.

### Teach it (the explanation)
Listen and repeat:
- **madre** (MA-dre) mother · **padre** father · **hermano** (er-MA-no) brother · **hermana** sister · **abuelo / abuela** grandfather / grandmother · **tío / tía** uncle / aunt · **primo / prima** cousin (boy/girl) · **bebé** baby.

Say "my": **mi madre**, **mi hermano**. For more than one person use **mis**: **mis abuelos**, **mis primos**.
Introduce someone: **Este es mi padre** (boy/man) / **Esta es mi madre** (girl/woman).

**Worked example.** Introduce a brother who is 7: *Este es mi hermano. Se llama Tom. Tiene siete años.* ("This is my brother. He's called Tom. He's seven.")

### Hooks (real-world angles)
- Draw a family tree and label it in Spanish.
- Introduce your real or pretend family to the tutor.
- Photos: point and say *Esta es mi abuela.*

### Misconceptions (wrong answer → why → fix)
- **Wrong:** *mi hermanos* for "my brothers." **Why:** forgetting plural *mis*. **Fix:** one = *mi*, more than one = *mis* → *mis hermanos*.
- **Wrong:** using *hermano* for a sister. **Why:** ignoring the -o/-a ending. **Fix:** -o is the boy form, -a the girl form: herman**o** / herman**a**, prim**o** / prim**a**.
- **Wrong:** "*Mi madre es siete años*" for age. **Why:** translating "is seven" literally. **Fix:** age uses *tener* (to have): *tiene siete años*.

### Checks (ready-to-use)
- **Q:** Say "my mother" and "my grandparents." **A:** *mi madre*, *mis abuelos*.
- **Q:** Introduce your brother named Sam. **A:** *Este es mi hermano. Se llama Sam.*
- **Q:** Say "my sister is eight." **A:** *Mi hermana tiene ocho años.*

### Activity / practice
**Speak:** the child introduces three family members in turn — *Este/Esta es mi…* + name + *Tiene ___ años.* The tutor asks *¿Cómo se llama?* and *¿Cuántos años tiene?* (how old). (`speak` block.)

### Resources
- **BBC Bitesize KS2 Spanish — *Family* (La familia)**.
- **Light Speed Spanish — *Family members* (beginner lesson)**.

## 6. Animals & pets (los animales)

**Year / Key Stage:** Year 4 (ages 8–9) · **Est. minutes:** 30 · **Subject strand:** Speaking & listening – nouns, *tener*

**Prerequisites:** Classroom objects, Colours, Numbers 0–31

### Key concepts
- Name pets and animals and say *Tengo un perro* (I have a dog).
- Recycle colours to describe an animal: *un gato negro*.
- Ask *¿Tienes un animal?* (Do you have a pet?).

### Prior knowledge assumed
- The child knows colours, *un/una*, and the idea of *tener* (to have) from age/family.

### Teach it (the explanation)
Listen and repeat:
- **un perro** (PEH-rro) dog (roll the *rr*!) · **un gato** cat · **un pez** fish · **un conejo** (ko-NEH-ho) rabbit · **un pájaro** (PA-ha-ro) bird · **un caballo** (ka-BA-yo) horse · **un ratón** mouse · **una tortuga** tortoise · **una vaca** cow · **un león** lion · **un elefante** elephant.

Say what you have: **Tengo un perro.** Don't have one: **No tengo un animal.** Ask: **¿Tienes un animal?**

**Worked example.** Describe a black cat: start with the animal, then the colour after — *Tengo un gato negro.* Make it match: a white cat → *un gato blanco*; a white cow → *una vaca blanca* (feminine *-a*).

### Hooks (real-world angles)
- Talk about your own pet (or dream pet) in Spanish.
- A zoo or farm tour: name each animal in Spanish.
- Animal sounds — does a Spanish dog say *guau*? (Yes!)

### Misconceptions (wrong answer → why → fix)
- **Wrong:** *perro* with an English "r" (sounds like *pero* = "but"). **Why:** no English rolled r. **Fix:** even a light tongue-tap separates *perro* (dog) from *pero* (but).
- **Wrong:** "un gato negra" for a black cat. **Why:** wrong agreement. **Fix:** *gato* is masculine → *negro*; only feminine nouns take *-a* (*vaca negra*).
- **Wrong:** *Yo tengo* dropped to "tengo… animal" with no article. **Fix:** keep *un/una*: *Tengo **un** perro.*

### Checks (ready-to-use)
- **Q:** Say "I have a dog." **A:** *Tengo un perro.*
- **Q:** Describe a black cat in Spanish. **A:** *un gato negro.*
- **Q:** Ask someone if they have a pet. **A:** *¿Tienes un animal?*

### Activity / practice
**Speak:** "Pet swap." Tutor: *¿Tienes un animal?* → child answers *Sí, tengo un…* (+colour) or *No, no tengo un animal*, then asks the tutor the same. Extend: child describes three animals by colour. (`speak` block.)

### Resources
- **BBC Bitesize KS2 Spanish — *Animals* (Los animales)**.
- **Rockalingua — *Animals* / *Pets* song and games**.

## 7. Food & drink (la comida)

**Year / Key Stage:** Year 5 (ages 9–10) · **Est. minutes:** 30 · **Subject strand:** Speaking & listening – likes/dislikes

**Prerequisites:** Colours, Animals & pets (for *me gusta*)

### Key concepts
- Name common foods/drinks and say what you like and dislike.
- *me gusta* (one thing) vs *me gustan* (more than one thing).
- Order politely: *Quiero…, por favor.*

### Prior knowledge assumed
- The child has met *me gusta* with colours, and *un/una*.

### Teach it (the explanation)
Listen and repeat:
- **el pan** bread · **el queso** cheese · **la fruta** fruit · **la manzana** apple · **el plátano** (PLA-ta-no) banana · **la naranja** orange · **el pollo** (PO-yo) chicken · **el pescado** fish (to eat) · **el agua** water · **la leche** milk · **el zumo** (THOO-mo) juice · **el helado** ice cream · **el chocolate**.

Likes: **Me gusta el queso** (one thing). For plural things use **me gustan**: **Me gustan las manzanas**. Dislike: **No me gusta el pescado.** Order: **Quiero un zumo, por favor.**

**Worked example.** Say you like bananas (plural) but not fish: *Me gustan los plátanos, pero no me gusta el pescado.* (Notice *gusta → gustan* because plátanos is plural.)

### Hooks (real-world angles)
- Spanish/Mexican foods: *tortilla, churros, tacos, paella*.
- Order a snack in a pretend café in Spanish.
- Rate your lunch: *me gusta / no me gusta*.

### Misconceptions (wrong answer → why → fix)
- **Wrong:** *me gusta las manzanas* (plural noun, singular verb). **Why:** treating *gusta* as fixed. **Fix:** plural thing → *me gust**an***: *me gustan las manzanas*.
- **Wrong:** "*Yo gusto* el pollo." **Why:** translating "I like" word-for-word. **Fix:** Spanish says it "the chicken pleases me": *me gusta el pollo*.
- **Wrong:** *plátano* stressed "pla-TA-no". **Why:** ignoring the accent. **Fix:** the accent shows the stress → **PLA**-ta-no.

### Checks (ready-to-use)
- **Q:** Say "I like cheese." **A:** *Me gusta el queso.*
- **Q:** Say "I like apples" (plural). **A:** *Me gustan las manzanas.*
- **Q:** Order a juice politely. **A:** *Quiero un zumo, por favor.*

### Activity / practice
**Speak:** café role-play. Tutor is the waiter: *¿Qué quieres?*; child orders *Quiero…, por favor* and says one like + one dislike (*me gusta… / no me gusta…*). (`speak` block; optional `categorize`: foods I like vs dislike.)

### Resources
- **BBC Bitesize KS2 Spanish — *Food* (La comida)**.
- **Light Speed Spanish — *Food and drink* beginner lesson**.

## 8. Days, months & birthdays (días, meses, cumpleaños)

**Year / Key Stage:** Year 4–5 (ages 8–10) · **Est. minutes:** 30 · **Subject strand:** Speaking & listening – calendar & dates

**Prerequisites:** Numbers 0–31

### Key concepts
- Say the days of the week and months of the year.
- Give a date: *el 4 de mayo* (the order is day-month).
- Say when your birthday is: *Mi cumpleaños es el…*

### Prior knowledge assumed
- The child can count to 31; knows English days/months.

### Teach it (the explanation)
Days (note: Spanish days are **lower-case** and the week starts Monday). Listen and repeat:
- **lunes, martes, miércoles, jueves, viernes, sábado, domingo.**
Months: **enero, febrero, marzo, abril, mayo, junio, julio, agosto, septiembre, octubre, noviembre, diciembre.**

A date is **day + de + month**: **el 4 de mayo** (the 4th of May). Ask: **¿Cuándo es tu cumpleaños?** (when's your birthday?) → **Mi cumpleaños es el 12 de junio.**

**Worked example.** Build "23rd of March": number first (*veintitrés*) + *de* + month (*marzo*) → *el veintitrés de marzo*.

### Hooks (real-world angles)
- Mark your own birthday on a Spanish calendar.
- Say today's day each lesson: *Hoy es lunes.*
- Spanish festivals by date: *el 6 de enero* (Three Kings' Day).

### Misconceptions (wrong answer → why → fix)
- **Wrong:** "mayo 4" (month-day, English order). **Why:** English date order. **Fix:** Spanish is day-month: *el 4 de mayo*.
- **Wrong:** capitalising *Lunes, Enero*. **Why:** English capitalises days/months. **Fix:** Spanish does not — *lunes*, *enero* stay lower-case.
- **Wrong:** *miércoles* mispronounced. **Fix:** mee-AIR-ko-les; the accent marks the stress.

### Checks (ready-to-use)
- **Q:** Say the days of the week in Spanish. **A:** lunes, martes, miércoles, jueves, viernes, sábado, domingo.
- **Q:** Say "the 10th of July." **A:** *el diez de julio.*
- **Q:** Answer *¿Cuándo es tu cumpleaños?* **A:** *Mi cumpleaños es el [number] de [month].*

### Activity / practice
**Speak:** the tutor asks *¿Qué día es hoy?* (child: *Hoy es…*) and *¿Cuándo es tu cumpleaños?* (child gives a full date). Then the tutor names a month; the child says a festival or season it makes them think of. (`speak` block; optional `ordering`: put the days in order.)

### Resources
- **BBC Bitesize KS2 Spanish — *Days of the week* and *Months* (Los días / Los meses)**.
- **Rockalingua — *Days of the week* / *Months* songs**.

## 9. Weather (el tiempo)

**Year / Key Stage:** Year 4 (ages 8–9) · **Est. minutes:** 25 · **Subject strand:** Speaking & listening – impersonal expressions

**Prerequisites:** Greetings & introductions

### Key concepts
- Describe today's weather: *Hace sol, hace frío…*
- Two patterns: *hace…* (it makes…) and *está…* (it is…).
- Use it daily — a perfect quick speaking routine.

### Prior knowledge assumed
- The child knows the greeting routine and a few adjectives.

### Teach it (the explanation)
Spanish often says weather with **hace** ("it makes"). Listen and repeat:
- **Hace sol** — it's sunny · **Hace calor** — it's hot · **Hace frío** — it's cold · **Hace viento** — it's windy · **Hace buen tiempo** — the weather's good · **Hace mal tiempo** — the weather's bad.
A few use **está**: **Está lloviendo** — it's raining · **Está nevando** — it's snowing · **Está nublado** — it's cloudy.

Ask: **¿Qué tiempo hace?** (what's the weather like?).

**Worked example.** It's cold and raining → *Hace frío y está lloviendo.* (Join two facts with *y* = "and".)

### Hooks (real-world angles)
- A daily weather report in Spanish, like a TV forecaster.
- Compare: *En España hace sol, en Inglaterra está lloviendo.*
- Match weather to clothes you'd wear.

### Misconceptions (wrong answer → why → fix)
- **Wrong:** "es sol" / "está sol." **Why:** picking the wrong verb. **Fix:** sun/heat/cold/wind use *hace*; rain/snow/cloud use *está*. Learn each as a chunk.
- **Wrong:** translating "it is hot" as *es caliente*. **Why:** literal translation. **Fix:** weather-hot is *hace calor*.
- **Wrong:** *frío* said "free-o" flat. **Fix:** FREE-oh, the accent marks the stress on the *í*.

### Checks (ready-to-use)
- **Q:** Say "it's sunny." **A:** *Hace sol.*
- **Q:** Say "it's raining." **A:** *Está lloviendo.*
- **Q:** Ask "what's the weather like?" **A:** *¿Qué tiempo hace?*

### Activity / practice
**Speak:** "Weather forecaster." Show or name a weather icon; the child gives the Spanish (*Hace… / Está…*). Then the child gives a two-part forecast joined with *y*. (`speak` block; optional `matchPairs`: icon → Spanish phrase.)

### Resources
- **BBC Bitesize KS2 Spanish — *The weather* (El tiempo)**.
- **Oak National Academy — *Spanish: the weather* unit**.

## 10. Hobbies & free time (los pasatiempos)

**Year / Key Stage:** Year 5 (ages 9–10) · **Est. minutes:** 30 · **Subject strand:** Speaking & listening – likes + activities

**Prerequisites:** Food & drink (for *me gusta*)

### Key concepts
- Say what you like to do: *Me gusta jugar al fútbol.*
- After *me gusta*, the next verb stays in its plain "-ar/-er/-ir" form.
- Give an opinion with a reason using *porque* (because).

### Prior knowledge assumed
- The child can use *me gusta / no me gusta* with nouns.

### Teach it (the explanation)
After *me gusta*, use the plain verb form. Listen and repeat:
- **jugar** to play · **leer** to read · **cantar** to sing · **bailar** to dance · **nadar** to swim · **dibujar** to draw · **correr** to run · **ver la tele** to watch TV.
- Sports with *jugar al…*: **jugar al fútbol**, **jugar al tenis**, **jugar al baloncesto** (basketball).

Say a preference: **Me gusta nadar.** Strong like: **Me encanta cantar** (I love singing). Add a reason: **Me gusta bailar porque es divertido** (because it's fun).

**Worked example.** "I like playing football because it's fun": *Me gusta jugar al fútbol porque es divertido.* Keep *jugar* in its plain form after *me gusta*.

### Hooks (real-world angles)
- Talk about your real hobbies in Spanish.
- Spanish football clubs (Real Madrid, Barça) — *me gusta el fútbol*.
- Plan a weekend: *Me gusta nadar y dibujar.*

### Misconceptions (wrong answer → why → fix)
- **Wrong:** *Me gusta juego* (changing the verb after *me gusta*). **Why:** over-conjugating. **Fix:** after *me gusta* the verb stays plain: *me gusta **jugar***.
- **Wrong:** "jugar fútbol" (no *al*). **Why:** English drops it. **Fix:** sports take *jugar **al**…*: *jugar al fútbol*.
- **Wrong:** using *me gusta* for "I love." **Fix:** stronger feeling = *me encanta*.

### Checks (ready-to-use)
- **Q:** Say "I like to swim." **A:** *Me gusta nadar.*
- **Q:** Say "I like playing tennis." **A:** *Me gusta jugar al tenis.*
- **Q:** Add a reason: why do you like dancing? **A:** e.g. *Me gusta bailar porque es divertido.*

### Activity / practice
**Speak:** the tutor asks *¿Qué te gusta hacer?* (what do you like doing?); the child answers with two activities joined by *y*, then adds a *porque* reason for one. Tutor asks *¿Y qué no te gusta?* for a dislike. (`speak` block.)

- **BBC Bitesize KS2 Spanish — *Hobbies / Free time* (Los pasatiempos)**.
- **Light Speed Spanish — *Talking about hobbies* lesson**.

## 11. The body (el cuerpo)

**Year / Key Stage:** Year 5 (ages 9–10) · **Est. minutes:** 25 · **Subject strand:** Speaking & listening – nouns, plurals, *me duele*

**Prerequisites:** Classroom objects (gender), Numbers (for "two eyes")

### Key concepts
- Name parts of the body and follow body-part instructions.
- Make plurals: add **-s** (vowel) or **-es** (consonant): *ojo → ojos*, *nariz → narices*.
- Say something hurts: *Me duele la cabeza.*

### Prior knowledge assumed
- The child knows *un/una* and can count to a few.

### Teach it (the explanation)
Listen and repeat:
- **la cabeza** head · **el pelo** hair · **la cara** face · **los ojos** eyes · **la nariz** nose · **la boca** mouth · **las orejas** ears · **el brazo** arm · **la mano** hand · **la pierna** leg · **el pie** foot · **la barriga** tummy.

Plurals: add **-s** after a vowel (*ojo → ojos*), **-es** after a consonant (*nariz → narices*). "I have two eyes": **Tengo dos ojos.** Say it hurts: **Me duele la cabeza** (my head hurts).

**Worked example.** Make "ears" plural and use it: *oreja → orejas* → *Tengo dos orejas.* For a hurt: *Me duele la barriga.*

### Hooks (real-world angles)
- Play "Simón dice" (Simon Says) with body parts.
- Sing a head-shoulders-knees-and-toes style song in Spanish.
- Pretend to be a doctor: *¿Qué te duele?*

### Misconceptions (wrong answer → why → fix)
- **Wrong:** *naris* / wrong plural *nariss*. **Why:** consonant-ending confusion. **Fix:** consonant ending → add *-es*: *nariz → narices*.
- **Wrong:** *el mano* (it looks masculine). **Why:** -o = masculine rule. **Fix:** *la mano* is an exception — feminine despite ending in -o.
- **Wrong:** "*Mi cabeza duele*." **Why:** copying English. **Fix:** Spanish says *Me duele **la** cabeza* (the head hurts to me).

### Checks (ready-to-use)
- **Q:** Name three body parts in Spanish. **A:** e.g. *la cabeza, los ojos, la mano*.
- **Q:** Make *ojo* plural and say "I have two eyes." **A:** *ojos* → *Tengo dos ojos.*
- **Q:** Say "my head hurts." **A:** *Me duele la cabeza.*

### Activity / practice
**Speak:** "Simón dice" — tutor says *Simón dice: toca la nariz* (touch your nose); child touches and names. Then child plays doctor: tutor mimes a pain, child asks *¿Qué te duele?* and the tutor answers. (`speak` block.)

### Resources
- **BBC Bitesize KS2 Spanish — *The body / parts of the body* (El cuerpo)**.
- **Rockalingua — *Parts of the body* song and game**.

## 12. Telling the time (¿qué hora es?)

**Year / Key Stage:** Year 5–6 (ages 9–11) · **Est. minutes:** 30 · **Subject strand:** Speaking & listening – time

**Prerequisites:** Numbers 0–31

### Key concepts
- Ask and tell the time on the hour and half-past.
- "It is one o'clock" uses *Es*; all other hours use *Son*.
- Half past = *…y media*; quarter past/to = *…y cuarto / menos cuarto*.

### Prior knowledge assumed
- The child can count to 31 and read a clock in English.

### Teach it (the explanation)
Ask: **¿Qué hora es?** (what time is it?).
- One o'clock is special: **Es la una.**
- Every other hour uses **Son las…**: **Son las dos**, **Son las tres**… up to **Son las doce**.
- Half past: add **y media** → *Son las cuatro y media* (half past four).
- Quarter past: **y cuarto** → *Son las cinco y cuarto*. Quarter to: **menos cuarto** → *Son las seis menos cuarto* (quarter to six).

**Worked example.** Build "half past seven": it's not one o'clock, so *Son las* + *siete* + *y media* → *Son las siete y media.*

### Hooks (real-world angles)
- Say what time you do things: *Como a la una* (I eat at one).
- A Spanish TV guide — what's on *a las ocho*?
- Set a toy clock and read it aloud in Spanish.

### Misconceptions (wrong answer → why → fix)
- **Wrong:** *Son la una* for one o'clock. **Why:** over-using *son las*. **Fix:** one o'clock is singular → *Es la una*; everything else *Son las…*.
- **Wrong:** *las cuatro y mitad* for half past. **Why:** translating "half." **Fix:** half past = *y media*.
- **Wrong:** treating "quarter to" like "quarter past." **Fix:** to = *menos cuarto* (literally "minus quarter").

### Checks (ready-to-use)
- **Q:** Say "it's three o'clock." **A:** *Son las tres.*
- **Q:** Say "it's half past six." **A:** *Son las seis y media.*
- **Q:** Say "it's one o'clock." **A:** *Es la una.*

### Activity / practice
**Speak:** the tutor names/shows a time (on the hour, half/quarter past, quarter to); the child says it in Spanish, starting *Es la…* or *Son las…*. Then the child sets times for the tutor to read. (`speak` block.)

### Resources
- **BBC Bitesize KS2 Spanish — *Telling the time* (La hora)**.
- **Light Speed Spanish — *Telling the time in Spanish* lesson**.

## 13. Simple present-tense verbs (-ar verbs)

**Year / Key Stage:** Year 6 (ages 10–11) · **Est. minutes:** 35 · **Subject strand:** Grammar – present tense

**Prerequisites:** Hobbies & free time, Family (for pronouns)

### Key concepts
- Most Spanish verbs end in **-ar**; you swap the ending to say who is doing it.
- *yo* (I) → **-o**, *tú* (you) → **-as**, *él/ella* (he/she) → **-a**.
- You usually drop the pronoun because the ending already shows who.

### Prior knowledge assumed
- The child has met plain verbs after *me gusta* (*jugar, cantar, bailar, nadar*).

### Teach it (the explanation)
A regular **-ar** verb is like a word with a swappable tail. Take **hablar** (to speak). Chop off **-ar** to get the stem **habl-**, then add the ending for the person:
- **yo hablo** — I speak
- **tú hablas** — you speak
- **él/ella habla** — he/she speaks

The same endings work for *cantar* (sing), *bailar* (dance), *nadar* (swim), *estudiar* (study), *jugar* (play): *yo canto, tú bailas, ella nada.* Spanish lets you drop *yo/tú* because the ending already tells you who — *hablo* alone means "I speak."

**Worked example.** Conjugate *cantar* for "she sings": stem *cant-* + the él/ella ending *-a* → **canta** → *Ella canta* (or just *canta*).

### Hooks (real-world angles)
- Describe your daily routine: *Estudio, juego, nado.*
- Report on someone: *Mi hermano juega al fútbol.*
- Turn hobby phrases into full sentences (*Yo dibujo*).

### Misconceptions (wrong answer → why → fix)
- **Wrong:** *yo habla* / *tú hablo* (mixed endings). **Why:** endings not yet automatic. **Fix:** drill the pattern: yo→-o, tú→-as, él/ella→-a. "I" always ends in **-o**.
- **Wrong:** always saying *yo, tú*. **Why:** copying English. **Fix:** the ending shows the person — *hablo* already means "I speak"; the pronoun is optional/for emphasis.
- **Wrong:** trying these endings on *comer/vivir* (-er/-ir verbs). **Fix:** these endings are for **-ar** verbs; -er/-ir differ — keep to -ar for now.

### Checks (ready-to-use)
- **Q:** Conjugate *cantar* for "I": ___. **A:** *(yo) canto.*
- **Q:** "You dance" — say it in Spanish. **A:** *(tú) bailas.*
- **Q:** "She studies." **A:** *(ella) estudia.*

### Activity / practice
**Speak:** verb chain. Tutor gives a verb + a person ("*nadar* — yo"); the child says the conjugated form (*nado*) in a full sentence (*Yo nado*). Cycle through yo/tú/él for *jugar, cantar, dibujar*. (`speak` block; optional `fillBlank`: *Yo ___ (hablar) español*.)

### Resources
- **BBC Bitesize KS2 Spanish — *Verbs / actions* and grammar pages**.
- **Light Speed Spanish — *Present tense -AR verbs* lesson**.

## 14. Descriptions: adjectives & agreement (las descripciones)

**Year / Key Stage:** Year 6 (ages 10–11) · **Est. minutes:** 35 · **Subject strand:** Grammar – adjective agreement

**Prerequisites:** Colours, Animals & pets, Family

### Key concepts
- Describe people/things: *Es alto. Es simpática.*
- Adjectives **agree**: change ending for masculine/feminine and singular/plural.
- The adjective usually comes **after** the noun: *un perro pequeño*.

### Prior knowledge assumed
- The child knows colours, *un/una*, and noun gender.

### Teach it (the explanation)
An adjective must "match" its noun — this is **agreement**. Listen and repeat some describing words:
- **alto/a** tall · **bajo/a** short · **grande** big · **pequeño/a** small · **simpático/a** kind/nice · **divertido/a** fun · **inteligente** clever · **bonito/a** pretty.

The rule:
- Masculine thing → adjective ends **-o**: *un gato negr**o***.
- Feminine thing → adjective ends **-a**: *una gata negr**a***.
- More than one → add **-s**: *gatos negr**os***, *gatas negr**as***.
- Adjectives ending in **-e** (*grande, inteligente*) don't change for gender — only add -s for plural.
The adjective normally goes **after** the noun: *una casa grande*.

**Worked example.** Describe a kind sister: *sister* is feminine → use the **-a** form → *Mi hermana es simpátic**a**.* For a tall brother (masculine): *Mi hermano es alt**o**.*

### Hooks (real-world angles)
- Describe family members or pets in Spanish (combine with Topics 5, 6).
- Describe a favourite character: *Es alto y divertido.*
- "Guess who?" — describe a person, tutor guesses.

### Misconceptions (wrong answer → why → fix)
- **Wrong:** *Mi hermana es simpático.* **Why:** forgetting agreement. **Fix:** feminine noun → feminine ending: *simpátic**a***.
- **Wrong:** "un negro gato" (adjective before noun). **Why:** English order. **Fix:** in Spanish describe-after: *un gato negro*.
- **Wrong:** changing *grande/inteligente* to "*granda*". **Why:** over-applying -o/-a. **Fix:** adjectives ending in -e don't change for gender — *un chico grande, una chica grande*.

### Checks (ready-to-use)
- **Q:** Make "tall" agree for a girl: *Ella es ___.* **A:** *alta.*
- **Q:** Describe two small dogs. **A:** *dos perros pequeños.*
- **Q:** Where does the adjective go — "a fun book"? **A:** *un libro divertido* (after the noun).

### Activity / practice
**Speak:** "Describe and guess." The child describes a family member, pet or character with two agreeing adjectives joined by *y* (*Es alta y simpática*); the tutor guesses who. Swap roles. (`speak` block; optional `fillBlank`: *Mi gata es negr__ y pequeñ__*.)

- **BBC Bitesize KS2 Spanish — *Describing people / adjectives* (Las descripciones)**.
- **Light Speed Spanish — *Adjective agreement* lesson**.
