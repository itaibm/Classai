# English — Lesson Bank (ready-to-run mini lessons, question bank & activities)

Six mini lesson outlines mapped to the app's beats (**hook → explain → example → check → practice → recap**), each on a high-value English topic, then a question bank (~12 with answers + a likely wrong answer each) and the highest-value practice activities. British spelling throughout.

---

## Lesson 1 — Inference: reading between the lines

- **Hook:** read "She slammed the door and refused her tea." Ask: how does she feel? (The word "angry" never appears.)
- **Explain:** inference = clues in the text + what you know; use the frame **"It says ___, so I think ___ because ___."**
- **Example:** "Tom's hands shook… then a slow smile spread." → nervous, then happy/relieved; point to the evidence.
- **Check:** "Her stomach rumbled as she stared at the empty fridge." (Hungry — evidence: rumbling, empty fridge.) Watch for answers with no evidence.
- **Practice:** multipleChoice extracts → choose the best inference, then justify aloud.
- **Recap:** "What three parts make a strong inference answer?" (point, evidence, explanation).

## Lesson 2 — The its / it's apostrophe trap

- **Hook:** show a real sign: "The dog lost it's bone." Right or wrong?
- **Explain:** two jobs — contraction (missing letters) vs possession; **it's = it is/has**, **its = belonging to it** (no apostrophe).
- **Example:** "The dog wagged **its** tail because **it's** happy." Test with "it is".
- **Check:** "It's/Its raining and the cat lost it's/its toy." (It's raining; its toy.) Watch for apostrophe on possessive *its*.
- **Practice:** categorize "needs apostrophe / no apostrophe"; fillBlank the its/it's trap.
- **Recap:** "How do you test for it's?" (expand to "it is").

## Lesson 3 — Writing a balanced argument

- **Hook:** pose a real debate: "Should children have homework?" Get a snap opinion, then challenge it.
- **Explain:** discussion ≠ persuasion — fair for/against paragraphs, then a reasoned conclusion; use *however / on the other hand*.
- **Example:** model one "for" paragraph and one "against", then "On balance… because…".
- **Check:** "How is a discussion text different from persuasion?" (gives both sides before concluding). Watch for one-sided writing.
- **Practice:** ordering jumbled paragraphs into for/against/conclusion; fillBlank the connectives.
- **Recap:** "What two jobs does the conclusion do?" (weigh both sides, give a reasoned view).

## Lesson 4 — Punctuating direct speech

- **Hook:** turn a comic strip's speech bubbles into written dialogue.
- **Explain:** the checklist — open inverted commas, capital, end punctuation **inside**, comma before the reporting clause; **new speaker = new line**.
- **Example:** `"Where have you been?" demanded Mum. // "I got lost," whispered Ben.`
- **Check:** punctuate "i'm hungry said Tom." → "I'm hungry," said Tom. Watch for punctuation outside the marks.
- **Practice:** fillBlank punctuate three lines; speak then write a two-character exchange.
- **Recap:** "Where does the end punctuation go?" (inside the inverted commas).

## Lesson 5 — Editing and proofreading

- **Hook:** "Be the editor" — hand over an error-filled, flat paragraph.
- **Explain:** two separate passes — **revise** (improve content/word choice) then **proofread** (fix errors); read aloud.
- **Example:** "the dog was nice and it run fast" → revise to "The energetic spaniel sprinted across the field"; then fix capital + tense.
- **Check:** "What's the difference between revising and proofreading?" (improve vs correct). Watch for "editing = spelling only".
- **Practice:** categorize each change as revise/proofread, then correct the passage.
- **Recap:** "Why read aloud?" (the brain auto-fills dropped words).

## Lesson 6 — Figurative language (simile, metaphor, personification)

- **Hook:** describe a storm — "the sky was an angry beast." Is that literal?
- **Explain:** simile uses *like/as*; metaphor says it *is*; personification gives objects human actions; all build imagery.
- **Example:** thunder "as loud as a drum" (simile), sky "was an angry beast" (metaphor), lightning "clawed at the clouds" (personification).
- **Check:** "The moon was a silver coin" — simile or metaphor? (metaphor). Watch for mixing simile/metaphor.
- **Practice:** categorize examples; speak — invent one of each for today's weather.
- **Recap:** "What's the one-word difference between a simile and a metaphor?" (*like/as*).

## Question bank (with answer + a likely wrong answer)

1. **"Her hands trembled as she opened the letter." How does she feel?** A: Nervous/anxious (evidence: trembling hands). Wrong: "happy" (ignores the clue).
2. **It's / Its the cat's fault it lost it's / its collar.** A: It's the cat's fault; its collar. Wrong: "its the cat's… it's collar" (reversed).
3. **Is "I bought three apple's" correct?** A: No — *apples* (plural, no apostrophe). Wrong: Yes (greengrocer's apostrophe).
4. **How is a balanced argument different from persuasion?** A: It gives both sides fairly before concluding. Wrong: "It's the same thing."
5. **Punctuate: where are you going asked Sam.** A: "Where are you going?" asked Sam. Wrong: "Where are you going" asked Sam? (? outside / misplaced).
6. **Simile or metaphor: "The classroom was a zoo."** A: Metaphor. Wrong: Simile (no *like/as*).
7. **Write a simile for "very tall".** A: e.g. "as tall as a giraffe." Wrong: "he is a giraffe" (that's a metaphor).
8. **Make formal: "Wanna come to mine?"** A: "Would you like to come to my house?" Wrong: leaves contraction/slang in.
9. **When do you start a new paragraph?** A: On a change of Time, Place, Topic or Person. Wrong: "every five lines."
10. **How many syllables per line in a haiku?** A: 5, 7, 5. Wrong: "it has to rhyme."
11. **Which comes first alphabetically: *plane* or *plant*?** A: *plane* (e before t). Wrong: *plant* (orders by length).
12. **Break *unhelpful* into parts.** A: *un-* + *help* + *-ful*. Wrong: *unhelp + ful* (wrong root).
13. **What's the difference between skimming and scanning?** A: Skim = gist quickly; scan = hunt a specific fact. Wrong: "they're the same."
14. **Standard English of "we was late"?** A: "We were late." Wrong: leaves "we was".

## Highest-value practice activities (English)

- **Read aloud daily, then talk about it** — re-tell, predict, and answer "how do you know?" inference questions (the biggest comprehension lever).
- **"Talk for writing"** — rehearse a sentence orally before writing it; act out dialogue before punctuating it.
- **Up-levelling / "say it better"** — swap overused words (*nice, said, big, good*) for stronger ones using a thesaurus.
- **Two-pass editing** — revise for content, then proofread for errors, on the child's own writing, reading aloud.
- **Sentence-combining and paragraph-sorting** — fix jumbled or run-on text into well-structured paragraphs.
- **Spelling investigation** — explore a rule (suffix, prefix, root) rather than rote-listing; build word families.
- **Real audience and purpose** — write postcards, instructions, reviews and emails to real people/situations.
- **Sound-it-out & word-building games** — phonics blending/segmenting (KS1) and root/affix building (KS2).
