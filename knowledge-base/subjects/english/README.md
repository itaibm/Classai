# English / Language Arts — Subject Overview (KS1–KS2, Years 1–6)

This is the self-contained English knowledge base for the Classai tutor, aligned to the **UK National Curriculum** for English, Key Stages 1 and 2 (Years 1–6, ages 5–11). It covers the statutory programmes of study: **word reading & phonics, reading comprehension, spelling, handwriting, writing composition, vocabulary/grammar/punctuation (VGP), and spoken language**.

Each `year-N.md` file contains 7–11 teachable TOPICS in the `_TEMPLATE.md` block. `misconceptions.md` consolidates the errors children actually make; `resources.md` lists real, free resources.

## How to teach primary English (the playbook)

Primary English rests on four pillars. The tutor should keep all four in mind every lesson, even when a topic is "about" only one of them.

### 1. Systematic synthetic phonics (SSP) — the route into reading

Reading in KS1 is taught through **synthetic phonics**: children learn the relationship between **graphemes** (letters/letter-groups written) and **phonemes** (the sounds they represent), then **blend** sounds left-to-right to read words and **segment** words into sounds to spell.

- **Phoneme** = smallest unit of sound (`/c/ /a/ /t/` = 3 phonemes in *cat*).
- **Grapheme** = the letter(s) that spell a phoneme (`igh` is one grapheme = one sound `/ie/` in *light*).
- **Blending** (for reading): say each sound and push them together → c-a-t → "cat".
- **Segmenting** (for spelling): hear the whole word and break it into sounds → "ship" → sh-i-p.
- **Digraph** = two letters, one sound (`sh`, `ch`, `ai`). **Trigraph** = three letters, one sound (`igh`, `ear`). **Split digraph** = two letters split by a consonant (`a_e` in *cake*).
- **Tricky / common exception words** = words you can't fully sound out yet (*the, said, was, one*). Teach the tricky part explicitly; read the rest phonically.

Phonics is taught in **phases** (the widely-used Letters and Sounds structure):
- **Phase 1** (Reception/pre-Year 1): listening, rhyme, oral blending — no letters yet.
- **Phase 2**: first 19 grapheme–phoneme correspondences (GPCs): `s a t p i n m d g o c k ck e u r h b f l ss`. Blend/segment VC and CVC words (*at, sit, pin*).
- **Phase 3**: the rest of the single letters plus consonant digraphs and vowel digraphs/trigraphs: `j v w x y z zz qu ch sh th ng ai ee igh oa oo ar or ur ow oi ear air ure er`.
- **Phase 4**: no new GPCs — blend/segment **adjacent consonants** (CVCC, CCVC: *frog, hand, twist*).
- **Phase 5** (Year 1): alternative spellings & pronunciations — split digraphs (`a_e i_e o_e u_e`), `ay/ou/ie/ea/oy/ir/ue/aw/wh/ph/ew/oe/au`, and that one grapheme can make several sounds.
- **Phase 6** (Year 2): spelling rules, suffixes, prefixes, fluent reading, building a wide bank of automatically-read words.

Golden rules: teach GPCs in a fixed order, **revisit daily**, apply immediately to real reading and writing, and never ask a child to read a word with a grapheme not yet taught.

### 2. Reading for comprehension — meaning is the point

Decoding gets words off the page; **comprehension** is understanding them. From Year 1 onward, teach reading *to* the child and *with* the child, and explicitly model the comprehension strategies skilled readers use:

- **Retrieval** — finding stated facts ("Where did the fox hide?").
- **Inference** — working out what the text implies but doesn't say, using clues + background knowledge ("How do we know the girl was frightened?").
- **Prediction** — using what's happened so far to guess what comes next.
- **Clarifying / vocabulary** — noticing and working out unfamiliar words.
- **Summarising** — saying what a paragraph or story is mostly about, in a sentence.
- **Questioning** — asking your own questions of the text.

Build comprehension on **background knowledge and vocabulary** — a child can only infer with what they already know. Read widely, talk about books, and re-read.

### 3. The writing process — composition is more than spelling

Good writers don't write a perfect first draft. Teach writing as a **process**: **plan → draft → revise → edit → publish**. Separate *composition* (ideas, structure, word choice) from *transcription* (spelling, handwriting, punctuation) so a child isn't crushed by both at once — let them get ideas down first, then polish.

- Teach **genres** explicitly with their purpose and features: narrative (story), recount (what happened), instructions (how to), persuasion (convince), explanation (how/why something works), report (information), letter, poetry.
- Use **shared writing** (tutor writes, thinking aloud) and **modelled examples** before asking the child to write alone.
- Always give the child a real **audience and purpose** ("write a postcard *to Grandma* about the trip").

### 4. The role of talk (oracy / spoken language)

Spoken language underpins everything: children who can *say* it can write it. Across all years, the tutor should: give the child time to articulate ideas aloud before writing, use full-sentence answers, build vocabulary through discussion, teach how to listen and take turns, and practise presenting, explaining, reasoning, and performing aloud. **Talk for writing** — rehearsing a sentence orally before writing it — is one of the highest-impact moves.

## Year 1 → 6 scope & sequence

The table below shows the NC strands progressing across the years. Use it to seed a syllabus or check coverage.

### Word reading / phonics
- **Y1:** Phase 5 phonics — all 40+ phonemes, alternative spellings, split digraphs; read CVC→CCVCC words by blending; read common exception words (*the, said, you, one*); read aloud accurately, sounding out unknown words.
- **Y2:** Phase 6 — secure phonic knowledge as the route to decode, read accurately words of two+ syllables and with known suffixes; read most common exception words; build automaticity (read on sight).
- **Y3–4:** Apply knowledge of root words, prefixes and suffixes to read aloud and understand new words; read further exception words.
- **Y5–6:** Apply morphology and etymology (word roots, e.g. Greek/Latin) to read and understand unfamiliar words; fluent silent reading.

### Reading comprehension
- **Y1:** Link to own experience; predict; join in with repeated phrases; recall key events; explain what's happening in a picture/text.
- **Y2:** Sequence events; make simple inferences; predict; ask & answer questions; discuss favourite words; recognise simple recurring language in stories/poems.
- **Y3–4:** Retrieve & record from non-fiction; draw inferences (feelings, thoughts, motives) and justify with evidence; predict; summarise main ideas; identify how language/structure contribute to meaning.
- **Y5–6:** Inference with evidence and justified opinion; summarise across paragraphs; compare texts; distinguish fact from opinion; analyse author's word choice and figurative language; discuss/evaluate texts, building on others' ideas.

### Spelling
- **Y1:** Words with taught GPCs; the prefix *un-*; suffixes *-s/-es, -ing, -ed, -er, -est* where no root change; days of the week; Y1 common exception words.
- **Y2:** Suffix rules (drop *e*, double consonant, *y → i*); *-ment, -ness, -ful, -less, -ly*; homophones (*there/their/they're, here/hear*); apostrophe for contraction & singular possession; Y2 common exception words.
- **Y3–4:** *-sion/-tion/-ssion/-cian*; prefixes (*dis-, mis-, in-, re-, sub-, super-, anti-, auto-*); *-ous*; the Y3/4 statutory word list (~100 words); homophones (*accept/except, affect/effect*).
- **Y5–6:** *-cious/-tious, -cial/-tial, -ant/-ent, -able/-ible*; silent letters; *ei/ie* rule; the Y5/6 statutory word list (~100 words, e.g. *accommodate, necessary, rhythm*).

### Handwriting
- **Y1:** Correct letter formation, starting points; capitals & digits; understand letter families.
- **Y2:** Diagonal & horizontal joining strokes; consistent size; spacing.
- **Y3–4:** Joined handwriting throughout; increasing legibility and fluency.
- **Y5–6:** Fast, fluent, legible joined hand; choose when print/joined is appropriate (e.g. labelling).

### Writing — composition
- **Y1:** Compose a sentence orally first, then write it; sequence sentences to form a short narrative; re-read to check it makes sense; read own writing aloud.
- **Y2:** Write narratives, real events, poetry, and for different purposes; plan/say out loud; write down ideas/key words; evaluate and make simple corrections.
- **Y3–4:** Plan by discussing structure & models; draft with rich vocabulary and grammar; organise paragraphs around a theme; create settings/characters/plot; use headings in non-fiction; assess & edit own and others' work.
- **Y5–6:** Plan for audience & purpose, drawing on reading models; develop setting/character/atmosphere; integrate dialogue to convey character; use cohesive devices across paragraphs; précis longer passages; proof-read for accuracy; perform own writing.

### Vocabulary, grammar & punctuation (VGP)
- **Y1:** Capital letters, full stops, question marks, exclamation marks; *and* to join; plural *-s/-es*; prefix *un-*; capital letters for names & *I*; the term *sentence*.
- **Y2:** Nouns, adjectives, verbs, adverbs; expanded noun phrases; statements/questions/exclamations/commands; co-ordination (*and, but, or*) & subordination (*when, if, that, because*); past & present tense; commas in lists; apostrophes (contraction + possession).
- **Y3–4:** Conjunctions/adverbs/prepositions for time, place, cause; fronted adverbials (+ comma); inverted commas for direct speech; *a/an*; possessive apostrophe with plurals; paragraphs; Standard English forms (*we were*, not *we was*).
- **Y5–6:** Relative clauses (*who, which, that, whose*); modal verbs; adverbs of possibility; brackets/dashes/commas for parenthesis; commas to clarify meaning; subjunctive form; the passive voice; semicolons, colons & dashes between clauses; bullet points; hyphens; ellipsis; cohesion.

### Spoken language (all years, woven through)
- Listen & respond; ask relevant questions; speak in full sentences; build vocabulary; give well-structured explanations; use Standard English; participate in discussion, performance, role-play and debate; consider and evaluate viewpoints, building on others' contributions.

## How the year files are organised

Each year file front-loads the **decoding/phonics & spelling** topics, then **comprehension**, then **grammar/punctuation**, then **writing composition & oracy**, so a tutor can move through them in a sensible order. Prerequisites are named on each topic. The four analysis fields (key concepts, prior knowledge, hooks, misconceptions) are always filled — those are what the Classai lesson designer needs most.
