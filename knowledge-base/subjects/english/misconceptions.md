# English — Consolidated Misconceptions (KS1–KS2)

The common, recurring errors children make in primary English, gathered in one place. Each entry gives the **wrong answer/idea**, **why** it happens (the faulty mental model), and the **fix** (the teaching move). These feed both `LessonAnalysis.misconceptions` and `BeatCheck.wrongAnswers` in Classai. Grouped by strand.

---

## Phonics & early reading

- **Says letter names instead of sounds** ("see-ay-tee" for *cat*).
  **Why:** confuses the alphabet song (names) with phonics (sounds).
  **Fix:** model pure sounds — `/c/` not "cuh"; "we say the *sound* the letter makes". Over-articulate.

- **Sounds each letter of a digraph separately** (*s-h-i-p* = 4 sounds).
  **Why:** doesn't see letter teams (digraphs/trigraphs) as one unit.
  **Fix:** draw one sound-button under the whole digraph; "spot the team first, then blend".

- **Pronounces the silent (magic) e** ("cak-e", "tim-e").
  **Why:** assumes every letter makes a sound.
  **Fix:** teach the final *e* as silent/lazy — it changes the vowel but makes no sound of its own. Compare *pin/pine*.

- **Tries to fully sound out a tricky word** (*the* → "thuh-eh", *said* by phonics).
  **Why:** applies regular blending to an irregular common-exception word.
  **Fix:** flag it as "tricky"; teach the irregular part on sight; quick flashcard practice.

- **Drops the middle (vowel) sound when spelling** (*nt* for *net*, *ct* for *cat*).
  **Why:** vowels are the hardest phonemes to hear.
  **Fix:** "robot voice" the word slowly, stretching the middle; one finger per sound before writing.

## Spelling: suffixes, prefixes & rules

- **Doesn't double the consonant** (*runing, hoping* for *running, hopping*).
  **Why:** ignores that a short-vowel CVC word doubles before a vowel suffix.
  **Fix:** test — short vowel + one final consonant → double it; compare *hoping*/*hopping*.

- **Keeps the silent e before a vowel suffix** (*bakeing, niceer*).
  **Why:** doesn't apply the drop-the-e rule.
  **Fix:** "drop the *e* before a vowel suffix" (*-ing, -ed, -er, -able*).

- **Keeps the y** (*happyer, cryed, happyness*).
  **Why:** forgets *y → i* before most suffixes.
  **Fix:** change *y → i* before *-er/-est/-ed/-ly/-ness* (but keep *y* before *-ing*: *crying*).

- **Spells -ful with two l's** (*carefull*).
  **Why:** copies the spelling of the word *full*.
  **Fix:** as a suffix it loses one *l* → *-ful*.

- **Drops a letter at a prefix join** (*mispell, disapear*).
  **Why:** assumes joins must smooth out.
  **Fix:** prefix + whole root, keep every letter: *mis+spell = misspell*, *dis+appear = disappear*.

- **Spells the /shun/ ending phonetically or always as -tion** (*acshun*, *divition*).
  **Why:** spells by sound / over-uses the commonest spelling.
  **Fix:** use root clues — jobs end *-cian*; *-de/-se* roots take *-sion*; *-ss* roots take *-ssion*; default *-tion*.

- **Misspells the Y3/4 & Y5/6 list words from careless speech** (*seperate, suprise, Febuary, recieve, neccessary, embarass*).
  **Why:** spelling by mispronunciation; wrong number of doubled letters.
  **Fix:** over-pronounce missing sounds; use mnemonics ("a rat in sepa**rat**e"; *necessary* = 1 collar, 2 socks); i-before-e **except after c** for the `/ee/` sound.

## Apostrophes

- **Apostrophe to make a plural** ("apple's 50p", "two car's", "the dog's are barking").
  **Why:** the "greengrocer's apostrophe" — sees apostrophes near *s* and over-applies.
  **Fix:** plurals NEVER take an apostrophe — only contraction or possession do. Ask "is something *missing* or *belonging*?"

- **Confuses its / it's.**
  **Why:** assumes the possessive always has an apostrophe.
  **Fix:** *it's* = "it is"/"it has" (apostrophe = missing letter); *its* (no apostrophe) = belonging to it. Test by saying "it is".

- **Wrong placement for plural possession** (*the dogs's leads, the childrens' toys*).
  **Why:** adds *'s* to a word already ending in *s*, or apostrophe-after on an irregular plural.
  **Fix:** regular plural ending in *s* → apostrophe **after** (*dogs'*); irregular plural (no *s*) → **'s** (*children's*).

## Homophones & confusables

- **Muddles their / there / they're.**
  **Why:** all three sound identical.
  **Fix:** pick by meaning — place → *there* (has *here* in it); belonging → *their*; "they are" → *they're*.

- **Muddles to / too / two.**
  **Why:** all sound like "too".
  **Fix:** *two* = the number; *too* = also/very (extra *o* for "extra"); *to* = everything else.

- **Muddles your / you're, were / we're / where.**
  **Why:** sound-alikes.
  **Fix:** *you're* = "you are"; *we're* = "we are"; *where* = a place.

- **Writes "could of / should of / would of / must of".**
  **Why:** mishears the contraction *could've* (= could **have**).
  **Fix:** it is always *could **have**, should **have**, would **have*** — never "of".

## Grammar

- **Confuses adjective and adverb** ("he ran quick").
  **Why:** adverbs feel like adjectives.
  **Fix:** adjectives describe nouns; adverbs describe verbs and often end *-ly* ("ran quickly").

- **Non-Standard subject–verb agreement** ("we was", "they was", "he don't").
  **Why:** copies spoken dialect.
  **Fix:** *I/he/she/it was*; *we/you/they **were***. *he/she/it **doesn't***. Use Standard English for writing/presenting.

- **Over-regularises irregular past tenses** (*goed, catched, seed, runned*).
  **Why:** applies the *-ed* rule to all verbs.
  **Fix:** learn irregular pairs as sets: *go→went, catch→caught, see→saw, run→ran*.

- **Mismatches relative pronoun** ("the man which…").
  **Why:** doesn't match the pronoun to the noun.
  **Fix:** *who* for people, *which* for things, *whose* for belonging.

- **Thinks the passive voice is "wrong".**
  **Why:** told to "avoid the passive".
  **Fix:** the passive is a deliberate *choice* — great for hiding/de-emphasising the doer; just don't overuse it.

- **"If I was you…" in formal writing.**
  **Why:** uses the everyday form instead of the subjunctive.
  **Fix:** hypotheticals/wishes take the subjunctive: "If I **were** you".

## Punctuation

- **Comma splice / run-on** (joining two sentences with only a comma: "It was late, we went home").
  **Why:** treats a comma as a full-stop substitute.
  **Fix:** use a full stop, a semicolon, or add a conjunction — a comma alone can't join two complete sentences.

- **Missing or misused end punctuation** (no capital/full stop; full stop on a question).
  **Why:** punctuation feels invisible/optional.
  **Fix:** "every sentence needs a capital 'hat' and a full stop 'shoe'"; a question needs *?*.

- **End punctuation outside the speech marks** (*"Run".*).
  **Why:** treats inverted commas like brackets.
  **Fix:** the punctuation that belongs to the spoken words goes **inside** the closing inverted comma.

- **Fronted adverbial without a comma** ("Later that day we left").
  **Why:** doesn't hear the pause.
  **Fix:** "front it, then comma" — read aloud and place the comma at the pause.

- **Unpaired or essential parenthesis** (one bracket only; sentence breaks when it's removed).
  **Why:** forgets parenthesis comes in pairs of only *extra* info.
  **Fix:** always open AND close; test by deleting — the sentence must still work.

- **Semicolon where there isn't a full sentence on both sides** ("I like apples; and oranges").
  **Why:** treats the semicolon like a comma.
  **Fix:** both sides of a semicolon must be complete sentences; no conjunction needed.

## Reading comprehension

- **"It doesn't say" to an inference question.**
  **Why:** thinks every answer must be written word-for-word in the text.
  **Fix:** "use the clues to work it out"; model "I think… because the text says…".

- **Inference with no evidence / pure imagination.**
  **Why:** confuses inference with random guessing.
  **Fix:** require evidence — "…because the text says…"; point to the clue.

- **Confuses fact and opinion** (a strong opinion stated as fact).
  **Why:** confident tone feels factual.
  **Fix:** ask "can it be checked/proven?" Judgements (*best, should, amazing*) are opinions.

- **Summary retells everything (as long as the original) or grabs a tiny detail.**
  **Why:** thinks summarising = copying, or can't rank importance.
  **Fix:** one sentence per paragraph / overall main idea; "would the meaning change if I cut this?"

- **Names a technique (simile/metaphor) but can't state the effect.**
  **Why:** stops at labelling.
  **Fix:** always add "…which makes the reader feel/picture…" (use PEE: Point–Evidence–Explain).

## Writing composition

- **Writes a story when asked for instructions / an explanation** ("I made a sandwich and…").
  **Why:** mixes genres.
  **Fix:** instructions use commands to *the reader*; explanations describe how/why it happens in the present tense — neither is a personal recount.

- **Run-on writing with no full stops; over-uses *and*.**
  **Why:** transcription overload; *and* feels like universal glue.
  **Fix:** "one idea, one sentence" — say it, write it, full stop; "two ideas, one *and*".

- **"Telling" emotions flatly** ("he was happy", "she was sad").
  **Why:** easier than showing.
  **Fix:** "show, don't tell" — reveal feeling through an action or the body ("her hands trembled").

- **Treats the first draft as final; same writing for every reader.**
  **Why:** doesn't value revising/editing or consider audience.
  **Fix:** plan → draft → revise → proof-read; decide audience & purpose first and match the register.
