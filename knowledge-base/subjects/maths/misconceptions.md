# Primary Maths — Consolidated Misconceptions (Years 1–6)

The wrong ideas children most reliably bring to primary maths, gathered in one place. Each has the **wrong answer/idea**, **why** it happens (the faulty mental model), and the **fix** (the teaching move that corrects it). These feed `LessonAnalysis.misconceptions` and `BeatCheck.wrongAnswers`. Grouped by domain; roughly easiest/earliest first.

> General principle for *every* fix: when the abstract symbols mislead, **go back a CPA stage** — get the cubes, the bar model, the number line, the 100-square out. Most misconceptions are the child over-extending a true rule into a place it doesn't apply.

---

## Counting & number sense

- **Wrong:** counts an object twice or skips one; wrong total.
  **Why:** not matching one number word to one object (one-to-one).
  **Fix:** move each object to a "counted" pile, or line them up and touch each once.

- **Wrong:** after counting, recounts when asked "how many?" instead of saying the last number.
  **Why:** hasn't grasped that the final word *is* the total (cardinality).
  **Fix:** count, then cover the objects and ask "so how many?"; stress the last number.

- **Wrong:** "one less than 10" = 11.
  **Why:** confuses less with more, or always counts forward.
  **Fix:** physically remove a cube for "less"; tie "less" to going back/down a number line.

## Place value (the most consequential family)

- **Wrong:** writes "twenty-three" as 203, or "three hundred and five" as 3005.
  **Why:** writes each spoken chunk literally instead of by column; misses placeholder zeros.
  **Fix:** build with base-10 (tens/ones, then H/T/O); empty columns get a 0. The 2 in 23 *means* two tens.

- **Wrong:** says 19 > 21, or 89 > 100, because "9 is a big digit."
  **Why:** comparing single digits, ignoring place value; thinks more/bigger digits = bigger number.
  **Fix:** compare the **biggest place first** (tens, then hundreds…); a three-digit number beats a two-digit one.

- **Wrong:** treats 34 and 43 as the same (same digits).
  **Why:** ignores that position decides value.
  **Fix:** model both with rods/blocks — 4 tens beats 3 tens. Position is everything.

- **Wrong:** partitions 50 as "5 and 0."
  **Why:** reading digits, not values.
  **Fix:** with rods the 5 is 5 *tens* = 50.

## The equals sign

- **Wrong:** reads `=` as "the answer comes next"; can't handle 7 = 3 + 4 or 5 + 2 = __ + 4.
  **Why:** sees `=` as an instruction ("do it") rather than a statement of balance.
  **Fix:** use a balance/scales image — both sides must weigh the same. Practise equations written "backwards" and with the gap on the left.

## Addition & subtraction

- **Wrong:** thinks 3 + 4 and 4 + 3 are different problems.
  **Why:** doesn't see addition as commutative.
  **Fix:** turn the same cubes around — total unchanged; learn one bond, get its partner free.

- **Wrong:** to add 6 + 3, counts all from one and loses track.
  **Why:** counting all instead of counting on from the larger number.
  **Fix:** "put 6 in your head, count on 3." Start from the bigger number.

- **Wrong:** for 13 − 5, counts back but includes the start number (gets 9).
  **Why:** counts the starting number as the first jump.
  **Fix:** on the number line the first jump *leaves* 13; count the jumps, not the start.

- **Wrong:** 45 + 38 = 713 (writes 7 tens and "13" ones).
  **Why:** doesn't regroup ten ones into a ten when carrying.
  **Fix:** with blocks, exchange 10 ones for 1 ten; 70 + 13 = 83.

- **Wrong:** in column subtraction (e.g. 52 − 27), does 7 − 2 because "you can't do 2 − 7."
  **Why:** subtracts smaller-from-larger in each column regardless of which is on top.
  **Fix:** **exchange** a ten so the top is genuinely bigger; show with base-10.

## Multiplication & division

- **Wrong:** thinks multiplication is *only* repeated addition; stalls on scaling, fractions, or "3 × 0.5."
  **Why:** the repeated-addition model is true for whole numbers but doesn't stretch.
  **Fix:** also teach multiplication as **arrays** and as **scaling** ("3 times as long"); this carries into fractions and decimals.

- **Wrong:** learns 3 × 4 but can't do 4 × 3 or 12 ÷ 3.
  **Why:** memorising one direction, ignoring commutativity and the inverse.
  **Fix:** teach the whole **fact family** from one array: 3×4, 4×3, 12÷3, 12÷4.

- **Wrong:** for 23 × 4, does 2×4 and 3×4 → "812"; or 96 ÷ 4 → splits 9÷4 and 6÷4.
  **Why:** operates on digits, not their place value.
  **Fix:** partition by value (20×4 and 3×4; 80÷4 and 16÷4); use the grid method.

- **Wrong:** "share 12 between 3" → guesses, ignores "equally."
  **Why:** doesn't connect division to equal sharing.
  **Fix:** deal one-to-each like cards until gone — 4 each *is* 12 ÷ 3.

- **Wrong:** in long division, stops early / drops or mishandles the remainder.
  **Why:** treats each digit independently.
  **Fix:** carry the remainder onto the next digit; keep going until every digit is used.

## Fractions (the second great misconception family)

- **Wrong:** thinks ⅓ > ½, or 2/4 > ½, because the numbers are bigger.
  **Why:** applies whole-number thinking — bigger denominator/numerator must mean bigger.
  **Fix:** a fraction wall — more parts means **smaller** parts; ⅓ is smaller than ½; 2/4 lines up exactly with ½.

- **Wrong:** calls any two pieces "halves," even unequal ones.
  **Why:** "half" understood as "a piece," not an **equal** piece.
  **Fix:** fold paper so the parts overlap exactly — only equal parts count.

- **Wrong:** ½ + ¼ = 2/6 (adds tops and bottoms).
  **Why:** treats a fraction as two separate whole numbers; adds straight across.
  **Fix:** parts must be the **same size** — re-cut: ½ = 2/4, then 2/4 + 1/4 = 3/4. The denominator names the part size and doesn't add.

- **Wrong:** to find ¾ of 12, divides by 3 (the numerator) or does 12 − 4 / 12 × 4.
  **Why:** doesn't link the denominator to "share into that many parts."
  **Fix:** **divide by the bottom, times by the top** — 12 ÷ 4 = 3, × 3 = 9. Deal into 4 equal piles.

- **Wrong:** makes "equivalent" fractions by **adding** the same number to top and bottom (½ → 3/4 by +2... etc.).
  **Why:** uses addition instead of multiplication.
  **Fix:** must **multiply** (or divide) both by the same number; check on the fraction wall.

- **Wrong:** to multiply fractions, finds a common denominator first (over-applies the addition rule).
  **Why:** assumes all fraction operations need matching denominators.
  **Fix:** multiplying just goes straight across (tops × tops, bottoms × bottoms) — no common denominator needed.

- **Wrong:** thinks ⅔ × ¾ must be bigger than ¾.
  **Why:** assumes multiplying always enlarges.
  **Fix:** a fraction *of* something is **smaller** — ⅔ of ¾ is part of ¾.

## Decimals

- **Wrong:** 0.45 > 0.5 because "45 > 5"; or 0.3 < 0.25.
  **Why:** compares the decimal part as a whole number, ignoring place value ("longer = bigger").
  **Fix:** line up the points and equalise places (0.50 vs 0.45 → 50 hundredths beats 45).

- **Wrong:** 4 ÷ 10 = 0.04; or thinks the decimal point "moves" when ×/÷ by 10.
  **Why:** miscounts places, or has the wrong mental image.
  **Fix:** ÷10 moves every **digit one place right** (4 → 0.4); the point stays put, the digits shift.

- **Wrong:** writes ¾ as a decimal as 0.34 (lines up the digits).
  **Why:** doesn't divide.
  **Fix:** fraction → decimal means divide top by bottom: 3 ÷ 4 = 0.75.

## Percentages

- **Wrong:** thinks 50% always equals 50.
  **Why:** treats percent as a fixed number, not a proportion of the whole.
  **Fix:** 50% is half of *whatever the whole is* — of 20 it's 10, of 200 it's 100.

- **Wrong:** 25% of 60 = 60 ÷ 25; or 0.6 = 6%.
  **Why:** divides by the percent number / forgets the ×100 between decimal and percent.
  **Fix:** 25% = ¼ → ÷4; decimal → % means ×100 (0.6 = 60%).

## Measurement

- **Wrong:** measures from the end of the ruler (or the 1), not 0.
  **Why:** doesn't align the object with zero.
  **Fix:** always start at 0; show that starting at 1 makes the answer one short.

- **Wrong:** finds a rectangle's perimeter by adding only two sides (length + width).
  **Why:** forgets the opposite sides.
  **Fix:** a rectangle has four sides; opposite sides are equal — count all four (or 2 × (l + w)).

- **Wrong:** confuses area and perimeter; or for area counts only the edge squares.
  **Why:** the two ideas blur; area mistaken for a boundary.
  **Fix:** perimeter = walk the edge (a length, cm); area = fill the inside with squares (cm²).

- **Wrong:** for volume, multiplies only two dimensions (treats it like area).
  **Why:** forgets the third dimension.
  **Fix:** volume = l × w × h; build it from cubes to see the layers (cm³).

- **Wrong:** triangle area = base × height (forgets the ½); or uses a slanted side as the height.
  **Why:** doesn't see a triangle as half a rectangle; confuses slant with perpendicular height.
  **Fix:** two copies of the triangle make a rectangle → area is *half* base × height; height is the perpendicular distance.

- **Wrong:** thinks the taller/bigger container always holds more.
  **Why:** judges capacity by one dimension.
  **Fix:** pour the same water between a tall thin and a short wide container — same amount.

## Time

- **Wrong:** reads "half past 3" as half past 4 (short hand near 4); or reads the minute hand on 4 as "4 minutes."
  **Why:** reads the nearer number / treats clock numbers as minutes.
  **Fix:** the short hand says the hour just *left*; the minute hand counts in 5s (4 = 20 minutes).

- **Wrong:** writes 3 pm as 13:00.
  **Why:** adds 12 to the wrong reference.
  **Fix:** 1 pm = 13:00, so 3 pm = 15:00 (add 12 to the pm hour).

- **Wrong:** 09:50 to 10:20 = 70 minutes.
  **Why:** subtracts minutes without crossing the hour.
  **Fix:** count up: to 10:00 is 10 min, then 20 more = 30 minutes.

## Geometry & position

- **Wrong:** says a triangle balanced on its point "isn't a triangle"; or calls a cube a square.
  **Why:** thinks orientation changes a shape; mixes 2-D (flat) names with 3-D (solid) names.
  **Fix:** rotate a cut-out — same sides/corners, same shape. Flat = 2-D name, solid you can hold = 3-D name.

- **Wrong:** thinks a bigger drawing of an angle (longer arms) is a bigger angle.
  **Why:** judges angle by line length, not amount of turn.
  **Fix:** the angle is the *opening*; lengthen the arms — the turn is unchanged.

- **Wrong:** finds a missing angle on a straight line with 360 − known.
  **Why:** confuses a straight line (180°) with a full turn (360°).
  **Fix:** a straight line is a half turn = 180°; triangle angles sum to 180° too (tear the corners onto a line).

- **Wrong:** plots (3, 2) as 3 up and 2 across (reads y first).
  **Why:** reverses coordinate order.
  **Fix:** "along the corridor (x) before up the stairs (y)" — x always first.

- **Wrong:** plots (−3, 2) by going right (ignores the minus); or reflects across the wrong axis.
  **Why:** treats negatives as positives; mixes up which coordinate a reflection changes.
  **Fix:** negative x = go left, negative y = go down; reflecting across the y-axis flips the *x* sign.

- **Wrong:** calls any equal-sided shape "regular" (e.g. a rhombus).
  **Why:** checks sides but not angles.
  **Fix:** regular needs equal sides *and* equal angles.

## Statistics

- **Wrong:** with a pictogram/pie chart, counts symbols/slices as exact values and ignores the key/total.
  **Why:** treats a symbol or slice as one item rather than a proportion.
  **Fix:** read the **key** first (one symbol = N), or take the slice's fraction/percentage **of the total**.

- **Wrong:** reads every bar-chart gridline as 1 when the scale goes up in 2s/5s.
  **Why:** ignores the scale interval.
  **Fix:** read the axis numbers first; each line is worth 2 (or 5).

- **Wrong:** answers "how many more" by reading only the bigger value.
  **Why:** forgets it's a difference.
  **Fix:** "more than" = subtract the smaller from the larger.

- **Wrong:** finds the mean by dividing by the wrong count, or forgetting to add first.
  **Why:** muddles the two steps.
  **Fix:** mean = (sum of values) ÷ (number of values); count the values carefully.

## Algebra (Year 6)

- **Wrong:** reads 2a as "two-a / twenty-something," or as 2 + a.
  **Why:** doesn't know a number beside a letter means multiply.
  **Fix:** 2a = 2 × a.

- **Wrong:** solves x + 5 = 12 by changing only one side.
  **Why:** breaks the balance.
  **Fix:** whatever you do to one side, do to the other — subtract 5 from both sides.

---

## Ratio (Year 6)

- **Wrong:** shares 20 in ratio 3 : 2 as "3 and 2"; or treats 3 : 2 as the fraction "3 of the whole."
  **Why:** treats ratio numbers as actual amounts, or confuses part-to-part with part-to-whole.
  **Fix:** the numbers are **parts** — find one part (total ÷ total parts), then multiply. 3 : 2 means 3 out of every **5**.
