# Mathematics — Subject Overview (UK National Curriculum, KS1–KS2, Years 1–6)

This subject covers primary mathematics for ages 5–11, aligned to the **UK National Curriculum** for Key Stage 1 (Years 1–2) and Key Stage 2 (Years 3–6). Each `year-N.md` file holds 7–11 topics written in the per-topic contract (`_TEMPLATE.md`). Read this overview to understand *how* to teach primary maths well, then teach from the year files.

## What primary maths is for

The aim is **fluent, confident, reasoning mathematicians**, not children who can only follow recipes. The national curriculum names three aims, and every lesson should touch them:

1. **Fluency** — quick, accurate recall and method (number bonds, times tables, written methods). Built by frequent, varied practice.
2. **Reasoning** — explaining *why*, spotting patterns, justifying, generalising ("I know 6 × 4 = 24, so 6 × 40 = 240").
3. **Problem-solving** — applying maths to unfamiliar problems, often in words or real contexts, breaking them into steps.

A child who can compute but can't reason or solve is only one-third taught. Always ask "how do you know?" and "can you show me another way?"

## How to teach it: Concrete → Pictorial → Abstract (CPA)

This is the single most important method in primary maths. Every new idea should travel through three stages:

- **Concrete** — the child handles real objects: counters, cubes, fingers, coins, fruit, Lego, a bead string. They *do* the maths with their hands.
- **Pictorial** — the child draws or sees a picture of it: dots, a bar model, a number line, a part-whole circle, an array of dots.
- **Abstract** — only now the symbols: `7 + 5 = 12`, `3 × 4`, `½`.

Do not rush to the abstract. A child who "knows" 8 − 3 = 5 but cannot show it with cubes does not really understand subtraction. When a child is stuck on the abstract, **go back a stage**: get the counters out.

## Number sense first

Underneath everything is **number sense** — a feel for how big numbers are, how they relate, and whether an answer is reasonable. Build it constantly:

- **Subitising** — instantly seeing "that's 4" without counting (dice patterns, fingers).
- **Number bonds** — pairs that make 10 (and later 20, 100): 6 + 4, 7 + 3. These are the building blocks of all calculation.
- **Place value** — knowing the 3 in 36 means thirty. This is the spine of the whole curriculum; revisit it every year with bigger numbers and decimals.
- **Estimation** — "about how much?" before calculating, then checking the answer is sensible.

## Key representations to keep using

- **Bar model** — a rectangle split into parts to show part–whole, comparison, and (later) fractions and ratio. The most transferable picture in primary maths.
- **Number line** — for counting, ordering, adding/subtracting by jumps, negatives, and rounding.
- **Part–whole model** — a whole with parts branching off; the foundation of bonds, addition/subtraction, and fractions.
- **Place-value columns / base-10 blocks** — ones, tens, hundreds; the basis of all written methods.
- **Arrays** — rows and columns of dots for multiplication and division.

## How to run a maths lesson (quick playbook)

1. **Retrieval starter** — a few quick recall questions from past topics (bonds, tables). Warms the brain and spaces practice.
2. **Hook** — connect to something real and to the child's interests.
3. **Teach with CPA** — model it, narrate your thinking out loud, do a worked example.
4. **We do** — work one together; the child talks you through it.
5. **You do** — the child practises; you watch for the misconceptions listed in each topic.
6. **Check & reason** — a check question plus "how do you know?"
7. **Keep it short and frequent.** Little and often beats long and rare, especially for tables and bonds.

## Year 1 → Year 6 scope & sequence

| Year | Number & place value | Calculation (+ − × ÷) | Fractions / decimals / % | Measurement | Geometry | Statistics / Algebra |
|------|----------------------|------------------------|--------------------------|-------------|----------|----------------------|
| **1** (5–6) | Count, read, write to 100; one more/less; tens & ones | Add & subtract within 20; number bonds to 20 | Halves and quarters of shapes/quantities | Length, mass, capacity, money (coins), time (o'clock/half past) | 2-D & 3-D shapes; position & direction | — |
| **2** (6–7) | Numbers to 100; place value (tens/ones); compare & order; count in 2s/3s/5s/10s | +/− within 100; ×/÷ with 2, 5, 10 tables; commutativity | ⅓ ½ ¼ ¾; fractions of quantities | Money (£ & p), measure & compare length/mass/capacity, time to 5 mins | Properties of 2-D/3-D shapes; symmetry; position & movement | **Pictograms, tally charts, block diagrams, tables** |
| **3** (7–8) | Numbers to 1000; hundreds/tens/ones; compare & order | Written column +/− (3-digit); 3, 4, 8 tables; ×/÷ methods | Tenths; equivalent fractions; add/subtract fractions (same denom) | mm/cm/m, perimeter, money, time to the minute, 12/24 hour | Right angles, horizontal/vertical, parallel/perpendicular; 2-D/3-D | Bar charts, pictograms, tables |
| **4** (8–9) | Numbers to 10,000; rounding; negative numbers; Roman numerals | All times tables to 12×12; column ×/÷; mental strategies | Decimals (tenths/hundredths); equivalent fractions; ÷ by 10/100 | Area, perimeter, converting units, money, time | Classify shapes, angles, symmetry; coordinates; translation | Discrete & continuous data; line graphs |
| **5** (9–10) | Numbers to 1,000,000; rounding; negatives; powers of 10; primes, factors, multiples, squares & cubes | Long multiplication; short division; mental & written | Add/subtract/multiply fractions; decimals to 3 places; percentages | Area & perimeter (composite), volume, unit conversion | Angles (measure & draw), regular polygons, reflection & translation | Line graphs; tables & timetables |
| **6** (10–11) | Numbers to 10,000,000; rounding; negatives in context; order of operations | Long multiplication & long division; all four operations fluently | Four operations with fractions; %, decimals & fraction equivalence | Area & volume formulae; unit conversion; metric/imperial | Circles (radius/diameter), angles, nets, coordinates in 4 quadrants | **Pie charts, mean (average), line graphs; ratio & proportion; simple algebra (formulae, equations, sequences)** |

Each year revisits **place value** and **the four operations** with bigger numbers — the curriculum is a spiral, not a list. By the end of Year 6 a child should be fluent in written methods for all four operations, confident with fractions/decimals/percentages, and able to reason through multi-step word problems.
