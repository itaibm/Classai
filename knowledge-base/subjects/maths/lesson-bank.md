# Mathematics — Lesson Bank (ready-to-run mini lessons, question bank & activities)

Six mini lesson outlines mapped to the app's beat structure (**hook → explain → example → check → practice → recap**), each on a high-value topic, followed by a maths question bank (~12 questions with answers + a likely wrong answer each) and the highest-value practice activities. All maths verified.

---

## Lesson 1 — Long division (bus stop, 2-digit divisor)

- **Hook:** "432 stickers, 15 friends — how many each, and how many left over?" Pose it; let them guess.
- **Explain:** the D-M-S-B loop — Divide, Multiply, Subtract, Bring down — written under a bus stop so every step shows.
- **Example:** work `432 ÷ 15` live → 28 r 12, then carry to a decimal → 28.8. Narrate each step.
- **Check:** `196 ÷ 14` (=14, exact). Watch for stopping early / reporting the remainder as the answer.
- **Practice:** numberEntry ladder — one exact (`247 ÷ 13 = 19`), one whole-remainder (`500 ÷ 16 = 31 r 4`), one decimal (`500 ÷ 16 = 31.25`); check each by inverse multiplication.
- **Recap:** "What are the four steps, and what can a remainder become?" (whole, fraction, decimal).

## Lesson 2 — Sharing in a ratio

- **Hook:** "Share 24 sweets between us in the ratio 1 : 3 — fair or not?"
- **Explain:** add the parts → find one part (total ÷ parts) → multiply each share; **check the shares sum to the whole**.
- **Example:** £40 in ratio 3 : 5 → 8 parts → £5 each → £15 and £25 (sum 40 ✓).
- **Check:** "Share 30 in ratio 2 : 3." (12 and 18). Watch for "just halve it."
- **Practice:** numberEntry word problems (paint 1 : 4, smoothie 2 : 3 scaling) with a built-in sum check.
- **Recap:** "Why do we count parts, not people?"

## Lesson 3 — Percentages of an amount (the 10% / 1% method)

- **Hook:** "A £40 game, 25% off — what do you pay?"
- **Explain:** 10% = ÷10, 1% = ÷100, 5% = half of 10%; build any % from these bricks. "Of" means multiply.
- **Example:** 35% of £80 → 10% = £8, 30% = £24, 5% = £4, total **£28**; verify 0.35 × 80 = 28.
- **Check:** "20% of £50." (£10). Watch for ÷100 to get 10%.
- **Practice:** matchPairs (½/¼/¾ ↔ decimal ↔ %) then numberEntry "% of an amount".
- **Recap:** "What's the difference between dividing by 10 and by 100?"

## Lesson 4 — Negative numbers across zero

- **Hook:** "It's −6 °C; it warms by 9 degrees. Warmer or colder than freezing now?"
- **Explain:** a number line with 0 in the middle — left is smaller/colder; cross zero in two hops to find a difference.
- **Example:** −6 + 9 → 6 right to 0, 3 more → **+3 °C**; difference −6 to 4 = 6 + 4 = **10**.
- **Check:** "Order 3, −7, 0, −2, 5." (−7, −2, 0, 3, 5). Watch for judging by the digit.
- **Practice:** ordering temperatures coldest→warmest, then a rise/fall numberEntry across zero.
- **Recap:** "Which is bigger, −8 or −3, and how do you know?"

## Lesson 5 — The mean (average), forwards and backwards

- **Hook:** "Four spelling tests; can you tell me one number that sums up the week?"
- **Explain:** mean = total ÷ count (share the pile level); reverse it: total = mean × count.
- **Example:** 7, 9, 4, 8, 7 → 35 ÷ 5 = **7**; then "fifth test to lift the average from 6 to 7" → 35 − 24 = **11**.
- **Check:** "Mean of 3, 5, 10." (6). Watch for dividing by the wrong count.
- **Practice:** numberEntry — three means then two reverse-mean "find the missing value" puzzles.
- **Recap:** "Must the mean be one of the numbers?" (No.)

## Lesson 6 — Two-step word problems (RUCSAC + bar model)

- **Hook:** "4 packs of 6 cakes at £3 each, paid with £20 — what's the change?"
- **Explain:** Read, Underline numbers/question, Choose operations, Solve in steps, Answer with units, Check; draw a bar.
- **Example:** step 1 cost 4 × £3 = £12; step 2 change £20 − £12 = **£8**; note "6 cakes" is a distractor.
- **Check:** "3 boxes of 8 pencils, 5 broken — how many work?" (19). Watch for stopping after step one.
- **Practice:** three two-step problems (one with a distractor); child describes the bar model first.
- **Recap:** "Did I answer the actual question, with units?"

## Question bank (with answer + a likely wrong answer)

1. **`432 ÷ 15` (remainder).** A: 28 r 12. Wrong: 28 r 2 (subtracts wrongly at the last step).
2. **`500 ÷ 16` as a decimal.** A: 31.25. Wrong: 31.4 (mishandles the remainder ÷16).
3. **Share £40 in ratio 3 : 5.** A: £15 and £25. Wrong: £20 and £20 (treats ratio as "split equally").
4. **Paint red : white = 1 : 4; 5 tins red → white?** A: 20 tins. Wrong: 9 tins (adds 4 instead of scaling).
5. **35% of £80.** A: £28. Wrong: £35 (reads the percent as pounds).
6. **Write ¾ as a percentage.** A: 75%. Wrong: 34% (reads ¾ as "3 over 4 → 34").
7. **−6 °C rises 9 degrees.** A: +3 °C. Wrong: +15 °C (adds without crossing zero).
8. **Order 3, −7, 0, −2, 5 smallest first.** A: −7, −2, 0, 3, 5. Wrong: 0, −2, −7, 3, 5 (orders negatives by digit).
9. **Mean of 7, 9, 4, 8, 7.** A: 7. Wrong: 8.75 (divides by 4, miscounts).
10. **Three numbers, mean 5; two are 4 and 6; third?** A: 5. Wrong: 10 (adds 4 + 6 instead of using total 15).
11. **Area of an L-shape: 6×4 with a 2×2 corner removed.** A: 20 cm². Wrong: 24 cm² (forgets to subtract the notch).
12. **Read the Roman numeral MCMXLIV.** A: 1944. Wrong: 1956 (adds the subtraction pairs instead of subtracting).
13. **Is 51 prime?** A: No (3 × 17). Wrong: Yes (doesn't test divisibility by 3 via digit sum).
14. **Solve `3n − 4 = 17`.** A: n = 7. Wrong: n = 4.33 (divides by 3 before undoing the −4).

## Highest-value practice activities (maths)

- **Hit the Button / Times Tables Rock Stars** — daily 3-minute bonds & tables fluency (the single biggest lever; little and often).
- **Bar-model drawing** — turn every word problem into a part–whole or comparison bar before calculating.
- **Concrete → Pictorial → Abstract trio** — when stuck on the abstract, go back to counters/base-10 blocks, then a drawing.
- **"How do you know?" reasoning prompts** — after every answer, ask for a justification or a second method.
- **Reverse problems** — give the answer, ask for the question (e.g. "the answer is 24, write a two-step problem").
- **Retrieval starters** — open each session with 4–5 quick recall questions from past topics (spaced practice).
- **Estimate-then-check** — round to estimate first, then compute, then test the answer is sensible.
- **Real-money / real-time tasks** — shopping change, journey durations, recipe scaling — for percentages, ratio and time.
