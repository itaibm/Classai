# Art & Design + Music — Subject Overview (UK National Curriculum, KS1–KS2, Years 1–6)

This subject covers **primary Art & Design** and **primary Music** for ages 5–11, aligned to the **UK National Curriculum** for Key Stage 1 (Years 1–2) and Key Stage 2 (Years 3–6). Maps to Classai `subjectKey` **`general`**. Two content files hold the teachable topics in the per-topic contract (`_TEMPLATE.md`):
- `art-ks1-ks2.md` — 11 art topics.
- `music-ks1-ks2.md` — 11 music topics.

Read this overview to understand *how* to teach primary art and music well, then teach from the topic files. Everything here is doable at home with **basic materials** (paper, pencil, paint or felt-tips, scissors, glue, household objects) and the child's own voice/body for music.

# Part A — Art & Design

## What primary art is for

The National Curriculum aims for art are that children should **produce creative work, become proficient in techniques (drawing, painting, sculpture), evaluate their own and others' work, and know about great artists and designers**. Art is a *making* subject — children learn by doing, looking, and improving, not by filling in worksheets. There are no "wrong" pictures, but there are skills that genuinely get better with practice and feedback.

## The formal elements of art

Almost all art teaching hangs off the **formal elements** — the basic building blocks every artwork uses. Teach and name them again and again so children have language to talk about art:

1. **Line** — marks of any kind: thick/thin, straight/curved, wavy, zig-zag, dotted. The foundation of drawing.
2. **Shape** — flat, 2-D areas: geometric (circle, square) and organic (leaf, blob).
3. **Tone** — how light or dark something is. Tone (shading) makes a flat drawing look round and 3-D.
4. **Colour** — primary, secondary, warm/cool; mixing and matching (see the colour wheel topic).
5. **Texture** — how a surface feels or looks like it would feel: rough, smooth, bumpy, soft. Real texture (collage) or implied (drawn).
6. **Pattern** — a design that repeats. Found in nature and made by printing/repeating.
7. **Form** — 3-D shape with volume: a sphere, a cube, a clay model. Form is shape you could walk around.
8. **Space** — the area in and around objects; how we show near/far (foreground, background, overlap, size) — leads to perspective in upper KS2.

When you teach any topic, name which elements it uses ("today we're working with *line* and *tone*"). This is the spine of primary art.

## How to teach it: sketchbooks, study, and the make–look–improve cycle

- **Sketchbooks are the engine.** From KS1 up, children keep a sketchbook to **collect, experiment, practise and record** — colour tests, line warm-ups, observational sketches, ideas, reflections. It is a place to *try*, not a gallery of finished pieces. Never grade a sketchbook for neatness.
- **Study real artists.** The curriculum requires children to learn about a **range of artists, craftspeople and designers**, and to describe differences and similarities between them. Look at a real artwork, talk about what you see (using the formal-element words), then make something *inspired by* it. This builds both knowledge and technique.
- **Observational drawing.** Draw from real objects, not from imagination or copying cartoons. "Draw what you actually see" — the slow looking is where the skill grows.
- **Make → look → improve.** Make something; step back and evaluate it ("what's working? what would you change?"); then try again or refine. Evaluating your own work is a curriculum aim, not an optional extra.
- **Process over product.** Praise effort, experiment and looking — not just the prettiest result. A messy, brave attempt teaches more than a tidy copy.

## Materials you need at home
Pencils (a soft one if possible), paper, an eraser; felt-tips or paints (red, blue, yellow, white, black is enough — you can mix the rest); scissors and glue; old magazines/leaves/sponges for printing and collage; junk-modelling boxes or play-dough/clay for 3-D. That's everything.

# Part B — Music

## What primary music is for

The National Curriculum aims for music are that children should **perform, listen, review and evaluate; create and compose; and understand and explore how music is created** through the inter-related dimensions of music. The cycle at the heart of primary music is **Listen → Perform → Compose** — children take in music, make it with voices and instruments, and invent their own.

## The inter-related dimensions of music

These are music's building blocks (the music equivalent of art's formal elements). Teach and name them throughout:

1. **Pulse (beat)** — the steady heartbeat you can clap or march to. Everything sits on the pulse.
2. **Rhythm** — the *pattern* of long and short sounds over the pulse (e.g. the rhythm of words/names). Rhythm ≠ pulse.
3. **Pitch** — how high or low a sound is. Melodies are patterns of pitch.
4. **Tempo** — the speed: fast or slow (and getting faster/slower).
5. **Dynamics** — the volume: loud or quiet (and getting louder/softer). Dynamics ≠ tempo.
6. **Timbre** — the *type* of sound / tone colour: what makes a drum sound different from a flute or a voice.
7. **Texture** — how many layers of sound at once: one voice (thin) vs a whole band (thick).
8. **Structure** — the shape/order of a piece: a beginning, repeats, verse–chorus, etc.

Children don't need to memorise definitions; they need to *hear, feel and use* these. Always demonstrate with body, voice or simple instruments.

## How to teach it: listen → perform → compose, with body and voice first

- **The voice and body are free instruments.** Singing, clapping, stamping, tapping — you need no equipment. Body percussion teaches pulse, rhythm, dynamics and tempo with zero cost.
- **Listen and appraise widely.** Play a short piece, then ask "what do you notice?" using the dimension words (Is the pulse fast or slow? Loud or quiet? High or low? What instruments?). Expose children to a *range of styles* — classical, folk, pop, music from around the world.
- **Perform together.** Sing songs, clap rhythms, keep a steady pulse, play simple patterns. Performing builds confidence and ensemble skills.
- **Compose simply.** Children invent: a rhythm from their name, a short pattern, a piece with a start–middle–end, a sound to represent a story or weather. Record it (a phone, or invent symbols) so it can be repeated.
- **Pitch the difficulty with body before notation.** Feel and do music first; formal notation (Topic 8) comes after children can already keep a pulse and copy rhythms.

## Materials you need at home
Your voice and body cover most of it. Add anything that makes a sound: pots/pans, a shaker (rice in a tub), a glass of water tapped with a spoon, a keyboard app or real instrument if you have one. A phone to record. That's plenty.

## KS1 → KS2 scope & sequence

| | **KS1 (Years 1–2, ages 5–7)** | **Lower KS2 (Years 3–4, ages 7–9)** | **Upper KS2 (Years 5–6, ages 9–11)** |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Art focus** | Explore the formal elements; draw, paint, print, collage; experiment with materials; talk about what you see; start a sketchbook | Develop techniques with control; sketchbooks to record/review; observational drawing; study artists and make in their style; pattern, printing, 3-D | Refine and combine techniques; deliberate use of tone, colour, texture; perspective & space; compare and analyse a range of artists; sustained projects |
| **Art skills built** | Line, shape, colour-mixing, simple printing, junk-model sculpture | Colour wheel, painting control, relief printing, collage, observational drawing, clay/3-D form, artist studies | Tone & shading, perspective/space, advanced colour, mixed media, evaluating & improving work |
| **Music focus** | Feel the pulse; copy rhythms; high/low (pitch); sing together; loud/quiet, fast/slow; explore instrument sounds | Pulse + rhythm with note values; pitch & simple melody; singing in tune; timbre & instrument families; begin reading/inventing notation | All dimensions combined; compose with structure; perform with control; simple staff notation; listen & appraise across styles; basic music history |
| **Music skills built** | Keep a steady beat, echo rhythms, sing in a group, name loud/quiet & fast/slow | Note values (crotchet/quaver), pitch shapes, dynamics & tempo language, timbre, graphic & first standard notation | Compose a short structured piece, read/write simple notation, evaluate performances, place music in style/era |

Both subjects **spiral**: the formal elements (art) and the dimensions (music) come back every year with more control, more vocabulary and more independence. By the end of KS2 a child should draw/paint/make with deliberate technique and talk about artists, and should keep a pulse, sing in tune, read simple rhythms, compose a short structured piece and discuss a range of music.
