# English / Literacy Progressions — UK NC ↔ US CCSS ELA ↔ IB PYP

Concept-by-concept progression tables for primary English/literacy (ages 5–11): reading (phonics → fluency → comprehension), writing, grammar/spelling, and speaking & listening.

**Code format:** CCSS ELA codes carry the prefix `CCSS.ELA-LITERACY.` (omitted below). Strands:
- **RF** = Reading: Foundational Skills (**K–5 only** — phonics & fluency)
- **RL** = Reading: Literature · **RI** = Reading: Informational Text
- **W** = Writing · **L** = Language (grammar, spelling, vocabulary) · **SL** = Speaking & Listening

**Remember the offset:** US Grade ≈ UK Year − 1 (Year 3 ≈ Grade 2). See `README.md`.

**IB PYP language** = 3 strands delivered as 4 continuums: *Oral language (listening & speaking); Written language (reading); Written language (writing); Visual language (viewing & presenting)*. IB **Language has 5 phases** (1–5); phase mapping is *approximate* (Ph 1 ≈ 3–5, Ph 2 ≈ 5–7, Ph 3 ≈ 7–9, Ph 4 ≈ 9–11, Ph 5 ≈ 11–12).

Codes verified against thecorestandards.org.

> **Key structural fact (verified):** RF Print Concepts (`RF.x.1`) and Phonological Awareness (`RF.x.2`) exist **only at K and Grade 1**. RF Phonics (`RF.x.3`) and Fluency (`RF.x.4`) run **K–5**. The whole RF strand **ends at Grade 5**. So `RF.2.1`, `RF.3.1`, `RF.2.2` … do **not** exist — do not cite them.

## 1. Reading — Foundational Skills (Phonics → Decoding → Fluency)

| Idea | UK NC Year | US CCSS code(s) | Grade | IB PYP (continuum · phase) |
|------|-----------|------------------|-------|-----------------------------|
| Print concepts (left-right, words, letters) | Reception/Y1 | RF.K.1, RF.1.1 | K, Gr 1 | Reading · Ph 1–2 |
| Phonological / phonemic awareness (rhyme, oral blend/segment) | Y1 (Phase 1–2 phonics) | RF.K.2, RF.1.2 | K, Gr 1 | Reading · Ph 1–2 |
| Phonics & word recognition — GPCs, blending CVC, digraphs | Y1 (5–6) | RF.K.3, RF.1.3 | K, Gr 1 | Reading · Ph 2 |
| Phonics — alternative spellings, common exception words, suffixes | Y2 (6–7) | RF.2.3 | Gr 2 | Reading · Ph 2–3 |
| Decoding multisyllable & morphology (prefixes/suffixes, roots) | Y3–Y4 (7–9) | RF.3.3, RF.4.3 | Gr 3, Gr 4 | Reading · Ph 3 |
| Fluent automatic word recognition; unfamiliar word strategies | Y5–Y6 (9–11) | RF.5.3 | Gr 5 | Reading · Ph 4 |
| Fluency — accuracy, rate, prosody, self-correction | Y1→Y6 | RF.K.4 → RF.5.4 (all grades K–5) | K–Gr 5 | Reading · Ph 2–4 |

*Note: UK's systematic synthetic phonics (Letters & Sounds Phases 1–6) front-loads decoding across Reception–Y2; CCSS RF spreads the same skills K–5 and adds explicit multisyllable/morphology decoding (RF.3.3–RF.5.3) that UK folds into spelling and reading.*

## 2. Reading — Comprehension (Literature & Informational)

| Idea | UK NC Year | US CCSS code(s) | Grade | IB PYP (continuum · phase) |
|------|-----------|------------------|-------|-----------------------------|
| Retrieval — ask/answer questions about a text | Y1–Y2 (5–7) | RL.K.1/RL.1.1/RL.2.1; RI.K.1/RI.1.1/RI.2.1 | K–Gr 2 | Reading · Ph 2 |
| Theme / central message / recount; main idea | Y2–Y3 (6–8) | RL.x.2; RI.x.2 (each grade) | K–Gr 5 | Reading · Ph 3 |
| Characters, setting, events; describe connections | Y2–Y4 (6–9) | RL.x.3; RI.x.3 (each grade) | K–Gr 5 | Reading · Ph 3 |
| Inference — what the text implies, with evidence | Y3–Y6 (7–11) | RL.3.1, RL.4.1, RL.5.1; RI.3.1, RI.4.1, RI.5.1 (cite evidence) | Gr 3–5 | Reading · Ph 3–4 |
| Compare texts; author's purpose; viewpoint; structure | Y5–Y6 (9–11) | RL.5.5–6, RI.5.5–6, RI.5.8 | Gr 5 | Reading / Viewing & presenting · Ph 4 |

*Note: the RL/RI strands run .1–.10 at every grade K–5 and share the same anchor standards, so the *number* (.1 = key ideas, .2 = central idea, .3 = characters/relationships) is stable across grades — only the difficulty rises. `RL.x.8` is a "not applicable to literature" placeholder; don't cite it.*

## 3. Writing — Composition

| Idea | UK NC Year | US CCSS code(s) | Grade | IB PYP (continuum · phase) |
|------|-----------|------------------|-------|-----------------------------|
| Compose sentences orally then write; sequence ideas | Y1 (5–6) | W.K.1–3, W.1.1–3 (draw/dictate/write) | K, Gr 1 | Writing · Ph 2 |
| Narrative & non-narrative; structure for purpose | Y2 (6–7) | W.2.1 (opinion), W.2.2 (informative), W.2.3 (narrative) | Gr 2 | Writing · Ph 2–3 |
| Paragraphs; plan, draft, edit; organise for the reader | Y3–Y4 (7–9) | W.3.1–3, W.4.1–3; W.x.4–5 (production, planning/revising) | Gr 3, Gr 4 | Writing · Ph 3 |
| Cohesion, formality, purpose & audience; evidence from texts | Y5–Y6 (9–11) | W.5.1–3; W.4.9/W.5.9 (draw evidence — **begins Grade 4**) | Gr 4–5 | Writing · Ph 4 |

*Note: across CCSS, `W.x.1` = **opinion** writing K–5 (becomes "argument" only at Grade 6+), `W.x.2` = informative/explanatory, `W.x.3` = narrative. Drawing evidence from texts (`W.x.9`) starts at Grade 4. UK's emphasis on cohesion/formality (Y5–Y6) aligns with W.5 plus the language strand.*

## 4. Grammar, Punctuation & Spelling (VGP)

| Idea | UK NC Year | US CCSS code(s) | Grade | IB PYP (continuum · phase) |
|------|-----------|------------------|-------|-----------------------------|
| Capital letters, full stops; basic sentence; finger spaces | Y1 (5–6) | L.K.1, L.K.2, L.1.1, L.1.2 | K, Gr 1 | Writing · Ph 2 |
| Word classes (noun/verb/adj); conjunctions; suffixes; commas in lists | Y2 (6–7) | L.2.1, L.2.2 | Gr 2 | Writing · Ph 2–3 |
| Tense; clauses & conjunctions; apostrophes; prefixes | Y3–Y4 (7–9) | L.3.1–2, L.4.1–2 | Gr 3, Gr 4 | Writing · Ph 3 |
| Clause types, modal verbs, relative clauses, punctuation for clarity | Y5–Y6 (9–11) | L.5.1, L.5.2 | Gr 5 | Writing · Ph 4 |
| Vocabulary — meaning from context, roots/affixes, word relationships | Y2–Y6 | L.x.4 (context/affixes), L.x.5 (figurative/relationships), L.x.6 (acquire & use) | K–Gr 5 | Writing/Reading · Ph 2–4 |
| Spelling — patterns, rules, homophones, the statutory word lists | Y1–Y6 | folded into RF.x.3 + L.x.2 (spelling conventions) | K–Gr 5 | Writing · Ph 2–4 |

*Note: UK NC pins much grammar to specific Years via the statutory VGP appendix; CCSS spreads equivalent conventions across `L.x.1` (grammar) and `L.x.2` (capitalisation/punctuation/spelling) at every grade K–5. `L.x.3` (knowledge of language) starts at Grade 2.*

## 5. Speaking & Listening (Spoken Language)

| Idea | UK NC Year | US CCSS code(s) | Grade | IB PYP (continuum · phase) |
|------|-----------|------------------|-------|-----------------------------|
| Listen & respond; take turns in discussion; ask questions | Y1–Y2 (5–7) | SL.K.1, SL.1.1, SL.2.1 (collaborative discussion) | K–Gr 2 | Listening & speaking · Ph 2 |
| Build on others' ideas; explain understanding; follow rules of talk | Y3–Y4 (7–9) | SL.3.1, SL.4.1 | Gr 3, Gr 4 | Listening & speaking · Ph 3 |
| Present/recount on a topic with facts & detail; tell a story | Y2–Y6 | SL.x.4 (report on a topic / present) — each grade | K–Gr 5 | Listening & speaking · Ph 2–4 |
| Debate, reason, use Standard English; adapt talk to audience | Y5–Y6 (9–11) | SL.5.1, SL.5.4, SL.5.6 | Gr 5 | Listening & speaking / Viewing & presenting · Ph 4 |

*Note: UK NC has a single overarching "Spoken language" programme that spans all Years rather than year-by-year statements; CCSS pins discussion to `SL.x.1` and presenting to `SL.x.4` at each grade K–5. IB's "Viewing and presenting" continuum has no clean CCSS twin — closest matches are SL.x.4–5 plus the RI/W media standards.*

---

### Cross-cutting note for the tutor
The three frameworks agree on the **arc**: oral language → phonics → decoding → fluency → comprehension, with writing and grammar growing alongside. They differ in **granularity**: UK NC is the most prescriptive (statutory word lists, a year-by-year grammar appendix); CCSS is strand-and-anchor based (same `.1/.2/.3` meaning at every grade, rising in difficulty); IB PYP is the loosest (developmental phases, with a distinctive **"viewing & presenting"** visual-literacy strand the others lack). When a US parent gives a grade, pull the UK Year one *higher* (Grade 3 → Year 4) and read the row across.
