# Humanities & Other Subjects — Crosswalk (UK NC ↔ US norms ↔ IB PYP)

A **lighter** cross-reference for history, geography, the arts, computing, world languages, and PSHE/life-skills. These subjects are **far less standardised internationally** than maths, literacy, and science, so this file maps *broad alignment* rather than exact codes.

**Why lighter:**
- The **US has no national Common Core for these subjects.** History/geography/civics in the US is guided by the **C3 Framework** (College, Career & Civic Life) and varies enormously **state by state** (each state writes its own social-studies standards). So "US code" below usually means a C3 dimension or a state-norm description, not a precise code.
- The **arts** use the **US National Core Arts Standards** (NCAS), organised by artistic process, not grade-locked the way CCSS is.
- **Computing** has the **CSTA K-12 Computer Science Standards** (US) and **ISTE** standards; UK has a strong statutory **Computing** programme.
- **World languages** use **ACTFL proficiency levels** (US) and **CEFR** (Europe/international) — both proficiency-based, not age-based.
- **IB PYP** delivers all of these through **transdisciplinary units of inquiry** under its 6 themes, with no discrete standards. The PYP theme is the most honest mapping.

**Remember the offset:** US Grade ≈ UK Year − 1. See `README.md`. Treat everything here as **approx.** unless a named framework is cited.

The 6 IB PYP themes (used throughout below): *Who we are · Where we are in place and time · How we express ourselves · How the world works · How we organize ourselves · Sharing the planet.*

---

## 1. History

UK NC has a detailed statutory History programme (KS1: within living memory, significant individuals; KS2: Stone Age→Iron Age, Romans, Anglo-Saxons, Vikings, Ancient civilisations, a local-history study). The US has **no national history curriculum** — the **C3 Framework** sets *inquiry* dimensions, and content is set per state.

| Idea | UK NC | US (C3 / state norms) | IB PYP theme |
|------|-------|------------------------|---------------|
| Changes within living memory; my family's past; old vs new | KS1 (Y1–Y2) | C3 Dim. 2 (History): change/continuity; varies by state | Where we are in place and time |
| Significant individuals & events (national/global) | KS1 (Y1–Y2) | C3 Dim. 2; varies by state | Where we are in place and time |
| Chronology, ancient civilisations, local history study | KS2 (Y3–Y6) | C3 Dim. 2–4 (causation, evidence, communicating conclusions); state-specific eras | Where we are in place and time |
| Using sources & evidence; interpreting the past | KS1–KS2 | **C3 Framework** Dimensions 1 (questions) → 3 (sources/evidence) → 4 (communicating) | Transdisciplinary research/thinking skills |

*Note: the C3 Framework is process-not-content, so it maps to UK's "historical enquiry" strand far better than to UK's specified eras. Specific periods (Romans, Vikings) are UK-particular; US states each pick different content (e.g. US state history, Native American history, US founding).*

## 2. Geography

UK NC Geography (KS1: local area, UK & world maps, hot/cold places; KS2: regions, rivers, mountains, climate zones, fieldwork). US again has **no national geography curriculum**; the older **National Geography Standards** and **C3 Dimension 2 (Geography)** are the reference points.

| Idea | UK NC | US (C3 / Geography standards) | IB PYP theme |
|------|-------|--------------------------------|---------------|
| Local area, simple maps, directional language, human/physical features | KS1 (Y1–Y2) | C3 Geo (spatial views of the world); state norms | Where we are in place and time / Sharing the planet |
| Continents, oceans, hot & cold places, comparing places | KS1 (Y1–Y2) | C3 Geo (human-environment interaction) | Sharing the planet |
| Maps, atlases, grid references, climate zones, biomes, rivers, mountains | KS2 (Y3–Y6) | C3 Geo Dim. 2; National Geography Standards (place, region, environment) | Where we are in place and time / Sharing the planet |
| Fieldwork, observation, measurement & recording | KS2 | C3 Dim. 3 (gather/evaluate sources) | Transdisciplinary research skills |

## 3. The Arts (Art & Design, Music)

US uses the **National Core Arts Standards (NCAS)** — organised by four artistic processes (**Creating, Performing/Producing, Responding, Connecting**) across grade bands, not single grades. UK NC has separate statutory **Art & Design** and **Music** programmes.

| Idea | UK NC | US (NCAS) | IB PYP theme |
|------|-------|-----------|---------------|
| Use materials/media; explore line, colour, shape, texture | Art KS1–KS2 | NCAS **Creating** (VA:Cr) grade bands | How we express ourselves |
| Study artists/craftspeople; respond to & evaluate art | Art KS1–KS2 | NCAS **Responding / Connecting** (VA:Re, VA:Cn) | How we express ourselves |
| Sing, play tuned/untuned instruments, pulse & rhythm | Music KS1–KS2 | NCAS **Performing** (MU:Pr) grade bands | How we express ourselves |
| Listen, appraise, improvise & compose; staff/other notation | Music KS1–KS2 | NCAS **Creating / Responding** (MU:Cr, MU:Re) | How we express ourselves |

*Note: NCAS is proficiency/process-based and uses grade bands (e.g. Pre-K, K, 1–8 by grade), so it does not pin to single UK Years. Map by **process** (Create / Perform / Respond / Connect), not by grade.*

## 4. Computing

UK NC **Computing** is statutory and strong (KS1: algorithms, simple programs, debugging, online safety; KS2: programming with variables/selection/repetition, networks, the internet, evaluating digital content). US references are the **CSTA K-12 CS Standards** and **ISTE Standards for Students**.

| Idea | UK NC | US (CSTA / ISTE) | IB PYP theme |
|------|-------|-------------------|---------------|
| What algorithms are; create & debug simple programs (e.g. Bee-Bot, Scratch Jr) | KS1 (Y1–Y2) | CSTA 1A-AP (algorithms & programming, K–2) | How the world works / How we organize ourselves |
| Logical reasoning to predict program behaviour | KS1–KS2 | CSTA 1A/1B-AP; ISTE "Computational Thinker" | How the world works |
| Programming with sequence, selection, repetition, variables | KS2 (Y3–Y6) | CSTA 1B-AP (grades 3–5) | How the world works |
| Networks, the internet, how data is transmitted | KS2 (Y3–Y6) | CSTA 1B-NI (networks & the internet) | How the world works / How we organize ourselves |
| Online safety; using technology responsibly | KS1–KS2 | CSTA Impacts of Computing; ISTE "Digital Citizen" | Who we are |

*Note: UK's statutory Computing is more prescriptive than US practice (CSTA/ISTE are voluntary, adopted unevenly by states). The **online-safety / digital-citizenship** strand maps cleanly across all three.*

## 5. World Languages

Neither UK Year nor US Grade is the right axis here — **second-language learning is proficiency-based, not age-based.** UK NC requires a foreign language at **KS2 (Y3–Y6)**. US uses **ACTFL** proficiency levels (Novice → Intermediate → Advanced) and the **5 C's** (Communication, Cultures, Connections, Comparisons, Communities); Europe/international uses **CEFR** (A1–C2).

| Idea | UK NC | US ACTFL / CEFR | IB PYP theme |
|------|-------|------------------|---------------|
| Listen & respond to spoken language; join in songs/rhymes | KS2 (Y3–Y6) | ACTFL **Novice-Low/Mid** (interpretive listening); CEFR **A1** | How we express ourselves |
| Speak in sentences; ask/answer simple questions; basic conversation | KS2 (Y3–Y6) | ACTFL **Novice-Mid/High** (interpersonal); CEFR **A1–A2** | How we express ourselves |
| Read & show understanding of words/phrases/simple texts | KS2 (Y3–Y6) | ACTFL Novice (interpretive reading); CEFR **A1** | How we express ourselves |
| Write words/phrases/short sentences from memory | KS2 (Y3–Y6) | ACTFL Novice (presentational writing); CEFR **A1** | How we express ourselves |
| Grammar — gender, key features, comparison with English | KS2 (Y3–Y6) | ACTFL "Comparisons" (the 5 C's) | How we express ourselves |

*Note: a typical primary learner finishing UK KS2 sits around **ACTFL Novice-Mid/High ≈ CEFR A1**. IB PYP's "additional language" learning is also phase-based (its 5 language phases), not age-locked. Map by **proficiency**, never by grade.*

## 6. PSHE / Life Skills (Health, Relationships, Citizenship, Wellbeing)

UK has statutory **Relationships Education** and **Health Education** (primary) plus non-statutory **PSHE**. The US has **no national standard**; the closest references are **CASEL** (Social & Emotional Learning competencies), the **National Health Education Standards (NHES)**, and state SEL standards. IB PYP weaves this through **"Who we are"** and its **attributes of the Learner Profile** + transdisciplinary self-management skills.

| Idea | UK (statutory RSHE / PSHE) | US (CASEL / NHES) | IB PYP theme |
|------|-----------------------------|--------------------|---------------|
| Feelings, self-awareness, managing emotions | Health Ed (mental wellbeing) | CASEL: Self-awareness, Self-management | Who we are |
| Friendships, families, respectful relationships | Relationships Ed | CASEL: Relationship skills, Social awareness | Who we are |
| Healthy bodies — diet, exercise, sleep, hygiene, dental | Health Ed | NHES (health-promoting behaviours) | Who we are |
| Staying safe — online, road, people who help us | Health Ed / Relationships Ed | NHES; ISTE Digital Citizen | Who we are / How we organize ourselves |
| Responsible decisions, money sense, community & citizenship | PSHE (non-statutory) | CASEL: Responsible decision-making | How we organize ourselves |

*Note: PSHE/life-skills is the **least standardised** area of all. UK's RSHE is statutory and specific; US is a patchwork of CASEL-informed SEL plus state health standards. Map to **CASEL's 5 SEL competencies** (self-awareness, self-management, social awareness, relationship skills, responsible decision-making) plus the PYP Learner Profile for the most stable cross-reference.*

### Cross-cutting note for the tutor
For these subjects, **content is genuinely different across countries** (UK children learn the Romans and Vikings; US children learn US history and state geography). So:
1. **Teach the skill/process, label it with the home framework.** History/geography enquiry skills, the four arts processes, computational thinking, language proficiency, and SEL competencies all transfer; specific content does not.
2. **Default to the IB PYP theme** as the lingua franca — it's the one framework explicitly built to be content-flexible and is named in every row above.
3. **Don't invent codes.** Where this file says "C3 Dim. 2", "NCAS Creating", "CSTA 1B-AP", "ACTFL Novice", "CASEL", or "CEFR A1", those are real named frameworks — but they are *bands/dimensions*, not grade-precise codes, and US state implementation varies. Say "approx." and name the framework rather than fabricating a code.
