# Method entry contract (every methods file follows this)

The **methods library** is the heart of the knowledge base — "all methods, all practices." Each file groups a *family* of related teaching methods. Every individual method is written in the block below so it can be browsed by a human and retrieved by the Classai AI when choosing how to teach.

Keep entries concrete and usable. This is a practitioner's reference, not a literature review — cite evidence in one line, then get practical.

---

## [Method name]

**Family:** (e.g. Retrieval & memory) · **Evidence:** strong / moderate / promising / mixed — (named source, e.g. "EEF +5mo; Roediger & Karpicke 2006") · **Best for:** ages / subjects / situations where it shines.

**What it is.** One or two plain sentences.

**Why it works.** The cognitive or motivational mechanism (load, retrieval strength, feedback, agency…).

**When to use / when NOT.** The situations it fits, and where it backfires (e.g. inquiry before basics; group work for a solo learner).

**How to run it (steps).** Numbered, concrete steps a teacher follows.

**1:1 AI-tutor adaptation.** How the Classai tutor specifically uses it — tie to its lesson beats (hook→explain→example→check→practice→recap), its block tool-belt, the wrong-answer→remedy loop, and its two-tier memory. This line is what makes the method *operational* for the app.

**Primary example.** A concrete ages-5–11 example (name the subject + topic).

**Pitfalls.** The 1–3 ways it's commonly done badly.

**Related.** Links to sibling methods: [[method-name]] (liberal cross-linking is encouraged).

---

> Writers: aim for breadth — cover every method in your assigned family, even briefly, rather than only the famous few. Always fill **Evidence**, **When to use / NOT**, **How to run**, and **1:1 AI-tutor adaptation** — those four are what the app and the teacher need most. Be honest about evidence strength; flag fads and debunked ideas (e.g. learning styles) explicitly as "avoid".
