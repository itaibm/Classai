## Year overview

This is the year children move from **sentences to paragraphs and from exchanges to real
conversations**. By July they can hold a 3–4 minute conversation about themselves, their routine,
school, town, food and festivals, what they did last weekend or holiday, and what they are going to
do — and write a connected paragraph using **three time frames** (present, preterite past and near
future) with opinions and reasons.

**What we assume from Year 5 (ages 10–11):** Spanish phonics; greetings and personal information;
the present tense of regular -ar, -er and -ir verbs in the singular; adjective agreement; some past
chunks (*fui*, *comí*, *vi*); *voy a* + infinitive; sequencing words; countries and capitals of the
Spanish-speaking world; retelling a simple story. **What Year 6 adds:** full present-tense paradigms
(all persons) and the key irregulars (*ser, estar, tener, ir, hacer*); reflexive verbs; *gustar* with
plurals and *encantar*; comparatives; *hay* and prepositions of place; ordering food politely; the
regular preterite in all persons plus the key irregulars *fui, hice, fue*; weather and the near future;
connectives and complex sentences; reading short authentic and graded texts. **What secondary relies
on:** secure present, preterite and near future; confident pronunciation; a vocabulary of about 600
words across everyday themes; the ability to understand short texts independently.

**Method.** Comprehensible input first (stories, songs, pictures, gestures), then structured speaking
(sentence builders, question–answer chains), then writing. Every lesson starts with a 5-minute
retrieval warm-up and includes speaking. Spanish is the main language of the classroom, with English
used briefly to explain grammar. The Spanish-speaking world (21 countries where Spanish is an official
language, and large communities elsewhere) is represented throughout — not only Spain. Two lessons a
week, 35–40 minutes.

## Unit 1 — ¡Hola de nuevo! Me and my world

**Essential question:** How much can I already say about myself in Spanish — and how can I say it better?

**Key vocabulary:** me llamo, tengo … años, vivo en, soy, tengo, mi familia, hermano/a, padre, madre, abuelo/a, alto/a, bajo/a, simpático/a, divertido/a, los ojos, el pelo, mi cumpleaños es el …, y, pero, también, porque

### Lesson 1 — Retrieval: introducing myself
- **Duration:** 35 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can introduce myself in five or more connected sentences (name, age, where I live, birthday, personality) with accurate pronunciation." (CEFR A1 spoken production; UK KS3 MFL)
- **Hook:** "Speed-dating en español": how much can you say in 30 seconds?
- **Key activity:** Retrieve Year 5 personal information with a sentence builder (*Me llamo…, tengo once años, vivo en…, mi cumpleaños es el…, soy…*); pronunciation focus on *ll*, *j* and *ñ*; question–answer chains (*¿Cómo te llamas? ¿Cuántos años tienes? ¿Dónde vives?*).
- **Check for understanding:** "Why do we say *tengo once años* and not *soy once años*?" → In Spanish age is something you "have" (*tener*), not something you "are".
- **Differentiation:** support: a sentence builder with sound-spelling cues / stretch: add a connective and an extra detail to every sentence (*y*, *pero*, *también*)
- **Materials:** sentence-builder mat, mini-whiteboards, audio of native-speaker models
- **Joy:** Speed-dating conversations with a timer

### Lesson 2 — Describing myself and others: ser and tener
- **Duration:** 35 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can describe appearance and personality using *ser* and *tener* with correct adjective agreement." (CEFR A1; UK KS3 MFL — grammar)
- **Hook:** "¿Quién es?" — a mystery-person guessing game with famous Spanish speakers (e.g. footballers, singers, scientists).
- **Key activity:** Revise *ser* (*soy, eres, es*) and *tener* (*tengo, tienes, tiene*); *Tengo el pelo castaño y los ojos verdes*; *Es alta y simpática*; agreement (*-o/-a*, *-os/-as*); describe a family member or a famous person.
- **Check for understanding:** "Correct and explain: *Mi hermana es alto.*" → *Mi hermana es alta*: the adjective must agree with the feminine noun *hermana*.
- **Differentiation:** support: an adjective card set colour-coded by gender / stretch: use *bastante*, *muy* and *un poco* and describe two people in one sentence with *pero*
- **Materials:** photos of famous Spanish speakers, adjective cards, mini-whiteboards
- **Joy:** "¿Quién es?" guessing game

### Lesson 3 — My family: possessives and the verb tener in all persons
- **Duration:** 35 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can talk about my family using *mi/mis, tu/tus, su/sus* and all forms of *tener*." (CEFR A1; UK KS3 MFL — grammar)
- **Hook:** A family tree of a famous fictional family: "¿Quién es la abuela de…?"
- **Key activity:** Present all persons of *tener* (*tengo, tienes, tiene, tenemos, tenéis, tienen* — noting that *ustedes tienen* replaces *vosotros tenéis* in Latin America); possessives with singular/plural; draw and describe own (or an invented) family tree.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why is it *mis hermanos* but *mi hermano*?" → *Mis* is used before a plural noun; *mi* before a singular one.
- **Differentiation:** support: a family-tree template with labels / stretch: describe a friend's family using *su/sus* and *tienen*
- **Materials:** family-tree templates, verb table card
- **Joy:** "Invent a crazy family" (e.g. *Tengo diecisiete hermanos y un abuelo que es astronauta*)

### Lesson 4 — Pets, likes and opinions
- **Duration:** 35 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can give opinions with reasons using *me gusta(n), me encanta(n), no me gusta(n) nada, prefiero* and *porque es…*." (CEFR A1; UK KS3 MFL)
- **Hook:** "¿Te gustan las arañas?" — a survey that gets strong reactions.
- **Key activity:** Teach that *gustar* agrees with the thing liked (*me gusta el fútbol* / *me gustan los perros*); opinions with reasons (*porque es divertido / son aburridos*); class survey and results in Spanish.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why is it *me gustan los gatos* but *me gusta mi gato*?" → The verb agrees with what is liked: *los gatos* is plural, *mi gato* is singular.
- **Differentiation:** support: an opinion ladder (from *me encanta* to *odio*) with symbols / stretch: use *a mi hermano le gusta…* and *nos gusta…*
- **Materials:** opinion ladder poster, survey sheets, animal pictures
- **Joy:** Class survey with a Spanish bar chart (Maths link)

### Lesson 5 — Reading: a pen-pal letter
- **Duration:** 35 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can understand the main points and some details of a short letter from a Spanish-speaking pen pal." (CEFR A1 reading; UK KS3 MFL)
- **Hook:** A letter from "Lucía" in Montevideo, Uruguay: "What can you find out about her?"
- **Key activity:** Read the letter; use cognates, context and known words; answer questions in English, then in Spanish; highlight new structures to reuse.
- **Check for understanding:** "How did you work out a word you hadn't learned?" → Using a cognate (e.g. *música*), the context, or a word family.
- **Differentiation:** support: a glossary and questions in English / stretch: find and explain three uses of connectives in the letter
- **Materials:** a printed pen-pal letter, highlighters, a map of South America
- **Joy:** Locate Montevideo and find three facts about Uruguay

### Lesson 6 — Writing: my first paragraph of the year and Unit 1 check
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can write a paragraph of 60–80 words replying to a pen pal, with opinions, reasons and connectives." (CEFR A1 written production; UK KS3 MFL)
- **Hook:** "Lucía is waiting for your reply!"
- **Key activity:** Plan with a writing frame (introduction, family, description, likes and dislikes, question to the pen pal); write; peer-check with a checklist (agreement, accents, connectives); short listening and speaking check.
- **Check for understanding:** "Name two ways to make your paragraph more interesting." → Use connectives (*y, pero, también, porque*) and opinions with reasons; ask a question.
- **Differentiation:** support: a writing frame with sentence starters / stretch: include a sentence about a friend or family member in the third person
- **Materials:** writing frames, dictionaries (paper or WordReference), checklist
- **Joy:** Letters sent to a real partner school class if possible (e.g. through an e-twinning scheme)

**End-of-unit check:** A 60–80-word reply to a pen pal (personal information, family, description, opinions with reasons), plus a 1-minute spoken self-introduction answering three questions.

## Unit 2 — Mi rutina diaria (daily routine)

**Essential question:** How can I describe my day in Spanish — and how do days differ around the Spanish-speaking world?

**Key vocabulary:** me despierto, me levanto, me ducho, me visto, me lavo los dientes, desayuno, voy al colegio, como, vuelvo a casa, hago los deberes, ceno, me acuesto, ¿a qué hora…?, a la una, a las dos y media, por la mañana, por la tarde, por la noche, primero, luego, después, finalmente, normalmente, a veces, siempre, nunca

### Lesson 7 — Telling the time
- **Duration:** 35 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can tell and ask the time in Spanish, including quarter past, half past and quarter to." (CEFR A1; UK KS3 MFL)
- **Hook:** Clocks showing the time in Madrid, Mexico City and Buenos Aires: "¿Qué hora es en…?"
- **Key activity:** *Es la una / Son las dos*; *y cuarto, y media, menos cuarto*; *¿A qué hora…?* → *A las ocho*; practise with real clocks; the 24-hour clock for timetables (*las quince horas*).
- **Check for understanding:** "Why do we say *es la una* but *son las tres*?" → One o'clock is singular (*una hora*); other hours are plural.
- **Differentiation:** support: a clock with Spanish labels / stretch: calculate time differences between Spanish-speaking capitals and say them in Spanish
- **Materials:** clock faces with movable hands, time-zone map
- **Joy:** "Reloj loco": race to set the clock to the time called

### Lesson 8 — Reflexive verbs: my morning
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can use reflexive verbs in the first person to describe my morning routine." (CEFR A1; UK KS3 MFL — grammar)
- **Hook:** Mime the morning: "Me despierto… me levanto… ¡me ducho!"
- **Key activity:** Teach reflexive verbs with actions (*me despierto, me levanto, me ducho, me visto, me lavo los dientes*); explain the reflexive pronoun ("myself"); sequence a morning with times and sequencing words.
- **Check for understanding:** "What does the *me* in *me levanto* mean, and why is it there?" → "Myself" — I get *myself* up; reflexive verbs show the action is done to oneself.
- **Differentiation:** support: picture cards with the verbs / stretch: add *normalmente, a veces, nunca* and a time to each sentence
- **Materials:** action picture cards, sequencing word cards
- **Joy:** "Mímica" (charades) — guess the routine verb

### Lesson 9 — Reflexive verbs in all persons
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can conjugate a reflexive verb in all persons and describe someone else's routine." (CEFR A1–A2; UK KS3 MFL — grammar)
- **Hook:** "¿A qué hora se levanta un astronauta en la Estación Espacial?"
- **Key activity:** Conjugate *levantarse* (*me levanto, te levantas, se levanta, nos levantamos, os levantáis, se levantan*); note stem change *despertarse* → *me despierto*; describe a family member's routine.
- **Check for understanding:** "Complete: *Mis padres ___ (levantarse) a las seis.*" → *se levantan*.
- **Differentiation:** support: a verb table with pronouns colour-coded / stretch: explain the stem change e → ie in *despertarse* and find another verb that does it (*empezar* → *empiezo*)
- **Materials:** verb tables, routine cards for different people
- **Joy:** "El día de un astronauta" — describe an astronaut's day

### Lesson 10 — Afternoon and evening: frequency expressions
- **Duration:** 35 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can describe my afternoon and evening routine using frequency expressions and irregular verbs *hacer* and *ir*." (CEFR A1–A2; UK KS3 MFL)
- **Hook:** "¿Qué haces después del colegio?"
- **Key activity:** *Vuelvo a casa, hago los deberes, veo la tele, juego al fútbol, ceno, me acuesto*; frequency (*siempre, normalmente, a veces, nunca*); *voy / hago* as irregular first persons.
- **Check for understanding:** "Where does *nunca* go in a sentence like 'I never watch TV'?" → Before the verb: *Nunca veo la tele* (or *No veo nunca la tele*).
- **Differentiation:** support: a sentence builder with frequency words / stretch: compare weekdays and weekends with *pero los fines de semana…*
- **Materials:** sentence builders, frequency cards
- **Joy:** "¿Verdad o mentira?" — true/false routine statements about the teacher

### Lesson 11 — Listening: routines around the Spanish-speaking world
- **Duration:** 35 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can understand the main points and times in short recordings of young people describing their routines." (CEFR A1–A2 listening; UK KS3 MFL)
- **Hook:** "In Spain, many families eat dinner at 9 p.m. or later. Would you like that?"
- **Key activity:** Listen to three short recordings (a child in Spain, Mexico and Argentina); note times and activities; compare with own routine; cultural discussion (meal times, the school day, siesta — a tradition that is less common today than people think).
- **Check for understanding:** "What strategy helps you catch a time in fast speech?" → Listen for the numbers and *y/menos*, and predict likely times from context.
- **Differentiation:** support: a grid with multiple-choice times / stretch: note extra details (opinions and reasons)
- **Materials:** audio recordings, listening grids
- **Joy:** "Mi día perfecto" vs a real day

### Lesson 12 — Speaking: interviewing a partner about their routine
- **Duration:** 35 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can ask and answer questions about daily routine in a short conversation." (CEFR A2 spoken interaction; UK KS3 MFL)
- **Hook:** "Periodista": you are a journalist interviewing a famous person about their day.
- **Key activity:** Question forms (*¿A qué hora te levantas? ¿Qué desayunas? ¿Cómo vas al colegio?*); conversation in pairs; record and self-assess pronunciation.
- **Check for understanding:** "If your partner asks *¿A qué hora te acuestas?*, how do you change *te acuestas* in your answer?" → Change to the first person: *Me acuesto a las nueve*.
- **Differentiation:** support: question and answer prompt cards / stretch: add follow-up questions (*¿Y los fines de semana?*)
- **Materials:** question cards, recording device
- **Joy:** Celebrity interview role play

### Lesson 13 — Writing: a day in my life
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can write 80–100 words about my daily routine with times, sequencing words, frequency expressions and opinions." (CEFR A2 written production; UK KS3 MFL)
- **Hook:** "Tu día en un vídeo": plan a caption script for a "day in my life" video.
- **Key activity:** Plan and write; check reflexive pronouns, verb endings and accents; illustrate with a timeline or comic strip.
- **Check for understanding:** "Find one sentence where you used a reflexive verb and check the pronoun matches the person." → e.g. *Me ducho* (first person with *me*).
- **Differentiation:** support: a writing frame / stretch: include a sentence about a family member's routine and a comparison
- **Materials:** writing frames, comic-strip templates, dictionaries
- **Joy:** "Un día en mi vida" comic strip

### Lesson 14 — Unit 2 check
- **Duration:** 35 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can show I can understand and describe daily routines in listening, reading, speaking and writing." (CEFR A1–A2; UK KS3 MFL)
- **Hook:** "Reto de la rutina" — a four-skills challenge circuit.
- **Key activity:** Short four-skill tasks; self-assessment with "can do" statements; targeted practice.
- **Check for understanding:** "Say three sentences about your weekday routine with times." → Three accurate sentences with times (e.g. *Me levanto a las siete*).
- **Differentiation:** support: picture prompts / stretch: include the third person and a contrast with *pero*
- **Materials:** circuit task cards, audio, can-do checklist
- **Joy:** Stamp card for each completed skill

**End-of-unit check:** Listening for times and activities, reading a short routine text, a 1-minute spoken routine with times, and 80–100 written words with reflexive verbs and frequency expressions.

## Unit 3 — Mi instituto (school)

**Essential question:** What is school like for young people in the Spanish-speaking world, and how can I describe and compare my own?

**Key vocabulary:** las asignaturas (las matemáticas, las ciencias, la historia, la geografía, el inglés, el español, el arte, la música, la educación física, la informática), el horario, el recreo, el profesor / la profesora, el uniforme, es fácil / difícil / útil / interesante / aburrido, más … que, menos … que, tan … como, hay, tengo que, se puede, no se puede

### Lesson 15 — School subjects and opinions
- **Duration:** 35 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can name school subjects and give opinions with reasons, using *me gusta(n)* correctly with plurals." (CEFR A1; UK KS3 MFL)
- **Hook:** "¿Cuál es tu asignatura favorita?" — vote with your feet.
- **Key activity:** Learn subjects with cognate strategies; opinions with reasons (*me encantan las ciencias porque son interesantes*); note plural subjects (*las matemáticas, las ciencias*) take *gustan*.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why is it *me gustan las matemáticas* and not *me gusta las matemáticas*?" → *Las matemáticas* is plural, so the verb must be plural (*gustan*).
- **Differentiation:** support: subject picture cards and an opinion ladder / stretch: add a teacher opinion (*porque el profesor es…*)
- **Materials:** subject cards, opinion ladder
- **Joy:** "Four corners" opinion vote

### Lesson 16 — My timetable and days of the week
- **Duration:** 35 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can describe my timetable with days, times and subjects." (CEFR A1; UK KS3 MFL)
- **Hook:** A real timetable from a school in Colombia or Spain: "¿Qué tienen los lunes a las nueve?"
- **Key activity:** Days of the week (lower case in Spanish); *los lunes tengo…*; read and write timetables; compare with own.
- **Check for understanding:** "What does *los lunes* mean compared with *el lunes*?" → *Los lunes* means "on Mondays" (every Monday); *el lunes* means "on Monday" (one particular Monday).
- **Differentiation:** support: a gap-fill timetable / stretch: describe your ideal timetable with reasons
- **Materials:** example timetables, blank timetable templates
- **Joy:** Design "el horario ideal"

### Lesson 17 — Comparatives
- **Duration:** 35 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can compare subjects, teachers and schools using *más … que, menos … que* and *tan … como*." (CEFR A2; UK KS3 MFL — grammar)
- **Hook:** "¿El arte es más divertido que las matemáticas?" — debate!
- **Key activity:** Teach comparatives with agreement (*la historia es más interesante que la geografía*); irregulars *mejor* and *peor*; compare subjects in pairs.
- **Check for understanding:** "Translate: 'Music is better than art.'" → *La música es mejor que el arte.*
- **Differentiation:** support: a comparative sentence builder / stretch: use *tan … como* and justify with *porque*
- **Materials:** sentence builders, subject cards
- **Joy:** "Debate de asignaturas"

### Lesson 18 — Describing my school: hay and adjectives
- **Duration:** 35 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can describe my school's facilities with *hay* and adjectives that agree." (CEFR A1–A2; UK KS3 MFL)
- **Hook:** Photos of schools in Chile, Spain and Equatorial Guinea: "¿Qué hay en este instituto?"
- **Key activity:** *Hay una biblioteca, un patio, muchas aulas, un comedor*; *no hay piscina*; adjectives (*moderno/a, antiguo/a, grande, pequeño/a*); describe own school.
- **Check for understanding:** "What does *hay* mean and does it change for plural things?" → "There is / there are"; no, *hay* stays the same (*hay un patio, hay dos gimnasios*).
- **Differentiation:** support: picture labels with *hay* / stretch: add opinions and a comparison with another school
- **Materials:** school photos, labelled school plan
- **Joy:** "Mi instituto de fantasía" — design a fantasy school

### Lesson 19 — School rules: tener que, se puede, no se puede
- **Duration:** 35 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can describe school rules and give opinions about them using *tengo que*, *se puede* and *no se puede*." (CEFR A2; UK KS3 MFL — grammar)
- **Hook:** "¿Se puede usar el móvil en clase?" — which rules are fair?
- **Key activity:** *Tengo que llevar uniforme; no se puede comer en clase; se puede hablar en el recreo*; the infinitive after these structures; opinion on rules (*es justo / injusto porque…*).
- **Check for understanding:** "What form of the verb follows *tengo que* and *se puede*?" → The infinitive (e.g. *llevar, comer, hablar*).
- **Differentiation:** support: rule picture cards / stretch: write rules for your ideal school with reasons
- **Materials:** rule cards, school rules from a Spanish-speaking school
- **Joy:** "Las reglas locas" — invent absurd rules

### Lesson 20 — Reading: a school in another country
- **Duration:** 35 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can understand a longer text about school life in a Spanish-speaking country and compare it with mine." (CEFR A2 reading; UK KS3 MFL)
- **Hook:** "In some parts of Latin America, many schools run two shifts — morning and afternoon — with different students in each."
- **Key activity:** Read a text by a student describing their school (e.g. in Peru); answer questions; compare with own school in a Venn diagram (in Spanish where possible).
- **Check for understanding:** "What is one similarity and one difference between your school and the one in the text?" → A correct similarity and difference, with evidence from the text.
- **Differentiation:** support: a glossary and English questions / stretch: questions in Spanish and a written comparison
- **Materials:** reading text, Venn diagram
- **Joy:** "Intercambio" — plan questions to ask a real student abroad

### Lesson 21 — Speaking: describing and comparing my school
- **Duration:** 35 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can speak for one minute about my school with opinions, comparisons and rules." (CEFR A2 spoken production; UK KS3 MFL)
- **Hook:** "Tour guide": show a visitor around your school in Spanish.
- **Key activity:** Prepare a one-minute talk with a mind map (not a script); practise with a partner; record.
- **Check for understanding:** "Which structure made your talk sound more advanced?" → e.g. a comparative, *tengo que* or an opinion with reason.
- **Differentiation:** support: a cue card with key words / stretch: answer an unexpected question
- **Materials:** mind-map templates, recording device
- **Joy:** A video tour of school in Spanish

### Lesson 22 — Unit 3 check
- **Duration:** 35 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can show my understanding of school vocabulary and grammar across four skills." (CEFR A1–A2; UK KS3 MFL)
- **Hook:** "Reto del instituto."
- **Key activity:** Listening, reading, speaking and writing tasks (80–100 words: subjects, opinions, comparisons, rules, school description).
- **Check for understanding:** "Write one sentence with a comparative and one with *tengo que*." → Two accurate sentences.
- **Differentiation:** support: frames and picture prompts / stretch: add a sentence in another person
- **Materials:** assessment tasks, can-do checklist
- **Joy:** Can-do certificate

**End-of-unit check:** An 80–100-word description of school with opinions, a comparison and a rule; a one-minute talk; listening and reading comprehension on school life.

## Unit 4 — Mi ciudad (my town)

**Essential question:** How can I describe where I live, find my way around a Spanish-speaking town, and say what there is to do?

**Key vocabulary:** la ciudad, el pueblo, el barrio, hay, no hay, el parque, la plaza, el mercado, la biblioteca, el cine, el museo, la estación, el polideportivo, la iglesia, la mezquita, la sinagoga, el templo, está, cerca de, lejos de, al lado de, enfrente de, entre, sigue todo recto, gira a la derecha / a la izquierda, toma la primera / segunda calle, voy a, al, a la, se puede

### Lesson 23 — Places in town with hay
- **Duration:** 35 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can name places in a town and say what there is and isn't in my area." (CEFR A1; UK KS3 MFL)
- **Hook:** A satellite tour of a plaza in Oaxaca, Mexico: "¿Qué hay en la plaza?"
- **Key activity:** Places in town with articles; *hay un/una…, hay muchos/muchas…, no hay…*; places of worship from many faiths; describe own town.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why do we say *no hay cine* without *un*?" → After a negative *hay*, the article is usually dropped (*no hay cine* — "there isn't a cinema").
- **Differentiation:** support: picture cards with articles / stretch: add adjectives and opinions (*hay un parque grande que me encanta*)
- **Materials:** place cards, town map, satellite images
- **Joy:** "Veo, veo" (I spy) with a picture of a town

### Lesson 24 — Where is it? Estar and prepositions
- **Duration:** 35 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can say where places are using *estar* and prepositions of place, including *al* and *del*." (CEFR A1–A2; UK KS3 MFL — grammar)
- **Hook:** "¿Dónde está el tesoro?" — a treasure map of a town.
- **Key activity:** *Estar* for location (*el cine está al lado del parque*); prepositions (*cerca de, lejos de, al lado de, enfrente de, entre*); contractions *de + el = del*, *a + el = al*; describe a town map.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why do we use *estar*, not *ser*, to say where the museum is?" → Location uses *estar* (*el museo está en el centro*).
- **Differentiation:** support: a preposition picture card / stretch: describe your route from home to school using three prepositions
- **Materials:** town maps, preposition cards, small objects to position
- **Joy:** "¿Dónde está el tesoro?" hunt

### Lesson 25 — Asking for and giving directions
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can ask for and follow directions in Spanish." (CEFR A2 spoken interaction; UK KS3 MFL)
- **Hook:** "You're lost in Seville with no phone battery. ¿Cómo se va a la catedral?"
- **Key activity:** *Perdone, ¿dónde está…? / ¿Cómo se va a…?*; *sigue todo recto, gira a la derecha / a la izquierda, toma la primera calle*; follow directions on a map; blindfolded direction game in the playground.

### Lesson 40 — Time expressions and the preterite of -ar verbs

- **Differentiation:** support: a verb table with endings highlighted / stretch: explain why *jugar* becomes *jugué* (to keep the hard "g" sound)

### Lesson 42 — Key irregulars: fui, hice, fue
- **Duration:** 40 min

## Unit 9 — Graduación: conversación y presentación

**Essential question:** Can I have a real conversation and give a presentation about my life in Spanish?

**Key vocabulary:** (all the year's vocabulary) + ¿Puedes repetir, por favor?, ¿Cómo se dice…?, no entiendo, ¡qué interesante!, en mi opinión, creo que, sin embargo, además, por eso

### Lesson 65 — Conversation strategies
- **Duration:** 35 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can use strategies to keep a conversation going (asking for repetition, fillers, follow-up questions, reacting)." (CEFR A2 spoken interaction; UK KS3 MFL)
- **Hook:** "What do you do when you don't understand — panic, or say *¿Puedes repetir, por favor?*"
- **Key activity:** Teach repair and reaction phrases (*¿Puedes repetir? ¿Cómo se dice…? ¡Qué guay! ¿Y tú?*); fillers (*pues, bueno, a ver*); practise conversation chains with rotating partners.
- **Check for understanding:** "Name two phrases you can use when you don't understand." → *¿Puedes repetir, por favor?* and *No entiendo* / *¿Qué significa…?*
- **Differentiation:** support: a strategy card / stretch: extend each answer with *además* and *por eso*
- **Materials:** strategy cards, timer
- **Joy:** "Conversation carousel"

### Lesson 66 — Preparing the graduation presentation
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can plan a 2-minute presentation "Mi vida" using three time frames, opinions and complex sentences." (CEFR A2 spoken production; UK KS3 MFL)
- **Hook:** "Mi vida: pasado, presente y futuro — your Spanish graduation speech."
- **Key activity:** Plan sections (me and my family, my routine and school, my town, a memorable past event, my future plans); create a visual (slides or poster with images, few words); practise from key-word notes.
- **Check for understanding:** "Where does your presentation use each of the three time frames?" → A specific sentence in each tense.
- **Differentiation:** support: a presentation frame with starters / stretch: include a *si* clause, a comparative and a question to the audience
- **Materials:** planning templates, slide software or poster card
- **Joy:** Designing the visuals

### Lesson 67 — Rehearsal and peer feedback
- **Duration:** 35 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can rehearse my presentation and improve pronunciation and fluency using feedback." (CEFR A2; UK KS3 MFL)
- **Hook:** "Record yourself — then listen like a coach."
- **Key activity:** Rehearse in pairs; record; self-assess pronunciation (vowels, *r/rr*, *j*, *ll*, stress and accents); peer feedback with a rubric.
- **Check for understanding:** "What did you change after listening to your recording?" → A specific improvement.
- **Differentiation:** support: shorter presentation and key-word notes / stretch: present without notes
- **Materials:** recording devices, pronunciation checklist, rubric
- **Joy:** "Before and after" recordings

### Lesson 68 — Graduation presentations
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can give a 2-minute presentation about my life in Spanish and answer a question about it." (CEFR A2 spoken production and interaction; UK KS3 MFL)
- **Hook:** "¡Damas y caballeros!"
- **Key activity:** Presentations to an audience (family, class or a video call with a partner school); each presenter answers one question.
- **Check for understanding:** "Answer the audience question in Spanish." → A relevant, understandable answer.
- **Differentiation:** support: a smaller audience or pre-recorded presentation / stretch: answer two unprepared questions
- **Materials:** presentation space, visuals
- **Joy:** Performing for family (links to the graduation exhibition)

### Lesson 69 — Final conversation assessment
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can hold a 3–4 minute conversation on the year's topics, using three time frames." (CEFR A2 spoken interaction; UK KS3 MFL)
- **Hook:** "Una conversación de verdad."

- **Check for understanding:** "Answer: *¿Qué hiciste el verano pasado y qué vas a hacer este verano?*" → Accurate past and future answers with opinions.
- **Differentiation:** support: topics shared in advance / stretch: unpredictable follow-up questions
- **Materials:** conversation question bank, assessment rubric
- **Joy:** A conversation with a real Spanish speaker (family friend or partner-school pupil)

### Lesson 70 — Celebration: fiesta de fin de curso
- **Duration:** 35 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can reflect on my progress in Spanish and set goals for secondary school." (IB MYP Approaches to Learning; UK KS3 MFL)

## Books & resources

- ***Pobre Ana* by Blaine Ray** — a classic graded reader written with high-frequency language; many learners' first whole book in Spanish.
- ***Brandon Brown quiere un perro* by Carol Gaab** — a funny, very accessible graded reader for building confidence (support route).
- ***Extr@ en español*** — a sitcom made for learners, with clear speech and cultural situations; perfect for listening at A1–A2.
- **Señor Wooly (senorwooly.com)** — catchy songs and stories written for Spanish learners; exceptionally popular with 11–12-year-olds.
- **BBC Bitesize KS3 Spanish** — free, clear explanations and practice for grammar and vocabulary.
- **WordReference (wordreference.com) and the *Collins Spanish Dictionary*** — reliable dictionaries with example sentences and regional variations.
- **Poetry and song:** José Martí, *Versos sencillos* (the source of "Guantanamera"); poems for children by Gabriela Mistral; the traditional song "Cielito lindo".
- **Culture sources:** festival resources for Día de Muertos, La Tomatina, Inti Raymi and Las Fallas; tourist information from Spanish-speaking cities; audio from native speakers from several countries.
- **Pen-pal and e-twinning schemes** — to give writing and speaking a real audience.

## End-of-year mastery checks

1. **Holds a 3–4 minute conversation** on personal information, routine, school, town, food, past events and future plans, using repair strategies. *See it:* the final conversation (Lesson 69).
2. **Uses three time frames accurately:** present (all persons, including *ser, estar, tener, ir, hacer* and reflexives), regular preterite and *fui/hice/fue*, and *ir a* + infinitive. *See it:* a 120–150-word three-tense paragraph.
3. **Gives opinions with reasons and comparisons** (*me gusta(n), me encanta(n), porque, más … que, mejor*). *See it:* the school and town writing.
4. **Handles transactions:** asks for directions, buys food at a market, orders in a restaurant. *See it:* the role plays.
5. **Understands short authentic and adapted texts and recordings** using strategies. *See it:* the Unit 8 check and the graded reader.
6. **Pronounces Spanish clearly** (vowels, *r/rr*, *j*, *ll*, *ñ*, stress and written accents). *See it:* the recorded presentation.
7. **Knows the Spanish-speaking world:** countries, capitals, festivals and Indigenous languages. *See it:* the map quiz and festival description.

## Teacher guidance

- **Common errors:** *gustar* agreement (*me gusta los perros* ✗); using *ser* for location and age; forgetting adjective agreement; missing preterite accents (*hablo/habló*); confusing *fui* (*ir*) and *fue* (*ser/ir*, third person); adding *un/una* before professions; *a el* instead of *al*; word-for-word translation from English.
- **Regional variety:** accept and teach both European and Latin American forms (*vosotros* vs *ustedes*, *zumo/jugo*, *coche/carro*, *ordenador/computadora*); expose children to accents from several countries.
- **Input before output.** Plenty of listening and reading at the right level (stories, songs, graded readers) before asking for production; sentence builders are scaffolds to be gradually removed.
- **Retrieval.** Start every lesson with 5 minutes of mixed retrieval from earlier units; keep a vocabulary log and use spaced practice (flashcards or an app).
- **Differentiation.** Support means more visual scaffolding, sentence builders and shorter tasks on the same topic; stretch means more persons and tenses, connectives, unprepared questions and longer texts — not new topics.
- **Swapping the language.** For Hebrew, French or another language, keep the unit themes and the sequence (present → past → future → three-tense paragraph; transactions; culture), replacing grammar points with the language's equivalents (e.g. Hebrew verb patterns by gender and number; French *passé composé* with *avoir/être*).
- **Vertical alignment.** Year 5 introduced the present in the singular, adjective agreement, past chunks and *voy a*; Year 6 completes the core A2 toolkit. Secondary (UK Year 8 / US Grade 7 / MYP 2) builds the imperfect, the full future and conditional, and longer texts on this base.
- **Cross-curricular links:** Maths (time, numbers, money, surveys), Geography (weather, the Spanish-speaking world, volcanoes), English (poetry — "Where I'm From" and Martí's lines), Music (songs), Life Skills (global citizenship).
