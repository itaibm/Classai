# Spanish — Year 6 (ages 10–11, KS2) — Lesson Outlines

> Part of **the best international primary school** (see [`../../../SCHOOL-CHARTER.md`](../../../SCHOOL-CHARTER.md)).
> This file breaks **Year 6 Spanish** into **20–40 minute lesson outlines**, drawn from
> `knowledge-base/subjects/languages/spanish-ks2.md` and `spanish-extension.md`.

> Instructions are in English; the language to practise is in Spanish. New language is presented
> **by ear first, spelling second**. Pronunciation hints are plain-English approximations with the
> **STRESSED** syllable in capitals.

## Year overview

Year 6 is the **capstone** year. Children take control of **verbs across all three families**
(-ar/-er/-ir present), reach into the **past** (*el pretérito*, as usable chunks) and the **near
future** (*voy a + verb*), write and speak in **short paragraphs**, retell a simple story, and
complete a **cultural project** on the Spanish-speaking world. This is the year their Spanish
becomes *productive and connected*, ready for secondary school.

- **Listening:** follow a short story or description told in present, past and future.
- **Speaking:** describe people with agreeing adjectives; talk about yesterday and tomorrow; retell a story; **present** a cultural project.
- **Reading:** read a short paragraph and a simple story and sequence its events.
- **Writing:** write a connected paragraph (5–8 sentences) mixing tenses and connectives.
- **Phonics/pronunciation:** accents that show past tense (*comí, jugó*); fluent linking in connected speech.
- **Vocabulary:** action verbs (3 families), describing words, sequencing words, countries & capitals.
- **Grammar:** regular -ar present (full pattern); -er/-ir present; **adjective agreement** (gender + number); **simple past** chunks; **near future** *voy a + verb*.
- **Intercultural:** the Spanish-speaking world (≈20 countries, capitals); a personal cultural project.

How it builds on Year 5: Year 5 connected sentences with opinions and reasons. Year 6 adds the
**time dimension** — present *and* past *and* future — and makes verbs systematic rather than
chunk-only. Adjective agreement, met for colours in Year 4, now extends to full descriptions with
gender *and* number. Connectives and sequencing words turn sentences into paragraphs and stories.

## Unit 1 — Los verbos: present tense across the families

**Essential question:** How do Spanish verbs change to show *who* is doing the action?

**Key vocabulary:** *hablar, cantar, bailar, nadar, estudiar, jugar, dibujar (-ar); comer, beber, leer, correr (-er); vivir, escribir (-ir); yo, tú, él/ella; ¿dónde vives?, vivo en…*

### Lesson 1 — Verbos -ar: yo, tú, él/ella
- **Duration:** 35 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can conjugate a regular -ar verb for I/you/he-she."
- **Hook:** A verb is a word with a swappable tail. Chop *hablar*'s tail and watch who appears!
- **Key activity:** Show stem *habl-* + endings: yo **-o**, tú **-as**, él/ella **-a** (*hablo, hablas, habla*). Spanish drops *yo/tú* — the ending shows who. Drill *cantar, bailar, estudiar, jugar.* Produce: verb chain (tutor gives verb + person → child says the form in a sentence).
- **Check for understanding:** "Conjugate *cantar* for 'I', 'you', 'she.'" → *canto, cantas, canta.*
- **Differentiation:** support: fill the ending from a cue / stretch: a full daily-routine sentence (*Estudio y juego*).
- **Materials:** verb-ending wheel, person cards.
- **Joy:** "verb chain" relay — keep the chain going without an error.

### Lesson 2 — Verbos -er e -ir: comer, vivir
- **Duration:** 35 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can conjugate regular -er and -ir verbs and say where I live."
- **Hook:** Two more verb families — and the good news is *yo* always ends in *-o*.
- **Key activity:** Show *comer* → *como, comes, come*; *vivir* → *vivo, vives, vive.* Contrast with -ar (tú/él use *-es/-e*, not *-as/-a*). *¿Dónde vives?* → *Vivo en…* Produce: verb chain mixing all three families.
- **Check for understanding:** "Say 'I eat', 'I live', and ask 'Where do you live?'" → *como, vivo, ¿Dónde vives?*
- **Differentiation:** support: choose ending from a card / stretch: report a friend (*Mi hermano lee mucho*).
- **Materials:** three-family verb mat, town cards.
- **Joy:** "where do you live?" map game — pin your town and answer in Spanish.

**End-of-unit check:** child conjugates one -ar, one -er and one -ir verb for yo/tú/él-ella and answers *¿Dónde vives?*

## Unit 2 — Las descripciones: adjectives & full agreement

**Essential question:** How do I describe people and things so the adjective truly agrees?

**Key vocabulary:** *alto/a, bajo/a, grande, pequeño/a, simpático/a, divertido/a, inteligente, bonito/a; -o/-a, -s/-es plural; adjective after the noun.*

### Lesson 3 — Adjetivos: gender agreement
- **Duration:** 35 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can make an adjective agree for masculine/feminine and place it after the noun."
- **Hook:** A *tall brother* and a *kind sister* — the describing word changes its ending. Why?
- **Key activity:** Listen → *alto/a, bajo/a, pequeño/a, simpático/a, divertido/a, bonito/a.* Rule: masc. → *-o* (*alto*), fem. → *-a* (*alta*); *-e* adjectives (*grande, inteligente*) don't change for gender. Adjective goes **after** the noun. Produce: describe family/pets.
- **Check for understanding:** "Make 'tall' agree for a girl: *Ella es ___*." → *alta.*
- **Differentiation:** support: choose *-o/-a* from a cue / stretch: use an *-e* adjective correctly (*una chica grande*).
- **Materials:** adjective cards (changing & non-changing), character pictures.
- **Joy:** "Guess Who?" — describe a character, others guess.

### Lesson 4 — Plurals & describe-and-guess
- **Duration:** 30 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can make adjectives plural and describe with two agreeing adjectives."
- **Hook:** Two small dogs — *dos perros pequeños*. The adjective grows an *-s* too!
- **Key activity:** Add plural *-s*: *gatos negros, gatas negras.* Combine two adjectives with *y*: *Es alta y simpática.* Produce: describe a family member/character with two agreeing adjectives.
- **Check for understanding:** "Describe two small dogs." → *dos perros pequeños.*
- **Differentiation:** support: one adjective agreeing / stretch: three agreeing adjectives with *y* and *pero*.
- **Materials:** noun+adjective cards, character set.
- **Joy:** "describe-and-draw" — describe a monster in Spanish; a partner draws it.

**End-of-unit check:** child describes a person, pet or character with two adjectives that agree in gender *and* number, placed after the noun.

## Unit 3 — El pasado: talking about yesterday

**Essential question:** How do I say what I *did* — finished, in the past?

**Key vocabulary:** *fui, comí, jugué, vi; ayer, el fin de semana; pattern hint -é (yo) / -ó (él/ella); ¿qué hiciste ayer?*

### Lesson 5 — El pretérito: useful past chunks
- **Duration:** 35 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can say a few things I did using past chunks."
- **Hook:** To talk about yesterday, the verb changes its ending. Collect four magic past words!
- **Key activity:** Learn as chunks: *fui* (I went/was) → *Fui al parque*; *comí* (ko-MEE, I ate) → *Comí pizza*; *jugué* (I played) → *Jugué al fútbol*; *vi* (I saw) → *Vi una película.* Pattern hint: -ar yo → **-é** (*hablé*), él/ella → **-ó** (*habló*) — but collect phrases, don't drill tables. Produce: two past actions.
- **Check for understanding:** "Say 'I went to the park' and 'I ate pizza.'" → *Fui al parque / Comí pizza.*
- **Differentiation:** support: one past chunk + picture / stretch: three past actions joined with *y*.
- **Materials:** past-chunk cards, "yesterday" timeline.
- **Joy:** "weekend diary" — draw three things you did, narrate in past Spanish.

### Lesson 6 — ¿Qué hiciste ayer? (past in connected speech)
- **Duration:** 30 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can answer 'What did you do yesterday?' with two joined past actions."
- **Hook:** Now and then are different worlds — *Hoy juego… ayer jugué…*
- **Key activity:** Practise *¿Qué hiciste ayer?* → *Ayer fui al parque y jugué al fútbol.* Contrast present vs past (*juego/jugué, como/comí*). Produce: a two-action "yesterday" report.
- **Check for understanding:** "Say 'Yesterday I played football and ate pizza.'" → *Ayer jugué al fútbol y comí pizza.*
- **Differentiation:** support: match present→past pairs (*juego→jugué*) / stretch: three past actions with connectives.
- **Materials:** present/past matching cards.
- **Joy:** "yesterday news report" — read your weekend like a newsreader.

**End-of-unit check:** child answers *¿Qué hiciste ayer?* with at least two past chunks joined by *y*.

## Unit 4 — El futuro próximo: what I'm going to do

**Essential question:** How do I say what I'm *going to* do?

**Key vocabulary:** *voy a + verbo, vas a…, va a…; mañana, este fin de semana; ¿qué vas a hacer mañana?*

### Lesson 7 — Voy a + verbo (near future)
- **Duration:** 30 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can say what I'm going to do using *voy a + plain verb*."
- **Hook:** Spanish has English's "going to" — *voy a jugar*. The second verb stays plain!
- **Key activity:** Listen → *Voy a jugar, voy a comer, voy a nadar.* Keep the *a*; keep the verb plain (like after *me gusta*). Add *mañana / este fin de semana.* Others: *vas a…, va a…* Produce: two plans with *y*.
- **Check for understanding:** "Say 'Tomorrow I'm going to play tennis.'" → *Mañana voy a jugar al tenis.*
- **Differentiation:** support: *Voy a ___* with a verb card / stretch: ask *¿Qué vas a hacer mañana?* and answer.
- **Materials:** plan cards, a weekend planner sheet.
- **Joy:** "weekend planner" — design your dream weekend in Spanish.

### Lesson 8 — Past + future together
- **Duration:** 30 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can say one thing I did and one thing I'm going to do, in one turn."
- **Hook:** Tell the story of your weekend — what happened *and* what's coming next.
- **Key activity:** Combine: *El fin de semana fui al parque y jugué al fútbol. Mañana voy a nadar.* Practise → produce: a past action + a future plan joined naturally.
- **Check for understanding:** "Say one thing you did and one you're going to do." → e.g. *Comí pizza. Voy a leer.*
- **Differentiation:** support: one sentence each tense / stretch: add reasons with *porque*.
- **Materials:** timeline mat (ayer | hoy | mañana).
- **Joy:** "time-traveller" report across yesterday, today and tomorrow.

**End-of-unit check:** child produces one past action and one near-future plan in connected Spanish (*Fui… Voy a…*).

## Unit 5 — Cuentos: retelling a story in paragraphs

**Essential question:** How do I retell a short story in connected Spanish?

**Key vocabulary:** *primero, después/luego, y entonces, al final; come, va, comió; la oruga, la mariposa; sequencing + connectives.*

### Lesson 9 — Palabras de secuencia (sequencing words)
- **Duration:** 30 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can sequence events with *primero, después, al final*."
- **Hook:** Stories need signposts — *first… then… in the end.* Learn the Spanish signposts!
- **Key activity:** Listen → *primero* (pree-MEH-ro)*, después/luego, y entonces, al final.* Order scrambled story pictures and narrate with sequencing words. Produce: a three-step mini-story.
- **Check for understanding:** "Give the Spanish for: first, then, in the end." → *primero, después, al final.*
- **Differentiation:** support: two-step sequence / stretch: a four-step sequence with a connective.
- **Materials:** scrambled story-picture cards.
- **Joy:** "story relay" — each child adds the next event with a sequencing word.

### Lesson 10 — Retelling a simple story (paragraph)
- **Duration:** 35 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can retell a familiar story in 4–6 connected Spanish sentences."
- **Hook:** Retell *La oruga muy hambrienta* (The Very Hungry Caterpillar) — the repeated lines do half the work!
- **Key activity:** Model: *Primero, la oruga come una manzana. Después come dos peras. Y al final, ¡es una mariposa!* Use present or a past chunk (*comió*). Produce: retell the gist in 4–6 sentences with ≥3 sequencing words.
- **Check for understanding:** "Retell two events using *primero* and *después*." → two sensible sequenced steps.
- **Differentiation:** support: picture prompts per line / stretch: add a past chunk and a connective.
- **Materials:** story pictures, sequencing word bank.
- **Joy:** act the story out with toys while narrating in Spanish.

**End-of-unit check:** child retells a familiar story in 4–6 connected sentences using at least three sequencing words and one connective.

## Unit 6 — El mundo hispanohablante: the Spanish-speaking world & project

**Essential question:** Where in the world is Spanish spoken, and how do I present a cultural project?

**Key vocabulary:** *España–Madrid, México–Ciudad de México, Argentina–Buenos Aires, Colombia–Bogotá, Perú–Lima, Chile–Santiago; vosotros/ustedes; el continente; mi proyecto.*

### Lesson 11 — Países y capitales (the Spanish-speaking world)
- **Duration:** 30 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can name several Spanish-speaking countries with capitals and locate them."
- **Hook:** About **20 countries** speak Spanish — and Mexico has more speakers than Spain!
- **Key activity:** Listen → *España–Madrid, México–Ciudad de México, Argentina–Buenos Aires, Colombia–Bogotá, Perú–Lima, Chile–Santiago.* Note variety: Spain uses *vosotros*, Latin America *ustedes*; but it's one language. Exception: **Brazil speaks Portuguese**. Produce: name three countries + capitals on a map.
- **Check for understanding:** "Which country has the most Spanish speakers, and name its capital." → Mexico → *Ciudad de México.*
- **Differentiation:** support: match country to capital / stretch: add the continent for each.
- **Materials:** world map, country/capital cards.
- **Joy:** pin a world map for every Spanish-speaking country you can name.

### Lesson 12 — Proyecto cultural: plan & build
- **Duration:** 35 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can plan a project about a Spanish-speaking country, festival or person."
- **Hook:** Choose a country to become the expert on — and present it next lesson.
- **Key activity:** Each child picks a country/festival/famous Spanish speaker. Gather: capital (in Spanish), one festival, one food, one fact, and one Spanish phrase to teach the class. Build a poster/slides. Produce: draft the spoken intro (*Mi país es… La capital es…*).
- **Check for understanding:** "What three things will your project include?" → e.g. capital, a festival, a food (all linkable to Spanish).
- **Differentiation:** support: a project template to fill / stretch: include past/future sentences (*Voy a hablar de…*).
- **Materials:** project template, atlas/reference cards, craft materials.
- **Joy:** free choice of country makes it personal — football, food or family heritage.

### Lesson 13 — Presentación: present the project
- **Duration:** 35 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can present my cultural project, including some Spanish, to an audience."
- **Hook:** Showtime — be the class expert and teach everyone one new Spanish phrase.
- **Key activity:** Each child presents (1–2 min): country + capital in Spanish, festival/food/fact, and teaches one phrase. Audience asks one question (e.g. *¿Cuál es la capital?*). Produce: a confident spoken presentation with embedded Spanish.
- **Check for understanding:** "Tell us your country and its capital in Spanish." → *Mi país es… La capital es…*
- **Differentiation:** support: cue cards + a partner / stretch: take questions in Spanish.
- **Materials:** posters/slides, a simple audience question prompt.
- **Joy:** an awards moment — vote for "best fact," "best phrase taught."

**End-of-unit check:** child presents a cultural project with the country and capital said in Spanish, plus one festival/food/fact and one taught phrase.

## Unit 7 — Repaso final: KS2 Spanish showcase

**Essential question:** Can I bring four years of Spanish together in connected, multi-tense speech?

**Key vocabulary:** review across all years; tenses and connectives recycled.

### Lesson 14 — Mi vida en español (capstone talk)
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can give a connected talk about me using present, past and future."
- **Hook:** Your KS2 Spanish finale — describe yourself across time, all in Spanish.
- **Key activity:** Recombine everything: who I am + family (present), what I like with reasons (*me gusta… porque…*), what I did last weekend (*fui, jugué*), and a plan (*voy a…*), joined with connectives. Practise → produce: a 6–8 sentence talk.
- **Check for understanding:** "Give one present, one past and one future sentence about you." → three correct, joined sentences.
- **Differentiation:** support: a three-tense planning frame / stretch: add an agreeing description and a contrast (*pero*).
- **Materials:** planning frame (present | past | future), topic icons.
- **Joy:** record the finale as a "Spanish vlog" to keep and share.

**End-of-unit check:** child delivers a 6–8 sentence connected talk using present, past *and* near-future, with connectives and at least one agreeing description.

## End-of-year can-do statements (Year 6)

By the end of Year 6, I can…
1. **Conjugate** regular -ar, -er and -ir verbs in the present (yo/tú/él-ella) and drop the pronoun.
2. **Describe** people and things with adjectives that agree in **gender and number**, placed after the noun.
3. **Say** what I did using simple past chunks (*fui, comí, jugué, vi*) and answer *¿Qué hiciste ayer?*
4. **Say** what I'm going to do with *voy a + plain verb* and answer *¿Qué vas a hacer mañana?*
5. **Combine** past, present and future in one connected turn.
6. **Retell** a simple story in 4–6 sentences using sequencing words (*primero, después, al final*).
7. **Name** Spanish-speaking countries and capitals and explain the language is shared across ≈20 countries.
8. **Present** a cultural project and give a connected 6–8 sentence talk about myself across all three tenses.
