# Year 6 PE & Health (ages 11–12) — Scope & Sequence

> Part of **the best international primary school** (see [`../../../SCHOOL-CHARTER.md`](../../../SCHOOL-CHARTER.md)).
> Built to [`../../PLANNING-BRIEF.md`](../../PLANNING-BRIEF.md). Aligned to the **UK National Curriculum
> Key Stage 3 Physical Education** (Year 7), **UK Health Education / RSHE (Key Stage 3)**, **SHAPE
> America National Physical Education Standards (Grade 6)**, the **US National Health Education
> Standards (grades 6–8)**, **IB MYP Year 1 Physical & Health Education** and the **WHO guidelines on
> physical activity (at least 60 minutes of moderate-to-vigorous activity a day for ages 5–17)**.

## Year overview

Year 6 PE & Health aims for a graduate who is **healthy and active in body and mind** (charter §1):
someone who moves with skill and confidence in many activities, understands how to train and look
after their body, can lead and officiate, and has the knowledge and habits to stay healthy through
adolescence. The year moves from **how the body works in exercise** (fitness and training principles)
through **games, athletics, dance, gymnastics, outdoor and water activities**, to a **health unit on
growing up**, and finishes with a **leadership unit** in which children plan, lead and officiate a
graduation sports festival.

**Delivery.** Almost every lesson is **active and human-delivered** — by a parent, teacher or coach —
outdoors or in a hall, with the Classai tutor used only to prepare, explain rules and tactics, record
scores and reflect afterwards. Each Key activity says what the adult and the children do. Lessons are
40–45 minutes of which most is moving; health lessons are discussion- and practice-based.

**Safety in every lesson:** a warm-up, appropriate footwear and clothing, water, sun protection
outdoors, checking the space for hazards, and a cool-down. Materials lists include specific safety
notes. Any child with a medical condition follows their care plan.

**What we assume from Year 5 (ages 10–11):** fundamental movement skills secure; basic skills and
rules in invasion, net/wall and striking/fielding games; running, jumping and throwing; simple dance
and gymnastic sequences; swimming 25 m and basic self-rescue; knowledge of healthy eating, sleep,
hygiene and body safety; first steps in managing emotions. **What Year 6 adds:** components of fitness
and training principles; tactics and decision-making; technique refinement in athletics; composing
and performing longer dance and gymnastic sequences; orienteering; age-appropriate puberty, mental
health, substances and first-aid education; leadership and officiating. **What secondary relies on:**
confident participation, understanding of fitness and tactics, and healthy habits that survive the
move to a bigger school.

## Unit 1 — Fitness and how the body trains

**Essential question:** What does it mean to be fit, and how can I train to improve safely?

**Key vocabulary:** cardiovascular endurance, muscular strength, muscular endurance, flexibility, speed, agility, balance, coordination, power, reaction time, heart rate, resting heart rate, recovery, warm-up, cool-down, FITT principle (frequency, intensity, time, type), progressive overload, specificity, reversibility

### Lesson 1 — What is fitness? Components of fitness
- **Duration:** 45 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can name and demonstrate the main components of fitness and match them to sports." (UK KS3 PE — health and fitness; SHAPE Std 3)
- **Hook:** "Is a marathon runner fitter than a gymnast? Is a sumo wrestler fit?"
- **Key activity:** Adult leads a warm-up, then a 10-station circuit (e.g. shuttle runs — speed; skipping — endurance; plank — muscular endurance; sit-and-reach — flexibility; one-leg stand — balance; ball juggling — coordination; standing long jump — power; ruler drop — reaction time); after each station, children name the component; discuss which components matter most in different sports.
- **Check for understanding:** "Which two components of fitness does a goalkeeper need most, and why?" → e.g. reaction time and agility (or power) — to react to shots and move quickly to save them.
- **Differentiation:** support: station cards with pictures and fewer repetitions / stretch: rank components for three different sports and justify the ranking
- **Materials:** cones, skipping ropes, mats, a ruler, balls, station cards, stopwatch; safety: clear space, mats for floor work, water available
- **Joy:** "Which athlete are you?" — discover your strongest component

### Lesson 2 — Heart rate and exercise
- **Duration:** 45 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can measure my heart rate at rest and after exercise and explain what the change shows." (UK KS3 PE; SHAPE Std 3; Science link)
- **Hook:** "Your heart is a muscle. What happens to it when you sprint?"
- **Key activity:** Find the pulse (wrist or neck) and count for 15 seconds × 4; record resting heart rate; do 2 minutes of light, then moderate, then vigorous exercise, measuring after each; measure recovery after 1, 2 and 3 minutes; plot a graph (Maths link); estimate maximum heart rate (roughly 220 − age, a rough guide only).
- **Check for understanding:** "Why does your heart rate go up during exercise, and what does a quick recovery suggest?" → Muscles need more oxygen, so the heart pumps faster; a fast recovery suggests good cardiovascular fitness.
- **Differentiation:** support: a partner counts the pulse or a heart-rate monitor is used / stretch: compare recovery rates and suggest why they differ
- **Materials:** stopwatches, heart-rate monitors (optional), graph paper; safety: children stop if dizzy or unwell
- **Joy:** Seeing your own heart-rate graph

### Lesson 3 — Warm-up and cool-down: why and how
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can plan and lead a three-phase warm-up (pulse raiser, mobility/dynamic stretches, skill rehearsal) and a cool-down." (UK KS3 PE; SHAPE Std 3)
- **Hook:** "Professional footballers spend about 20 minutes warming up before a match. Why bother?"
- **Key activity:** Adult demonstrates each phase; children learn dynamic stretches (leg swings, walking lunges, arm circles); in small groups they plan and lead a warm-up for a chosen sport; finish with a cool-down (light jog, static stretches).
- **Check for understanding:** "Why are dynamic stretches better before exercise and static stretches after?" → Dynamic stretches raise temperature and mobilise joints through movements like the sport; static stretches help muscles relax and recover when warm.
- **Differentiation:** support: a warm-up card to follow / stretch: design a sport-specific warm-up and explain each part
- **Materials:** cones, warm-up cards, music; safety: space between groups
- **Joy:** Leading the class for the first time

### Lesson 4 — Fitness testing: my baseline
- **Duration:** 45 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can carry out standard fitness tests accurately and record my baseline results." (UK KS3 PE — personal bests; SHAPE Std 3)
- **Hook:** "You can't improve what you don't measure."
- **Key activity:** Adult supervises standardised tests: multistage fitness (bleep) test or a timed 1 km run/walk, sit-and-reach, standing long jump, 30 m sprint, 60-second sit-ups or plank hold; partners record; results are personal and private (no public rankings).
- **Check for understanding:** "Why must a fitness test be done the same way each time?" → So the results can be compared fairly to see real improvement (a fair test — Science link).
- **Differentiation:** support: modified tests (e.g. walk–run, knee plank) / stretch: act as test supervisor for one station and record accurately
- **Materials:** bleep-test audio, measuring tape, cones, sit-and-reach box or ruler, stopwatches, record cards; safety: suitable surface, stop if unwell, inclusive modifications
- **Joy:** Setting your personal baseline — the only person you compete with is you

### Lesson 5 — The FITT principle and training types
- **Duration:** 45 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can explain the FITT principle and experience continuous, interval and circuit training." (UK KS3 PE; SHAPE Std 3)
- **Hook:** "Two runners train for a race. One runs slowly every day; one does sprints three times a week. Who wins?"
- **Key activity:** Explain Frequency, Intensity, Time, Type; three short training sessions: continuous (steady 6-minute jog), interval (8 × 20 s fast / 40 s walk), circuit (6 stations × 30 s); compare how each felt and what it trains.
- **Check for understanding:** "What would you change using FITT to make a training plan harder?" → Increase one element: more often (frequency), harder (intensity), longer (time), or a more demanding type.
- **Differentiation:** support: work at a lower intensity with more rest / stretch: design an interval session for a specific sport
- **Materials:** cones, stopwatch, interval timer app, circuit cards; safety: hydration, rest between intervals
- **Joy:** Interval "race the clock" games

### Lesson 6 — Principles of training: overload, specificity, reversibility
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can explain progressive overload, specificity and reversibility with examples." (UK KS3 PE; SHAPE Std 3)
- **Hook:** "If you stop training for a month, what happens to your fitness?"
- **Key activity:** Short active demonstrations (adding one repetition per round — overload; sport-specific drills — specificity); discuss reversibility; case studies of athletes returning from injury.
- **Check for understanding:** "A swimmer only trains by running. Which principle is being ignored?" → Specificity: training should match the demands of the sport.
- **Differentiation:** support: picture cards for each principle / stretch: apply all three principles to a personal plan
- **Materials:** principle cards, cones, balls; safety: correct technique for strength exercises (bodyweight only)
- **Joy:** "Principle charades"

### Lesson 7 — My personal training plan
- **Duration:** 45 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can write a 4-week personal fitness plan using FITT and training principles and start it safely." (UK KS3 PE; SHAPE Std 3; IB MYP PHE Criterion B)
- **Hook:** "Choose one component you want to improve."
- **Key activity:** Use baseline results to set a SMART goal; design a 3-sessions-a-week plan (at home or in the park) with a parent; carry out week-1 session together.
- **Check for understanding:** "How does your plan use progressive overload over the four weeks?" → A specific increase each week (e.g. more repetitions, longer time).
- **Differentiation:** support: a template plan to personalise / stretch: include a rest day and explain why recovery matters
- **Materials:** plan templates, baseline record cards; safety: plans checked by an adult; no heavy weights
- **Joy:** Owning your own plan

### Lesson 8 — Re-test and review
- **Duration:** 45 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can re-test my chosen component, compare results and evaluate my training plan." (UK KS3 PE; SHAPE Std 3)
- **Hook:** "Four weeks later — did it work?"
- **Key activity:** Re-test the chosen component (after four weeks of the plan running alongside Unit 2); compare with baseline; evaluate what worked; set a new goal.
- **Check for understanding:** "If you didn't improve, give two possible reasons." → e.g. the plan wasn't followed consistently, the intensity was too low, the test was done differently, or illness/rest.
- **Differentiation:** support: a guided review sheet / stretch: a graph of weekly progress and a written evaluation
- **Materials:** testing equipment, record cards
- **Joy:** Celebrating personal bests

**End-of-unit check:** Name and demonstrate six components of fitness; explain heart-rate changes during exercise; lead a three-phase warm-up; explain FITT and one training principle using your own plan.

## Unit 2 — Invasion games: tactics and teamwork

**Essential question:** How do teams create and deny space to score and to defend?

**Key vocabulary:** attack, defence, possession, width, depth, support, creating space, off-the-ball movement, marking, man-to-man, zonal defence, pressing, transition, give-and-go, overlap, communication, sportsmanship

### Lesson 9 — Principles of attack: creating space
- **Duration:** 45 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can move off the ball to create space and support the ball carrier in a small-sided game." (UK KS3 PE — tactics; SHAPE Std 2)
- **Hook:** "Most of a football match, a player doesn't have the ball. What should they be doing?"
- **Key activity:** Adult-led: 4 v 2 possession in a grid (move to create passing angles); discuss width and depth; 4 v 4 game with "freeze moments" to spot space.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why should a supporting player move away from the ball carrier rather than towards them?" → To create space and a passing angle; moving close brings a defender closer too.
- **Differentiation:** support: 4 v 1 with more time / stretch: 3 v 3 in a smaller space
- **Materials:** footballs or handballs, bibs, cones for grids; safety: suitable surface, shin pads for football
- **Joy:** "Keep-ball" competition

### Lesson 10 — Passing and the give-and-go
- **Duration:** 45 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can use a give-and-go (one-two) to beat a defender in basketball or handball." (UK KS3 PE; SHAPE Std 1, 2)
- **Hook:** Video clip of a slick one-two in a professional game: "How did they get past two defenders with one pass?"
- **Key activity:** Drill: pass, cut, receive return pass; 2 v 1 then 3 v 2; apply in a small-sided basketball or handball game.
- **Check for understanding:** "When is the best moment to run after passing in a give-and-go?" → Immediately after passing, while the defender watches the ball, into the space behind them.
- **Differentiation:** support: 2 v 0 then passive defender / stretch: 3 v 2 with a condition (must use a give-and-go to score)
- **Materials:** basketballs or handballs, bibs, cones, hoops or goals
- **Joy:** Scoring your first give-and-go goal

### Lesson 11 — Principles of defence: marking and pressure
- **Duration:** 45 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can mark an opponent, stay goal-side and apply pressure to win the ball." (UK KS3 PE — tactics; SHAPE Std 2)
- **Hook:** "Defence wins championships. What's the secret?"
- **Key activity:** Shadow marking; stay between opponent and goal ("goal-side"); defensive stance; 1 v 1 then 2 v 2; team defence in a netball or basketball game.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why should a defender stay between the opponent and the goal?" → To block the most direct route to goal and force the attacker away from danger.
- **Differentiation:** support: defend at walking pace first / stretch: communicate as a defensive unit (calling "mine", "switch")
- **Materials:** netballs or basketballs, bibs, cones; safety: no contact in netball-style games
- **Joy:** "Shadow" game — can your partner lose you?

### Lesson 12 — Zonal vs man-to-man defence
- **Duration:** 45 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can explain and play both man-to-man and zonal defence and say when each works best." (UK KS3 PE — tactics; SHAPE Std 2)
- **Hook:** "Should you follow a player everywhere, or guard an area?"
- **Key activity:** Try man-to-man in a basketball game, then a 2-1-2 zone; compare; discuss strengths and weaknesses.
- **Check for understanding:** "When might a zone defence be better than man-to-man?" → When the opponents are faster individually, or to protect the area near the goal and force long shots.
- **Differentiation:** support: coloured zones marked with cones / stretch: switch between systems on the teacher's call
- **Materials:** basketballs, bibs, cones to mark zones
- **Joy:** Tactics board "coach's time-out"

### Lesson 13 — Ultimate frisbee: spirit of the game
- **Duration:** 45 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can play ultimate frisbee, using the backhand throw, pivoting and self-refereeing with 'Spirit of the Game'." (UK KS3 PE; SHAPE Std 1, 4)
- **Hook:** "A sport with no referee — the players call their own fouls. Could that work?"
- **Key activity:** Backhand and forehand throws; catching (pancake catch); pivoting; 5 v 5 game; players resolve disputes themselves; end with a spirit circle.
- **Check for understanding:** "What does 'Spirit of the Game' ask players to do?" → Play fairly, respect opponents and the rules, and resolve disputes honestly without a referee.
- **Differentiation:** support: larger, softer discs and shorter throws / stretch: introduce the stack formation in attack
- **Materials:** flying discs, cones for end zones, bibs
- **Joy:** The spirit circle — giving an award to the fairest opponent

### Lesson 14 — Tag rugby: running lines and support
- **Duration:** 45 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can play tag rugby, passing backwards, running onto the ball and supporting the ball carrier." (UK KS3 PE; SHAPE Std 1, 2)
- **Hook:** "In rugby you can only pass backwards — so how do you go forwards?"
- **Key activity:** Backward passing on the move; running at angles; tagging (removing a tag instead of tackling); 5 v 5 tag rugby game.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why must support players stay behind the ball carrier in rugby?" → Passes must go backwards, so they need to be behind to receive a legal pass.
- **Differentiation:** support: walking pace drills and a larger space / stretch: introduce the "switch" move
- **Materials:** tag belts, rugby balls, cones, bibs; safety: tags only, no contact
- **Joy:** Scoring a try

### Lesson 15 — Transition: turning defence into attack
- **Duration:** 45 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can respond quickly when possession changes, switching between attack and defence." (UK KS3 PE — tactics; SHAPE Std 2)
- **Hook:** "The most dangerous moment in a game is the second after you lose the ball."
- **Key activity:** Transition games (when the ball is won, the team must attack a different goal immediately); counter-attack drills; discuss the "five-second rule" (press immediately to win the ball back).
- **Check for understanding:** "What should a team do in the first seconds after losing possession?" → Press quickly to win it back or recover goal-side to defend.
- **Differentiation:** support: fewer players and more space / stretch: add a "golden goal" for scoring within 10 seconds of winning the ball
- **Materials:** balls, bibs, four small goals or target zones
- **Joy:** "Counter-attack challenge"

### Lesson 16 — Team tactics: planning a game strategy
- **Duration:** 45 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can plan a simple team strategy (formation, roles, set play) and adapt it during a game." (UK KS3 PE — tactics; SHAPE Std 2)
- **Hook:** "Every great team has a game plan. What's yours?"
- **Key activity:** Teams use a tactics board to plan a formation and one set play (e.g. a free pass routine); play; half-time review and adaptation.
- **Check for understanding:** "What did your team change at half-time, and why?" → A specific tactical change in response to the opponent (e.g. more width, a zone).
- **Differentiation:** support: choose from pre-designed plans / stretch: act as team captain leading the half-time talk
- **Materials:** tactics boards (whiteboards with magnets), balls, bibs, cones
- **Joy:** The half-time team talk

### Lesson 17 — Fair play, rules and officiating
- **Duration:** 45 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can officiate a small-sided game using the key rules and hand signals and show good sportsmanship as a player." (UK KS3 PE; SHAPE Std 4)
- **Hook:** "Is it harder to play or to referee?"
- **Key activity:** Learn key rules and signals for one sport; children take turns refereeing small-sided games; discuss handling disagreement respectfully.
- **Check for understanding:** "How should a player respond when they disagree with the referee?" → Accept the decision calmly, speak respectfully (through the captain if needed) and carry on playing.
- **Differentiation:** support: co-referee with an adult / stretch: referee independently and explain decisions
- **Materials:** whistles, rule cards, bibs, balls
- **Joy:** Wearing the referee's whistle

### Lesson 18 — Invasion games tournament and assessment
- **Duration:** 45 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can apply attacking and defending principles in a tournament and evaluate my own and my team's performance." (UK KS3 PE; SHAPE Std 2, 4; IB MYP PHE Criterion D)
- **Hook:** "Tournament day!"
- **Key activity:** Round-robin tournament (children choose the sport or rotate); peer observation checklist on one player (creates space, marks, communicates); self-evaluation.
- **Check for understanding:** "Name one attacking and one defending principle you used, and when." → Specific examples from the tournament.
- **Differentiation:** support: mixed-ability teams with clear roles / stretch: team captain and tactical decisions
- **Materials:** balls, bibs, cones, scoreboards, observation checklists
- **Joy:** Tournament with a "spirit award"

**End-of-unit check:** Observed in a small-sided game: moves to create space, supports the ball carrier, marks goal-side, transitions quickly; explains one attacking and one defending principle; officiates a short game fairly.

## Unit 3 — Health: growing up and staying well

**Essential question:** How can I look after my body and mind as I grow up, and help others in an emergency?

**Key vocabulary:** puberty, hormones, hygiene, nutrients, balanced diet, free sugars, hydration, sleep, circadian rhythm, mental health, wellbeing, stress, anxiety, coping strategies, peer pressure, drugs, alcohol, nicotine, vaping, medicines, consent, first aid, DRSABC, recovery position, CPR, choking

### Lesson 19 — Balanced diet and energy
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can explain the main nutrients, what a balanced plate looks like and how to read a food label." (UK KS3 Health Education; NHES Std 1)
- **Hook:** "A sports drink, a banana and a glass of water — which is the best choice after a football match, and does it depend?"
- **Key activity:** Nutrients and their jobs (carbohydrates, protein, fats, vitamins and minerals, fibre, water); balanced plate models from different countries (e.g. the UK Eatwell Guide, Harvard Healthy Eating Plate); read traffic-light and nutrition labels; plan a balanced day of meals from the family's own cuisine.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why do athletes need carbohydrates, and why is fibre important too?" → Carbohydrates are the body's main energy source; fibre keeps the digestive system healthy and helps you feel full.
- **Differentiation:** support: picture sorting of foods onto a plate / stretch: compare the sugar in three drinks using labels and the WHO advice on free sugars
- **Materials:** food packaging with labels, plate diagrams, calculators; safety: be sensitive to family diets, culture, allergies and food insecurity
- **Joy:** Design a balanced "pre-match meal" from your family's cuisine

### Lesson 20 — Sleep, screens and energy
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can explain why sleep matters for growth, learning and mood, and plan a healthy sleep and screen routine." (UK KS3 Health Education; NHES Std 7)
- **Hook:** "Children aged 6–12 need about 9–12 hours of sleep a night. How much did you get last night?"
- **Key activity:** Keep a one-week sleep diary (started earlier); learn what sleep does (memory, growth hormone, mood); how screen light and late-night notifications affect sleep; plan a wind-down routine.
- **Check for understanding:** "Give two ways screens can reduce sleep, and one change that would help." → Blue light and stimulating content delay sleepiness; notifications wake you; charge devices outside the bedroom.
- **Differentiation:** support: a sleep-routine picture planner / stretch: analyse your sleep diary and link it to mood and energy
- **Materials:** sleep diaries, routine planners
- **Joy:** "Sleep challenge" — a week of your planned routine

### Lesson 21 — Puberty: changes for everyone
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can describe the physical and emotional changes of puberty for all bodies and know they are normal and happen at different times." (UK KS3 RSHE; NHES Std 1)
- **Hook:** Anonymous question box: "What do you want to know about growing up?"
- **Key activity:** Taught in line with the family's or school's relationships-and-sex-education policy (parents informed in advance): hormones and growth spurts; changes to bodies, skin, hair and voice; periods explained factually for all children; emotional changes; normal variation in timing; answer anonymous questions.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why is it unhelpful to compare your body's changes with a friend's?" → Puberty starts and progresses at different ages for everyone (roughly between 8 and 14 to begin); all timings within a wide range are normal.
- **Differentiation:** support: clear diagrams and simple language, with an adult to talk to afterwards / stretch: explain the role of hormones in simple terms
- **Materials:** age-appropriate diagrams, question box, trusted resources (e.g. NHS or national health service puberty pages); safety: calm, respectful classroom agreement; parents informed
- **Joy:** (Sensitive lesson) — relief and confidence from knowing it's normal; children leave with a "who I can ask" card

### Lesson 22 — Hygiene and self-care during puberty
- **Duration:** 35 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can plan a personal hygiene routine and explain why it matters more during puberty." (UK KS3 Health Education; NHES Std 7)
- **Hook:** "Why do teenagers suddenly need deodorant?"
- **Key activity:** Sweat glands and bacteria; skin care; hair care; dental care; period products (factual, for everyone); make a personal hygiene kit list and routine.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why does body odour increase during puberty, and what reduces it?" → Sweat glands become more active and bacteria break down sweat; washing daily, clean clothes and deodorant reduce it.
- **Differentiation:** support: a picture routine planner / stretch: design an information leaflet for younger children about hand and dental hygiene
- **Materials:** example products (unopened), routine planners
- **Joy:** Design your own "self-care kit" checklist

### Lesson 23 — Mental health and wellbeing
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can explain that everyone has mental health, recognise signs of stress and anxiety, and use at least three coping strategies." (UK KS3 Health Education; NHES Std 7, 8)
- **Hook:** "We all have physical health. We all have mental health too. What keeps yours strong?"
- **Key activity:** The mental-health continuum; signs of stress (body and mind); practise strategies: box breathing (4-4-4-4), grounding (5-4-3-2-1 senses), movement, talking to a trusted adult, journalling; make a personal "wellbeing toolkit".
- **Check for understanding:** "Name two signs your body might be stressed and one strategy you will use." → e.g. a racing heart and a tight stomach; box breathing.
- **Differentiation:** support: a picture-based strategy card / stretch: explain how exercise improves mood (endorphins, sleep, confidence)
- **Materials:** toolkit templates, calm music; safety: signpost help (trusted adults, national child helplines)
- **Joy:** A calming yoga or stretch sequence to finish

### Lesson 24 — Drugs, alcohol, tobacco and vaping
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can describe the effects and risks of alcohol, nicotine (including vaping) and misuse of medicines, and know the laws in my country." (UK KS3 Health Education — drugs, alcohol and tobacco; NHES Std 1, 5)
- **Hook:** "Vapes are often flavoured like sweets. Who do you think they're marketed to — and why?"
- **Key activity:** Facts about alcohol (effects on the developing brain), nicotine (highly addictive, in cigarettes and most vapes), medicines (safe use: only as directed by a doctor/pharmacist and adult); analyse marketing tactics; laws on age limits in your country.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why is nicotine especially risky for young people?" → It is highly addictive and the developing brain is more vulnerable to addiction.
- **Differentiation:** support: fact cards with pictures / stretch: evaluate a real anti-vaping or anti-smoking campaign
- **Materials:** fact cards (from national health services), advert examples; safety: factual, non-judgemental; some children may have family members who use substances
- **Joy:** Design a counter-advert to "unmask" vape marketing

### Lesson 25 — Peer pressure and making safe choices
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can recognise peer pressure (including online) and use refusal strategies confidently." (UK KS3 Health Education; NHES Std 4, 5)
- **Hook:** "Everyone's doing it" — is that ever a good reason?
- **Key activity:** Scenarios (a dare, a vape at a party, sharing a photo, skipping a meal to look a certain way); practise refusal skills (say no clearly, give a reason, suggest an alternative, walk away); role plays.
- **Check for understanding:** "Name three ways to say no to a friend who is pressuring you." → e.g. "No thanks, I'm not into that"; suggest something else; use humour; leave the situation; get an adult.
- **Differentiation:** support: refusal phrase cards / stretch: coach a younger child with a scenario
- **Materials:** scenario cards, refusal-strategy poster
- **Joy:** Role-play "the confident no"

### Lesson 26 — First aid: DRSABC and the recovery position
- **Duration:** 45 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can use the DRSABC approach to assess a casualty and place someone in the recovery position." (UK KS3 Health Education — basic first aid; NHES Std 7)
- **Hook:** "Someone collapses in the park. What are the first three things you do?"
- **Key activity:** DRSABC: Danger, Response, Send for help (know your local emergency number — e.g. 112 in the EU and many other countries, 911 in North America, 999 in the UK), Airway, Breathing, CPR; practise the recovery position in pairs on mats.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why do we check for danger before helping?" → So the helper doesn't become a second casualty.
- **Differentiation:** support: a picture flowchart / stretch: explain why the recovery position keeps the airway open
- **Materials:** mats, DRSABC flowchart, emergency-number cards; safety: gentle practice, supervised
- **Joy:** "First-aid champion" certificate

### Lesson 27 — First aid: CPR, choking, bleeding and burns
- **Duration:** 45 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can describe hands-only CPR, what to do for choking, and first aid for bleeding and burns." (UK KS3 Health Education — basic first aid; NHES Std 7)
- **Hook:** "Pressing hard and fast on the centre of the chest can keep someone alive until help arrives."
- **Key activity:** Hands-only CPR on a training manikin or cushion (100–120 compressions a minute — to the beat of "Stayin' Alive"); choking (encourage coughing, back blows, abdominal thrusts for children over one and adults — demonstrated, not practised forcefully on each other); bleeding (apply pressure); burns (cool under running water for 20 minutes).
- **Check for understanding:** "How long should you cool a burn under running water, and why?" → About 20 minutes, to stop the burning, reduce pain and limit damage.
- **Differentiation:** support: a picture flowchart / stretch: explain what an AED (defibrillator) does and where the nearest one is
- **Materials:** CPR manikin or firm cushion, first-aid kit, flowcharts, "Stayin' Alive" recording; safety: never practise real compressions or abdominal thrusts on a person
- **Joy:** CPR to the beat

### Lesson 28 — Unit 3 review: my health plan
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can create a personal health plan covering activity, food, sleep, hygiene and wellbeing, and know where to get help." (UK KS3 Health Education; NHES Std 6, 8)
- **Hook:** "You're heading to secondary school. What habits will you take with you?"
- **Key activity:** Review the unit through a quiz game; write a personal health plan with three goals; list trusted adults and services.
- **Check for understanding:** "Name one goal in your plan and how you'll know you're achieving it." → A specific, measurable goal (e.g. "screens out of my room by 9 p.m. five nights a week").
- **Differentiation:** support: a plan template / stretch: link goals to evidence from the unit (sleep diary, heart-rate data)
- **Materials:** plan templates, quiz cards
- **Joy:** "Health quiz show"

**End-of-unit check:** A personal health plan; explain puberty changes as normal; demonstrate DRSABC and the recovery position; explain hands-only CPR and burns first aid; name three coping strategies and two refusal strategies.

## Unit 4 — Dance: composing and performing

**Essential question:** How can movement communicate ideas and feelings, and how do choreographers build a dance?

**Key vocabulary:** choreography, motif, unison, canon, formation, levels, pathways, dynamics, action–space–dynamics–relationships, counterpoint, mirroring, stimulus, performance skills, focus, projection

### Lesson 29 — Motif and development
- **Duration:** 45 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can create a short movement motif from a stimulus and develop it by changing speed, level and size." (UK KS3 PE — dance; SHAPE Std 1)
- **Hook:** A photo stimulus (e.g. a storm, a city at night, a flock of birds): "Show it with your body — no words."
- **Key activity:** Warm-up; create a 4-count motif from the stimulus; develop it (faster/slower, high/low, big/small, reversed); perform in pairs.
- **Check for understanding:** "Show your motif, then show one way you developed it. What changed?" → A clear demonstration and a named change (level, speed, size or direction).
- **Differentiation:** support: choose from a bank of starting movements / stretch: develop using two variables at once
- **Materials:** stimulus images, music player, open space; safety: bare feet or suitable shoes, clear floor
- **Joy:** "Storm in a room" group performance

### Lesson 30 — Choreographic devices: unison, canon and formations
- **Duration:** 45 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can use unison, canon and changing formations in a group dance." (UK KS3 PE — dance; SHAPE Std 1, 2)
- **Hook:** A clip of a large group dance (e.g. a flash mob or a K-pop group performance): "How do they look so powerful together?"
- **Key activity:** Groups combine their motifs; perform in unison, then in canon; try three formations (line, V, circle); film and review.
- **Check for understanding:** "What is the difference between unison and canon, and what effect does each create?" → Unison: all together at once — strong, powerful; canon: the same movement starting one after another — a ripple or wave effect.
- **Differentiation:** support: a smaller group and a formation card / stretch: add a counterpoint section (different movements at the same time)
- **Materials:** music, formation cards, a tablet to film
- **Joy:** Watching the canon ripple on video

### Lesson 31 — Dance from around the world: Bollywood
- **Duration:** 45 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can perform a short Bollywood-style routine with characteristic hand gestures, footwork and expression." (UK KS3 PE — dance from different cultures; SHAPE Std 1)
- **Hook:** Bollywood film dance clips: "Why is dance so central in Indian cinema?"
- **Key activity:** Learn key moves (e.g. "screw the light bulb", "pat the dog", shoulder shimmies) and mudra-inspired hand gestures; a short routine to a Bollywood song; discuss its roots in Indian classical and folk dance.
- **Check for understanding:** "How does facial expression add to a Bollywood performance?" → It tells the story and shows emotion, just as much as the movements do.
- **Differentiation:** support: fewer moves repeated more / stretch: add a section in canon
- **Materials:** Bollywood music, video examples, open space
- **Joy:** Performing with energy and big smiles

### Lesson 32 — Dance from around the world: capoeira movement
- **Duration:** 45 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can perform basic capoeira-inspired movements (ginga, esquiva) in the roda with rhythm and control." (UK KS3 PE — dance; SHAPE Std 1)
- **Hook:** "A Brazilian art that is part dance, part martial art, part music — created by enslaved Africans in Brazil."
- **Key activity:** History (African roots, Brazil); the *ginga* (rocking base step), *esquiva* (dodge) and non-contact kicks performed slowly with control; form a *roda* (circle) with clapping and songs; pairs "play" without contact.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why is the *ginga* so important in capoeira?" → It keeps you constantly moving and balanced, ready to attack or dodge; it's the base of everything else.
- **Differentiation:** support: ginga and one dodge only / stretch: add a cartwheel (*aú*) where safe
- **Materials:** capoeira music (berimbau), open space, mats for cartwheels; safety: no contact, controlled kicks, spacing
- **Joy:** The *roda* with clapping and singing

### Lesson 33 — Composing a group dance
- **Duration:** 45 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can compose a 1–2 minute group dance with a clear structure (beginning, middle, end) using a motif and choreographic devices." (UK KS3 PE — dance; SHAPE Std 1, 2)
- **Hook:** "Choose a theme you care about — the dance is your message."
- **Key activity:** Groups choose a theme (possibly linked to the capstone), a stimulus and music; plan a structure (e.g. ABA); rehearse with unison, canon and formations.
- **Check for understanding:** "How does the structure of your dance help tell its story?" → A clear explanation of the beginning, middle and end and how they link to the theme.
- **Differentiation:** support: a structure template / stretch: a group leader role and a written choreographer's note
- **Materials:** music choices, planning sheets, a tablet to film
- **Joy:** Creative ownership

### Lesson 34 — Dance performance and appreciation
- **Duration:** 45 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can perform a group dance with focus and projection and appreciate others' dances using dance vocabulary." (UK KS3 PE — dance; SHAPE Std 4)
- **Hook:** "Showtime."
- **Key activity:** Performances; audience feedback using "action, space, dynamics, relationships" vocabulary; self-evaluation.
- **Check for understanding:** "Describe one choreographic device in another group's dance and its effect." → e.g. "They used canon, which made the movement look like a wave."
- **Differentiation:** support: a feedback sentence frame / stretch: a written review of the performance
- **Materials:** performance space, feedback sheets
- **Joy:** The dance showcase

**End-of-unit check:** A performed group dance showing a developed motif, unison, canon and formations, and a spoken appreciation of another group's dance using dance vocabulary.

## Unit 5 — Gymnastics: partner sequences and flight

**Essential question:** How can control, balance and flight be combined into a sequence that is safe and beautiful?

**Key vocabulary:** balance, counterbalance, counter-tension, weight transfer, roll, flight, take-off, landing, vault, symmetry, asymmetry, matching, mirroring, contrasting, sequence, extension, control

### Lesson 35 — Balances: counterbalance and counter-tension
- **Duration:** 45 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can perform partner counterbalances (pushing) and counter-tension balances (pulling) safely and with control." (UK KS3 PE — gymnastics; SHAPE Std 1)
- **Hook:** "Two people can balance in positions neither could hold alone. How?"
- **Key activity:** Adult demonstrates safe grips; pairs explore counterbalances (leaning in, pushing) and counter-tension (leaning out, pulling); hold for 3 seconds with extension.
- **Check for understanding:** "What is the difference between counterbalance and counter-tension?" → Counterbalance: partners push against each other (leaning in); counter-tension: partners pull away from each other (leaning out).
- **Differentiation:** support: low, stable balances with wide bases / stretch: balances on fewer points of contact or at different levels
- **Materials:** mats, balance cards; safety: matched partners, mats, no stacking on backs, adult spotting
- **Joy:** Photographing the class's best balances

### Lesson 36 — Rolls and weight transfer
- **Duration:** 45 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can perform at least three rolls (e.g. log, forward, backward or shoulder roll) with control and link them to balances." (UK KS3 PE — gymnastics; SHAPE Std 1)
- **Hook:** "How do judo athletes and parkour runners fall without getting hurt?"
- **Key activity:** Progressions for each roll (with wedges where needed); linking a balance into a roll and out into a new balance; safety of the neck (tuck chin).
- **Check for understanding:** "Why must you tuck your chin in a forward roll?" → To protect the neck — the back of the shoulders and back take the weight, not the head.
- **Differentiation:** support: log and teddy-bear rolls, wedge mats / stretch: backward roll to straddle stand, or dive roll only with specialist teaching
- **Materials:** mats, wedge mats; safety: only teach rolls that the adult is confident to spot; no rolls for children with relevant medical conditions
- **Joy:** Smooth "roll-to-balance" links

### Lesson 37 — Flight: jumps and vaulting
- **Duration:** 45 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can perform shaped jumps (tuck, straddle, pike, star) and a simple vault with a safe take-off and landing." (UK KS3 PE — gymnastics; SHAPE Std 1)
- **Hook:** "Gymnasts can seem to hang in the air. What's the secret?"
- **Key activity:** Safe landing (bend knees, arms forward, "stick it"); shaped jumps from a low box or bench; springboard take-off (two feet) onto a box and a straddle or squat-on vault, with an adult spotting.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why do gymnasts bend their knees when they land?" → To absorb the force of landing gradually and protect joints.
- **Differentiation:** support: jumps from a low bench only / stretch: add a half-turn in flight
- **Materials:** benches, low vaulting box, springboard, crash mats; safety: adult spotting at all times, one at a time, clear landing zone
- **Joy:** Sticking the landing

### Lesson 38 — Symmetry, matching and mirroring in pairs
- **Duration:** 45 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can create a pair sequence using matching, mirroring and contrasting movements." (UK KS3 PE — gymnastics; SHAPE Std 1, 2)
- **Hook:** Synchronised swimming or diving: "What makes two athletes look like one?"
- **Key activity:** Explore symmetric and asymmetric balances; perform them matching (side by side) and mirroring (facing); add a contrasting section; plan transitions.
- **Check for understanding:** "What's the difference between matching and mirroring?" → Matching: both do the same movement with the same side; mirroring: partners face each other and move as reflections.
- **Differentiation:** support: two actions in the sequence / stretch: include a counter-tension balance and canon timing
- **Materials:** mats, benches, sequence planning cards
- **Joy:** "Mirror challenge"

### Lesson 39 — Composing a partner sequence
- **Duration:** 45 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can compose and refine a partner sequence of 8–10 elements including balances, rolls, a counterbalance and flight." (UK KS3 PE — gymnastics; SHAPE Std 1, 2)
- **Hook:** "Your routine, your score — design for the judges."
- **Key activity:** Plan using a sequence sheet (start and finish positions, elements, transitions, use of apparatus); rehearse; film and refine.
- **Check for understanding:** "Where in your sequence is the hardest transition, and how did you make it smoother?" → A specific transition and the change made (e.g. a roll instead of a step).
- **Differentiation:** support: 6 elements with a template / stretch: include apparatus (bench or box) and a timing contrast
- **Materials:** mats, benches, box, sequence sheets, tablet; safety: rehearsed elements only
- **Joy:** Seeing your routine improve on video

### Lesson 40 — Gymnastics performance and judging
- **Duration:** 45 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can perform my sequence with control and extension and judge others using clear criteria." (UK KS3 PE — gymnastics; SHAPE Std 4)
- **Hook:** "Judges' panel: scores for difficulty and execution."
- **Key activity:** Performances; peer judging with simple criteria (control, extension, flow, variety, synchronisation); give one positive and one improvement.
- **Check for understanding:** "What did you look for when judging 'control'?" → Held balances without wobbling, smooth transitions and safe landings.
- **Differentiation:** support: a criteria card with pictures / stretch: head judge role
- **Materials:** mats, apparatus, score cards
- **Joy:** "Olympic" scoring

**End-of-unit check:** A performed partner sequence with a counterbalance, rolls, flight and matching/mirroring, judged against criteria, and a spoken explanation of how control and extension were achieved.

## Unit 6 — Athletics: technique and personal bests

- **Objective:** "By the end, I can use a crouch or standing start and sprint with good arm drive, high knees and an upright posture." (UK KS3 PE — athletics; SHAPE Std 1)

### Lesson 42 — Relay: the baton changeover
- **Duration:** 45 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can perform a smooth baton changeover at speed in a relay team." (UK KS3 PE — athletics; SHAPE Std 1, 2)
- **Hook:** "Relay races are often won or lost in the changeovers."
- **Key activity:** Upsweep and downsweep techniques; the incoming runner calls "hand"; outgoing runner starts on a check mark; practise in teams of four; team relay races.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why does the outgoing runner start running before the baton arrives?" → To be close to full speed at the changeover so the baton keeps moving fast.

### Lesson 43 — Middle distance: pacing

- **Hook:** "Why do so many runners set off too fast and struggle at the end?"
- **Key activity:** Predict a target time; run laps with a partner calling split times; compare even pacing vs a fast start; discuss how the heart-rate work (Unit 1) applies.
- **Check for understanding:** "What is even pacing, and why is it usually faster overall?" → Running each lap at about the same speed; it avoids early exhaustion so you keep a good speed throughout.
- **Differentiation:** support: a shorter distance (400–600 m) with walk breaks / stretch: plan a negative split (second half faster)

### Lesson 44 — Long jump and triple jump
- **Duration:** 45 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can perform a long jump with an accurate approach and take-off, and a standing or short-approach triple jump (hop, step, jump)." (UK KS3 PE — athletics; SHAPE Std 1)
- **Hook:** "The long jump world record is over 8.9 metres. How far can you go?"
- **Key activity:** Approach run with a check mark; take off from one foot; flight and landing; triple-jump rhythm "same–other–both" (hop on the same foot, step onto the other, jump to land on both).

### Lesson 45 — Throwing: shot put (push) and javelin (pull)
- **Duration:** 45 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can use a push technique for shot put and a pull technique for javelin with safe routines." (UK KS3 PE — athletics; SHAPE Std 1)
- **Hook:** "Why can't you throw a shot like a baseball?"

- **Check for understanding:** "Why is shot put a 'push' and not a 'throw'?" → The rules require the shot to be pushed from the shoulder; throwing it would risk injury to the arm and is not allowed.
- **Differentiation:** support: lighter implements and standing throws / stretch: add a glide or step approach

- **Joy:** Distance markers with names on

### Lesson 46 — Hurdles and rhythm

- **Materials:** foam or collapsible hurdles, cones, stopwatches; safety: hurdles that fall forward easily
- **Joy:** Hurdle races

### Lesson 47 — Combined events: our class decathlon
- **Duration:** 45 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can compete in a combined-event challenge and use a points table to compare performances across events." (UK KS3 PE — athletics; SHAPE Std 3; Maths link)
- **Hook:** "Who is the best all-round athlete? The decathlon decides."

### Lesson 48 — Athletics review: analysing technique

- **Check for understanding:** "What does it mean to 'orientate' a map, and why does it help?" → Turn the map so its features line up with the real ones around you; then left on the map is left in real life.
- **Differentiation:** support: pairs with an adult nearby and fewer controls / stretch: a longer line course with route choices
- **Materials:** site maps, control markers and punches or codes, stopwatches; safety: boundaries, meeting point, whistles, pairs
- **Joy:** The orienteering race

### Lesson 58 — Compass skills
- **Duration:** 45 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can use a compass to find north, take a simple bearing and follow it." (UK KS3 PE — OAA; Geography and Maths link — angles)
- **Hook:** "Explorers crossed deserts and oceans with a needle that points north."
- **Key activity:** Parts of a baseplate compass; find north; set and walk a bearing (e.g. 90°, 30 paces) around a course of cones; compass "treasure hunt".
- **Check for understanding:** "If you walk on a bearing of 90°, which direction are you going?" → East.
- **Differentiation:** support: the four cardinal directions only / stretch: follow a three-leg bearing course and return to the start
- **Materials:** baseplate compasses, cones, bearing cards; safety: clear, bounded space
- **Joy:** Compass treasure hunt

### Lesson 59 — Team problem solving
- **Duration:** 45 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can work with a team to plan, communicate and solve physical challenges, reflecting on roles." (UK KS3 PE — OAA; SHAPE Std 4)
- **Hook:** "Get the whole team across the 'river' using only three stepping stones."
- **Key activity:** Circuit of team challenges (stepping-stone river crossing with mats, the "blind" maze guided by voice, the "tarp flip" — turn over a tarp while everyone stands on it); plan–do–review after each.
- **Check for understanding:** "What role did you play in your team, and what helped the team succeed?" → A specific role (leader, planner, encourager) and a specific helpful behaviour (listening, clear instructions).
- **Differentiation:** support: clear role cards / stretch: silent challenges (non-verbal communication) or leadership roles
- **Materials:** mats or carpet squares, blindfolds, a tarp, cones, rope; safety: supervision, soft surfaces
- **Joy:** Cracking the hardest challenge together

### Lesson 60 — Water safety: understanding the dangers
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can explain the main dangers of open water (cold-water shock, rip currents, hidden hazards) and the actions that keep people safe." (UK KS3 PE — water safety; NHES Std 7)
- **Hook:** "Most drownings happen to people who never meant to be in the water."
- **Key activity:** Water-safety messages (e.g. from the RNLI, Royal Life Saving Society or national lifesaving organisations): swim at lifeguarded places between the flags; cold-water shock and "Float to Live" (lean back, extend arms and legs, float until breathing is under control); how to spot and escape a rip current (swim parallel to the shore); call for help; "throw, don't go" to help others.
- **Check for understanding:** "If you're caught in a rip current, what should you do?" → Don't fight it by swimming straight back; float, then swim parallel to the shore until free, and raise an arm to call for help.
- **Differentiation:** support: picture cards for each message / stretch: design a water-safety poster for a local beach, river or lake
- **Materials:** water-safety resources and videos, flag images, rope or throw bag
- **Joy:** "Throw bag" accuracy challenge (on land)

### Lesson 61 — Swimming and self-rescue (pool session)
- **Duration:** 45 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can swim at least 50 m using an efficient stroke, tread water for 1 minute, float, and perform a safe self-rescue." (UK KS3 PE — swimming; UK KS2 swimming standard extended)
- **Hook:** "Could you keep yourself safe if you fell into water in your clothes?"
- **Key activity:** Pool session with qualified lifeguard/instructor: stroke improvement; treading water; "Float to Live" practice; swimming in light clothing; reaching and throwing rescues from the side.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why should you try to float before swimming if you fall into cold water?" → Floating helps you get through cold-water shock and control your breathing before using energy to swim.
- **Differentiation:** support: buoyancy aids and shallower water, smaller distances / stretch: 100 m continuous and survival sculling
- **Materials:** swimming pool with qualified supervision, buoyancy aids, light clothing; safety: qualified lifeguard and instructor ratios; medical needs checked
- **Joy:** Earning a personal survival swimming award

### Lesson 62 — Outdoor journey: plan and go
- **Duration:** 45 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can help plan a short outdoor journey (route, kit, risk assessment) and complete it as a team." (UK KS3 PE — OAA; Geography link)
- **Hook:** "An expedition: route, kit, weather, safety — and fun."
- **Key activity:** Plan a walk in a local park or countryside (route on a map, timings, kit list, weather check, risk assessment); complete the walk with adult leaders, using map and compass at checkpoints.
- **Check for understanding:** "Name two risks on your route and how you reduced them." → e.g. roads (cross at crossings, high-visibility vests), getting lost (map, meeting point, pairs), weather (right clothing, water).
- **Differentiation:** support: a shorter route and a pre-filled kit list / stretch: navigation leader for one leg
- **Materials:** maps, compasses, first-aid kit, water, high-visibility vests, risk-assessment sheet; safety: adult ratios, mobile phone, emergency plan
- **Joy:** The expedition itself

**End-of-unit check:** Navigate an orienteering course with an orientated map and a compass bearing; describe cold-water shock, rip currents and "Float to Live"; complete a planned outdoor journey as a contributing team member; swimming and self-rescue assessed where facilities allow.

## Unit 9 — Leadership and the graduation sports festival

**Essential question:** How can I lead, coach and officiate so that everyone enjoys sport and improves?

**Key vocabulary:** leader, coach, demonstration, instruction, feedback, inclusion, adaptation (STEP: space, task, equipment, people), officiating, organisation, festival, fair play, reflection

### Lesson 63 — What makes a good sports leader?
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can describe the qualities of a good sports leader and plan a short activity for younger children." (UK KS3 PE — leadership; SHAPE Std 4)
- **Hook:** "Think of the best coach you've had. What did they do?"
- **Key activity:** List qualities (clear instructions, enthusiasm, safety, encouragement, inclusion); plan a 10-minute game for 7-year-olds with a partner.
- **Check for understanding:** "What makes a good instruction when leading younger children?" → Short, clear, with a demonstration and a check that they understood.
- **Differentiation:** support: choose a game from a card bank / stretch: plan progressions to make it harder or easier
- **Materials:** game card bank, planning sheets, cones, balls
- **Joy:** Planning to lead "the little ones"

### Lesson 64 — Adapting for inclusion: the STEP principle
- **Duration:** 45 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can adapt an activity using STEP (space, task, equipment, people) so everyone can take part and be challenged." (UK KS3 PE; SHAPE Std 4)
- **Hook:** Paralympic sports (e.g. wheelchair basketball, goalball, boccia): "How do rules and equipment make sport for everyone?"
- **Key activity:** Try goalball (blindfolded, a ball with bells) and seated volleyball; adapt a class game using STEP for different needs.
- **Check for understanding:** "Give an example of changing 'equipment' in STEP to include a player." → e.g. a larger, softer or bell ball; a lower net; a bigger target.
- **Differentiation:** support: adapt one element with a prompt card / stretch: adapt all four elements for one game
- **Materials:** a goalball or ball with a bell inside, blindfolds, a low net, STEP cards
- **Joy:** Playing goalball

### Lesson 65 — Leading a session with younger children
- **Duration:** 45 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can lead a warm-up and a game for younger children safely, adapting as needed." (UK KS3 PE — leadership; SHAPE Std 4)
- **Hook:** "You're the coach today."
- **Key activity:** Pairs lead their planned warm-up and game with a group of younger children (or family members), with an adult supervising; adapt using STEP on the spot.
- **Check for understanding:** "What did you change during your session, and why?" → A specific adaptation in response to what happened (e.g. made the space bigger because it was crowded).
- **Differentiation:** support: co-lead with a partner and an adult prompt / stretch: lead independently and give individual feedback
- **Materials:** cones, balls, bibs, a whistle, session plans; safety: adult supervision throughout
- **Joy:** Being thanked by younger children

### Lesson 66 — Officiating and organising a tournament
- **Duration:** 45 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can organise a tournament (draw, fixtures, scoring) and officiate matches fairly." (UK KS3 PE; SHAPE Std 4; Maths link)
- **Hook:** "Six teams, one afternoon — how do you make sure everyone plays everyone?"
- **Key activity:** Plan a round-robin draw (Maths link: with 6 teams there are 15 matches); timings; scoring tables; practise officiating.
- **Check for understanding:** "How many matches are needed if 6 teams each play every other team once? Explain." → 15: each of the 6 teams plays 5 others, 6 × 5 = 30, but each match is counted twice, so 30 ÷ 2 = 15.
- **Differentiation:** support: a fixture template for four teams / stretch: design a group stage plus knockout format
- **Materials:** fixture templates, whiteboard, whistles, rule cards
- **Joy:** Designing the festival fixtures

### Lesson 67 — Planning the graduation sports festival
- **Duration:** 45 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can plan my role in the graduation sports festival — events, equipment, safety, scoring and inclusion." (UK KS3 PE — leadership; SHAPE Std 4)
- **Hook:** "Your festival for families and younger children — you run it."
- **Key activity:** Committees (events, equipment, safety and first aid, scoring and results, welcome and announcements); plan stations (athletics, team games, a dance showcase, a fun family relay); risk assessment.
- **Check for understanding:** "What is one risk at your station and how will you manage it?" → A specific risk and control measure.
- **Differentiation:** support: a committee with an adult mentor / stretch: festival coordinator role
- **Materials:** planning sheets, maps of the space, equipment lists, risk-assessment forms
- **Joy:** Owning the whole event

### Lesson 68 — Festival rehearsal
- **Duration:** 45 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can rehearse my festival role and refine the plan based on a trial run." (UK KS3 PE; SHAPE Std 4)
- **Hook:** "Dress rehearsal — find the problems before the guests do."
- **Key activity:** Walk-through of all stations; test timings and equipment; fix problems.
- **Check for understanding:** "What problem did the rehearsal reveal, and how did your committee fix it?" → A specific issue and solution.
- **Differentiation:** support: a checklist for the role / stretch: troubleshoot for another committee
- **Materials:** all festival equipment, checklists
- **Joy:** Seeing the festival take shape

### Lesson 69 — The graduation sports festival
- **Duration:** 45 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can run my part of a sports festival confidently and take part with skill and fair play." (UK KS3 PE; SHAPE Std 4, 5)
- **Hook:** "Welcome, everyone!"
- **Key activity:** The festival (part of the graduation celebrations): children lead stations, officiate, score, cheer and compete.
- **Check for understanding:** "How did you make sure everyone at your station was included and safe?" → Specific actions (adaptations, clear instructions, encouragement).
- **Differentiation:** support: a buddy leader / stretch: overall coordinator
- **Materials:** festival equipment, medals or certificates, first-aid kit, water
- **Joy:** The graduation sports festival

### Lesson 70 — Reflection: my active future
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can reflect on my growth in PE and Health this year and plan how to stay active and healthy at secondary school." (UK KS3 PE; SHAPE Std 5; NHES Std 6)
- **Hook:** "Compare your fitness test in September with now."
- **Key activity:** Final fitness re-test (optional); review the year's achievements; research clubs and activities available locally; set an "active future" goal (60 minutes a day).
- **Check for understanding:** "Which physical activity do you most want to keep doing, and how will you make time for it?" → A named activity and a realistic plan.
- **Differentiation:** support: a reflection frame / stretch: write advice for next year's class
- **Materials:** fitness records, local club information, reflection sheets
- **Joy:** Celebration and personal-best certificates

**End-of-unit check:** Plan and lead a safe, inclusive activity for younger children; officiate and organise a tournament; take a leadership role in the graduation sports festival; reflect on personal fitness and health goals.

## End-of-year mastery checks

1. **Understands fitness:** names components, measures heart rate, explains FITT and training principles, and follows a personal plan. *See it:* baseline and re-test records with an evaluation.
2. **Plays games tactically:** creates and denies space in invasion games, places shots in net games, bats into gaps and fields effectively. *See it:* observed tournament play.
3. **Refines technique:** in sprinting, jumping and throwing, with personal bests recorded. *See it:* athletics record sheet and video analysis.
4. **Composes and performs:** a group dance and a partner gymnastics sequence with control and choreographic/gymnastic devices. *See it:* the showcases.
5. **Navigates and stays safe outdoors and near water:** orienteering, compass bearings, "Float to Live", rip-current escape, self-rescue. *See it:* the orienteering course and water-safety explanation.
6. **Knows how to stay healthy:** balanced diet, sleep, hygiene, puberty as normal, mental-health strategies, risks of substances. *See it:* the personal health plan.
7. **Can give first aid:** DRSABC, recovery position, hands-only CPR, burns and bleeding. *See it:* the first-aid practical.
8. **Leads others:** plans, adapts (STEP) and leads an inclusive activity and officiates fairly. *See it:* the sports festival.

## Teacher guidance

- **Mostly human-delivered and active.** The tutor app prepares children (rules, tactics, safety, health facts) and records reflections; the lesson itself happens with a parent, teacher or coach, on a field, in a hall or at a pool. Keep talk short and activity high.
- **Safety.** Warm-up and cool-down every lesson; check space and equipment; follow safe-practice guidance for gymnastics (spotting, no untaught skills), throwing (strict commands and sectors), and water (qualified lifeguards and instructors only); know children's medical conditions and care plans; carry a first-aid kit and phone.
- **Inclusion.** Adapt with STEP; celebrate personal bests over rankings; keep fitness results private; offer choice of activities; respect cultural and religious needs (clothing, fasting periods, mixed-gender activities) in consultation with families.
- **Health education is sensitive.** Inform parents before puberty and substances lessons; use an anonymous question box; answer factually and age-appropriately; signpost trusted adults and helplines; never single out a child; be alert to safeguarding disclosures and follow your safeguarding procedure.
- **Common misconceptions:** stretching cold muscles prevents injury (warm up first); more sweat means more fat burned; you have to be "sporty" to be fit; sports drinks are needed for ordinary exercise (water is usually enough); vaping is harmless water vapour; you can swim your way out of a rip current by heading straight for the shore; a person having a seizure should have something put in their mouth (never do this).
- **Vertical alignment.** Year 5 secured sports skills, basic rules, swimming 25 m and core health knowledge; Year 6 adds fitness science, tactics, technique analysis, composition, navigation, first aid, puberty and substances education, and leadership — preparing children for secondary PE (UK Year 8 / US Grade 7 / MYP 2) and a healthy adolescence.
- **Cross-curricular links:** Science (heart rate, energy, forces in sport), Maths (heart-rate graphs, points tables, fixtures, bearings), Geography (maps and orienteering), Life Skills (resilience, wellbeing, leadership and the graduation capstone), Music (dance and rhythm).
