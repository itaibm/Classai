> Part of **the best international primary school** (see [`../../../SCHOOL-CHARTER.md`](../../../SCHOOL-CHARTER.md)).
> Drawn from `knowledge-base/subjects/maths/year-6.md`. Follows the lesson-outline format and
> the 8 design principles in [`../../year-1/README.md`](../../year-1/README.md).

## Year overview

Year 6 is the end of primary maths and the SATs year: it consolidates every strand and adds two
brand-new domains. Place value reaches **10,000,000** and gains the **order of operations
(BIDMAS)**; the written methods climb to **long multiplication and long division by two-digit
divisors**; fractions become a complete **four-operation** toolkit, joined to **fraction–decimal–
percentage equivalence**. The two new domains are **ratio and proportion** (sharing in a ratio,
scaling) and **algebra** (formulae, expressions, solving equations and sequences). Measurement
introduces **area formulae for triangles and parallelograms** alongside cuboid volume; geometry
adds **circles, angle facts (triangle = 180°, vertically opposite) and nets**; coordinates extend
to **four quadrants**; and statistics introduces **pie charts and the mean**. Children work fluently
in the abstract, using concrete or pictorial models to introduce or unstick each new idea (tearing
a triangle's corners, balancing scales for equations). Most lessons run **28–38 minutes**, the upper
end of the band. Lessons are numbered continuously (1–32) across nine units, with retrieval, reasoning
and review lessons woven in.

## Unit 1 — Place value and order of operations

**Essential question:** How big can numbers get, and what order do we carry out a mixed calculation in?

**Key vocabulary:** ten millions, group in threes, round, negative, order of operations, BIDMAS,
brackets, indices, equal rank, left to right.

### Lesson 1 — Numbers to 10,000,000
- **Duration:** 26 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can read, write, compare and round numbers to 10,000,000." (Y6 number & place value)
- **Hook:** "A country's population is 8 digits long — how do we read it?"
- **Key activity:** Extend place value to ten millions; group in threes. Compare from the biggest place; round large numbers; use negatives in context across zero.
- **Check for understanding:** "Round a 7-digit number to the nearest million." → correct rounded value.
- **Differentiation:** support: group digits in threes from the right. / stretch: order four seven-digit numbers.
- **Materials:** large place-value chart, numeral cards.
- **Joy:** "read the population" of the world's biggest countries.

### Lesson 2 — Order of operations (BIDMAS)
- **Duration:** 28 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can apply BIDMAS to a mixed calculation." (Y6 four operations)
- **Hook:** "2 + 3 × 4 — is it 20 or 14? There can only be one right answer."
- **Key activity:** Order: Brackets, Indices, Division/Multiplication (left to right), Addition/Subtraction (left to right). 2 + 3 × 4 = 14; (2 + 3) × 4 = 20; 5 + 2 × 3² = 23 (indices first).
- **Check for understanding:** "3 + 4 × 2 = ?" → 11. "(3 + 4) × 2 = ?" → 14.
- **Differentiation:** support: underline the ×/÷ to do first (KB fix for "20"). / stretch: 20 − 6 ÷ 2 → 17 (÷ before −).
- **Materials:** operation cards, mini-whiteboards.
- **Joy:** "where do the brackets go?" puzzle to hit a target answer.

**End-of-unit check:** (1) 3 + 4 × 2 → 11. (2) (3 + 4) × 2 → 14. (3) 20 − 6 ÷ 2 → 17. (4) 5 + 2 × 3² → 23.

## Unit 2 — Long multiplication and long division

**Essential question:** How do we multiply and divide large numbers by two-digit numbers fluently?

**Key vocabulary:** long multiplication, placeholder zero, long division, divisor, facts list,
bring down, remainder as fraction/decimal, estimate, inverse.

### Lesson 3 — Long multiplication (4-digit × 2-digit)
- **Duration:** 30 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can multiply a 4-digit number by a 2-digit number fluently." (Y6 multiplication & division)
- **Hook:** "1,234 boxes with 24 items each — how many items in the warehouse?"
- **Key activity:** Multiply by the ones, then the tens (0 placeholder), then add. 234 × 23 = 702 + 4680 = 5,382. Estimate first; check by inverse.
- **Check for understanding:** "234 × 23 = ?" → 5,382.
- **Differentiation:** support: write the placeholder zero before the tens row. / stretch: 1,234 × 24.
- **Materials:** columned paper, place-value chart.
- **Joy:** the rows "stack" into a five-digit total.

### Lesson 4 — Long division by a 2-digit divisor
- **Duration:** 32 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can divide by a two-digit number using long division." (Y6 multiplication & division)
- **Hook:** "800 people, coaches that hold 53 — how many coaches do we need?"
- **Key activity:** Write a facts list of the divisor (12: 12, 24, 36, 48…). Divide left to right, subtract the biggest multiple that fits, bring down the next digit. 432 ÷ 12 = 36. Estimate; check.
- **Check for understanding:** "528 ÷ 12 = ?" → 44.
- **Differentiation:** support: keep going — bring down every digit (KB fix for stopping early). / stretch: 437 ÷ 15 → 29 r2.
- **Materials:** columned paper, facts-list templates.
- **Joy:** the facts list makes a "scary" division feel easy.

### Lesson 5 — Remainders as fractions and decimals
- **Duration:** 28 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can express a remainder as a whole number, a fraction or a decimal." (Y6 multiplication & division)
- **Hook:** "437 ÷ 15 leaves something over — how do we write the leftover three different ways?"
- **Key activity:** A remainder can be left as r, written as r/divisor, or carried into decimals by adding .0s. Practise all three forms on the same calculation.
- **Check for understanding:** "437 ÷ 15 (whole + remainder)?" → 29 remainder 2.
- **Differentiation:** support: carry the remainder into the next digit (KB fix for mishandling it). / stretch: express the same remainder as a decimal.
- **Materials:** columned paper.
- **Joy:** "three ways to show the leftover" challenge.

### Lesson 6 — Retrieval & fluency: written methods
- **Duration:** 22 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can use long ×, long ÷ and BIDMAS at speed." (Y6 — review)
- **Hook:** "Methods sprint — multiply, divide, and order operations."
- **Key activity:** Mixed: "234 × 23", "528 ÷ 12", "3 + 4 × 2", plus one estimate.
- **Check for understanding:** "Estimate 432 ÷ 12." → about 36 (≈ 430 ÷ 12).
- **Differentiation:** support: facts list / columned paper available. / stretch: timed, no tools.
- **Materials:** mini-whiteboards, facts-list templates.
- **Joy:** beat-your-own-time streak.

**End-of-unit check:** (1) 234 × 23 → 5,382. (2) 528 ÷ 12 → 44. (3) 437 ÷ 15 → 29 r2. (4) Estimate 432 ÷ 12 → ≈ 36.

## Unit 3 — Four operations with fractions

**Essential question:** How do we add, subtract, multiply and divide with fractions?

**Key vocabulary:** common denominator, multiply across, fraction of a fraction, divide a fraction
by a whole number, simplify.

### Lesson 7 — Add and subtract fractions (unlike denominators)
- **Duration:** 28 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can add and subtract fractions using a common denominator." (Y6 fractions)
- **Hook:** "½ + ⅓ — how do we make the pieces the same size?"
- **Key activity:** Common denominator, then add/subtract numerators: ½ + ⅓ = 3/6 + 2/6 = 5/6; ¾ − ⅖ = 15/20 − 8/20 = 7/20. Simplify the answer.
- **Check for understanding:** "¾ + ⅙ = ?" → 9/12 + 2/12 = 11/12.
- **Differentiation:** support: multiply both top and bottom together. / stretch: add three unlike fractions.
- **Materials:** fraction wall, mini-whiteboards.
- **Joy:** "make them match" — re-cut to a common size.

### Lesson 8 — Multiply fractions
- **Duration:** 28 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can multiply two fractions by multiplying tops and bottoms." (Y6 fractions)
- **Hook:** "⅔ of ¾ of a cake — is the answer bigger or smaller than ¾?"
- **Key activity:** Multiply across: ⅔ × ¾ = 6/12 = ½. A fraction of a fraction is *smaller*. No common denominator needed.
- **Check for understanding:** "⅖ × ½ = ?" → 2/10 = 1/5.
- **Differentiation:** support: shade a fraction of a fraction on a grid (KB fix for "should be bigger"). / stretch: simplify ⅔ × ¾ to lowest terms.
- **Materials:** fraction grids, mini-whiteboards.
- **Joy:** the surprise that multiplying makes it *smaller*.

### Lesson 9 — Divide a fraction by a whole number
- **Duration:** 26 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can divide a fraction by a whole number." (Y6 fractions)
- **Hook:** "Half a pizza shared among 3 people — how much each?"
- **Key activity:** ½ ÷ 3 means split a half into 3 equal parts → 1/6 (multiply the denominator by 3). Simplify answers.
- **Check for understanding:** "⅓ ÷ 2 = ?" → 1/6.
- **Differentiation:** support: physically split a fraction strip into more parts. / stretch: ¾ ÷ 2.
- **Materials:** fraction strips.
- **Joy:** split a fraction into ever-smaller fair shares.

### Lesson 10 — Retrieval & fluency: fractions
- **Duration:** 22 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can use all four operations with fractions at speed." (Y6 fractions — review)
- **Hook:** "Fraction quick-fire — all four operations in one round."
- **Key activity:** Mixed: "½ + ¼", "⅔ × ¾", "⅓ ÷ 2", "¾ − ⅖", simplify each.
- **Check for understanding:** "⅖ × ½ = ?" → 1/5.
- **Differentiation:** support: fraction wall available. / stretch: timed, no tools.
- **Materials:** mini-whiteboards, fraction wall.
- **Joy:** streak counter.

**End-of-unit check:** (1) ¾ + ⅙ → 11/12. (2) ⅔ × ¾ → ½. (3) ⅓ ÷ 2 → 1/6. (4) ⅖ × ½ → 1/5.

## Unit 4 — Fractions, decimals and percentages

**Essential question:** How do we convert freely between fractions, decimals and percentages?

**Key vocabulary:** equivalence, fraction → decimal (divide), decimal → percentage (× 100),
percentage of an amount, common set.

### Lesson 11 — Converting between FDP
- **Duration:** 26 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can convert any fraction, decimal or percentage into the other forms." (Y6 fractions, decimals & percentages)
- **Hook:** "'25% off', '¼ off', '0.25 of the price' — are these the same deal?"
- **Key activity:** Fraction → decimal: divide (¾ = 3 ÷ 4 = 0.75). Decimal → percentage: × 100 (0.75 = 75%). Percentage → fraction: over 100 and simplify. Memorise the common set (½, ¼, ¾, 1/10, 1/5, ⅛).
- **Check for understanding:** "Write ⅘ as a decimal and a percentage." → 0.8 and 80%.
- **Differentiation:** support: a divide-to-convert prompt (KB fix for "¾ = 0.34"). / stretch: convert ⅛ all three ways.
- **Materials:** FDP triple cards, mini-whiteboards.
- **Joy:** "three names, one deal" matching game.

### Lesson 12 — Percentage of an amount
- **Duration:** 26 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can find a percentage of an amount using building blocks or a fraction." (Y6 fractions, decimals & percentages)
- **Hook:** "60% of 35 marks — what's the score?"
- **Key activity:** 10% of 35 = 3.5, × 6 = 21. Or convert to a fraction. Check against benchmarks (½, ¼).
- **Check for understanding:** "Find 75% of 40." → 30.
- **Differentiation:** support: build from 10% and 1% (KB fix for "÷ by the percent"). / stretch: a reverse problem ("15 is 25% of what?").
- **Materials:** mini-whiteboards, 100-square.
- **Joy:** work out real test-score percentages.

**End-of-unit check:** (1) ⅘ as a decimal and percentage → 0.8, 80%. (2) What fraction is 40%? → ⅖. (3) Find 75% of 40 → 30. (4) ⅗ as a percentage → 60%.

## Unit 5 — Ratio and proportion

**Essential question:** How do we compare quantities with a ratio and scale things up or down fairly?

**Key vocabulary:** ratio, for every, parts, total parts, one part, share in a ratio, proportion,
scale, part-to-part, part-to-whole.

### Lesson 13 — Understanding ratio
- **Duration:** 26 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can read a ratio as 'for every… there are…'." (Y6 ratio & proportion)
- **Hook:** "Squash mixed 1 part juice to 4 parts water — how much water for 1 cup of juice?"
- **Key activity:** Concrete: for every 3 red counters, put 2 blue (3 : 2). Distinguish part-to-part (3 : 2) from part-to-whole (3 out of 5).
- **Check for understanding:** "Squash is 1 : 4 juice to water; for 1 cup of juice, how much water?" → 4 cups.
- **Differentiation:** support: lay out the counters in the ratio (KB fix for confusing with a fraction). / stretch: express the ratio the other way round.
- **Materials:** two-colour counters, squash bottle props.
- **Joy:** mix a pretend drink to the right ratio.

### Lesson 14 — Sharing in a ratio
- **Duration:** 28 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can share a quantity in a given ratio." (Y6 ratio & proportion)
- **Hook:** "30 marbles split 2 : 3 between Amy and Ben — who gets how many?"
- **Key activity:** Add the parts (2 + 3 = 5); one part = total ÷ total parts (30 ÷ 5 = 6); multiply (Amy 2 × 6 = 12, Ben 3 × 6 = 18). Check the total.
- **Check for understanding:** "Share 15 in the ratio 2 : 3." → 6 and 9.
- **Differentiation:** support: find one part first (KB fix for "just split off 3 and 2"). / stretch: share in a three-way ratio.
- **Materials:** counters, sharing mats.
- **Joy:** deal out a "prize fund" in the right shares.

### Lesson 15 — Proportion and scaling
- **Duration:** 26 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can scale recipes and prices proportionally." (Y6 ratio & proportion)
- **Hook:** "2 apples cost 60p — how much for 6?"
- **Key activity:** Scale both quantities by the same factor (×3 → 6 apples cost £1.80). Find a unit value first (1 apple = 30p), then scale. Apply to recipes.
- **Check for understanding:** "If 3 pens cost 90p, how much for 5?" → £1.50 (one pen 30p).
- **Differentiation:** support: find one unit first, then multiply. / stretch: scale a recipe down by a fraction.
- **Materials:** price/recipe cards, mini-whiteboards.
- **Joy:** scale a real recipe up for a party.

**End-of-unit check:** (1) Share 15 in ratio 2 : 3 → 6 and 9. (2) Squash 1 : 4, 1 cup juice → 4 cups water. (3) 3 pens cost 90p, 5 pens → £1.50. (4) Share 30 in 2 : 3 → 12 and 18.

## Unit 6 — Algebra

**Essential question:** How do we use letters to write rules and solve for an unknown?

**Key vocabulary:** variable, unknown, expression, formula, substitute, equation, balance, both
sides, one-step, two-step, sequence, rule, enumerate.

### Lesson 16 — Expressions and substitution
- **Duration:** 26 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can substitute a value into a formula or expression." (Y6 algebra)
- **Hook:** "If a = 3, what is 2a + 1? (Hint: 2a means 2 × a.)"
- **Key activity:** A letter is a number we don't know yet; 2a = 2 × a. Substitute: a = 3 → 2a + 1 = 7. Use a real formula (cost = 5 × items + 2).
- **Check for understanding:** "If y = 4, what is 3y + 2?" → 14.
- **Differentiation:** support: rewrite 3y as 3 × y first (KB fix for "2a = twenty-something"). / stretch: substitute into 2(l + w).
- **Materials:** expression cards, mini-whiteboards.
- **Joy:** plug numbers into a "cost machine" formula.

### Lesson 17 — Solving one-step equations
- **Duration:** 26 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can solve a one-step equation by balancing both sides." (Y6 algebra)
- **Hook:** "x + 5 = 12 — what is x, and how do we keep the scales level?"
- **Key activity:** Do the same to both sides to undo: x + 5 = 12 → subtract 5 both sides → x = 7. Model with a balance.
- **Check for understanding:** "Solve x − 7 = 10." → x = 17.
- **Differentiation:** support: a balance picture — same to both sides (KB fix for one-sided change). / stretch: a multiplication one-step (4x = 20).
- **Materials:** balance scales/picture, equation cards.
- **Joy:** keep the see-saw level as you solve.

### Lesson 18 — Solving two-step equations
- **Duration:** 28 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can solve a two-step equation." (Y6 algebra)
- **Hook:** "2x + 3 = 11 — undo the +3, then the ×2."
- **Key activity:** Subtract 3 both sides (2x = 8), then ÷2 (x = 4). Check by substituting back. Solve 3n − 4 = 11 → n = 5.
- **Check for understanding:** "Solve 2x + 1 = 9." → x = 4.
- **Differentiation:** support: undo operations in reverse order with prompts. / stretch: 3n − 4 = 11.
- **Materials:** balance picture, equation cards.
- **Joy:** "undo the machine" two steps back to the start.

### Lesson 19 — Sequences and enumerating possibilities
- **Duration:** 24 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can continue a sequence by its rule and list value-pairs." (Y6 algebra)
- **Hook:** "Matchstick patterns grow by the same amount each time — what's the rule?"
- **Key activity:** Spot the rule (+3 each time) and continue. Enumerate pairs that fit a + b = 10 → (1,9), (2,8)…
- **Check for understanding:** "Continue 2, 5, 8, … and state the rule." → 11, 14; rule +3.
- **Differentiation:** support: write the step between terms. / stretch: find the nth term informally.
- **Materials:** matchsticks/tiles, sequence cards.
- **Joy:** build a growing matchstick pattern.

### Lesson 20 — Retrieval & fluency: algebra
- **Duration:** 22 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can substitute, solve and continue sequences at speed." (Y6 algebra — review)
- **Hook:** "Algebra quick-fire — substitute, solve, sequence."
- **Key activity:** Mixed: "3y + 2 when y = 4", "x − 7 = 10", "2x + 1 = 9", "next in 2, 5, 8…".
- **Check for understanding:** "Solve 2x + 1 = 9." → x = 4.
- **Differentiation:** support: balance picture available. / stretch: timed, no tools.
- **Materials:** mini-whiteboards, equation cards.
- **Joy:** streak counter.

**End-of-unit check:** (1) 3y + 2 when y = 4 → 14. (2) x − 7 = 10 → 17. (3) 2x + 1 = 9 → 4. (4) Next in 2, 5, 8, … → 11 (rule +3).

## Unit 7 — Area, perimeter, volume and circles/nets

**Essential question:** How do we find areas with formulae, and what are the parts of a circle and a net?

**Key vocabulary:** triangle area ½ × base × height, parallelogram, perpendicular height, cuboid
volume, convert units, radius, diameter, circumference, net.

### Lesson 21 — Area of triangles and parallelograms
- **Duration:** 28 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can find the area of a triangle and a parallelogram with formulae." (Y6 measurement)
- **Hook:** "A triangle is just half a rectangle — so what's its area?"
- **Key activity:** Cut a rectangle diagonally → two equal triangles, so area = ½ × base × height. A parallelogram = base × height (slide the end triangle across). Use the *perpendicular* height.
- **Check for understanding:** "Area of a triangle, base 10 cm, height 6 cm?" → 30 cm².
- **Differentiation:** support: two triangle copies make a rectangle (KB fix for forgetting ½). / stretch: a parallelogram with a slanted side given (use perpendicular height).
- **Materials:** paper rectangles, scissors, grid paper.
- **Joy:** cut a rectangle in half to "discover" the triangle formula.

### Lesson 22 — Volume of cuboids and unit conversion
- **Duration:** 26 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can find cuboid volume and convert between units." (Y6 measurement)
- **Hook:** "Will these items fit in the packing box? Find its volume."
- **Key activity:** Volume = l × w × h (5 × 4 × 3 = 60 cm³). Convert: 1 m = 100 cm, 1 l = 1000 ml, 1 cm² = 100 mm². Choose sensible units.
- **Check for understanding:** "Volume of a 2 × 3 × 4 cm cuboid?" → 24 cm³. "ml in 1.5 litres?" → 1500 ml.
- **Differentiation:** support: build the cuboid from cubes. / stretch: convert an area unit (cm² to mm²).
- **Materials:** cm cubes, boxes, conversion table.
- **Joy:** check whether real items fit a box by volume.

### Lesson 23 — Circles, angle facts and nets
- **Duration:** 26 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can name circle parts, use angle facts, and match nets to solids." (Y6 geometry)
- **Hook:** "The diameter is always twice the radius — and a triangle's angles always make 180°."
- **Key activity:** Radius = centre to edge; diameter = across through centre = 2 × radius; circumference = perimeter. Triangle angles sum to 180° (tear the corners onto a straight line); vertically opposite angles equal. Match a cube net (6 squares) to its solid.
- **Check for understanding:** "A circle's diameter is 10 cm — what's the radius?" → 5 cm. "Triangle angles 70° and 60° — the third?" → 50°.
- **Differentiation:** support: tear a paper triangle's corners onto a line (KB fix for "360°"). / stretch: find a missing angle at crossing lines.
- **Materials:** circles, paper triangles, net cards, solids.
- **Joy:** fold a net into a 3-D shape.

### Lesson 24 — Retrieval & fluency: measurement and geometry
- **Duration:** 22 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can quickly use area/volume formulae and circle/angle facts." (Y6 — review)
- **Hook:** "Shape quick-fire — formulae and facts together."
- **Key activity:** Mixed: "triangle area base 8, height 5", "volume 2×3×4", "radius if diameter 10", "third angle of a triangle (90°, 45°)".
- **Check for understanding:** "Area of a triangle, base 8, height 5?" → 20 cm².
- **Differentiation:** support: formula card available. / stretch: timed, no tools.
- **Materials:** mini-whiteboards, formula cards.
- **Joy:** streak counter.

**End-of-unit check:** (1) Triangle area, base 10, height 6 → 30 cm². (2) Volume of 2×3×4 → 24 cm³. (3) Diameter 10 cm → radius 5 cm. (4) Triangle angles 90°, 45° → third 45°.

## Unit 8 — Coordinates and statistics

**Essential question:** How do we plot points in all four quadrants, and how do we read pie charts and find the mean?

**Key vocabulary:** quadrant, positive, negative, plot, reflect across an axis, pie chart, slice,
360°, proportion, mean, average.

### Lesson 25 — Four-quadrant coordinates
- **Duration:** 26 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can plot and read points in all four quadrants." (Y6 geometry — position)
- **Hook:** "Now the grid goes negative — where is (−3, 2)?"
- **Key activity:** Extend both axes through zero into four quadrants. Plot (−3, 2): 3 left, 2 up. Reflect: across the y-axis flips x sign; across the x-axis flips y sign.
- **Check for understanding:** "Which quadrant is (−4, −1) in?" → bottom-left (both negative).
- **Differentiation:** support: negative x means go *left* (KB fix for ignoring the minus). / stretch: find a missing vertex of a shape.
- **Materials:** four-quadrant grids.
- **Joy:** "explore the negative zone" of the grid.

### Lesson 26 — Reflection across the axes
- **Duration:** 24 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can reflect points across the x- and y-axes, including across zero." (Y6 geometry — position)
- **Hook:** "Reflecting across the y-axis — which coordinate changes sign?"
- **Key activity:** Reflect (3, 2) across the y-axis → flip x → (−3, 2); across the x-axis → flip y → (3, −2). Reflect (4, −1) across the x-axis → (4, 1).
- **Check for understanding:** "Reflect (4, −1) across the x-axis." → (4, 1).
- **Differentiation:** support: y-axis is vertical → flip the x sign (KB fix for flipping wrong coordinate). / stretch: reflect a whole shape across an axis.
- **Materials:** four-quadrant grids, mirrors.
- **Joy:** mirror a shape into the opposite quadrant.

### Lesson 27 — Pie charts
- **Duration:** 26 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can read a pie chart using fractions, percentages or angles." (Y6 statistics)
- **Hook:** "Half the pie is 'football' — how many children is that?"
- **Key activity:** The whole circle = 360° = 100%. A slice that's ¼ = 90° = 25%. To find a count, take the slice's fraction/percentage of the total (50% of 20 = 10).
- **Check for understanding:** "A pie-chart slice is 25% of 40 people — how many?" → 10.
- **Differentiation:** support: a slice is a *fraction of the total* (KB fix for reading it as a count). / stretch: convert an angle slice (90°) to a count.
- **Materials:** pie-chart cards, protractor.
- **Joy:** read a "how I spend my day" pie chart.

### Lesson 28 — The mean (average)
- **Duration:** 24 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can calculate the mean of a data set." (Y6 statistics)
- **Hook:** "Four scores, one 'typical' score — how do we even it out?"
- **Key activity:** Mean = total ÷ number of values. Scores 4, 6, 8, 6 → 24 ÷ 4 = 6. Concrete: share the total equally to picture the mean.
- **Check for understanding:** "Find the mean of 3, 7, 5." → 5 (15 ÷ 3).
- **Differentiation:** support: add first, then divide by how many (KB fix for wrong count). / stretch: compare two data sets by their means.
- **Materials:** counters, data cards.
- **Joy:** "even out the towers" — level cube towers to the mean height.

**End-of-unit check:** (1) Quadrant of (−4, −1) → bottom-left. (2) Reflect (4, −1) across the x-axis → (4, 1). (3) Slice 25% of 40 → 10. (4) Mean of 3, 7, 5 → 5.

## Unit 9 — Year-end consolidation (SATs readiness)

**Essential question:** Can I combine all of primary maths to reason, solve and check under exam conditions?

**Key vocabulary:** (all year vocabulary revisited) BIDMAS, long division, ratio, algebra,
formula, pie chart, mean, four quadrants.

### Lesson 29 — Reasoning: number, calculation and fractions
- **Duration:** 30 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can solve multi-step number and fraction problems and check my work." (Y6 — review)
- **Hook:** "Multiple operations, one answer — and BIDMAS decides the order."
- **Key activity:** Multi-step problems with the four operations, fractions and BIDMAS; estimate, solve, check by inverse. e.g. "234 × 23, then 25% of it."
- **Check for understanding:** "20 − 6 ÷ 2, then double it?" → 34.
- **Differentiation:** support: one step at a time with prompts. / stretch: a four-step problem.
- **Materials:** problem cards, columned paper.
- **Joy:** crack a chained reasoning puzzle.

### Lesson 30 — Reasoning: ratio, algebra and measure
- **Duration:** 30 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can solve problems mixing ratio, algebra and measurement." (Y6 — review)
- **Hook:** "Share a budget, solve for an unknown, and find an area — all in one task."
- **Key activity:** "Share £30 in ratio 2 : 3"; "solve 2x + 1 = 9"; "area of a triangle base 8, height 5". Combine in one scenario.
- **Check for understanding:** "Share 30 in ratio 2 : 3, then find ½ of the bigger share?" → 18 and 12; ½ of 18 = 9.
- **Differentiation:** support: tackle one concept at a time. / stretch: a problem needing all three.
- **Materials:** problem cards, counters, formula card.
- **Joy:** run a "mini project" using several skills.

### Lesson 31 — Reasoning: geometry, position and data
- **Duration:** 28 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can solve problems mixing coordinates, shape facts and statistics." (Y6 — review)
- **Hook:** "Plot, reflect, find an angle, and read a pie chart in one go."
- **Key activity:** "Plot and reflect a point across the y-axis"; "third angle of a triangle"; "read a pie-chart slice"; "find a mean". Combine in one scenario.
- **Check for understanding:** "Triangle angles 70° and 60° — the third?" → 50°.
- **Differentiation:** support: one concept at a time. / stretch: a multi-concept problem.
- **Materials:** grids, protractor, pie-chart card.
- **Joy:** solve a mixed "geometry detective" case.

## End-of-year mastery checks

Spanning the whole year — a child secure in Year 6 maths (and ready to leave primary school) can:

1. **Place value & BIDMAS:** read/round numbers to 10,000,000 and apply order of operations (3 + 4 × 2 → 11; 5 + 2 × 3² → 23).
2. **Written methods:** long-multiply (234 × 23 → 5,382) and long-divide (528 ÷ 12 → 44), expressing remainders as fractions or decimals.
3. **Four operations with fractions:** ¾ + ⅙ → 11/12; ⅔ × ¾ → ½; ⅓ ÷ 2 → 1/6.
4. **Fraction–decimal–percentage:** convert freely (⅘ → 0.8 → 80%) and find percentages of amounts (75% of 40 → 30).
5. **Ratio & proportion:** share in a ratio (15 in 2 : 3 → 6 and 9) and scale proportionally (3 pens 90p → 5 for £1.50).
6. **Algebra:** substitute (3y + 2, y = 4 → 14), solve one- and two-step equations (2x + 1 = 9 → 4), and continue a sequence.
7. **Measurement & geometry:** triangle area (base 10, height 6 → 30 cm²), cuboid volume (24 cm³), circle parts (diameter 10 → radius 5), and triangle angle sum (→ third angle).
8. **Position & statistics:** plot/reflect in four quadrants ((4,−1) → (4,1)), read pie charts (25% of 40 → 10), and find the mean (3, 7, 5 → 5).
