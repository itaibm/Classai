## Year overview

Life Skills in the graduation year is where the charter's **vision of the graduate** comes together:
*resilient and self-directed*, *a kind, globally-minded citizen*, *a confident communicator*, *a
curious inquirer* and *a creative maker*. Children deepen their understanding of **who they are**
(values, strengths, goals), **how to manage themselves** (emotions, setbacks, stress), **how to relate
to others** (listening, assertiveness, conflict, healthy friendships online and offline), **how money
works** (interest, credit, pay and tax, enterprise) and **how to act in the world** (rights,
sustainability, democracy, service). Three lessons prepare them practically for **secondary school**.

The year culminates in the **Graduation Capstone: the Change-Maker Project**, a cross-subject project in
which every child (alone or in a small team) identifies a real problem in their community, researches
it, gathers data, designs and carries out an action, and presents it at the **graduation exhibition**.
Life Skills is the hub; the work draws on English (research, persuasive speech — Units 4–5 and 9),
Maths (survey statistics and budgets — Units 12–13), Geography (fieldwork and the SDGs — Units 5 and 7),
Computing (data analysis and the capstone program — Units 4 and 7), Art & Design (poster and exhibition —
Units 6–7), Science (investigation — Unit 9), History (oral history — Unit 6) and Music and PE (the
graduation concert and sports festival).

**What we assume from Years 4 and 5:** an identity map, character strengths, growth-mindset language,
conflict steps, "I" statements and SMART goals (Year 4); identity across groups and influences, values
dilemmas and precise emotion words; changing friendships, group chats, dares and risk, bullying and the
upstander toolkit, disagreeing well and personal safety; performance nerves, problem-solving, change and
loss, and helping a struggling friend (Year 5); simple interest, borrowing and a market stall (Year 4);
bank accounts, scams, unit pricing, tax and a class event budget (Year 5); key children's rights,
stereotypes and service (Year 4); a behaviour-change campaign measured with data, the
stereotype–prejudice–discrimination chain, the social model of disability, a class election and a letter
to a decision-maker (Year 5). Puberty, mental-health basics and first aid are taught in PE & Health.
**What Year 6 adds:** values and purpose as a compass; the 24 VIA character strengths; growth mindset
done right; WOOP goals and habit design; the neuroscience of emotions, thinking traps and
self-compassion; deeper listening, assertiveness in hard situations, mediation, consent and boundaries,
and the bystander effect; compound interest, credit, payslips and a mini-business with break-even;
rights in tension, how laws are made, sustainable consumption and carbon footprints; secondary-school
readiness; and a full, self-directed change-making project. One lesson a week, 35–45 minutes.

## Unit 1 — Who I am and who I want to become

**Essential question:** What do I value, what are my strengths, and how do I become the person I want to be?

**Key vocabulary:** identity, values, character strengths, integrity, growth mindset, metacognition, goal, SMART goal, WOOP (wish, outcome, obstacle, plan), habit, role model, purpose

### Lesson 1 — My values and my purpose
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can choose three core values, explain how they have shaped a real decision, and name an issue I care about enough to act on." (CASEL self-awareness; PSHE KS3 Health and wellbeing)
- **Hook:** Open the sealed letters written at the end of Year 5 Lesson 16: "What did your younger self hope for — and what has changed?"
- **Key activity:** Brief retrieval of the Year 4 identity map and Year 5 values dilemmas; a values card sort (e.g. kindness, honesty, courage, fairness, curiosity, family, freedom, creativity) narrowed to three, with evidence of each from the past year; "purpose" — what problems in the world make you care enough to act? Start a list of issues that will seed the Change-Maker capstone (Lesson 27).
- **Check for understanding:** "Describe a real decision this year where one of your values guided you — and one where you acted against it. What was different?" → Two specific situations with honest reflection on what helped or got in the way (e.g. pressure, tiredness, wanting to fit in).
- **Differentiation:** support: picture-supported value cards and a sentence frame / stretch: explain how your values connect to the issue you care about most
- **Materials:** identity-map templates, values cards
- **Joy:** "Identity suitcase" art piece

### Lesson 2 — Character strengths
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can identify my top character strengths and explain how to use them in school and friendships." (CASEL self-awareness; PSHE KS3)
- **Hook:** "What are you like at your best?"
- **Key activity:** Explore the 24 character strengths from the VIA classification (e.g. curiosity, perseverance, kindness, humour, leadership, fairness); children identify their top five (with family or friend input); "strength spotting" in peers and in characters from English texts (Stanley, Jonas, Salva).
- **Check for understanding:** "Name one of your strengths and give an example of using it this week." → A specific strength with a concrete example.
- **Differentiation:** support: a strengths card set with pictures / stretch: choose a "stretch strength" to develop and plan how
- **Materials:** strengths cards (based on the VIA classification), reflection journals
- **Joy:** "Strength-spotting" notes to classmates

### Lesson 3 — Growth mindset done right: effort, strategy and help
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can explain why effort alone isn't enough, spot 'false growth mindset', and use plan–monitor–evaluate questions to change strategy when I'm stuck." (CASEL self-management; IB MYP ATL self-management)
- **Hook:** "'I tried really hard and still failed — so growth mindset is rubbish.' Is this person right?"
- **Key activity:** Retrieve Year 4 (the brain strengthens connections with practice; 'yet'). New: Carol Dweck's warning about 'false growth mindset' (praising effort that isn't working; saying 'just try harder'); the three ingredients — effort, effective strategies and help from others; the 'learning pit' idea that feeling stuck is part of learning; apply plan–monitor–evaluate questions to a real piece of work and change one strategy.
- **Check for understanding:** "A friend has practised the same times-table the same way for two weeks and still gets it wrong. Using growth mindset properly, what would you advise?" → Not just 'try harder': change the strategy (e.g. self-testing, spotting patterns, a different method) and ask for help — then check whether it's working.
- **Differentiation:** support: a question card for each stage of the cycle / stretch: explain how a teacher's praise can accidentally encourage a fixed mindset
- **Materials:** metacognition prompt cards, 'learning pit' diagram, journals
- **Joy:** "Strategy swap" — each pupil shares one technique that got them out of the pit

### Lesson 4 — Setting goals that work: WOOP
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can set a meaningful goal using WOOP (Wish, Outcome, Obstacle, Plan) and an if–then plan." (CASEL self-management; PSHE KS3)
- **Hook:** "Lots of New Year's resolutions are given up within weeks. Why — and what works better?"
- **Key activity:** Retrieve Year 4's SMART goals. New: teach WOOP (researched by psychologist Gabriele Oettingen) — why simply imagining success can make people less likely to act, while planning for the obstacle helps; each child writes a WOOP for a real goal this term (learning, health, friendship or a skill) with an "if [obstacle], then I will [action]" plan; compare with SMART goals.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why does thinking about the obstacle make you more likely to succeed?" → You plan ahead for what will get in the way, so when it happens you already know what to do.
- **Differentiation:** support: a WOOP template with an example / stretch: set a long-term goal with milestones
- **Materials:** WOOP templates, journals
- **Joy:** A "goal buddy" to check in with each month

### Lesson 5 — Habits and role models
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can explain how habits form (cue, routine, reward) and design one new habit, drawing inspiration from a role model." (CASEL self-management; PSHE KS3)
- **Hook:** "About how much of what you do each day is habit rather than a decision?"
- **Key activity:** The habit loop; design a small daily habit linked to the WOOP goal (e.g. "after dinner, I read for 20 minutes"); research a role model from any field or culture and one habit that helped them (e.g. practice routines of athletes, musicians, scientists).
- **Check for understanding:** "Design a habit using 'After I…, I will…' and explain the cue." → e.g. "After I brush my teeth, I will pack my bag" — brushing teeth is the cue.
- **Differentiation:** support: a habit-planning frame / stretch: design a habit tracker and plan what to do on a day you miss it
- **Materials:** habit trackers, role-model research sheets
- **Joy:** 30-day habit challenge

**End-of-unit check:** A personal "compass" page: three values, top strengths, a WOOP goal with an if–then plan and a habit tracker, discussed with the teacher in a short conference.

## Unit 2 — Resilience and emotional intelligence

**Essential question:** How can I understand and manage my emotions, bounce back from setbacks and understand others' feelings?

**Key vocabulary:** emotion, emotional intelligence, amygdala, prefrontal cortex, "flipping the lid", trigger, self-regulation, reframing, resilience, setback, perfectionism, self-compassion, realistic optimism, empathy, perspective-taking, compassion

### Lesson 6 — My brain and my emotions
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can explain how strong emotions affect thinking (using the hand model of the brain) and name strategies to calm down." (CASEL self-awareness and self-management; PSHE KS3)
- **Hook:** "Have you ever done something when you were angry that you'd never do when calm? Why?"
- **Key activity:** Retrieve Year 5's precise emotion words and the body's alarm response. New: Dr Dan Siegel's hand model of the brain (a simplified model: the "lid" of the thinking brain flips when the alarm system — including the amygdala — takes over); triggers and body signals; calming strategies (breathing, movement, naming the emotion — "name it to tame it"); personal calm plan.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why is it hard to make good decisions when you've 'flipped your lid'?" → The alarm part of the brain takes over and the thinking part (prefrontal cortex) is less able to plan and control actions until you calm down.
- **Differentiation:** support: an emotions vocabulary chart with faces / stretch: explain why naming an emotion helps calm it
- **Materials:** hand-model diagram, emotions vocabulary chart, calm-plan templates
- **Joy:** "Name it to tame it" emotion vocabulary challenge (how many feeling words do you know?)

### Lesson 7 — Reframing and self-talk
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can notice unhelpful thoughts and reframe them into more balanced, helpful ones." (CASEL self-management; PSHE KS3)
- **Hook:** "'I got 5 out of 20 — I'm useless at maths.' Is that thought true, helpful, or kind?"
- **Key activity:** Common thinking traps (all-or-nothing, catastrophising, mind-reading); a thought-reframe table (situation → first thought → evidence → balanced thought); practise with scenarios and one's own examples.
- **Check for understanding:** "Reframe: 'Nobody likes me because no one sat with me at lunch.'" → e.g. "Today no one sat with me, but my friends were at a club; tomorrow I'll ask someone to sit together."
- **Differentiation:** support: choose the balanced thought from options / stretch: identify the thinking trap in each scenario
- **Materials:** thinking-trap cards, reframe tables
- **Joy:** "Thought detective" role play

### Lesson 8 — Bouncing back from failure
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can describe how people bounce back from setbacks and plan how I will respond to my own." (CASEL self-management; PSHE KS3)
- **Hook:** Famous failures: J. K. Rowling's first Harry Potter book was rejected by many publishers; James Dyson made thousands of prototypes before his first vacuum cleaner worked.
- **Key activity:** Stories of setbacks and resilience from around the world (including Salva Dut from English Unit 5); the resilience toolkit (support network, perspective, problem-solving, self-care, humour); write a "comeback plan" for a recent setback.
- **Check for understanding:** "What is one thing that helps people bounce back, and how could you use it?" → A named factor (e.g. asking for help, breaking the problem into steps) with a personal application.
- **Differentiation:** support: a comeback-plan frame / stretch: interview an adult about a setback they overcame
- **Materials:** story cards, comeback-plan templates
- **Joy:** "Failure wall" — celebrating mistakes that taught us something

### Lesson 9 — Self-compassion, perfectionism and realistic optimism
- **Duration:** 35 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can tell high standards from perfectionism, speak to myself with self-compassion after a mistake, and explain realistic optimism." (CASEL self-awareness and self-management; PSHE KS3)
- **Hook:** "You got 19 out of 20. Do you celebrate the 19 — or think about the one you got wrong all evening?"
- **Key activity:** High standards (aiming high, learning from mistakes) vs perfectionism (fear of any mistake, not starting or giving up, harsh self-criticism); self-compassion — treat yourself as you would a good friend: notice it's hard, remember everyone struggles, speak kindly; realistic optimism vs toxic positivity (it's OK not to be OK); retrieve Year 4's gratitude practice as one tool among several.
- **Check for understanding:** "Priya re-starts her poster five times because it isn't 'perfect' and misses the deadline. Is this high standards or perfectionism? What would a self-compassionate response sound like?" → Perfectionism: fear of mistakes stops her finishing; e.g. "It doesn't have to be perfect to be good — I'll finish it, then improve one thing."
- **Differentiation:** support: sort statements into 'high standards' and 'perfectionism' / stretch: explain why harsh self-criticism can make people perform worse, not better
- **Materials:** statement cards, journals
- **Joy:** "Kind coach" — write the note a kind coach would give you after a mistake

### Lesson 10 — Empathy and perspective-taking
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can take another person's perspective and respond with empathy and compassion." (CASEL social awareness; PSHE KS3)
- **Hook:** "Margot in 'All Summer in a Day' — how might the story change if one child had shown empathy?"
- **Key activity:** Empathy vs sympathy; perspective-taking with scenarios and characters from English texts; empathic responses (listen, acknowledge, ask what would help); unit review.
- **Check for understanding:** "What is the difference between empathy and sympathy?" → Empathy is feeling *with* someone — understanding their feelings from their point of view; sympathy is feeling sorry *for* them from the outside.
- **Differentiation:** support: response phrase cards / stretch: analyse how a conflict looks from three perspectives
- **Materials:** scenario cards, English class texts
- **Joy:** "Walk in my shoes" drama activity

**End-of-unit check:** A personal calm plan, a reframed thought and a comeback plan, plus a scenario discussion showing empathic perspective-taking.

## Unit 3 — Relationships and communication

**Essential question:** How can I build healthy relationships and handle conflict respectfully, face to face and online?

**Key vocabulary:** active listening, assertive, passive, aggressive, "I" statement, DESC script, conflict resolution, compromise, mediation, healthy relationship, boundary, consent, respect, peer pressure, bullying, cyberbullying, bystander, bystander effect, upstander

### Lesson 11 — Active listening
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can use active-listening skills (attention, open questions, paraphrasing, not interrupting) in a conversation." (CASEL relationship skills; PSHE KS3)
- **Hook:** "Tell your partner about your weekend while they look at their phone. How did it feel?"
- **Key activity:** Contrast poor and good listening; teach active listening (eye contact, open body language, open questions, paraphrasing "So you're saying…"); practise in threes (speaker, listener, observer).
- **Check for understanding:** "Why does paraphrasing help a conversation?" → It shows you understood and gives the speaker a chance to correct any misunderstanding.
- **Differentiation:** support: a listening checklist / stretch: summarise a speaker's feelings as well as their words
- **Materials:** listening checklists, conversation topic cards
- **Joy:** "Listening challenge" — can you repeat back three details?

### Lesson 12 — Assertiveness in hard situations
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can stay assertive in harder situations — with older pupils, with adults and when standing up for someone else — using the DESC script." (CASEL relationship skills; PSHE KS3)
- **Hook:** "It's easy to say 'I feel…' to a friend. What about to an older pupil who pushes in, or an adult who has got something wrong about you?"
- **Key activity:** Quick retrieval of passive, aggressive and assertive styles and "I" statements (Year 4). New: the DESC script — **D**escribe what happened, **E**xpress how you feel, **S**pecify what you'd like, **C**onsequence (the positive result); the 'broken record' technique for pushback; respectful assertiveness with adults; speaking up for someone else; role-play in threes with an observer.
- **Check for understanding:** "A teacher blames you for a mess you didn't make. Write a DESC response that is assertive but respectful." → e.g. "I know the table's messy (D). I feel upset because I didn't do it (E). Could I explain what happened? (S) Then we can sort out who should tidy it (C)."
- **Differentiation:** support: a DESC frame with sentence starters / stretch: stay assertive while the other person pushes back twice
- **Materials:** scenario cards, DESC frames
- **Joy:** Role-play "remix" — replay the scene assertively

### Lesson 13 — Resolving conflict
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can use a step-by-step conflict-resolution process and act as a peer mediator." (CASEL relationship skills and responsible decision-making; PSHE KS3)
- **Hook:** "Two friends both want to lead the group project. Is there a win–win?"
- **Key activity:** Steps: cool down, each side tells their story (without interruption), identify needs, brainstorm solutions, agree and check back; practise mediation in threes; compromise vs win–win.
- **Check for understanding:** "What is the difference between a compromise and a win–win solution?" → In a compromise each side gives something up; in a win–win both sides' needs are fully met in a new way.
- **Differentiation:** support: a step card with prompts / stretch: mediate a three-person conflict
- **Materials:** conflict-resolution step cards, scenario cards
- **Joy:** "Mediator badge"

### Lesson 14 — Healthy relationships, boundaries and consent
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can describe features of healthy and unhealthy friendships and relationships, set boundaries and explain consent in everyday situations (age-appropriate)." (CASEL relationship skills; PSHE KS3 Relationships)
- **Hook:** "A friend says 'Real friends share passwords.' Is that true?"
- **Key activity:** Healthy vs unhealthy relationship features (respect, trust, honesty vs control, jealousy, pressure) — online and offline; personal boundaries (body, time, belongings, information); consent as asking and respecting "no" in everyday contexts (hugs, borrowing, sharing photos); where to get help (taught in line with the family's or school's RSE policy).
- **Check for understanding:** "Why should you always ask before posting a photo of a friend?" → It's their image and their choice; posting without consent ignores their boundaries and can't easily be undone.
- **Differentiation:** support: a sorting activity (healthy/unhealthy) with pictures / stretch: write advice for a friend in an unhealthy friendship
- **Materials:** scenario cards, sorting cards, help-line information; safety: calm, respectful classroom agreement; parents informed; safeguarding procedures
- **Joy:** "Friendship recipe" — ingredients for a healthy friendship

### Lesson 15 — Bullying, cyberbullying and being an upstander
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can recognise bullying and cyberbullying, and use safe upstander strategies." (CASEL social awareness and responsible decision-making; PSHE KS3)
- **Hook:** "What's the difference between banter, conflict and bullying?"
- **Key activity:** Quick retrieval of Year 5's definition (repeated, intentional, imbalance of power) and upstander actions. New: 'banter' vs bullying (it stops being a joke when the target isn't laughing or can't say stop); prejudice-based bullying (about race, religion, disability, sexuality or gender) and why schools record it; online forms (fake accounts, image-sharing, pile-ons); the bystander effect — the more people who watch, the less likely any one person is to help (diffusion of responsibility); how to overcome it; unit review.
- **Check for understanding:** "Thirty people saw a cruel comment in a group chat and nobody did anything. Why does that happen, and what could one person do to break the pattern?" → Each person assumes someone else will act (diffusion of responsibility); one person can act first — not reacting, supporting the target privately, reporting, saving evidence and telling an adult — which makes it easier for others to follow.
- **Differentiation:** support: scenario cards with choices / stretch: design a class anti-bullying charter that tackles the bystander effect
- **Materials:** scenario cards, charter template, reporting information
- **Joy:** Class upstander pledge

**End-of-unit check:** A role-play demonstrating active listening, an assertive "I" statement and conflict-resolution steps; a correct distinction between healthy and unhealthy relationship behaviours; and three upstander strategies.

## Unit 4 — Money, work and enterprise

**Essential question:** How can I make, save, spend and grow money wisely — and use it to make a difference?

**Key vocabulary:** income, expenses, budget, saving, interest, simple interest, compound interest, bank account, debit card, credit, loan, debt, APR, gross pay, net pay, payslip, deduction, pension, profit, loss, revenue, cost, entrepreneur, enterprise, social enterprise

### Lesson 16 — Budgets for real life
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can create a monthly budget that balances income and expenses, including saving and giving." (National Standards for Personal Financial Education — spending and saving; PSHE KS3 Living in the wider world; Maths link)
- **Hook:** "You're 18 and have your first job. Can you make your wages last the month?"
- **Key activity:** A budget simulation for a young adult (income from a part-time job; rent share, food, transport, phone, fun, saving, giving); balance the budget; handle a surprise expense.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why should a budget include money for unexpected costs?" → Emergencies (a broken phone, a medical cost) happen; an emergency fund stops you from going into debt.
- **Differentiation:** support: a simplified budget with round numbers / stretch: a budget using percentages (e.g. 50% needs, 30% wants, 20% saving) and a comparison of two cities' costs
- **Materials:** budget templates, cost cards, calculators
- **Joy:** "Life happens" surprise-event cards

### Lesson 17 — Saving and the magic of compound interest
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can calculate simple and compound interest and explain why saving early matters." (National Standards for Personal Financial Education — saving and investing; Maths link — percentages)
- **Hook:** "Would you rather have 1,000 today, or 1 cent doubled every day for 30 days?" (On day 30 alone the pile is over 536 million cents — more than 5 million in whole units.)
- **Key activity:** Simple interest vs compound interest (100 at 10% a year: 110, 121, 133.10…); a spreadsheet showing savings growing over 10, 20 and 40 years; the "rule of 72" as a rough estimate for doubling time.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why does compound interest grow faster than simple interest?" → You earn interest on your interest as well as on the original amount, so each year's growth is bigger.
- **Differentiation:** support: a completed table to extend by one year / stretch: compare starting to save at 20 vs 30 with the same monthly amount
- **Materials:** calculators, spreadsheet software, compound-interest table
- **Joy:** The penny-doubling reveal

### Lesson 18 — Borrowing, credit and debt
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can explain how loans and credit cards work, what interest costs a borrower, and how to avoid problem debt." (National Standards for Personal Financial Education — managing credit; PSHE KS3)
- **Hook:** "Buy now, pay later. What's the catch?"
- **Key activity:** How borrowing works (the lender charges interest); compare the total cost of a phone bought outright vs on credit; "buy now, pay later" schemes; how debt can grow; good and bad reasons to borrow.
- **Check for understanding:** "A phone costs 300 cash or 30 a month for 12 months. Which is cheaper and by how much?" → Cash is cheaper: on credit you pay 12 × 30 = 360, which is 60 more than 300.
- **Differentiation:** support: a guided comparison table / stretch: calculate the effect of only paying the minimum on a credit card balance (simplified)
- **Materials:** comparison cards, calculators
- **Joy:** "Deal or no deal?" — spot the hidden costs

### Lesson 19 — Work, pay and tax: reading a payslip
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can read a simple payslip, calculate take-home pay from gross pay and deductions, and explain what the deductions pay for." (National Standards for Personal Financial Education — earning income; PSHE KS3 Living in the wider world; Maths link)
- **Hook:** "Your first job pays 2,000 a month. Why does only about 1,640 arrive in your bank account?"
- **Key activity:** Retrieve Year 5's taxes and public services lesson. New: a simplified payslip — gross pay, income tax, social-security contributions, pension saving, net ("take-home") pay; hourly pay, overtime and minimum wages (many countries set one); calculate net pay for several job cards; connect deductions to public services and to saving for later life; discuss why tax rates differ between countries.
- **Check for understanding:** "Gross pay 2,000; income tax 200; other contributions 100; pension 60. What is the take-home pay, and why is the pension deduction good for the worker?" → 2,000 − 360 = 1,640; the pension is the worker's own money saved (often with extra from the employer) for when they stop working.
- **Differentiation:** support: a payslip with round numbers and labelled boxes / stretch: compare take-home pay for an hourly job with and without overtime
- **Materials:** simplified payslips, job cards, calculators
- **Joy:** "Payday" — match each job card to its correct payslip

### Lesson 20 — Enterprise: planning a mini-business
- **Duration:** 45 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can plan a mini-enterprise with a product, costs, price, expected profit and a purpose (e.g. to fund the capstone project or a charity)." (National Standards for Personal Financial Education — earning income; PSHE KS3; Maths link)
- **Hook:** "With 20 of start-up money, how much could your team raise in two weeks?"
- **Key activity:** Teams choose a product or service (bake sale, upcycled crafts, a car wash, tutoring younger children in a game); market research (a quick survey); calculate costs, price and break-even; decide what the profit will fund; learn about social enterprises.
- **Check for understanding:** "If each item costs 0.80 to make and sells for 2.00, how many must you sell to cover 20 of costs?" → 1.20 profit each; 20 ÷ 1.20 = 16.7, so 17 items.
- **Differentiation:** support: a business-plan template with worked examples / stretch: include a pricing experiment or a marketing plan
- **Materials:** business-plan templates, calculators, survey sheets
- **Joy:** "Dragons' den" pitch to parents for start-up money

### Lesson 21 — Running and evaluating the enterprise
- **Duration:** 45 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can run the mini-enterprise, keep accurate records, calculate profit or loss and evaluate what went well." (National Standards for Personal Financial Education; PSHE KS3; IB MYP ATL)
- **Hook:** "Open for business!"
- **Key activity:** Run the enterprise (at a school event or a family market); record sales; calculate revenue, costs and profit; evaluate; donate profits to the chosen cause (or the capstone); unit check.
- **Check for understanding:** "What is the difference between revenue and profit?" → Revenue is all the money taken in; profit is what's left after subtracting costs.
- **Differentiation:** support: a sales tally sheet and profit calculator / stretch: a written evaluation with suggestions for a second run
- **Materials:** products, a float of coins, record sheets, calculators
- **Joy:** Handing over the profits to a good cause

**End-of-unit check:** A balanced budget with a saving goal; a compound-interest calculation and explanation; a comparison of cash vs credit costs; the enterprise records with profit calculation and evaluation.

## Unit 5 — Global citizenship and sustainability

**Essential question:** What are my rights and responsibilities as a citizen of my community and the world, and how can I help create change?

**Key vocabulary:** human rights, UDHR, children's rights, UNCRC, responsibility, rights clash, ombudsperson, democracy, vote, government, local council, petition, campaign, sustainability, consumerism, circular economy, reduce–reuse–recycle, carbon footprint, fast fashion, volunteering, service, social action

### Lesson 22 — Human rights in tension: who protects them?
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can explain how the UDHR (1948) and the UNCRC (1989) protect rights, reason through a case where two rights clash, and name who upholds rights." (UNESCO GCED; PSHE KS3 Living in the wider world; History link)
- **Hook:** "The UNCRC is the most widely ratified human-rights treaty in the world. So why are some children still not in school?"
- **Key activity:** Retrieve Year 4's key UNCRC articles and responsibilities (education — Article 28, to be heard — Article 12, to play — Article 31, protection from harm — Article 19). New: the UDHR (1948, from History) as the parent document; when rights clash (free expression vs protection from harm, privacy vs safety online, a parent's choices vs a child's right to be heard) — reason through cases in groups; who upholds rights (governments that ratify treaties, courts, children's commissioners or ombudspersons, charities, a free press); why rights on paper are not always rights in practice.
- **Check for understanding:** "A pupil posts a true but embarrassing story about a classmate and says 'It's free speech.' Which two rights clash, and how would you decide?" → Freedom of expression vs privacy and protection from harm; free speech has limits when it harms someone else, so the classmate's privacy and wellbeing should win here.
- **Differentiation:** support: picture-supported rights cards and a simpler clash (my right to play vs your right to learn) / stretch: research a child-rights issue in one country and the organisation working to fix it
- **Materials:** simplified UNCRC (UNICEF), UDHR summary, rights-clash case cards
- **Joy:** "Rights court" — groups judge a case and explain the verdict

### Lesson 23 — Democracy and how change happens
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can explain how decisions are made in my country and community and describe ways citizens (including young people) influence them." (UNESCO GCED; PSHE KS3; C3 D2.Civ)
- **Hook:** "At what age can you vote in your country? Some countries allow 16-year-olds to vote. Should we?"
- **Key activity:** Retrieve Year 5's class election and letter to a decision-maker. New: levels of government (local, national, international); how laws are made (in your own country — a worked example from idea to law); ways to influence (voting, petitions, contacting representatives, peaceful campaigns, youth councils); case studies of young change-makers (Malala Yousafzai; students in the Soweto uprising — History Unit 5; young climate campaigners).
- **Check for understanding:** "Name two ways a young person who can't vote can still influence a decision." → e.g. writing to a representative, starting a petition, joining a youth council, peaceful campaigning, raising awareness.
- **Differentiation:** support: a picture map of local government / stretch: compare how two countries' governments make decisions
- **Materials:** local government information, case-study cards
- **Joy:** A mock council meeting: pupils play councillors and residents debating a real local proposal (links to English speeches)

### Lesson 24 — Consumerism and the circular economy
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can explain the environmental and social costs of "take–make–waste" consumption and how a circular economy works." (UNESCO GCED; SDG 12; Geography link)
- **Hook:** "One estimate says a cotton T-shirt can take around 2,700 litres of water to make — roughly what one person drinks in two and a half years."
- **Key activity:** Trace the life cycle of a T-shirt (cotton growing, manufacture, transport, use, disposal); fast fashion; the circular economy (repair, reuse, share, recycle); audit one's own clothes or belongings; plan a swap shop.
- **Check for understanding:** "What is the difference between recycling and a circular economy?" → Recycling is one step; a circular economy designs products to be used longer, repaired, reused and then recycled so waste is avoided from the start.
- **Differentiation:** support: a life-cycle picture sequence / stretch: evaluate a company's sustainability claim (link to English — spotting spin)
- **Materials:** life-cycle cards, clothing labels, *The Story of Stuff* (short film)
- **Joy:** Class clothes/toy swap shop

### Lesson 25 — My carbon footprint and taking action
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can estimate my household's main sources of emissions and choose effective actions to reduce them." (UNESCO GCED; SDG 13; Geography and Science links)
- **Hook:** "Which saves more carbon: switching off lights, or eating one less beef meal a week?"
- **Key activity:** Use a child-friendly footprint calculator (transport, food, energy, stuff); rank actions by impact (not all actions are equal); plan a family "climate action" with measurable steps (links to Geography Unit 3).
- **Check for understanding:** "Why is it important to focus on the actions with the biggest impact?" → Time and effort are limited; big-impact actions (e.g. travel and diet choices, home heating) reduce far more emissions than small symbolic ones.
- **Differentiation:** support: a picture-based impact ranking / stretch: calculate an estimated saving over a year
- **Materials:** footprint calculator, impact-ranking cards
- **Joy:** Family climate pledge (continuing from Geography)

### Lesson 26 — Volunteering and service
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can explain the value of service to others and plan a volunteering action that meets a real need." (CASEL social awareness; UNESCO GCED; PSHE KS3)
- **Hook:** "What need exists within one kilometre of your home?"
- **Key activity:** Needs mapping in the local community (older people, the environment, animals, younger children, refugees); meet or hear from a local volunteer or charity; plan a service action (e.g. a litter pick, reading to younger children, a card-writing project for a care home).
- **Check for understanding:** "How can you make sure your help is what people actually need?" → Ask them or the organisation that serves them; listen first rather than assuming.
- **Differentiation:** support: choose from prepared service ideas / stretch: plan a service project with a partner organisation
- **Materials:** local community map, charity information
- **Joy:** Carrying out the service action

### Lesson 27 — Launching the Change-Maker Project
- **Duration:** 45 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can choose a real local or global issue for my capstone project and write a focused question and goal." (CASEL responsible decision-making; UNESCO GCED; IB MYP ATL research)
- **Hook:** "You've learned about rights, sustainability, money, data, persuasion and research. Now: change something."
- **Key activity:** Introduce the capstone (see Unit 7); brainstorm issues from the year (water, waste, a local park, loneliness among older people, a safer route to school, reading for younger children, wildlife); form teams or work alone; write a "How might we…?" question and an achievable goal; map which subjects will contribute (research in English, data in Maths and Computing, fieldwork in Geography, design in Art).
- **Check for understanding:** "Is your goal achievable in six weeks? How will you know if you've succeeded?" → A specific, realistic goal with a measure (e.g. "collect 200 signatures", "reduce classroom waste by a third").
- **Differentiation:** support: choose from a curated list of issues with guiding questions / stretch: an ambitious goal with a partner organisation
- **Materials:** capstone project booklet, issue cards, SDG icons
- **Joy:** Choosing your own cause

**End-of-unit check:** Reason through a case where two rights clash and name who upholds rights; describe two ways young people can influence decisions; explain the circular economy; rank climate actions by impact; a launched capstone plan with a question and goal.

## Unit 6 — Ready for secondary school

**Essential question:** How can I make the move to secondary school a confident, positive new start?

**Key vocabulary:** transition, timetable, planner, independence, organisation, study skills, retrieval practice, routine, worry, coping, new friendships

### Lesson 28 — Organisation and independence
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can use a planner and timetable to organise homework, equipment and deadlines independently." (CASEL self-management; PSHE KS3; IB MYP ATL self-management)
- **Hook:** A secondary-school timetable with ten subjects and a map of a big school: "Where do you go at 10:45 on Tuesday?"
- **Key activity:** Read a sample timetable and school map; plan a week of homework with a planner; pack a bag from a timetable; practise journey planning (safe routes, public transport, what to do if something goes wrong).
- **Check for understanding:** "You have three pieces of homework due Monday, Wednesday and Friday. How would you plan them?" → Spread them out, starting with the earliest deadline, breaking big tasks into steps.
- **Differentiation:** support: a planner template with example entries / stretch: plan for a clash (two deadlines and a sports event on the same day)
- **Materials:** sample timetables, school maps, planners
- **Joy:** "Timetable treasure hunt"

### Lesson 29 — Study skills for secondary school
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can use effective study techniques (retrieval practice, flashcards, spacing, mind maps) and create a study plan for a test." (CASEL self-management; IB MYP ATL self-management)
- **Hook:** "Two students revise for the same test for the same time. One gets 90%, one gets 55%. What did they do differently?"
- **Key activity:** Revisit Lesson 3 (plan–monitor–evaluate) and Year 4's retrieval practice. New: why self-testing beats re-reading; make flashcards with questions on one side; the Leitner box system; interleaving topics; a spaced study timetable for a mock test; practise with content from Maths or Science.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why does spacing your study over several days work better than cramming the night before?" → Revisiting information after a gap strengthens long-term memory; cramming fades quickly.
- **Differentiation:** support: pre-made flashcards to start with / stretch: design a full two-week revision timetable for three subjects
- **Materials:** index cards, boxes for the Leitner system, study planners
- **Joy:** Flashcard "battle" games

### Lesson 30 — Worries, hopes and new friendships
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can name my hopes and worries about secondary school, know who to ask for help, and practise starting new friendships." (CASEL self-awareness and relationship skills; PSHE KS3)
- **Hook:** Letters from former students (or older children) about their first week at secondary school.
- **Key activity:** Anonymous "worry box" and class responses; problem-solving common worries (getting lost, homework, older students, making friends); conversation starters and joining clubs; a "who can help me" map (form tutor, school counsellor, family).
- **Check for understanding:** "Name one worry and a plan to handle it." → A specific worry with a concrete plan (e.g. "If I get lost, I'll ask a teacher or look at my map").
- **Differentiation:** support: a worry-and-plan frame / stretch: write a welcome guide for next year's Year 6 about moving on
- **Materials:** worry box, letters from older students, help-map template
- **Joy:** "Time capsule" letter to your future self in secondary school

**End-of-unit check:** A completed weekly planner and study plan; a worries-and-plans page with named sources of help; a demonstration of starting a conversation with someone new.

## Unit 7 — Graduation capstone: the Change-Maker Project

**Essential question:** How can I use everything I have learned to make a real, positive difference?

**Key vocabulary:** issue, stakeholder, research, evidence, survey, data, action plan, prototype, campaign, impact, audience, pitch, exhibition, reflection, legacy

> **How the capstone runs.** The five Life Skills lessons below are the project's backbone. Between them,
> the work is done across subjects in Term 3: **English** (research notes, a persuasive speech and the
> graduation speech), **Maths** (the survey analysis and budget in Units 12–13), **Geography** (the
> fieldwork enquiry), **Computing** (the data analysis and capstone program), **Art & Design** (posters,
> the exhibition design), **Science** (an investigation if the issue calls for one), **History** (the
> oral-history interview can inform local issues) and **Music/PE** (the graduation concert and festival
> where the projects are celebrated). The project is presented at the **graduation exhibition**.

### Lesson 31 — Research: understanding the issue
- **Duration:** 45 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can research my capstone issue using at least three reliable sources and one primary source (a survey, interview or observation), and record evidence with citations." (IB MYP ATL research; CASEL responsible decision-making; English and Maths links)
- **Hook:** "Before changing something, understand it — who does it affect, and why does it happen?"
- **Key activity:** A stakeholder map (who is affected, who has power); research using English Unit 5 skills (evaluate sources, notes in own words, bibliography); design a short survey or interview (Maths Unit 12 statistical questions); collect primary data.
- **Check for understanding:** "Who are the stakeholders in your issue, and whose voice might be missing?" → A named list of stakeholders with at least one under-heard group identified.
- **Differentiation:** support: a research template with suggested sources / stretch: interview an expert or official
- **Materials:** capstone booklet, devices, survey templates, source-evaluation checklists
- **Joy:** Talking to real people about a real problem

### Lesson 32 — Planning action: from evidence to a plan
- **Duration:** 45 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can analyse my evidence and design an action plan with steps, roles, a timeline, a budget and a measure of success." (CASEL responsible decision-making; IB MYP Design cycle; Maths link)
- **Hook:** "Ideas are easy. Plans make them happen."
- **Key activity:** Summarise survey data (averages, charts — Maths and Computing); choose an action (a campaign, a product, an event, a guide, a petition, a service); write an action plan (who, what, when, cost, risks); decide the success measure.
- **Check for understanding:** "How does your evidence support the action you chose?" → A clear link between a finding (e.g. "70% of pupils said the playground has too little shade") and the action.
- **Differentiation:** support: an action-plan template with examples / stretch: include a risk plan and a budget with a funding source (e.g. enterprise profits from Unit 4)
- **Materials:** data from Lesson 31, action-plan templates, calculators or spreadsheets
- **Joy:** Seeing your data tell a story

### Lesson 33 — Taking action
- **Duration:** 45 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can carry out my planned action, keep a log of what happens and adapt when something doesn't go to plan." (CASEL self-management and relationship skills; IB MYP ATL)
- **Hook:** "Go!"
- **Key activity:** Teams carry out the action (e.g. deliver a letter to the council, run a campaign stall, build and install a bird feeder trail, run a reading club for younger children, launch a recycling system); keep an impact log with photos (with permission) and data; mid-point review and adaptation.
- **Check for understanding:** "What didn't go to plan, and how did you adapt?" → A specific problem and a sensible adaptation (link to Unit 2 resilience).
- **Differentiation:** support: an adult mentor and a checklist of steps / stretch: lead coordination with an outside organisation
- **Materials:** project materials, impact logs, cameras (with permission)
- **Joy:** Making something real happen

### Lesson 34 — Measuring impact and preparing the pitch
- **Duration:** 45 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can measure the impact of my action against my success measure and prepare a persuasive 3-minute pitch with visuals." (IB MYP ATL communication; English Unit 4 link)
- **Hook:** "Did it work? How do you know? And how will you inspire others to continue it?"
- **Key activity:** Compare results with the success measure (before-and-after data); prepare a pitch using rhetoric from English (hook, evidence, story, call to action); design an exhibition board or display (Art link) with data visualisation (Maths/Computing).
- **Check for understanding:** "What evidence shows the impact of your project?" → Specific before-and-after data or feedback, honestly reported (including partial success).
- **Differentiation:** support: a pitch frame and display template / stretch: include a plan for how the project can continue after graduation (legacy)
- **Materials:** impact data, display boards, art materials, presentation tools
- **Joy:** Rehearsing with a real audience in mind

### Lesson 35 — The graduation exhibition and reflection
- **Duration:** 45 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can present my Change-Maker Project at the graduation exhibition and reflect on how I have grown towards the graduate I want to be." (CASEL all competencies; IB MYP ATL reflection)
- **Hook:** "Doors open: families, teachers, community partners and younger pupils."
- **Key activity:** The graduation exhibition — capstone displays alongside the art exhibition, science fair posters, geography fieldwork, oral histories and computing programs; pitches to visitors; a final reflection against the school's "vision of the graduate" (literate and numerate, confident communicator, curious inquirer, creative maker, healthy and active, kind and globally minded, resilient and self-directed); handing on the project to next year's class or a partner organisation.
- **Check for understanding:** "Which part of the 'vision of the graduate' did your capstone show most, and what is your next step?" → A specific quality with evidence from the project and a meaningful next step.
- **Differentiation:** support: a reflection frame and a partner to co-present / stretch: act as an exhibition guide or give the opening speech
- **Materials:** exhibition space, displays, reflection sheets, certificates
- **Joy:** The graduation exhibition — the celebration of six years of learning

**End-of-unit check:** The Change-Maker Project, assessed on: understanding of the issue (research and stakeholders), use of evidence and data, a clear action plan, action taken and adapted, honestly measured impact, a persuasive pitch, and a reflection linked to the vision of the graduate.

## End-of-year mastery checks

1. **Knows and directs themselves:** can state their values and strengths and sets goals with WOOP and habits, reviewing progress. *See it:* the Unit 1 "compass" page and habit tracker.
2. **Manages emotions and setbacks:** uses a calm plan, reframes unhelpful thoughts and makes a comeback plan. *See it:* Unit 2 artefacts and real-life use reported in reflections.
3. **Communicates and relates well:** listens actively, speaks assertively, resolves conflict, respects boundaries and consent, acts as an upstander. *See it:* Unit 3 role plays.
4. **Manages money:** balances a budget, explains compound interest and the cost of credit, reads a payslip and runs an enterprise. *See it:* Unit 4 records.
5. **Acts as a global citizen:** explains rights and responsibilities, how to influence decisions and how to live more sustainably, and has served the community. *See it:* Unit 5 work and service action.
6. **Is ready for secondary school:** organises a timetable and homework, uses effective study strategies and knows where to get help. *See it:* Unit 6 planner and study plan.
7. **Makes change happen:** completes the Change-Maker Project from research to impact and presents it confidently. *See it:* the graduation exhibition.

## Teacher guidance

- **Safe, respectful space.** Agree ground rules (listen, respect, confidentiality with its limits, the right to pass); use anonymous question boxes; never pressure children to share personal stories; follow safeguarding procedures for any disclosure.
- **Sensitive topics.** Relationships, boundaries and consent are taught age-appropriately and in line with the family's or school's RSE policy, with parents informed. Money lessons must be sensitive to different family incomes — use simulations, not children's real family finances.
- **Common misconceptions:** resilience means not feeling upset (it means recovering and adapting); growth mindset means "just try harder" (it means effective strategies, help-seeking and effort); assertive means aggressive; bullying is any conflict (it is repeated, intentional and involves a power imbalance); a bigger number of small eco-actions beats a few big ones; credit is "free money"; children can't make real change (the capstone proves otherwise).
- **The capstone.** Keep projects small, local and achievable; success includes honest partial success and what was learned; involve families and community partners; celebrate every project at the exhibition.
- **Differentiation.** Support means frames, examples, choice from prepared options and adult mentoring; stretch means leadership roles, outside partners, deeper analysis and more ambitious goals.
- **Vertical alignment.** Year 4 built the foundations (identity, strengths, growth mindset, conflict steps, SMART goals, simple interest, a market stall, a waste audit, rights and stereotypes); Year 5 applied them to harder situations (influence, mixed emotions, group chats, risk, bullying, personal safety, nerves, loss, banks and scams, tax, a data-led campaign, prejudice, democracy and letters to decision-makers). Year 6 retrieves these briefly and deepens each into self-direction, emotional intelligence, assertiveness in hard situations, financial concepts, rights and citizenship, and a full change-making project. Secondary PSHE (UK Year 8 / US Grade 7 / MYP 2) builds on these foundations in adolescence.
- **Cross-curricular links:** every subject contributes to the capstone (see Unit 7); English (speeches, research), Maths (budgets, interest, statistics), Geography (SDGs, sustainability), History (rights and change-makers), Computing (data, AI and online safety), PE & Health (wellbeing, leadership), Art (exhibition design).
