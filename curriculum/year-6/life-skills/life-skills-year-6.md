# Year 6 Life Skills (ages 10–11, KS2) — Lesson Outlines

> Part of **the best international primary school** (see [`../../../SCHOOL-CHARTER.md`](../../../SCHOOL-CHARTER.md)).
> Year 6 scope drawn from [`knowledge-base/subjects/computing-life-skills/life-skills.md`](../../../knowledge-base/subjects/computing-life-skills/life-skills.md).

## Year overview

Year 6 is the capstone of primary school — the launchpad to secondary — so Life Skills consolidates six years of growth and points it forward to the bigger, more independent world ahead. Ten- and eleven-year-olds can reason about complex situations, manage their own feelings and friendships, weigh evidence and trade-offs, and take real responsibility. So this year takes every strand to its primary-school summit: emotional literacy becomes **managing stress, change and the transition to secondary**; relationships add **navigating bigger social worlds, online relationships and pressure with maturity**; growth mindset becomes **independence, leadership and lifelong learning**; health completes **puberty, wellbeing and looking after a maturing body and mind, including managing risk**; personal safety becomes **real-world independence, risk and decision-making (online and offline)**; money reaches **independent budgeting, ethical and informed consumer choices**; critical thinking becomes **evaluating information, bias and misinformation**; and citizenship becomes **active, informed citizenship and global responsibility**. The tone stays warm, honest and **never shaming** — change is exciting and a little scary, and the message is *you have the skills, the judgement and the support to step up.* Most lessons run 30–40 minutes. Seven units, twenty-one lessons, every one anchored to the knowledge base.

**How it builds on Year 5:** Y5 built identity and self-esteem; Y6 manages stress and the big transition to secondary. Y5 learned healthy relationships; Y6 navigates wider social worlds and online relationships with mature judgement. Y5 learned how to learn; Y6 takes responsibility, leads and embraces lifelong learning. Y5 introduced puberty; Y6 completes the picture, including managing emotions, wellbeing and risk in a maturing body. Y5 explored value and enterprise; Y6 budgets independently and makes ethical, informed consumer choices. Y5 spotted misleading media; Y6 evaluates bias and misinformation. Y5 valued diversity; Y6 becomes an active, informed global citizen. New this year: managing the transition to secondary, and active citizenship.

## Unit 1 — Managing stress & change (emotional wellbeing)

**Essential question:** How do I stay steady through big changes like starting secondary school?
**Key vocabulary:** transition, change, stress, coping, adapt, uncertainty, support network, perspective, fresh start.
**KB source:** Topic 8 — Feelings & wellbeing; Topic 9 — Resilience.

### Lesson 1 — Change is normal (and survivable)
- **Duration:** 30 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can name the mix of feelings change brings and that they're normal."
- **Hook:** "Starting secondary school: scary or exciting? Could it be both at once?"
- **Key activity:** Normalise the mix of nervous + excited about big changes. Children list past changes they've survived (new class, new home) and what helped. Build confidence: "I've handled change before."
- **Check for understanding:** Is it normal to feel both excited and nervous about a big change? → Yes — mixed feelings about change are completely normal.
- **Differentiation:** support: name one past change they handled / stretch: explain what helped them adapt before.
- **Materials:** change-timeline sheet.
- **Joy:** the empowering "look how much change I've already conquered" reflection.

### Lesson 2 — My stress-management toolkit
- **Duration:** 35 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can choose strategies to manage stress before a big event."
- **Hook:** "Athletes get nervous before a final too. What do they do to perform under pressure?"
- **Key activity:** Consolidate six years of tools (breathing, exercise, talking, planning, sleep, perspective, self-talk) into a personal stress-management toolkit. Children plan how they'll handle a specific stressor (a test, the first day at secondary).
- **Check for understanding:** Name two strategies to manage stress before a big event. → Any two of: breathing, exercise, talk to someone, plan/prepare, sleep, helpful self-talk, keep perspective.
- **Differentiation:** support: choose tools from a menu / stretch: build a full plan for a real upcoming stressor.
- **Materials:** toolkit template.
- **Joy:** assembling their "ready for anything" stress toolkit.

### Lesson 3 — Getting ready for secondary
- **Duration:** 35 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can name what to expect at secondary school and where to get help."
- **Hook:** "Bigger school, more teachers, a timetable, new people. What questions are buzzing in your head?"
- **Key activity:** Discuss realistic changes (moving classes, lockers, homework, making new friends, getting lost — and that everyone's in the same boat). Children write their worries and questions, then match each to who could help (form tutor, older pupil, parent).
- **Check for understanding:** If you're worried about something at secondary school, what can you do? → Ask a trusted person (form tutor, teacher, older pupil, parent) — everyone needs help settling in.
- **Differentiation:** support: match one worry to a helper / stretch: make a "first week" plan for settling in.
- **Materials:** worry/question slips, "who can help" chart.
- **Joy:** the reassuring "everyone feels this — you've got this" group share.

**End-of-unit check:** The child accepts mixed feelings about change, chooses stress-management strategies, and knows what to expect at secondary and where to get help.

## Unit 2 — Relationships in a bigger world

**Essential question:** How do I build good relationships and handle pressure in a wider, more grown-up world?
**Key vocabulary:** respect, peer influence, assertive, online relationships, reputation, boundaries, support, mature, integrity.
**KB source:** Topic 10 — Social skills; Topic 12 — Staying safe (relationships).

### Lesson 4 — Making and keeping good friends
- **Duration:** 30 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can name how to make new friends and be a good friend in a new place."
- **Hook:** "New school, no familiar faces. How do friendships actually start?"
- **Key activity:** Teach friendship-building (be approachable, find common interests, be kind, join in, give it time) and being a good friend (loyal, honest, supportive). Children practise conversation-starters and reflect on the kind of friend they want to be.
- **Check for understanding:** Name one way to start a new friendship. → e.g. say hello, find a shared interest, join an activity, be kind and approachable.
- **Differentiation:** support: practise a conversation-starter / stretch: plan how they'd help someone *else* feel included.
- **Materials:** conversation-starter cards.
- **Joy:** the "speed-friending" conversation game.

### Lesson 5 — Standing firm with maturity
- **Duration:** 35 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can handle peer pressure assertively and keep my integrity."
- **Hook:** "At secondary, the pressures get bigger and trickier. How do you stay true to your values?"
- **Key activity:** Deepen Y4 peer pressure to mature, higher-stakes scenarios (being asked to exclude someone, hide something, take a risk). Teach assertive responses, knowing your values, and choosing real friends. Role-play firm-but-kind refusals.
- **Check for understanding:** What helps you stand firm when pressured to do something against your values? → Knowing your values, saying no assertively, and surrounding yourself with friends who respect you.
- **Differentiation:** support: practise an assertive "no" / stretch: handle escalating pressure while keeping the friendship if possible.
- **Materials:** mature peer-pressure scenario cards.
- **Joy:** role-playing confident, even witty, ways to hold their ground.

### Lesson 6 — Online relationships and reputation
- **Duration:** 35 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can explain how to handle online relationships safely and protect my reputation."
- **Hook:** "More online life is coming — group chats, gaming, social media. What are the new rules of the game?"
- **Key activity:** Consolidate online safety (Y4) and footprint (Y5) for a bigger online world: group-chat etiquette, never sharing private things, screenshots last forever, pressure to share images is a red flag (tell an adult), being an upstander online. Children write their own "online code."
- **Check for understanding:** What should you do if someone online pressures you to share something private? → Refuse, don't do it, and tell a trusted adult — that pressure is a red flag.
- **Differentiation:** support: choose safe online actions / stretch: write a full personal "online code" with reasons.
- **Materials:** online-scenario cards, "online code" template.
- **Joy:** designing their own personal online code of honour.

**End-of-unit check:** The child names how to make and be a good friend, handles peer pressure assertively, and explains safe online relationships and protecting reputation.

## Unit 3 — Independence, leadership & lifelong learning (growth mindset)

**Essential question:** How do I take charge of my own learning, lead others, and keep growing for life?
**Key vocabulary:** independence, responsibility, leadership, initiative, organise, lifelong learning, role model, goal.
**KB source:** Topic 9 — Growth mindset; Topic 4 — Critical thinking.

### Lesson 7 — Taking responsibility for my learning
- **Duration:** 30 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can organise myself and take responsibility for my own tasks."
- **Hook:** "At secondary, no one will chase you for every homework. Who's in charge of your learning then?"
- **Key activity:** Teach self-organisation: a planner/diary, breaking work into chunks, deadlines, packing your bag the night before. Children set up a simple homework-organisation system and a daily routine.
- **Check for understanding:** Name one way to take responsibility for your own learning. → e.g. use a planner, note deadlines, break work up, prepare ahead, ask for help when needed.
- **Differentiation:** support: fill a part-made planner / stretch: design a full weekly self-organisation system.
- **Materials:** sample planner/diary, routine template.
- **Joy:** setting up their own "command centre" planner.

### Lesson 8 — Being a leader
- **Duration:** 35 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can describe what makes a good leader and show one leadership skill."
- **Hook:** "What makes someone a GREAT team captain — bossing people, or something better?"
- **Key activity:** Teach leadership as serving the team: listening, encouraging, being fair, taking initiative, sharing credit (not bossing). In groups, children take turns leading a short challenge and reflect on what good leadership looked like.
- **Check for understanding:** Name one quality of a good leader. → e.g. listens, is fair, encourages others, takes initiative, shares credit (not bossy).
- **Differentiation:** support: name one leadership quality / stretch: lead a group task and reflect on their leadership.
- **Materials:** team-challenge materials.
- **Joy:** the buzz of leading a real team challenge.

### Lesson 9 — Learning for life
- **Duration:** 30 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can explain that learning continues all through life and set a self-chosen goal."
- **Hook:** "Do grown-ups stop learning? What new things might YOU still want to learn at 30, 50, 80?"
- **Key activity:** Teach that learning never stops — adults learn new jobs, skills and hobbies; a growth mindset is for life. Children set one self-chosen learning goal beyond school and plan the first step, drawing on all their growth-mindset tools.
- **Check for understanding:** Does learning stop when school ends? → No — people keep learning new things all their lives; a growth mindset is lifelong.
- **Differentiation:** support: pick a learning goal from ideas / stretch: plan steps and how they'll stay motivated.
- **Materials:** lifelong-learning goal cards.
- **Joy:** dreaming up and committing to a "thing I'll teach myself" goal.

**End-of-unit check:** The child organises their own learning, describes and shows good leadership, and sets a self-chosen lifelong-learning goal.

## Unit 4 — Healthy mind & body for life (health & wellbeing)

**Essential question:** How do I look after my maturing body and mind and make safe choices about risk?
**Key vocabulary:** puberty, wellbeing, balance, risk, healthy choices, mental health, support, peer influence, self-care.
**KB source:** Topic 6 — Healthy eating; Topic 7 — Sleep & exercise; Topic 8 — Wellbeing (puberty completed; risk awareness, RSHE-aligned).

> **Teacher note:** Deliver factually, calmly and inclusively, with no shame. Keep content age-appropriate and aligned to your setting's RSHE policy. Risk-awareness lessons focus on healthy choices, recognising pressure, and where to get help — not on detailed substance content. Direct deeper questions to a trusted adult.

### Lesson 10 — My body and mind, growing up
- **Duration:** 35 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can explain that puberty affects both body and emotions, and that everyone's journey differs."
- **Hook:** "Last year we learned bodies change. This year: how do those changes affect feelings and friendships too?"
- **Key activity:** Build on Y5: revisit that puberty changes bodies *and* emotions, at different times for everyone, and that's normal. Discuss managing stronger feelings, supporting friends going through it, and that comparing your timing to others isn't helpful. Use a question box.
- **Check for understanding:** Does puberty affect only the body? → No — it affects emotions and feelings too, and happens at different times for everyone; all normal.
- **Differentiation:** support: confirm "everyone's different, it's normal" / stretch: explain how to support a friend going through changes.
- **Materials:** an anonymous question box, an age-appropriate "growing up" overview.
- **Joy:** the respectful, judgement-free "ask anything" question box.

### Lesson 11 — Looking after my mental health
- **Duration:** 35 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can name ways to look after my mental health and where to get help."
- **Hook:** "We exercise to keep our bodies healthy. What keeps our *minds* healthy?"
- **Key activity:** Teach mental health as something everyone has and can care for: talking, sleep, exercise, hobbies, connection, asking for help when low. Normalise that everyone struggles sometimes and help is a strength. Children list their personal "look after my mind" habits and name who to tell if they're really struggling.
- **Check for understanding:** Name one way to look after your mental health and one person you could talk to if you felt low. → e.g. talk, sleep, exercise, hobbies, ask for help; a trusted adult/parent/teacher/Childline.
- **Differentiation:** support: pick habits and one trusted person / stretch: explain why asking for help is a strength, not a weakness.
- **Materials:** mental-health habit cards, a "who can help" reminder.
- **Joy:** building a personal "mind care" plan that feels their own.

### Lesson 12 — Making safe choices about risk
- **Duration:** 35 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can recognise risky situations and pressures and choose a safe, healthy response."
- **Hook:** "Growing up means more freedom — and more choices, some risky. How do you decide what's safe?"
- **Key activity:** Teach a calm framework for risk: pause, think of consequences, consider who benefits, trust your gut, it's okay to say no or leave. Cover age-appropriate examples (peer dares, going somewhere unsafe, pressure to try things that harm health). Practise the "stop and think" decision and getting out of a risky spot.
- **Check for understanding:** Name one step for deciding what to do in a risky situation. → e.g. pause and think of consequences, ask "is this safe?", say no or leave, get help from a trusted adult.
- **Differentiation:** support: choose the safe option in a scenario / stretch: explain how to leave a risky situation while saving face.
- **Materials:** risk-scenario cards, a "stop and think" decision poster.
- **Joy:** role-playing confident "I'm out of here" exits from risky scenes.

**End-of-unit check:** The child explains puberty affects body and emotions for everyone differently, names mental-health habits and who to tell, and shows a safe response to a risky situation.

## Unit 5 — Independence & safety in the real world (personal safety)

**Essential question:** How do I stay safe and make good decisions with more independence?
**Key vocabulary:** independence, judgement, emergency, plan, awareness, trust your instincts, help, consequences.
**KB source:** Topic 12 — Staying safe; Topic 4 — Critical thinking applied to decisions.

### Lesson 13 — Out and about on my own
- **Duration:** 35 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can plan how to stay safe when out with more independence."
- **Hook:** "Walking to school, getting the bus, being out with friends — more freedom means more to think about. What's your safety plan?"
- **Key activity:** Teach being safe with independence: tell someone your plan, know your route, keep money/phone safe, trust your instincts, know what to do if lost or a plan changes. Children make a personal "out and about" safety plan.
- **Check for understanding:** Name two things that help you stay safe when out with more independence. → Any two of: tell someone your plan, know your route, keep belongings safe, trust your instincts, have a backup plan.
- **Differentiation:** support: complete a part-made safety plan / stretch: plan for "what if my plan goes wrong."
- **Materials:** safety-plan template, a sample route map.
- **Joy:** mapping out their own real-life independence plan.

### Lesson 14 — Emergencies and first aid
- **Duration:** 35 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can act calmly in an emergency and know basic first-aid steps."
- **Hook:** "If someone collapsed in front of you right now — what's the very first thing you'd do?"
- **Key activity:** Build on Y1's 999 with more capability: stay calm, check for danger, call 999 and give clear information (what, where), basic help (don't move someone badly hurt, comfort them, get an adult). Practise a clear 999 call and a simple first-aid scenario.
- **Check for understanding:** In an emergency, what information should you give when you call 999? → What's happened, where you are, and what help is needed.
- **Differentiation:** support: practise the calm 999 call / stretch: handle a multi-step first-aid scenario calmly.
- **Materials:** a (toy) phone, first-aid scenario cards.
- **Joy:** the realistic "you're the calm hero" emergency role-play.

### Lesson 15 — Good decisions and consequences
- **Duration:** 30 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can think through the consequences before making a decision."
- **Hook:** "Every choice has a 'what happens next.' How do good decision-makers see around the corner?"
- **Key activity:** Teach a decision framework: options → consequences (good and bad, short and long term) → who's affected → choose → reflect. Children apply it to a realistic dilemma (a dare, a shortcut, a tricky friend choice).
- **Check for understanding:** Before making a big decision, what's wise to think about? → The consequences (good and bad, now and later) and who it affects.
- **Differentiation:** support: list one good and one bad consequence / stretch: weigh a dilemma fully and justify a choice.
- **Materials:** decision-framework template, dilemma cards.
- **Joy:** being the "wise decision detective" predicting consequences.

**End-of-unit check:** The child plans for safe independence, acts calmly in an emergency with a clear 999 call, and thinks through consequences before deciding.

## Unit 6 — Money for the real world (independent budgeting & ethics)

**Essential question:** How do I manage my own money wisely and spend in line with my values?
**Key vocabulary:** budget, income, expense, save, debt, ethical, fair trade, sustainable, scam, consumer, value.
**KB source:** Topic 3 — Saving, spending & budgeting; Topic 5 — critical thinking; Topic 2 — needs vs wants.

### Lesson 16 — Managing my own money
- **Duration:** 35 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can make and balance an independent budget."
- **Hook:** "Imagine a monthly allowance to cover your own snacks, trips and savings. Could you make it last?"
- **Key activity:** Consolidate budgeting into independent money management: list income and expenses, plan spend/save/give, handle an unexpected cost. Children balance a realistic monthly budget and adjust when a surprise expense hits.
- **Check for understanding:** What do you do if an unexpected cost appears in your budget? → Adjust the plan — cut a want, dip into savings, or rebalance so it still adds up.
- **Differentiation:** support: balance a simple budget / stretch: rebalance after a surprise cost and still meet a savings goal.
- **Materials:** monthly-budget sheet, "surprise expense" cards, play money.
- **Joy:** running their own grown-up monthly budget.

### Lesson 17 — Spending with my values
- **Duration:** 35 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can explain how my spending choices can reflect what I care about."
- **Hook:** "Two identical t-shirts: one made fairly and eco-friendly, one not. Does it matter which you buy?"
- **Key activity:** Connect Y3 citizenship and Y4 value with ethical consuming: fair trade, sustainability, supporting good causes, less waste. Discuss that money is a kind of "vote." Children plan one value-led spending choice and explain the trade-off.
- **Check for understanding:** How can your spending reflect your values? → By choosing fair, sustainable or ethical options where you can — your money is a kind of "vote."
- **Differentiation:** support: pick the more ethical option in a pair / stretch: weigh ethics against price and justify a choice.
- **Materials:** ethical/standard product cards.
- **Joy:** the "your money is a vote" discussion that feels powerful.

### Lesson 18 — Spotting scams and protecting my money
- **Duration:** 30 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can recognise common money scams and protect my money."
- **Hook:** "'You've won £1000! Just send your bank details!' Real... or a trap?"
- **Key activity:** Teach common scams (too-good-to-be-true, fake prizes, phishing messages, pressure to act fast) and how to protect money (never share bank/passwords, check with a trusted adult, if it seems too good to be true it usually is). Children spot scams in sample messages.
- **Check for understanding:** Name one warning sign of a money scam. → e.g. too good to be true, asks for bank details/passwords, rushes you, unexpected "prize."
- **Differentiation:** support: spot the scam in a clear example / stretch: explain why scams use urgency and big rewards.
- **Materials:** sample scam messages.
- **Joy:** "scam-busting" tricky messages like a money detective.

**End-of-unit check:** The child makes and balances an independent budget, explains value-led spending, and recognises and resists money scams.

## Unit 7 — Active, informed citizen (critical thinking & citizenship)

**Essential question:** How do I judge what's true and act to make the world fairer?
**Key vocabulary:** bias, misinformation, evidence, source, perspective, vote, voice, activism, global, responsibility.
**KB source:** Topic 4 — Asking good questions; Topic 5 — Facts vs opinions; citizenship/global extension.

### Lesson 19 — Bias and misinformation
- **Duration:** 35 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can spot bias and check whether information is true."
- **Hook:** "Two news headlines about the same event tell very different stories. Why? Who's right?"
- **Key activity:** Consolidate Y3 fact/opinion, Y4 sources and Y5 media into evaluating information: spot bias (one-sided, leaves things out), check multiple reliable sources, recognise misinformation and "fake news." Children compare slanted reports of one event and judge what's reliable.
- **Check for understanding:** Name one way to check whether something online is true. → e.g. check reliable sources, look for evidence, see if other trusted sources agree, watch for bias.
- **Differentiation:** support: spot the more reliable of two sources / stretch: explain how bias can make something misleading without lying outright.
- **Materials:** contrasting reports of one event.
- **Joy:** "fact-checking" real-feeling headlines like a journalist.

### Lesson 20 — Using my voice
- **Duration:** 35 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can explain how people make change and how I can use my voice."
- **Hook:** "Children have changed laws and saved forests. How does a young person make a difference?"
- **Key activity:** Teach ways people make change (voting, petitions, campaigns, speaking up, peaceful action, supporting causes) and that young voices count. Children pick an issue they care about and plan one realistic way to use their voice.
- **Check for understanding:** Name one way people can make their voice heard to change things. → e.g. vote, petition, campaign, speak up, support a cause, contact someone in charge.
- **Differentiation:** support: choose a "use my voice" action from cards / stretch: plan a small campaign for an issue they care about.
- **Materials:** issue cards, campaign-planning sheet.
- **Joy:** planning a real mini-campaign for a cause they believe in.

### Lesson 21 — A global citizen (capstone)
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can explain what it means to be a responsible global citizen and pledge an action."
- **Hook:** "We share one planet with billions of people. What does it mean to be a citizen of the *whole world*?"
- **Key activity:** Capstone bringing together six years: global challenges (fairness, environment, kindness), and how everyday choices connect us all. Children reflect on the kind of person and citizen they want to be at secondary and beyond, and make a "global citizen pledge."
- **Check for understanding:** Name one thing a responsible global citizen does. → e.g. cares for the planet, treats everyone fairly, helps others, makes thoughtful choices, uses their voice for good.
- **Differentiation:** support: choose a pledge from examples / stretch: connect a personal action to a wider global benefit.
- **Materials:** global-citizen pledge cards, a world map.
- **Joy:** signing and sharing a global-citizen pledge as a proud send-off to secondary school.

**End-of-unit check:** The child spots bias and checks information, explains how to use their voice for change, and pledges an action as a responsible global citizen.

## End-of-year outcomes

By the end of Year 6, a child should reliably show:

1. **Resilience through change** — manages stress and the transition to secondary, with a personal toolkit and support network.
2. **Mature relationships** — makes and keeps good friends, stands firm against pressure with integrity, and handles online relationships safely.
3. **Independence & leadership** — takes responsibility for their own learning, leads fairly, and embraces lifelong learning.
4. **Whole-person health** — understands puberty's effect on body and mind, cares for their mental health, and makes safe choices about risk.
5. **Real-world safety** — plans for safe independence, acts calmly in an emergency, and weighs consequences before deciding.
6. **Independent money management** — makes and balances a budget, spends in line with values, and resists scams.
7. **Active, informed citizenship** — evaluates bias and misinformation, uses their voice for change, and acts as a responsible global citizen.
8. **Confident, principled character** — steps into a bigger, more independent world with judgement, kindness, resilience and the knowledge that they can always ask for help.
