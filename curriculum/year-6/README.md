# Year 6 (ages 11–12) — The Graduation Year

> Part of **the best international primary school** (see [`../../SCHOOL-CHARTER.md`](../../SCHOOL-CHARTER.md)).
> Built to the full-year standard in [`../PLANNING-BRIEF.md`](../PLANNING-BRIEF.md).
> **Equivalents:** UK Year 7 (Key Stage 3) · US Grade 6 · IB MYP Year 1 · Singapore Secondary 1 foundations ·
> Cambridge Lower Secondary Stage 7.

Year 6 is the last year a child spends with us. It has two jobs: to **complete a gap-free primary
education at the level of the world's best systems**, and to **send out a graduate who matches the
charter's "vision of the graduate"** — ready for any secondary school in the world. Every subject file
below is a full scope and sequence (every lesson with objective, hook, key activity, check for
understanding, differentiation, materials and joy), followed by books and resources, end-of-year mastery
checks and teacher guidance.

## How Year 6 delivers the "vision of the graduate"

The charter (§1) describes a 12-year-old leaving this school. Year 6 is designed so that **each quality
is taught explicitly, practised, and then shown in public** at graduation.

| The graduate is… | Where it is built this year | Where it is shown |
|---|---|---|
| **Literate and numerate to a high standard** — reads fluently and critically, writes clearly, reasons mathematically | English: six whole books, a Shakespeare play and 20+ poems; analytical essays, argument, research. Maths: integers, fraction division, algebra, ratio, geometry, statistics to UK Year 7 / US Grade 6 mastery | English graduation portfolio; Maths final assessment and graduation project; mastery checks in every file |
| **A confident communicator in English plus another language** | English speeches, debate, Shakespeare performance, graduation speech; Spanish to CEFR A2 (three time frames, 3–4 minute conversations) | Graduation speech; Spanish presentation "Mi vida" and final conversation |
| **A curious inquirer** who investigates and learns independently | Science independent investigation; History oral-history enquiry; Geography fieldwork enquiry; English research report; Maths statistical investigation | Science fair; the graduation exhibition displays |
| **A creative maker** in art, music, design and technology | Art personal project; Music songwriting and recording; Design cycle in Art; Python game and capstone program | Art exhibition; graduation concert; capstone program demo |
| **Healthy and active** in body and mind | PE & Health: fitness science, games, dance, athletics, water safety, first aid, puberty and mental health; Life Skills: emotions and resilience | Graduation sports festival (led by the children); personal health plan |
| **A kind, globally-minded citizen** | History of a connected world; Geography of global issues and the SDGs; Life Skills rights, democracy, sustainability and service; English texts from five continents | The Change-Maker Project |
| **Resilient and self-directed** — sets goals, persists, bounces back | Life Skills values, WOOP goals, habits, reframing, study skills and transition; every subject's mastery checks and personal-best tracking | The capstone (planned, adapted and evaluated by the child); the reflection against this table at the exhibition |

## The graduation capstone: the Change-Maker Project

In Term 3 every child — alone or in a small team — tackles a **real problem in their community** (a
local park, waste, water, loneliness among older neighbours, a safer route to school, reading for
younger children, local wildlife…). The project is run from **Life Skills Unit 7** and drawn from
every subject:

| Stage | What children do | Subjects feeding it |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Choose & question | Pick an issue (first listed in Life Skills L1); write a "How might we…?" question and a measurable goal | Life Skills L1 and L27; Geography (SDGs) |
| 2. Research | Reliable sources with citations; interviews; stakeholder map | Life Skills L31; English Unit 5 (research skills); History Unit 6 (interviewing) |
| 3. Gather data | Design a survey or fieldwork; analyse with averages, spread and charts | Maths Unit 12; Geography Unit 7; Computing Unit 4 |
| 4. Plan & budget | Action plan with roles, timeline, risks and a budget (enterprise profits can fund it) | Life Skills L20–21 and L32; Maths Unit 13 |
| 5. Act | Carry out the action and log its impact; adapt when things go wrong | Life Skills L33; Science Unit 9 if an investigation is needed |
| 6. Communicate | A persuasive pitch, a poster and exhibition board, a small program or data tool | English Units 4 and 9; Art Unit 6–7; Computing Unit 7 |
| 7. Celebrate & reflect | Measure impact and rehearse the pitch; present at the graduation exhibition; reflect against the vision of the graduate; hand the project on | Life Skills L34–35 |

**Graduation week** brings everything together: the **graduation exhibition** (Change-Maker Projects,
the art exhibition, science-fair posters, geography fieldwork, oral histories, computing programs),
the **graduation concert**, the **sports festival** led by the children, and the **graduation speeches**
(English, with a Spanish presentation for the languages strand).

## Vertical alignment: what Year 6 builds on and hands on

- **Builds on Year 5 (ages 10–11, UK Year 6 / US Grade 5)** — see [`../year-5/`](../year-5/) and
  `knowledge-base/subjects/*/year-6.md`: the full KS2 toolkit (long multiplication and division, four
  operations with fractions, first ratio and algebra, the mean, pie charts; KS2 grammar and spelling;
  classification, circulation, evolution, light, electricity, the particle model and first chemical
  reactions, atmosphere and climate; source evaluation, the Ottoman Empire, the Renaissance, the
  transatlantic slave trade and the Second World War home fronts; map projections and GIS, Kenya,
  water, trade and the polar regions; Scratch, the micro:bit, **first Python** and binary; A1 Spanish
  with the present tense and *ir a* + infinitive; choral harmony, ukulele, keyboard and compound time;
  illustration, collage and sculpture; changing friendships, risk, banks, scams and a data-led
  sustainability campaign). Year 4 taught networks and the web, and a first class enterprise and service
  project. Each Year 6 file starts with brief retrieval and then goes deeper. The review in
  [`../../docs/reviews/years-5-6.md`](../../docs/reviews/years-5-6.md) records where Year 5–6 repeats were
  removed.
- **Hands on to secondary (UK Year 8 / US Grade 7 / MYP 2):** proportional reasoning and multi-step
  equations; thesis-driven analytical and argument essays, heritage texts and Shakespeare; the cell,
  particle, energy and force models; source evaluation and interpretation; case-study geography and
  fieldwork; Python fundamentals, networks and data; A2 Spanish in three time frames; and the habits of
  a self-directed learner.

  continuously, durations 30–45 minutes.
