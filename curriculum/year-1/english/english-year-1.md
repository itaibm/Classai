> Part of **the best international primary school** (see [`../../../SCHOOL-CHARTER.md`](../../../SCHOOL-CHARTER.md)).
> Built to [`../../PLANNING-BRIEF.md`](../../PLANNING-BRIEF.md). Standards reference:
> `knowledge-base/subjects/english/year-2.md` (UK Year 2 = our Year 1). Codes: **UK NC Y2** strand
> (WR = word reading, RC = reading comprehension, SP = spelling, HW = handwriting, WC = writing composition,
> VGP = vocabulary, grammar & punctuation, SL = spoken language) and the nearest **CCSS** Grade 1/2 standard.

## Year overview

Year 1 turns a decoder into a reader and a sentence-maker into a writer. It rests on four daily habits:
**explicit systematic synthetic phonics**, **decodable and then real-book reading every day**, **talk before
you write**, and **a great book at the centre of every unit**.

**What this year assumes (from Foundation, ages 5–6):** blending and segmenting CVC and CCVC words; the
Phase 3 digraphs and trigraphs; the split digraphs (a-e, i-e, o-e, u-e, e-e); first contact with Phase 5
alternative spellings; around 40 common exception ("tricky") words; forming lowercase letters; capital
letters, full stops and *and*; retelling a story in order. Lesson 1 is a phonics baseline, so any gap is
found in week 1 and closed in daily phonics warm-ups (see Teacher guidance).

**Phonics and spelling spine.** Term 1 consolidates **Phase 5**: every vowel sound and its alternative
spellings (/ai/, /ee/, /igh/, /oa/, /oo/, /or/, /ur/, /ow/, /oi/, /air/, /ear/), alternative pronunciations
(*ow* in snow/cow, *ea* in sea/head), then consonant alternatives (soft *c* and *g*, *-dge*, silent letters,
*ph*, *-le/-el/-al/-il*). Terms 2–3 move to **Phase 6 spelling**: suffix rules (just add, drop the *e*, double
the consonant, *y → i*), plurals, *-ful/-less/-ness/-ly/-ment*, contractions and the possessive apostrophe,
*-tion*, homophones, and the Year 1 common exception words (door, because, children, could, people…).
Every phonics lesson ends with **decodable** sentences or a decodable book matched to the sounds taught —
the child reads words they can decode, not guess.

**Reading.** Fluency (accuracy → automaticity → expression) is taught explicitly and re-read daily at home.
Comprehension moves from retrieval to **inference backed by a clue from the text**, comparing versions of a
tale, and explaining how characters change.

**Writing and grammar.** Each unit ends with a real piece of writing for a real reader, taught through the
class text: a poem, a new adventure story, an information page, a postcard, a retold traditional tale, a
diary, instructions, a persuasive letter and a finished story. Grammar is taught *in service of that writing*:
nouns/adjectives/verbs and expanded noun phrases (for description), the four sentence types (for
non-fiction), past and progressive tense (for recounts and diaries), co-ordination and subordination
(*and, but, or, because, when, if, that*) for stories, commas in lists, and apostrophes.

**Handwriting.** Correct letter size and spacing, then the first **diagonal and horizontal joins**, so that
Year 2 can write fully joined.

**Class-text spine (whole books read aloud and studied; details in Books & resources):**

**Reading volume.** Beyond the class texts, every child takes home a decodable book matched to their phonics
stage *and* a "reading for pleasure" book chosen from the class library (see Books & resources) every week.

**What Year 2 relies on:** secure Phase 5/6 phonics so decoding is automatic; the Year 1 common exception
words; the four suffix rules; apostrophes for contraction and singular possession; sentences joined with
*and/but/or/because/when/if/that*; past tense used consistently; writing a short structured piece
(beginning–middle–end, or headed sections); and the first joins in handwriting. Year 2 adds prefixes,
*-ly/-ous/-tion* families, the Year 3/4 word list, inverted commas for speech, paragraphs and fully joined
handwriting.

## Time budget

around one or two class texts and interleaves the strands: roughly 6 phonics/spelling, 5 reading, 5 writing
(plan → draft → edit → publish), 1 handwriting, 1 poetry/performance and 1 check per unit. Daily outside these
lessons: 10 minutes of home reading and 5 minutes of phonics/spelling retrieval.

## Unit 1 — The sound code: vowel spellings

**Essential question:** How can one sound be spelled in different ways — and how do I choose the right one?

**Key vocabulary:** sound (phoneme), letter(s) (grapheme), digraph, trigraph, split digraph, alternative spelling, blend, segment, syllable, tricky word, fluency, predict, character, feeling, evidence, poem, rhyme, rhythm, capital letter, full stop

### Lesson 1 — Sound check: what phonics do I know?
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can read real and nonsense words using the sounds I know, so my teacher knows what to teach me next." (UK NC Y2 WR — apply phonics; CCSS RF.1.3)
- **Hook:** "Alien words! Can you read 'blurk' and 'zaip'? Only a real phonics expert can read words nobody has seen before."
- **Key activity:** A calm, game-like baseline: read grapheme cards (Phase 3–5), a list of real and nonsense words, and 5 common exception words; spell 6 dictated words. The teacher records gaps on a class phonics tracker that drives the daily 5-minute warm-ups.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why can you read 'zaip' even though it isn't a real word?" → Because I know the sounds z-ai-p and can blend them — reading is working out the sounds, not remembering pictures of words.
- **Differentiation:** support: stop at the first stage the child cannot read fluently and note it; keep it brief and warm / stretch: add Phase 5 alternatives and two-syllable words (rabbit, garden)
- **Materials:** grapheme flashcards, real/nonsense word list, tracker sheet, sticker chart
- **Joy:** "Alien words" read to a puppet alien who cheers every correct blend.

### Lesson 2 — Meet Plop: listening, predicting and asking questions
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can predict what a story will be about from its title and cover and ask a good question about it." (UK NC Y2 RC — predict, ask questions; CCSS RL.2.1)
- **Hook:** Show the cover of *The Owl Who Was Afraid of the Dark*: "An owl who is scared of the dark? Is that even possible?"
- **Key activity:** I do: model a prediction with "I think… because…". We do: read chapter 1 aloud ("Dark is nasty!"), pausing to check predictions. You do: each child writes or says one question they want answered by the end of the book, posted on a "Wonder Wall".
- **Check for understanding:** "Why is it funny that Plop is afraid of the dark?" → Owls are night birds that hunt in the dark, so being afraid of it is the opposite of what we expect.
- **Differentiation:** support: sentence starters "I think… because…" and picture prompts / stretch: predict how Plop will change by the end and give a reason from chapter 1
- **Materials:** *The Owl Who Was Afraid of the Dark* by Jill Tomlinson, sticky notes, Wonder Wall, a toy owl
- **Joy:** Reading in a darkened room by torchlight.

### Lesson 3 — One sound, many spellings: /ai/ as ai, ay and a-e
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can read and spell the /ai/ sound as ai, ay and a-e, and know that ay usually comes at the end of a word." (UK NC Y2 SP / WR — Phase 5 alternatives; CCSS RF.1.3c)
- **Hook:** "Three different spellings, one sound — rain, day, cake. Why does English do this to us?"
- **Key activity:** I do: sound buttons under rain, day, cake. We do: sort word cards into three houses (ai, ay, a-e) and discover the position rule (ay at the end: play, stay). You do: read a decodable passage and highlight /ai/ spellings; spell 5 words from dictation.
- **Check for understanding:** "Which spelling of /ai/ would you choose to end the word 'displ_' and why?" → ay — /ai/ at the end of a word is usually spelled ay.
- **Differentiation:** support: two spellings (ai, ay) first with magnetic letters / stretch: find /ai/ words that break the pattern (they, eight) — preview of Lesson 4
- **Materials:** magnetic letters, word cards, three "sound houses", decodable passage, whiteboards
- **Joy:** Sound-house sorting race against a sand timer.

### Lesson 4 — More spellings of /ai/: eigh, ey and a
- **Duration:** 22 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can read words with the rarer /ai/ spellings eigh, ey and a (eight, they, apron)." (UK NC Y2 WR — Phase 5 alternatives; CCSS RF.2.3)
- **Hook:** "Eight has five letters but only two sounds. Can you find them?"
- **Key activity:** I do: sound-button eight (eigh-t), they (th-ey), apron (a-pr-o-n). We do: add these to the /ai/ sound house. You do: read a decodable "eight grey reindeer" passage; spell eight, weigh, they, grey.
- **Check for understanding:** "How many sounds in 'weigh'? Which letters make which sound?" → Two: w + eigh.
- **Differentiation:** support: eigh words only / stretch: read neighbour, obey, bacon and explain each /ai/
- **Materials:** sound-house display, decodable passage, magnetic letters
- **Joy:** "Weigh the eight" — weigh eight toys on a scale while reading the word cards.

### Lesson 5 — Decodable reading: fluency with /ai/ words
- **Duration:** 22 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can read a decodable book accurately and re-read it more smoothly." (UK NC Y2 WR — read books matched to phonics; CCSS RF.2.4)
- **Hook:** "First read: detective (sound it out). Second read: smooth. Third read: storyteller voice!"
- **Key activity:** Pre-teach 3 tricky words; child reads a decodable book at their stage three times — accuracy, then fluency, then expression — with the teacher noting errors and modelling phrasing. Talk about the story after the third read.
- **Check for understanding:** "You read 'train' as 'tran' the first time. What did you notice and fix?" → I missed the ai digraph; ai says /ai/, so it's train.
- **Differentiation:** support: a book at a lower phonics stage with echo-reading / stretch: a longer decodable text plus retelling in own words
- **Materials:** decodable books matched to stage (e.g. Big Cat Phonics for Little Wandle, Songbirds, Dandelion Readers), reading record
- **Joy:** Recording the third read as a "radio story" to play back.

### Lesson 6 — The /ee/ sound: ee, ea, e-e, y, ey, ie
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can read the six spellings of /ee/ and spell the common ones (ee, ea, y) in words." (UK NC Y2 SP / WR — Phase 5; CCSS RF.1.3c)
- **Hook:** "The /ee/ sound is a master of disguise — six costumes! Can you spot them all in this silly sentence?"
- **Key activity:** I do: sound buttons in "Pete's monkey sees a thief eat a happy pea". We do: sound-house sort. You do: highlight /ee/ spellings in a decodable passage; spell sea, green, happy, key.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why is 'happy' in the /ee/ house when it ends with y?" → Here y at the end of a longer word says /ee/.
- **Differentiation:** support: ee, ea, y only / stretch: homophones sea/see and meet/meat — which is which?
- **Materials:** silly-sentence card, sound houses, decodable passage, whiteboards
- **Joy:** Disguise game — /ee/ words wear paper costumes to be unmasked.

### Lesson 7 — Handwriting: posture, grip and letter families
- **Duration:** 20 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can sit well, hold my pencil comfortably and form each letter family starting in the right place, with letters the right size." (UK NC Y2 HW — form lowercase letters of correct size)
- **Hook:** "Your pencil is a racing car. It needs the right grip and the right starting line!"
- **Key activity:** I do: model posture and tripod grip, then the four letter families (curly caterpillar c a d g o q e s f; long ladder l i t u j y; one-armed robot r b n h m k p; zigzag monster v w x z). We do: sky-write and trace. You do: copy a line per family on lined paper, checking ascenders and descenders.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why do a, d and g all start at the same place as c?" → They are all 'curly caterpillar' letters — they start with the c shape.
- **Differentiation:** support: raised-line paper and pencil grips / stretch: write a short sentence using every letter family with even size
- **Materials:** lined handwriting paper with ascender/descender lines, pencil grips, sand trays
- **Joy:** Sand-tray and shaving-foam letter writing.

### Lesson 8 — The /igh/ sound: igh, ie, i-e, y, i
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can read and spell /igh/ as igh, ie, i-e, y and i, and use y for /igh/ at the end of short words." (UK NC Y2 SP — /ai/ spelled -y at end of words; CCSS RF.1.3c)
- **Hook:** "Why is 'fly' spelled with a y but 'high' with igh? Let's crack the code."
- **Key activity:** I do: sort night, pie, bike, fly, find. We do: notice y at the end of short words (my, by, try, cry, sky). You do: decodable passage; spell 6 words.
- **Check for understanding:** "Kai spelled 'try' as 'trigh'. What tip would you give him?" → At the end of a short word, /igh/ is usually spelled y — try.
- **Differentiation:** support: igh and i-e only / stretch: find /igh/ in find, kind, mind, behind (common exception words) and explain the pattern
- **Materials:** word cards, sound houses, decodable passage
- **Joy:** Night-sky sort — words written on paper stars.

### Lesson 9 — Plop's feelings: character and evidence
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can say how Plop feels at different points in the story and point to the words that show it." (UK NC Y2 RC — discuss characters, infer feelings; CCSS RL.2.3)
- **Hook:** "Plop says 'Dark is nasty!' — how do you know how he's feeling without him saying 'I'm scared'?"
- **Key activity:** Read the next chapters. I do: model finding evidence ("He wobbled… so I think he's nervous"). We do: plot Plop's feelings on a "feelings graph" across chapters. You do: pick one moment and give a feeling + evidence.
- **Check for understanding:** "Find one sentence that shows Plop is starting to change. What does it show?" → A sentence where he listens to someone describe the dark and becomes curious — it shows he is less scared.
- **Differentiation:** support: feelings picture cards to choose from / stretch: explain what causes each change in Plop's feelings
- **Materials:** *The Owl Who Was Afraid of the Dark*, feelings graph poster, feelings word cards
- **Joy:** Hot-seating: a child plays Plop and the class interviews him.

### Lesson 10 — The /oa/ sound: oa, ow, o-e, oe, o
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can read and spell /oa/ as oa, ow, o-e, oe and o." (UK NC Y2 SP / WR — Phase 5; CCSS RF.1.3c)
- **Hook:** "A goat, a bow, a bone, a toe and a cold nose walked into a story…"
- **Key activity:** I do: sort the silly-story words. We do: notice ow at the end (snow, grow, slow) and o on its own in words like go, no, cold, both, most, only (common exception words). You do: decodable passage; dictation.
- **Check for understanding:** "Which is the /oa/ spelling in 'snow' — and how is ow in 'cow' different?" → In snow, ow says /oa/; in cow, ow says /ow/ — the same letters can make two sounds.
- **Differentiation:** support: oa, ow, o-e only / stretch: find words where o alone says /oa/ (cold, gold, told, old) and explain
- **Materials:** word cards, sound houses, decodable passage
- **Joy:** Silly-story read-aloud with actions.

### Lesson 11 — Decodable reading: which sound? ow, ea and ie
- **Duration:** 22 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can try both sounds of a spelling like ow or ea and choose the one that makes a real word that fits." (UK NC Y2 WR — alternative pronunciations; CCSS RF.2.3c)
- **Hook:** "Read this: 'The cow sat in the snow.' Same letters, different sounds! How did you know?"
- **Key activity:** I do: model "try it both ways" (snow/cow; sea/head; pie/chief). We do: read sentence cards and vote on pronunciation. You do: decodable passage with mixed pronunciations; circle the words where you had to switch.
- **Check for understanding:** "You read 'head' as 'heed'. What should you do next?" → Try the other sound of ea (/e/) — 'head' — and check it makes sense.
- **Differentiation:** support: ow only / stretch: ou in shout/soup/could and explain the three sounds
- **Materials:** sentence cards, decodable passage, "try it both ways" bookmark
- **Joy:** "Switcheroo" — a flip card that swaps the sound and makes silly non-words.

### Lesson 12 — Poetry: "Dark is…" — listening and performing
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can listen to a poem, say what I notice about its words and rhythm, and perform part of it with expression." (UK NC Y2 RC — recite poems; SL — perform; CCSS RL.2.4)
- **Hook:** Turn out the lights and read a night poem from *Out and About* (Shirley Hughes) by torchlight.
- **Key activity:** Read two seasonal/night poems twice. We do: spot rhymes and repeating words; clap the rhythm. You do: in pairs, learn four lines by heart and perform with actions and a "night voice".
- **Check for understanding:** "Why does the poet repeat that line? What does it make you feel?" → The repetition makes it sound like a chant/lullaby and sticks in your head — it builds the feeling of the night.
- **Differentiation:** support: echo-perform two lines with the teacher / stretch: add a new verse using the same pattern
- **Materials:** *Out and About* by Shirley Hughes, torches, percussion (shakers)
- **Joy:** Torchlit poetry performance.

### Lesson 13 — The /oo/ and /yoo/ sounds: oo, ue, ew, u-e
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can read and spell words with oo, ue, ew and u-e and hear the difference between /oo/ (moon) and /yoo/ (cube)." (UK NC Y2 SP — Phase 5; CCSS RF.1.3c)
- **Hook:** "Blue, grew, moon and flute all rhyme — but look at the spellings!"
- **Key activity:** I do: sort moon, blue, grew, flute; then few, cube, rescue (/yoo/). We do: sound-house sort. You do: decodable passage; dictation.
- **Check for understanding:** "Say 'moon' and 'music'. Which one has a /y/ sound hiding at the start of the u?" → Music — /myoo/.
- **Differentiation:** support: oo and ew only / stretch: short oo (book, foot) vs long oo (moon, food) — sort them
- **Materials:** word cards, sound houses, decodable passage
- **Joy:** "Moon-walk" — step on /oo/ words across the floor.

### Lesson 14 — Sentences: capital letters, full stops and I (review)
- **Duration:** 22 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can write sentences that start with a capital letter, end with a full stop and use a capital I." (UK NC Y2 VGP — demarcate sentences; CCSS L.1.2)
- **Hook:** A letter from Plop with no capitals or full stops: "Help! I can't tell where my sentences stop!"
- **Key activity:** I do: read the letter without punctuation — out of breath. We do: fix it together. You do: write three sentences about the dark, then swap and check a partner's with a "capital and full stop" checklist.
- **Check for understanding:** "How do you know where a sentence ends when you read it aloud?" → Where the idea is complete and you'd pause; that's where the full stop goes.
- **Differentiation:** support: sentence frames and a word bank / stretch: include one sentence with a name (a proper noun) needing a capital
- **Materials:** Plop's letter, sentence strips, checklist cards
- **Joy:** Reading the unpunctuated letter aloud in one breath — then the fixed one.

### Lesson 15 — Vocabulary: owl and night words
- **Duration:** 22 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can explain and use five new words from the book in my own sentences." (UK NC Y2 VGP — vocabulary; CCSS L.2.4)
- **Hook:** "Plop meets the word 'nocturnal'. What could it mean? Let's be word detectives."
- **Key activity:** Teach 5 Tier 2/3 words from the text (nocturnal, feathers, fireworks, beautiful, exciting) with pictures, actions and "say it in a sentence". Sort them on a "how I feel about the dark" line (scary → wonderful).
- **Check for understanding:** "Is an owl nocturnal? Is a person? Explain." → An owl is — it's active at night; people are mostly active in the day.
- **Differentiation:** support: picture cards and gesture for each word / stretch: find synonyms for scary (spooky, frightening, eerie) and rank them
- **Materials:** word cards with pictures, the class text, feelings line
- **Joy:** Word "charades" — act the word, the class guesses.

### Lesson 16 — Planning my "Dark is…" poem
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can plan a poem by collecting ideas and describing words about the dark." (UK NC Y2 WC — plan by noting ideas; CCSS W.2.5)
- **Hook:** "Each person Plop meets says 'Dark is…' something different: kind, fun, exciting. What is dark to you?"
- **Key activity:** Re-read the "Dark is…" pattern from the book. Senses web: what can you see, hear, feel in the dark? Talk before you write: say each line aloud to a partner before noting it.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why do we say our lines aloud before writing them?" → To hear if they make sense and sound good, and fix them before writing.
- **Differentiation:** support: picture prompts and a word bank / stretch: include a line with a surprising comparison ("Dark is a warm blanket")
- **Materials:** senses web template, word bank, torches
- **Joy:** "Dark den" — ideas collected under a blanket fort.

### Lesson 17 — Writing my "Dark is…" poem
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can write a pattern poem of at least five lines using descriptive words." (UK NC Y2 WC — write poetry; CCSS W.2.3)
- **Hook:** "Poets pick every word on purpose. Today you are the poet."
- **Key activity:** I do: model writing two lines from the plan, thinking aloud about word choice. We do: one line together. You do: write the poem from your plan; re-read each line aloud before moving on.
- **Check for understanding:** "Which word in your poem did you choose most carefully, and why?" → A specific word and reason ("glittering, because the stars sparkle").
- **Differentiation:** support: poem frame "Dark is ___. Dark is ___." with word bank / stretch: add rhyme or repetition on purpose
- **Materials:** plans, poem paper, word bank
- **Joy:** Poems written on black paper with white pencil.

### Lesson 18 — Editing and performing my poem
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can re-read my poem to improve one word and fix one mistake, then perform it clearly." (UK NC Y2 WC — evaluate and edit, read aloud; SL — perform)
- **Hook:** "Every real poet redrafts. Let's make it even better."
- **Key activity:** Model an edit (swap a plain word for a stronger one; fix a capital). Pairs give one "star and wish". Children edit, then perform to the class with expression.
- **Check for understanding:** "What did you change and why is it better now?" → A named change and reason ("I changed 'nice' to 'silver' so you can see it").
- **Differentiation:** support: one edit with the teacher / stretch: two improvements and a performance with a pause for effect
- **Materials:** poems, editing pens, microphone (real or toy)
- **Joy:** Poetry café performance.

### Lesson 19 — Unit check: vowel spellings and reading fluency
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can show which vowel spellings I can read and spell, and read a passage accurately and smoothly." (UK NC Y2 WR/SP — assessment)
- **Hook:** "Sound code champions: show what you've cracked!"
- **Key activity:** Low-stakes check: read 20 words with mixed vowel spellings (real and nonsense), spell 10 dictated words, read a short passage aloud (the teacher notes accuracy and fluency), answer two questions on the class text. Update the phonics tracker.
- **Check for understanding:** "Spell 'play' and 'rain'. Why does one use ay and the other ai?" → ay is at the end of the word; ai is in the middle.
- **Differentiation:** support: fewer items, teacher reads questions aloud / stretch: two-syllable words and a longer passage
- **Materials:** word lists, dictation sheet, short passage, tracker
- **Joy:** "Sound code champion" sticker for each spelling mastered.

**End-of-unit check:** Read aloud a short passage containing every vowel spelling taught (at least 95% accuracy), spell eight dictated words choosing the correct alternative spelling, and explain how Plop changed from the start to the end of the book, using one clue.

## Unit 2 — Longer words and lively descriptions

**Essential question:** How do I read long words smoothly, and how do I choose words that paint a picture?

**Key vocabulary:** syllable, beat, chunk, fluency, expression, noun, adjective, verb, expanded noun phrase, comma, soft c, soft g, character, setting, story map, beginning, middle, end, edit

### Lesson 20 — Meet Traction Man: reading a comic-style picture book
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can read a picture book that uses speech bubbles and captions and explain how the pictures add to the words." (UK NC Y2 RC — discuss books; CCSS RL.2.7)
- **Hook:** "A toy action hero in a kitchen sink? Where do you read first on this page?"
- **Key activity:** Read *Traction Man Is Here!* by Mini Grey; model reading order (caption, pictures, speech bubbles). We do: find one joke that only works because of the picture. You do: pick a favourite page and tell a partner what the words say and what the picture adds.
- **Check for understanding:** "Find a page where the picture tells us something the words don't. What is it?" → e.g. the words sound heroic but the picture shows he's only in the bath or the sink — the joke is in the picture.
- **Differentiation:** support: echo-read captions / stretch: explain why the author uses dramatic words for small events
- **Materials:** *Traction Man Is Here!* by Mini Grey, toy action figure
- **Joy:** A toy action figure "visits" the class and reappears in odd places all unit.

### Lesson 21 — The /or/ sound: or, aw, au, al, ore, our
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can read and spell /or/ as or, aw, au, al, ore and our." (UK NC Y2 SP — /ɔ:/ spelled a before l and ll; CCSS RF.1.3c)
- **Hook:** "Paul saw four more horses walk to the shore — six spellings of one sound in one sentence!"
- **Key activity:** I do: sound buttons in the sentence. We do: sort into sound houses; notice a before l and ll (ball, talk, walk, all, call). You do: decodable passage; dictation of ball, walk, saw, more.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why is 'walk' in the /or/ house even though we can't hear the l?" → al makes /or/ here — the l is silent in walk, talk and chalk.

## Unit 4 — Suffixes and letters home

**Essential question:** How do I add an ending to a word so it's still spelled correctly — and how do I write to someone far away?

**Key vocabulary:** root word, suffix, vowel suffix, consonant suffix, drop the e, double, change y to i, plural, past tense, present tense, irregular verb, letter, postcard, address, greeting, sign-off, character, adventure

### Lesson 59 — Meet Flat Stanley: predicting from a strange event
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can predict what might happen to a character after a strange event and give a reason." (UK NC Y2 RC — predict; CCSS RL.2.1)
- **Hook:** "One morning a noticeboard falls on Stanley in the night and he is… flat! What could a flat boy do that you can't?"
- **Key activity:** Read chapter 1 of *Flat Stanley* by Jeff Brown. We do: list ideas for "things a flat boy could do". You do: predict one adventure and justify it with a detail from the chapter.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why is sliding under a door a good prediction for Stanley?" → He is now only about half an inch thick, so he could fit through a small gap.
- **Differentiation:** support: picture prompts and "I think… because…" starter / stretch: predict a problem being flat might cause, too
- **Materials:** *Flat Stanley* by Jeff Brown, paper, a cardboard "Flat Stanley" cut-out
- **Joy:** Make a paper Flat Stanley who will "travel" during the unit.

### Lesson 60 — Just add it on: -ing, -ed, -er, -est
- **Duration:** 22 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can add a suffix to a word that needs no spelling change." (UK NC Y2 SP — adding suffixes; CCSS L.2.2d)
- **Hook:** "jump → jumping, jumped, jumper. Did the root word change at all?"
- **Key activity:** I do: root word + suffix with word-and-suffix cards (jump + ing; fast + est). We do: build words and read them in sentences about Stanley. You do: add suffixes to 10 words and use two in sentences.
- **Check for understanding:** "What happens to 'help' when you add -ed, and why don't you change anything?" → helped — it ends in two consonants, so you just add the suffix.
- **Differentiation:** support: -ing and -ed only / stretch: sort which suffixes make a verb, which make a comparing adjective
- **Materials:** root-word and suffix cards, whiteboards
- **Joy:** "Word train" — suffix carriages coupled to root-word engines.

### Lesson 61 — Drop the e (hope → hoping)
- **Duration:** 22 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can drop a silent e before adding a vowel suffix." (UK NC Y2 SP — drop e before -ing, -ed, -er, -est)
- **Hook:** "hope + ing = hopeing? Something looks wrong!"
- **Key activity:** I do: the silent e steps aside when a vowel suffix arrives (hope → hoping, hoped; nice → nicer); it stays for consonant suffixes (hopeful). We do: build words with magnetic letters. You do: 10 words, then sentences.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why do we keep the e in 'hopeful' but drop it in 'hoping'?" → -ful starts with a consonant, so the e stays; -ing starts with a vowel, so the e drops.
- **Differentiation:** support: -ing only with magnetic letters / stretch: explain 'being' (the e in be isn't a silent e, so it stays)
- **Materials:** magnetic letters, word cards, "e-eraser" (a small rubber)
- **Joy:** The "e-eraser" — rub out the e with a flourish.

### Lesson 62 — Reading *Flat Stanley*: character and cause
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can explain why a character does something, using evidence from the story." (UK NC Y2 RC — inference; CCSS RL.2.3)
- **Hook:** "Why does Stanley agree to be flown like a kite? Would you?"
- **Key activity:** Read the kite chapter. We do: build a "because" chain (Arthur feels left out → Stanley is kind → they share the kite). You do: explain one choice Stanley makes with "because" and a quote.
- **Check for understanding:** "How do the brothers feel about each other? Find two clues." → Mixed — they love each other but Arthur is sometimes jealous; clues from his actions and words.
- **Differentiation:** support: choose the reason from three cards / stretch: compare Stanley and Arthur's feelings at two moments
- **Materials:** *Flat Stanley*, because-chain strips
- **Joy:** Fly a paper Stanley kite outside.

### Lesson 63 — Double the consonant (run → running)
- **Duration:** 22 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can double the final consonant of a short-vowel word before a vowel suffix." (UK NC Y2 SP — doubling)
- **Hook:** "Is it 'runing' or 'running'? Which one sounds like 'ruin'?"
- **Key activity:** I do: one short vowel + one consonant at the end → double before -ing, -ed, -er, -est (run → running, hop → hopped, big → bigger). Not for two consonants (jump → jumping) or x (box → boxing). We do: sort. You do: 10 words, then sentences.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why do we double the p in 'hopping' but not in 'hoping'?" → hop has a short vowel and one consonant, so we double to keep it short; hope has a silent e that drops.
- **Differentiation:** support: -ing only with CVC words / stretch: mixed words where the child decides "double, drop or just add"
- **Materials:** word cards, doubling "twins" cards, whiteboards
- **Joy:** "Twin letters" — a pair of puppets jump in to double.

### Lesson 64 — y → i (happy → happier)
- **Duration:** 22 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can change a final y to i before -er/-est/-ed (but keep y before -ing)." (UK NC Y2 SP — y to i)
- **Hook:** "happy + er = happyer? The y is tired — it turns into an i!"
- **Key activity:** I do: after a consonant, y changes to i before -er, -est, -ed (happier, cried); keep y before -ing (crying) because English avoids ii; after a vowel keep y (played). We do: sort. You do: 10 words, then sentences.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why is it 'played' but 'cried'?" → In play, y comes after a vowel (a), so it stays; in cry it comes after a consonant, so it changes to i.
- **Differentiation:** support: happier/happiest pairs only / stretch: find a word that keeps y before -ing and explain (flying, crying)
- **Materials:** word cards, y/i flip cards
- **Joy:** y/i flip cards the child "transforms".

### Lesson 65 — Plurals: -s, -es and -ies
- **Duration:** 22 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can make plurals by adding -s, -es (after s, x, z, sh, ch) or -ies (after a consonant + y)." (UK NC Y2 SP — -es, y to ies)
- **Hook:** "One fox, two… foxs? One baby, two… babys?"
- **Key activity:** I do: -s most of the time; -es after hissing sounds (boxes, wishes, churches); consonant + y → ies (babies, cities); vowel + y just add s (keys, days). We do: sort. You do: pluralise 12 nouns from Stanley's travels.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why is it 'keys' but 'babies'?" → key ends in a vowel + y, so just add s; baby ends in a consonant + y, so change y to i and add es.
- **Differentiation:** support: -s and -es only / stretch: irregular plurals (children, mice, feet) as a preview
- **Materials:** noun cards, plural rule mats
- **Joy:** "Doubling machine" box — one goes in, many come out.

### Lesson 66 — Suffix sort (which rule?) — retrieval
- **Duration:** 22 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can choose the right suffix rule for any root word." (UK NC Y2 SP — retrieval)
- **Hook:** "Four rules, one mystery word. Which rule will you use?"
- **Key activity:** Retrieval: the four rules (just add, drop e, double, y → i). Sort 20 root words by the rule they need before adding -ing or -ed, then write them. Explain choices to a partner.
- **Check for understanding:** "Which rule does 'shop + ing' need, and how do you know?" → Double — short vowel o and a single consonant p, so shopping.
- **Differentiation:** support: two rules at a time / stretch: words where two rules compete (try → tried vs trying)
- **Materials:** sorting mats with four rule houses, word cards
- **Joy:** Sort race against the teacher.

### Lesson 67 — Word-builders: -ful, -less, -ness, -ly, -ment
- **Duration:** 22 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can add a meaning-changing suffix to build a new word." (UK NC Y2 SP — -ment, -ness, -ful, -less, -ly)
- **Hook:** "Add -less to 'fear' and you get a hero. Add -ful and you get… a scaredy cat?"
- **Key activity:** I do: each suffix's meaning (-ful full of, -less without, -ness a state, -ly how, -ment the act/result). -ful has one l. After consonant + y, change y to i (happiness, happily). We do: build words and define them. You do: describe Stanley with built words.
- **Check for understanding:** "What does 'careless' mean, and how does the suffix help you know?" → Without care — -less means without.
- **Differentiation:** support: -ful and -less only / stretch: -ness and -ly with y → i (happiness, happily)

- **Joy:** Build a "superhero name" from suffix words (the Fearless, the Helpful).

### Lesson 68 — Past and present tense

### Lesson 69 — Handwriting: joining suffixes

- **Joy:** Handwriting "hall of fame" board.

## End-of-year mastery checks

1. **Decoding:** reads unfamiliar real and nonsense words with any Phase 5 alternative spelling, and syllable-chops unfamiliar three- and four-syllable words. *(Seen: phonics word list, 90%+ accuracy.)*
2. **Fluency:** reads an unseen age-appropriate passage accurately and with expression at around 90 words correct per minute. *(Seen: one-minute timed read, noted without pressure.)*
3. **Comprehension:** answers retrieval questions and makes an inference backed by a clue; compares two versions of a tale. *(Seen: talk and short written answers.)*
4. **Spelling:** applies the four suffix rules, plurals (-s/-es/-ies), and spells most Year 1 common exception words and taught alternatives correctly. *(Seen: dictation.)*
5. **Punctuation:** uses capitals, full stops, question and exclamation marks, commas in lists, and apostrophes for contraction and singular possession. *(Seen: independent writing.)*
6. **Grammar for writing:** uses expanded noun phrases, co-ordination and subordination (and/but/or/because/when/if/that) and consistent past or present tense, including the progressive. *(Seen: independent writing.)*
7. **Composition:** plans, writes, edits and publishes a structured story and a non-fiction piece for a real reader. *(Seen: published work in portfolio.)*
8. **Handwriting:** forms letters of correct relative size with even spacing, and uses diagonal and horizontal joins in common letter pairs. *(Seen: best copy.)*
9. **Speaking:** retells a tale, performs a poem by heart and gives a short talk, speaking clearly and answering a question. *(Seen: performance.)*

## Teacher guidance

**Phonics first, every day.** The class phonics tracker from Lesson 1 drives daily 5-minute warm-ups. Any child not secure in Phase 3–5 gets an additional short catch-up session daily (small group or one-to-one) from the programme's "keep-up" materials — Year 2 cannot build on insecure decoding. Always give children books they can decode; don't send home books that require guessing from pictures.

**Common misconceptions and fixes**
- *Guessing from the first letter or picture* → cover the picture, point to each grapheme, "sound it out, then say it".
- *Choosing the wrong alternative spelling* (e.g. "rane") → use sound houses and position rules (ay at the end; -dge after a short vowel), then a word list to check.
- *Suffix mistakes* (hopeing, runing, happyer) → the four-rule sort; say the rule aloud before writing.
- *Apostrophes in plurals* ("apple's") → ask "Is something missing? Does something belong?" If neither, no apostrophe.
- *Homophone confusion* → check by meaning (they're = they are).
- *Run-on sentences* → read work aloud; where you pause and the idea ends, put a full stop.
- *Tense slips* → "When did it happen?" check on every verb.
- *Robot reading* → echo-reading and repeated reading; model phrasing.

**Making it international.** The spine deliberately includes tales and settings from China, Zimbabwe, Kenya, the Caribbean, the Amazon and the Kalahari, and letters that "travel" the world. Invite families to share traditional tales and rhymes from their own cultures and languages — they fit directly into Unit 5.

**Vertical alignment.** Foundation gives Phase 2–5 phonics, first tricky words, letter formation, simple sentences with *and*, and retelling. Year 2 (ages 7–8) adds prefixes (*un-, dis-, mis-, re-, pre-*), *-ly/-ous/-tion* families, the Year 3/4 statutory word list, *a/an*, inverted commas for speech, paragraphs, recounts, and fully joined handwriting — so secure Year 1 suffix rules, apostrophes and sentence punctuation are its launchpad.

**App lessons.** Lessons 23, 24, 26–29, 42, 43, 60, 61, 63, 64, 66, 67, 80, 81, 84, 98–100, 102, 103, 118, 126 and 170 have fully authored, app-runnable versions in `lessons/`. They teach the same objective with their own short example texts; in class, connect them to the unit's class text in the hook and the recap.
