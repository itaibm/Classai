# Year 1 Geography (ages 6–7) — Scope & Sequence

> Part of **the best international primary school** (see [`../../../SCHOOL-CHARTER.md`](../../../SCHOOL-CHARTER.md)).
> Built to [`../../PLANNING-BRIEF.md`](../../PLANNING-BRIEF.md). Standards reference:
> `knowledge-base/subjects/history-geography/geography-ks1.md` (UK Key Stage 1 = our Foundation and Year 1).
> Codes: **UK NC KS1 Geography** (LK = locational knowledge, PK = place knowledge, HP = human & physical geography,
> SF = geographical skills & fieldwork).

## Year overview

Year 1 geography makes six-year-olds into **mapmakers and place detectives**. They learn to read and draw maps,
to go outside and collect real data (fieldwork), to name and locate the world's continents, oceans and their own
country, and to compare their own place with a very different place far away. Sustainability runs through the
year: every unit ends by asking how people can care for the places they've studied.

**What this year assumes (from Foundation, ages 5–6):** a map is a view from above; simple symbols and a key;
north, south, east and west; the seven continents and five oceans named on a globe; hot and cold places; daily
weather and the four seasons.

**How the year runs.** *Unit 1* builds map skills — plan views, symbols and keys, compass directions and
simple grid references — using the school grounds. *Unit 2* is a fieldwork study of the local area (land use,
traffic, likes and dislikes, one improvement). *Unit 3* zooms out: continents, oceans, the child's own country
and capital, the Equator and the poles. *Unit 4* compares the home area with a contrasting place: a small town
in the highlands of Kenya (with alternative case studies for teachers who prefer another region). *Unit 5* looks
at weather and climate around the world. *Unit 6* asks how we can care for our world — rainforests, oceans and
our own school.

**What Year 2 relies on:** reading a map with a key and four-point compass; using simple grid references;
locating the continents, oceans and own country; describing physical and human features with correct vocabulary;
collecting and presenting fieldwork data; comparing two places. Year 2 (ages 7–8) adds latitude and longitude,
hemispheres and time zones, settlements and rivers.

## Unit 1 — Maps and mapmakers

**Essential question:** How do maps help us understand and find our way around places?

**Key vocabulary:** map, plan, bird's-eye view, aerial photograph, symbol, key, compass, north, south, east, west, route, grid, grid reference, left, right, near, far, scale

### Lesson 1 — A bird's-eye view
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can draw objects from above (plan view) and explain how a map is different from a picture." (UK NC KS1 SF — plan perspectives)
- **Hook:** "Imagine you're a bird flying over the classroom. What would the table look like?"
- **Key activity:** Draw around objects on a table from above (cup, book, pencil case); compare side view and plan view; draw a bird's-eye plan of a desk.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why does a cup look like a circle on a map?" → From above you only see the round top, not the sides.
- **Differentiation:** support: trace around real objects / stretch: plan of a whole table with several objects in the right places
- **Materials:** everyday objects, paper, pencils, a toy bird or drone picture
- **Joy:** Standing on a (safe) chair to see the "bird's-eye view".

### Lesson 2 — Aerial photographs and maps
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can match features on an aerial photo to the same features on a map." (UK NC KS1 SF — aerial photographs)
- **Hook:** "This is our school from a satellite! Can you find your classroom?"
- **Key activity:** Use an aerial photo (e.g. from an online satellite map) and a simple map of the school or local area; find and match features (field, roads, buildings, trees).
- **Check for understanding:** "What can you see on the aerial photo that the map leaves out? Why?" → e.g. cars and shadows — maps show only the important, permanent features.
- **Differentiation:** support: match 3 features with a partner / stretch: find a route on both
- **Materials:** printed aerial photo and map of the school area, tablets with a satellite map (optional)
- **Joy:** Finding your own home or school from space.

### Lesson 3 — Symbols and keys
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can use and design symbols and a key for a map." (UK NC KS1 SF — simple maps and keys)
- **Hook:** "Maps can't write 'tree' everywhere — they use a secret code!"
- **Key activity:** Read symbols on real maps; design clear symbols for school features (library, playground, office); make a key.
- **Check for understanding:** "Here is a map with three symbols but no key. What problem will a visitor have, and how would you fix it?" → They can't tell what the symbols mean (is the triangle a tent or a tree?); add a key that shows each symbol next to its meaning.
- **Differentiation:** support: match given symbols to pictures / stretch: design symbols that work without colour
- **Materials:** local maps, symbol cards, paper
- **Joy:** Inventing a secret-code map.

### Lesson 4 — North, south, east and west
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can use a compass and the four compass points to give and follow directions." (UK NC KS1 SF — four compass directions)
- **Hook:** "Which way is north? Let's ask a compass!"
- **Key activity:** Use a real compass outside; label N, S, E, W on walls; play "compass says" (take 3 steps east); mnemonic (e.g. "Naughty Elephants Squirt Water", clockwise from north).
- **Check for understanding:** "If you face north and turn to your right, which way are you facing?" → East.
- **Differentiation:** support: labelled compass rose on the floor / stretch: introduce north-east etc. as a preview
- **Materials:** magnetic compasses, compass-point signs, playground chalk
- **Joy:** Compass treasure hunt.

### Lesson 5 — Grid references
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can find and give the position of something on a map using a simple grid reference (e.g. B3)." (UK NC KS1 SF — locational language)
- **Hook:** "Where's the treasure? It's in square C2!"
- **Key activity:** Floor grid with letters and numbers; place objects and give references; then on paper maps; play "battleships"-style finding games.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why do we say the letter first (C) and then the number (2)?" → So everyone reads the same square — we agree an order, like 'along the corridor, then up the stairs'.
- **Differentiation:** support: 3 × 3 grid / stretch: 6 × 6 grid and giving routes square by square
- **Materials:** floor grid (tape), grid maps, counters
- **Joy:** Treasure-map grid game.

### Lesson 6 — Routes and directions
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can describe a route on a map using compass directions and landmarks." (UK NC KS1 SF — directional language; link: Maths position)
- **Hook:** "Rosie the hen goes for a walk. Can you map her route?"
- **Key activity:** Read *Rosie's Walk* by Pat Hutchins; draw her route; then plan a route from the classroom to the hall using directions and landmarks; partner follows.
- **Check for understanding:** "Which landmark helped your partner most, and why?" → A clear, easy-to-see feature (e.g. the big tree).
- **Differentiation:** support: route with picture landmarks / stretch: two different routes and compare
- **Materials:** *Rosie's Walk* by Pat Hutchins, school map, direction cards
- **Joy:** Following each other's routes.

### Lesson 7 — Unit check: map our school
- **Duration:** 30 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can make a map of our school grounds with symbols, a key, a compass and a grid." (UK NC KS1 SF — assessment)
- **Hook:** "New children are arriving. They need a map!"
- **Key activity:** In pairs, walk the grounds and make a map; add key, compass, grid; test it with another pair.
- **Check for understanding:** "Use your map to tell me where the playground is using a grid reference and a direction." → e.g. "In B4, north of the hall."
- **Differentiation:** support: base map to add symbols to / stretch: add a route with directions
- **Materials:** clipboards, base maps, compasses
- **Joy:** A map used by real new children.

**End-of-unit check:** On a map of the school, find three places using a key and grid references, and give directions from one to another using north, south, east and west.

## Unit 2 — Our local area: fieldwork

**Essential question:** What is our local area like, and how could we make it better?

**Key vocabulary:** local, area, fieldwork, survey, tally, human feature, physical feature, house, shop, office, factory, farm, park, road, river, hill, land use, traffic, opinion, improve, sustainable

### Lesson 8 — Human and physical features
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can sort features of our area into human (made by people) and physical (natural)." (UK NC KS1 HP — vocabulary)
- **Hook:** Photo cards: a river, a supermarket, a hill, a bridge. "Who made each one — people or nature?"
- **Key activity:** Sort local and world photos; learn vocabulary (beach, cliff, coast, forest, hill, mountain, river, valley; city, town, village, factory, farm, port, shop); spot examples on a local map.
- **Check for understanding:** "Is a park a human or physical feature? Explain." → Human — people designed and made it, even though it has natural things like grass.
- **Differentiation:** support: 6 cards with clear examples / stretch: tricky cases (a reservoir, a canal)
- **Materials:** photo cards, local map, sorting hoops
- **Joy:** "Human or nature?" quick-fire game.

### Lesson 9 — Planning our fieldwork
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can plan a fieldwork walk: our question, what we will record and how to stay safe." (UK NC KS1 SF — fieldwork)
- **Hook:** "Geographers go outside to find out. What do we want to know about our area?"
- **Key activity:** Choose questions (What buildings are there? How busy is the road? What do people like here?); design tally charts and a land-use map; agree safety rules.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why is a tally chart a good way to record cars passing?" → It's quick to add a mark each time and easy to count in fives.
- **Differentiation:** support: pre-made recording sheets / stretch: design your own tally chart
- **Materials:** clipboards, tally sheets, base maps, hi-vis vests
- **Joy:** Becoming "real geographers" with clipboards.

### Lesson 10 — Fieldwork walk
- **Duration:** 30 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can collect data outside by mapping, counting and photographing." (UK NC KS1 SF — observational skills)
- **Hook:** "Geographers, clipboards ready!"
- **Key activity:** Walk the local area with adults: mark land use on a base map (homes, shops, green space), tally traffic for 5 minutes, photograph features, record likes and dislikes.
- **Check for understanding:** "What did you notice most about our area?" → A specific observation with evidence (e.g. "lots of houses — I coloured 12 houses red").
- **Differentiation:** support: one job (tally cars) / stretch: two jobs and a sketch
- **Materials:** clipboards, base maps, coloured pencils, cameras, hi-vis vests
- **Joy:** The walk itself.

### Lesson 11 — What did we find? Presenting data
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can show our fieldwork data in a pictogram or block diagram and say what it shows." (UK NC KS1 SF; link: Maths statistics)
- **Hook:** "Our numbers can tell a story. What story?"
- **Key activity:** Turn tallies into block diagrams; colour the land-use map; answer questions ("What is the most common land use?").
- **Check for understanding:** "What does our traffic chart show about the busiest time?" → The time with the tallest bar had most cars.
- **Differentiation:** support: pre-drawn axes / stretch: compare two counts (morning vs afternoon)
- **Materials:** tally sheets, squared paper
- **Joy:** Class data display.

### Lesson 12 — What do people think? Likes, dislikes and improvements
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can give my opinion about our area, with a reason, and suggest one improvement." (UK NC KS1 HP; link: Life skills)
- **Hook:** "If you were mayor for a day, what would you change?"
- **Key activity:** Share likes/dislikes from fieldwork; vote on the biggest issue (litter, traffic, not enough trees); design one improvement (a crossing, more bins, a garden).
- **Check for understanding:** "How would your improvement make the area better for people or nature?" → A clear reason (e.g. a crossing keeps children safe).
- **Differentiation:** support: choose from improvement cards / stretch: think about who might disagree and why
- **Materials:** fieldwork data, design paper
- **Joy:** "Mayor for a day" plan.

### Lesson 13 — Unit check: our area report
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can present what our area is like using our fieldwork evidence." (UK NC KS1 SF/HP — assessment)
- **Hook:** "The town council wants to hear from young geographers!"
- **Key activity:** Create a one-page report: map, a chart, three facts, one improvement. Present (or send to the local council).
- **Check for understanding:** "What evidence from our fieldwork supports your improvement?" → Data from the survey (e.g. many cars counted).
- **Differentiation:** support: report frame / stretch: add a before-and-after sketch
- **Materials:** report frames, data
- **Joy:** Sending the report to a real council or head teacher.

**End-of-unit check:** Describe the local area using three human and two physical features, show one piece of fieldwork data in a chart, and suggest one improvement with a reason based on the evidence.

## Unit 3 — Our world: continents, oceans and my country

**Essential question:** Where are we in the world, and how is the world organised?

**Key vocabulary:** world, globe, atlas, continent, ocean, sea, country, capital city, border, Equator, North Pole, South Pole, hot, cold, Africa, Antarctica, Asia, Australia (Oceania), Europe, North America, South America, Atlantic, Pacific, Indian, Arctic, Southern

### Lesson 14 — The seven continents
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can name and locate the seven continents on a globe and world map." (UK NC KS1 LK — continents)
- **Hook:** Continent song: "Seven continents — can you find them all?"
- **Key activity:** Retrieve Foundation learning; label a world map; continent jigsaw; compare globe and flat map.
- **Check for understanding:** "Antarctica is a continent, but the Arctic is not. Use the globe to explain why." → Antarctica is a huge area of land (covered in ice); the Arctic around the North Pole is mostly frozen ocean, with no continent underneath.
- **Differentiation:** support: 4 continents with colour cues / stretch: order continents by size
- **Materials:** globe, world maps, continent jigsaw
- **Joy:** Continent song with actions.

### Lesson 15 — The five oceans
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can name and locate the five oceans and say which continents they touch." (UK NC KS1 LK — oceans)
- **Hook:** "Most of the Earth is water. Which ocean is the biggest?"
- **Key activity:** Label oceans; find which continents each ocean touches; ocean animals match.
- **Check for understanding:** "A ship sails from Kenya to Australia. Which ocean does it cross, and how can you tell from the map?" → The Indian Ocean — it is the water between the east coast of Africa and the west coast of Australia.
- **Differentiation:** support: 3 oceans / stretch: plan a sea voyage from our country to another continent and name the oceans crossed
- **Materials:** globe, world maps, ocean animal cards
- **Joy:** "Voyage" game with toy boats on a floor map.

### Lesson 16 — My country and its capital
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can locate my country on a map, name its capital city and describe its neighbours and seas." (UK NC KS1 LK — own country)
- **Hook:** "Zoom in from space to our country — then to our town!"
- **Key activity:** Use a globe, atlas and map to zoom in: continent → country → capital → our town. Worked example: the UK's four countries and capitals (London, Cardiff, Edinburgh, Belfast); children do their own country (and families share countries they're from).
- **Check for understanding:** "Someone says 'Kenya is bigger than Africa.' Explain why that can't be right, using the words country and continent." → Kenya is one country inside the continent of Africa; a continent is a huge area of land made up of many countries (Africa has 54), so it must be bigger.
- **Differentiation:** support: labelled map to colour / stretch: name neighbouring countries and a sea
- **Materials:** atlases, country maps, "zoom" nesting cards
- **Joy:** "Zoom" nesting cards from space to classroom.

### Lesson 17 — Countries and capital cities around the world
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can locate some countries and capitals on different continents, including those our families come from." (UK NC KS1 LK)
- **Hook:** "Our class has connections all over the world. Let's map them!"
- **Key activity:** Class map with pins for family countries; locate five capitals (e.g. Nairobi, Tokyo, Brasília, Canberra, Cairo); postcard pictures.
- **Check for understanding:** "Tokyo, Asia, Japan: which is the city, which is the country and which is the continent? Show how they fit inside each other." → Tokyo is a (capital) city inside the country Japan, which is inside the continent of Asia — like nesting boxes.
- **Differentiation:** support: match pictures to places / stretch: find each capital's continent and nearest ocean
- **Materials:** world map, pins, capital-city picture cards
- **Joy:** Family-connection map.

### Lesson 18 — Hot and cold places: the Equator and the poles
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can locate the Equator and the North and South Poles and explain why places near the Equator are hot." (UK NC KS1 LK/HP — hot and cold areas)
- **Hook:** "Why is it hot in Brazil and freezing in Antarctica?"
- **Key activity:** Torch-on-globe demonstration (direct light at the Equator is concentrated, spread out at the poles); colour hot and cold zones; place animals.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why is it colder near the poles?" → Sunlight hits the poles at a low angle and is spread over a bigger area, so it's weaker.
- **Differentiation:** support: colour-coded zones / stretch: explain why mountains near the Equator can have snow (height)
- **Materials:** globe, torch, world map, animal cards
- **Joy:** Torch demonstration in the dark.

### Lesson 19 — Unit check: world explorer quiz
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can locate continents, oceans, my country, the Equator and the poles." (UK NC KS1 LK — assessment)
- **Hook:** "Passport stamps for every right answer!"
- **Key activity:** Label a blank world map; passport stamps for tasks; explain hot/cold zones.
- **Check for understanding:** "Point to the Equator and name a continent it crosses." → e.g. Africa or South America.
- **Differentiation:** support: word bank / stretch: add capitals
- **Materials:** blank maps, passports
- **Joy:** Passport stamps.

**End-of-unit check:** Label the seven continents and five oceans on a blank world map, mark our country and its capital, and draw the Equator, explaining why places near it are hot.

## Unit 4 — Two places compared: home and a town in Kenya

**Essential question:** How is a place far away similar to and different from where I live?

**Key vocabulary:** Kenya, Africa, highlands, savannah, mountain, Mount Kenya, Equator, climate, farm, market, crops, tea, coffee, maize, school, journey, similar, different, compare, human feature, physical feature

### Lesson 20 — Where is Kenya?
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can locate Kenya, its capital and the Equator, and describe its position using compass directions." (UK NC KS1 LK/PK)
- **Hook:** "The Equator runs right through this country. What would you expect the weather to be like?"
- **Key activity:** Find Kenya on the globe (East Africa, Indian Ocean coast, crossed by the Equator); locate Nairobi and Mount Kenya; describe direction from home.
- **Check for understanding:** "Mount Kenya is right near the Equator, but it has snow on top. Why?" → It's very high; the higher you go, the colder it gets.
- **Differentiation:** support: labelled map / stretch: plan a route from home to Kenya naming continents and oceans
- **Materials:** globe, atlas, map of Kenya, photos of Mount Kenya
- **Joy:** "Flight to Kenya" role play with passports.

### Lesson 21 — Physical features: land and weather
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can describe the physical features and weather of the Kenyan highlands and compare them with home." (UK NC KS1 PK/HP)
- **Hook:** Photos: green farmland, a mountain, savannah with animals. "Is this what you expected Africa to look like?"
- **Key activity:** Examine photos; describe physical features (mountain, hills, rivers, forests, savannah) and weather (long and short rainy seasons, warm days); compare with home in a table.
- **Check for understanding:** "What is one physical feature in both places, and one found only in Kenya?" → e.g. both have hills; Kenya has a snow-capped mountain on the Equator (or savannah).
- **Differentiation:** support: picture comparison / stretch: explain why there are "rainy seasons" rather than four seasons near the Equator
- **Materials:** photo pack of the Kenyan highlands, comparison table
- **Joy:** Photo "detective" zoom frames.

### Lesson 22 — Human features: homes, farms and markets
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can describe human features of a Kenyan highland town (homes, farms, markets, school) and compare them with home." (UK NC KS1 PK/HP)
- **Hook:** "What would you buy at a Kenyan market?"
- **Key activity:** Photos and a short video of a market, tea farms, homes and school; list human features; compare with local shops and farms.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why are tea and coffee grown in the Kenyan highlands?" → The climate there is warm with plenty of rain and good soil — right for those crops.
- **Differentiation:** support: picture sort / stretch: explain how a product (tea) might travel to our shops
- **Materials:** photo pack, market role-play props (fruit, baskets), a tea packet from Kenya
- **Joy:** Market role play.

### Lesson 23 — A day in the life of a child in Kenya
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can compare a day in the life of a child in Kenya with my own day, avoiding stereotypes." (UK NC KS1 PK)
- **Hook:** "Is every child in Africa the same? Is every child in our country the same?"
- **Key activity:** Read *Lila and the Secret of Rain* by David Conway and *Handa's Surprise* by Eileen Browne; watch or read an authentic account of a child's day (travel, school, play, food, chores); compare with own day in a Venn.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why is it wrong to say 'everyone in Africa lives in a village'?" → Africa is a huge continent with 54 countries, big cities like Nairobi and many different ways of life.
- **Differentiation:** support: picture Venn / stretch: write questions to ask a child in Kenya
- **Materials:** *Lila and the Secret of Rain*, *Handa's Surprise*, Venn hoops
- **Joy:** Letters to a partner school (if possible).

### Lesson 24 — Same and different: comparing two places
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can compare our area and the Kenyan town using human and physical features and weather." (UK NC KS1 PK — compare)
- **Hook:** "Two places, one big comparison chart!"
- **Key activity:** Complete a class comparison chart (weather, land, homes, jobs, transport, school); write "Both places…", "In Kenya…, but here…".
- **Check for understanding:** "Give one similarity and one difference and explain the reason for the difference." → e.g. both have schools; Kenya has two rainy seasons but we have four seasons because Kenya is on the Equator (if our home has four seasons; a school in the tropics compares wet and dry seasons instead).
- **Differentiation:** support: sentence frames / stretch: explain two differences with reasons
- **Materials:** comparison chart, photos, fieldwork data from Unit 2
- **Joy:** Comparison gallery.

### Lesson 25 — Caring for places: wildlife and people in Kenya
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can explain how people in Kenya protect wildlife and land, and how that connects to our lives." (UK NC KS1 HP; link: Science — Wangari Maathai)
- **Hook:** "Elephants, lions and giraffes live in Kenya's national parks. Who looks after them?"
- **Key activity:** National parks, rangers and tree-planting (the Green Belt Movement); discuss why protecting wildlife helps people too (jobs, water, soil).
- **Check for understanding:** "How does planting trees help both people and animals?" → Trees hold soil, keep water, give shade and homes for animals, and wood and fruit for people.
- **Differentiation:** support: picture cards / stretch: compare with a conservation project at home
- **Materials:** national park photos, *Wangari's Trees of Peace*
- **Joy:** Design a ranger badge.

### Lesson 26 — Unit check: two places
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can present a fair comparison of our area and the Kenyan town." (UK NC KS1 PK — assessment)
- **Hook:** "Make a travel guide for a Kenyan child visiting us — and for us visiting them!"
- **Key activity:** Double-page guide with a map, two physical and two human features for each place, and a comparison sentence.
- **Check for understanding:** "What would surprise a Kenyan child most about our area, and why?" → A reasoned difference.
- **Differentiation:** support: guide frame / stretch: add weather data comparison
- **Materials:** guide templates, photos
- **Joy:** Travel-guide display.

*Alternative case studies (same lesson shape):* a village in the Andes of Peru, a town on the coast of Vietnam, or an island community in Fiji — choose one with authentic photo sources and, ideally, a partner school.

**End-of-unit check:** Locate Kenya and the Equator, describe two physical and two human features of the Kenyan town, and compare it with home with one reason for a difference.

## Unit 5 — Weather and climate around the world

**Essential question:** How does weather change, and why is it different in different places?

**Key vocabulary:** weather, climate, temperature, rainfall, wind, sunny, cloudy, rainy, snowy, stormy, forecast, season, rainy season, dry season, thermometer, rain gauge, wind sock, symbol

### Lesson 27 — Weather or climate?
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can explain the difference between weather (today) and climate (usually, over many years)." (UK NC KS1 HP — weather patterns)
- **Hook:** "It's raining today — does that mean it's a rainy place?"
- **Key activity:** Sort statements into weather and climate; compare today's weather with a city on another continent using a weather website.
- **Check for understanding:** "'The Sahara is hot and dry' — weather or climate? Why?" → Climate — it describes what it's usually like over many years.
- **Differentiation:** support: picture sort / stretch: explain how one rainy day in a desert doesn't change its climate
- **Materials:** statement cards, weather website or printout
- **Joy:** World weather reporter.

### Lesson 28 — Build a weather station
- **Duration:** 30 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can make and use simple instruments to measure weather (thermometer, rain gauge, wind sock)." (UK NC KS1 SF — fieldwork; link: Science, Maths)
- **Hook:** "Can we predict the weather better than the TV forecast?"
- **Key activity:** Make a rain gauge (cut bottle — adult cuts) and a wind sock; place a thermometer; record daily for two weeks.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why must the rain gauge be in an open space, not under a tree?" → Otherwise the tree would stop some rain getting in, so the reading would be wrong.
- **Differentiation:** support: record with symbols / stretch: measure rainfall in mm with a ruler
- **Materials:** plastic bottles (cut by an adult), rulers, fabric, thermometers, recording sheets
- **Joy:** Running a class weather station.

### Lesson 29 — Seasons around the world
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can explain that when it's summer in one half of the world, it's winter in the other, and that places near the Equator have wet and dry seasons." (UK NC KS1 HP — seasonal patterns)
- **Hook:** "In December, children in Australia go to the beach. Why?"
- **Key activity:** Globe and lamp model showing tilt (simple); compare seasons in our country, Australia and Kenya; calendar comparison.
- **Check for understanding:** "Explain why children in Sydney can go to the beach in December while children in Canada are building snowmen." → They are in different halves (hemispheres) of the Earth; because the Earth is tilted, in December the southern half leans towards the Sun (summer) and the northern half leans away (winter).
- **Differentiation:** support: picture calendar comparison / stretch: explain why the Equator doesn't have four seasons
- **Materials:** globe, lamp, season picture cards
- **Joy:** "Beach Christmas" photos from Australia.

### Lesson 30 — Extreme weather and staying safe
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can describe some extreme weather (storms, floods, heatwaves, snowstorms) and how people stay safe." (UK NC KS1 HP; link: PE & Health)
- **Hook:** "What should you do if there's a thunderstorm while you're outside?"
- **Key activity:** Photos of extreme weather around the world; safety rules for each; design a safety poster.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why should you drink more water and stay in the shade in a heatwave?" → Your body loses water when hot; shade keeps you cooler and prevents sunburn.
- **Differentiation:** support: match weather to safety pictures / stretch: explain how a forecast helps people prepare
- **Materials:** extreme weather photos, poster paper
- **Joy:** Weather-safety poster competition.

### Lesson 31 — Unit check: weather reporter
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can present a weather report using our station data and compare it with another place." (UK NC KS1 HP/SF — assessment)
- **Hook:** "Lights, camera, forecast!"
- **Key activity:** Read the class station data; present a report with symbols on a map; compare with a city on another continent.
- **Check for understanding:** "What does our rain gauge data show about this fortnight?" → e.g. which day was wettest and how much rain fell.
- **Differentiation:** support: script frame / stretch: make a prediction for tomorrow and explain it
- **Materials:** weather data, map, symbols, camera
- **Joy:** Filming a weather forecast.

**End-of-unit check:** Explain the difference between weather and climate with examples, read two days of data from the class weather station, and explain why seasons are opposite in the two halves of the world.

## Unit 6 — Caring for our world

**Essential question:** How can we look after our planet's special places?

**Key vocabulary:** environment, rainforest, ocean, pollution, plastic, litter, recycle, reduce, reuse, protect, endangered, habitat, sustainable, action

### Lesson 32 — Rainforests
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can locate the world's biggest rainforests and explain why they matter." (UK NC KS1 LK/HP; link: English Unit 8, *The Great Kapok Tree*)
- **Hook:** Rainforest soundscape: "Where in the world are we?"
- **Key activity:** Locate the Amazon, Congo and South-East Asian rainforests near the Equator; learn layers (forest floor, understorey, canopy, emergent); discuss why rainforests matter (animals, plants, rain, air).
- **Check for understanding:** "Why are rainforests found near the Equator?" → It's hot and very wet there all year — the conditions rainforests need.
- **Differentiation:** support: colour rainforests on a map / stretch: explain how cutting forests affects animals and people
- **Materials:** world map, rainforest photos, layer diagram
- **Joy:** Build a four-layer rainforest in a shoebox.

### Lesson 33 — Oceans and plastic
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can explain how plastic reaches the oceans and suggest ways to stop it." (UK NC KS1 HP; link: Science materials)
- **Hook:** Read *One Plastic Bag* by Miranda Paul: "How did one woman in the Gambia change her town?"
- **Key activity:** Trace a bottle's journey from a street drain to a river to the ocean; list ways to reduce plastic; count plastic in the school bins for a day.
- **Check for understanding:** "How can a bottle dropped in a street end up in the sea?" → Rain washes it into drains and rivers, which flow to the sea.
- **Differentiation:** support: picture journey sequence / stretch: plan a plastic-free lunch challenge
- **Materials:** *One Plastic Bag* by Miranda Paul, picture sequence cards, gloves for bin audit
- **Joy:** Plastic-free lunch challenge.

### Lesson 34 — Our school green plan
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can use fieldwork to find one environmental problem at school and plan an action to help." (UK NC KS1 SF/HP — sustainability)
- **Hook:** "Geographers don't just study the world — they help it!"
- **Key activity:** Quick audit (litter, lights left on, bins, green space); choose one action (a planting patch, recycling monitors, a litter-pick rota); make posters.
- **Check for understanding:** "How will you know if your action worked?" → Measure again later (e.g. count litter before and after).
- **Differentiation:** support: choose an action card / stretch: plan a before/after survey
- **Materials:** audit sheets, poster paper
- **Joy:** Launching the green plan in assembly.

### Lesson 35 — Year review: our geography passport
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can show my map skills, world knowledge and place comparison in a geography passport." (UK NC KS1 — end-of-year review)
- **Hook:** "Your geography passport is ready for its final stamps!"
- **Key activity:** Stations: map reading with a grid and compass, blank world map labelling, comparing two places, human/physical sort, weather data question.
- **Check for understanding:** "Use the map to direct me from the library to the gate using compass directions." → Accurate directions with landmarks.
- **Differentiation:** support: stations with picture prompts / stretch: bonus challenges (capitals, rainforests)
- **Materials:** station cards, passports, maps
- **Joy:** Completed passports with stamps.

**End-of-unit check:** Explain one way people harm and one way people protect rainforests or oceans, and describe our school green-plan action and how we will measure whether it worked.

## Teacher guidance

**Misconceptions:** "Africa is a country" → it is a continent of 54 countries; "maps are pictures" → maps show a view from above using symbols; "north means up the hill / towards the top of the room" → north is a direction on Earth — use a compass; "it's always hot in Africa" → Mount Kenya has snow; "the Equator is a real line" → it's an imaginary line on maps and globes.

**Avoiding stereotypes.** When studying a contrasting place, show cities and villages, wealth and hardship, modern and traditional life; use authentic photos and voices (a partner school is ideal); compare *fairly* — like with like.

**Local and international.** Unit 2 and the local parts of Units 3 and 6 are written for any country: substitute your own area, country and capital. Keep safety ratios for fieldwork and get permissions for photos.

**Vertical alignment.** Foundation gave map basics, continents and oceans by name, and weather. Year 2 (ages 7–8) moves to latitude and longitude, hemispheres and time zones, settlements and rivers — so secure grid references, compass directions and world-map knowledge are essential by the end of this year.
