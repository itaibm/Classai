# Year 1 Languages — Spanish (ages 6–7) — Scope & Sequence

> Part of **the best international primary school** (see [`../../../SCHOOL-CHARTER.md`](../../../SCHOOL-CHARTER.md)).
> Built to [`../../PLANNING-BRIEF.md`](../../PLANNING-BRIEF.md). Standards reference:
> `knowledge-base/subjects/languages/README.md` and `spanish-ks2.md` (pedagogy and topic sequence; the KB's
> Spanish begins at UK Year 3, so this is an **early-start** year). Codes: **CEFR** pre-A1 → A1 descriptors
> (L = listening, S = speaking, R = reading, W = writing, IC = intercultural) and **ACTFL** Novice Low/Mid.

## Year overview

Year 1 Spanish is a **listening and speaking year**. Six-year-olds learn a language best the way they learned
their first one: lots of meaningful, understandable input (songs, stories, games, actions, puppets), then
confident speaking in short chunks, with reading and writing of single familiar words only once the sounds are
secure. Every lesson follows the same rhythm: **greeting routine → song → new words with actions and pictures →
game that makes children say the words many times → one-minute "can-do" check → goodbye song.** Vocabulary is
recycled all year (each unit reuses numbers, colours and *me gusta*), so nothing is learned once and forgotten.

**Spanish phonics from day one.** Spanish spelling is very regular, so each unit teaches one sound rule: the five
pure vowels; silent *h* and the throaty *j*; *ñ* and *ll*; tapped *r* and rolled *rr*; *z* and *ce/ci*; *ch* and
*qu*; hard and soft *g*; and word stress with accents. By the end of the year children can read aloud familiar
words correctly — a huge head start for Year 2.

**Culture.** Spanish is spoken in more than 20 countries. Each unit visits one or two: Spain, Mexico, Argentina,
Colombia, Peru, Cuba, Chile, Guatemala, Equatorial Guinea and more — through songs, festivals (Día de los Muertos,
Los Reyes Magos, La Tomatina, Carnaval), food and stories. Where the two main accents differ (e.g. *z* and *ce/ci*
said /th/ in most of Spain and /s/ in Latin America; *zumo* vs *jugo*), both are taught as correct.

**What this year assumes:** nothing — it is the first year of Spanish. Children who already speak Spanish at home
become "expert helpers" and are stretched with reading and extended talk.

**What Year 2 relies on:** greetings and feelings, numbers 0–20, colours, family, animals, body, food, weather and
days as heard-and-spoken vocabulary; *me gusta / no me gusta*, *tengo*, *hay*; the Spanish sound rules; confidence
to speak in short sentences. Year 2 extends numbers to 31, adds classroom objects and gender (*el/la, un/una*),
and begins writing short phrases.

**Swapping the language.** The same unit structure works for another language — e.g. Hebrew
(`knowledge-base/subjects/hebrew/kita-1-2.md`) or French (`knowledge-base/subjects/french/french-foundations.md`):
keep the topics, rhythm and sound-rule lesson in each unit and substitute the target-language words, songs and
cultures.

## Unit 1 — ¡Hola! Greetings and feelings

**Essential question:** How do we greet people and say how we feel in Spanish?

**Key vocabulary:** hola, adiós, hasta luego, buenos días, buenas tardes, buenas noches, ¿Cómo te llamas?, Me llamo…, ¿Cómo estás?, Estoy bien / mal / regular, contento/contenta, triste, cansado/cansada, gracias, por favor, sí, no, la vocal (vowel)

### Lesson 1 — ¡Hola! Where in the world is Spanish spoken?
- **Duration:** 20 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can say hola and adiós and point to three countries where people speak Spanish." (CEFR pre-A1 S; IC)
- **Hook:** A puppet, Paco, arrives with a suitcase covered in flag stickers: "¡Hola! ¡Hola! ¡Hola!"
- **Key activity:** Paco greets each child; children wave and reply "¡Hola, Paco!". On a world map, stick flags for Spain, Mexico, Argentina, Colombia and Peru. Learn the goodbye song ("Adiós, amigos").
- **Check for understanding:** "Paco waves as he leaves. What do you say — hola or adiós? Why?" → Adiós — it means goodbye; hola is hello.
- **Differentiation:** support: wave-and-echo with the puppet / stretch: say "¡Hola! Me llamo…" to the puppet
- **Materials:** hand puppet, world map, flag stickers, suitcase
- **Joy:** Paco the travelling puppet (he'll visit every lesson).

### Lesson 2 — ¿Cómo te llamas? Me llamo…
- **Duration:** 20 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can ask someone's name and say my name in Spanish." (CEFR pre-A1 S/L)
- **Hook:** Paco has forgotten everyone's name! "¿Cómo te llamas?"
- **Key activity:** Model the question and answer with two puppets; pass a ball around the circle — whoever catches it answers "Me llamo…" and asks the next child.
- **Check for understanding:** "Paco asks '¿Cómo te llamas?'. What is he asking, and how do you answer?" → He's asking my name — "Me llamo [name]".
- **Differentiation:** support: answer with just the name after the model / stretch: ask and answer with "Y tú, ¿cómo te llamas?"
- **Materials:** two puppets, soft ball
- **Joy:** Name-ball circle game.

### Lesson 3 — Buenos días, buenas tardes, buenas noches
- **Duration:** 20 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can choose the right greeting for the morning, afternoon and night." (CEFR pre-A1 S; IC)
- **Hook:** A sun, a sunset and a moon on cards: "Spanish has a different greeting for each!"
- **Key activity:** Learn the three greetings with actions (sun rising, sun setting, sleeping); "What time is it?" game — show a picture, children shout the greeting.
- **Check for understanding:** "It's 8 o'clock in the morning. Which greeting do you use?" → Buenos días.
- **Differentiation:** support: two greetings / stretch: add "hasta luego" and "hasta mañana"
- **Materials:** sun/sunset/moon cards, clock
- **Joy:** Freeze-greeting game.

### Lesson 4 — The five Spanish vowels
- **Duration:** 20 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can say the five Spanish vowels a, e, i, o, u with their pure sounds." (CEFR pre-A1 phonology; KB pronunciation rules)
- **Hook:** "In English, 'a' can make lots of sounds. In Spanish, each vowel makes only ONE sound. Magic!"
- **Key activity:** Sing the vowel song ("A, E, I, O, U, el burro sabe más que tú" — the traditional rhyme); vowel actions; read hola, adiós, uno, mesa aloud with pure vowels.
- **Check for understanding:** "How is the Spanish 'i' in 'sí' different from the English name of the letter i?" → In Spanish, i always says /ee/.
- **Differentiation:** support: echo each vowel with an action / stretch: read three new words (mono, pera, luna) aloud correctly
- **Materials:** vowel cards, song
- **Joy:** The donkey vowel song.

### Lesson 5 — ¿Cómo estás? How are you?
- **Duration:** 20 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can ask how someone is and answer bien, mal or regular." (CEFR pre-A1 S/L)
- **Hook:** Paco looks sad… then happy… then so-so. "¿Cómo estás, Paco?"
- **Key activity:** Thumbs up (bien), down (mal), sideways (regular); pair dialogue; "feelings" mirror game.
- **Check for understanding:** "Your friend puts a thumb sideways and says 'regular'. How do they feel?" → OK / so-so — not great, not bad.
- **Differentiation:** support: answer with thumbs + one word / stretch: add "¿Y tú?" and "gracias"
- **Materials:** puppet, emoji cards
- **Joy:** Thumbs game.

### Lesson 6 — Feelings: contento, triste, cansado
- **Duration:** 20 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can say how I feel with 'Estoy…' and a feelings word." (CEFR pre-A1 S)
- **Hook:** Feelings faces on a spinner: "Spin it and act it!"
- **Key activity:** Teach contento/a, triste, cansado/a, enfadado/a with faces and actions; note that girls often say contenta and boys contento (an early glimpse of agreement); spin-and-say game.
- **Check for understanding:** "Paco yawns and stretches. What does he say?" → "Estoy cansado."
- **Differentiation:** support: point-and-say / stretch: "Estoy contento porque…" with an English reason (a bridge to Year 2)
- **Materials:** feelings spinner, face cards
- **Joy:** Feelings charades.

### Lesson 7 — Por favor and gracias: polite Spanish
- **Duration:** 20 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can use por favor, gracias and de nada in a short exchange." (CEFR pre-A1 S; IC)
- **Hook:** Paco grabs a sticker without asking. "¡Paco! ¿Qué se dice?" (What do you say?)
- **Key activity:** Role-play asking for stickers and toys: "Un [colour sticker], por favor." / "Gracias." / "De nada."
- **Check for understanding:** "Someone says 'gracias' to you. What do you reply?" → "De nada."
- **Differentiation:** support: gracias and por favor only / stretch: full exchange with a greeting
- **Materials:** stickers, puppet
- **Joy:** Sticker "shop".

### Lesson 8 — Unit check: my first conversation
- **Duration:** 20 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can greet, give my name, say how I feel and say goodbye in a short conversation." (CEFR pre-A1 S — assessment)
- **Hook:** "Paco's cousin Lola from Mexico is visiting. Let's talk to her!"
- **Key activity:** Pairs perform a 4-line conversation with puppets; the teacher listens and ticks can-do statements.
- **Check for understanding:** "Lola says '¿Cómo te llamas?' — answer and ask her back." → "Me llamo… ¿Y tú?"
- **Differentiation:** support: with the teacher and picture prompts / stretch: add a greeting for the time of day and a feeling with a reason
- **Materials:** puppets, can-do sheet
- **Joy:** Performing with puppets.

**End-of-unit check:** Hold a four-line puppet conversation: a time-of-day greeting, name, how you feel, goodbye — and say the five vowels.

## Unit 2 — Los números 0–20

**Essential question:** How do we count, say our age and play number games in Spanish?

**Key vocabulary:** cero, uno, dos, tres, cuatro, cinco, seis, siete, ocho, nueve, diez, once, doce, trece, catorce, quince, dieciséis, diecisiete, dieciocho, diecinueve, veinte, ¿Cuántos…?, ¿Cuántos años tienes?, Tengo … años, hay

### Lesson 9 — Uno, dos, tres: numbers 0–5
- **Duration:** 20 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can count from 0 to 5 in Spanish and show the number with my fingers." (CEFR pre-A1 L/S)
- **Hook:** The rhyme "Cinco lobitos" (five little wolf cubs) with finger actions.
- **Key activity:** Learn 0–5 with fingers; the rhyme; "show me" game (teacher says a number, children show fingers).
- **Check for understanding:** "I show 4 fingers. What do you say?" → Cuatro.
- **Differentiation:** support: 1–3 / stretch: count backwards from 5
- **Materials:** number cards, finger puppets
- **Joy:** "Cinco lobitos" rhyme.

### Lesson 10 — Numbers 6–10
- **Duration:** 20 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can count from 0 to 10 in Spanish and recognise the numbers out of order." (CEFR pre-A1 L/S)
- **Hook:** A rocket countdown: "¡Diez, nueve, ocho… despegue!"
- **Key activity:** Teach 6–10 with actions; countdown; number bingo 0–10.
- **Check for understanding:** "Which number comes after siete?" → Ocho.
- **Differentiation:** support: 0–10 with number cards visible / stretch: count in 2s (dos, cuatro, seis…)
- **Materials:** number cards, bingo boards
- **Joy:** Rocket countdown.

### Lesson 11 — Silent h and throaty j
- **Duration:** 20 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can say words with a silent h (hola, hay) and the throaty j (jirafa, julio)." (CEFR pre-A1 phonology)
- **Hook:** "The letter h in Spanish is a ninja — completely silent!"
- **Key activity:** Ninja-h words (hola, hay, hermano, helado); the j sound like clearing your throat gently (jirafa, jugar, ojo); sort picture cards by sound.
- **Check for understanding:** "How do you say 'hola' — and why don't we hear the h?" → "Ola" — in Spanish h is always silent.
- **Differentiation:** support: echo with actions / stretch: read hay, hijo, jamón aloud
- **Materials:** sound cards, picture cards
- **Joy:** Ninja-h game (silent tiptoe when you see an h).

### Lesson 12 — ¿Cuántos años tienes?
- **Duration:** 20 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can ask and say my age: '¿Cuántos años tienes?' 'Tengo seis años.'" (CEFR pre-A1 S)
- **Hook:** Paco's birthday cake has candles: "¿Cuántos años tiene Paco?"
- **Key activity:** Count candles; model the question and answer; ask 5 classmates and record ages with tallies.
- **Check for understanding:** "What does 'Tengo siete años' mean?" → I am seven years old (literally, "I have seven years").
- **Differentiation:** support: answer only / stretch: ask about a family member ("Mi hermano tiene…")
- **Materials:** birthday cake picture, candles (paper), survey sheet
- **Joy:** Singing "Cumpleaños feliz".

### Lesson 13 — Numbers 11–15
- **Duration:** 20 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can say and recognise the numbers 11 to 15." (CEFR pre-A1 L/S)
- **Hook:** "Once… it sounds like the English word 'once' but it means 11!"
- **Key activity:** Teach once, doce, trece, catorce, quince with a counting-stick; "stand up if you hear your number" game.
- **Check for understanding:** "What number is 'doce'?" → 12.
- **Differentiation:** support: 11–13 / stretch: count from 10 to 15 and back
- **Materials:** counting stick, number cards
- **Joy:** Stand-up number game.

### Lesson 14 — Numbers 16–20: the joining trick
- **Duration:** 20 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can say numbers 16 to 20 and explain the 'diez y seis → dieciséis' pattern." (CEFR pre-A1 L/S)
- **Hook:** "Sixteen in Spanish is 'ten and six' squished together!"
- **Key activity:** Show dieciséis = diez y seis, diecisiete = diez y siete…; count to 20 with a jump each number; hopscotch counting.
- **Check for understanding:** "How does 'dieciocho' help you know it means 18?" → It's diez (10) y ocho (8) joined — 10 and 8.
- **Differentiation:** support: count to 20 with the stick / stretch: say "veinte" and predict "veintiuno" (21)
- **Materials:** counting stick, chalk hopscotch
- **Joy:** Spanish hopscotch.

### Lesson 15 — Hay…: counting things
- **Duration:** 20 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can answer '¿Cuántos … hay?' with a number: 'Hay ocho.'" (CEFR pre-A1 S)
- **Hook:** A jar of sweets (or buttons): "¿Cuántos caramelos hay?"
- **Key activity:** Count objects in pictures and jars; answer "Hay…"; partner quiz with picture cards.
- **Check for understanding:** "What does 'hay' mean, and how do you say it?" → "There is / there are" — said like "eye" (the h is silent).
- **Differentiation:** support: count to 10 / stretch: add a colour ("Hay tres pelotas rojas") — preview of Unit 3
- **Materials:** jars of objects, picture cards
- **Joy:** Guess-the-jar game.

### Lesson 16 — Unit check: number games
- **Duration:** 20 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can count to 20, say my age and understand numbers I hear." (CEFR pre-A1 L/S — assessment)
- **Hook:** "Number Olympics!"
- **Key activity:** Stations: count aloud to 20, say your age, listen and point to numbers, count objects with "hay".
- **Check for understanding:** "I say 'catorce'. Point to it." → Points to 14.
- **Differentiation:** support: 0–10 stations / stretch: count backwards from 20
- **Materials:** number cards, station cards
- **Joy:** Medals for each station.

**End-of-unit check:** Count aloud to 20, answer "¿Cuántos años tienes?", point to five numbers heard out of order, and count a group of objects using "Hay…".

## Unit 3 — Los colores y las formas

**Essential question:** How do we describe colours and shapes — and celebrate — in Spanish?

**Key vocabulary:** rojo, azul, amarillo, verde, naranja, rosa, morado, blanco, negro, marrón, gris, el círculo, el cuadrado, el triángulo, el rectángulo, la estrella, el corazón, me gusta, no me gusta, ¿Te gusta…?, ñ, ll

### Lesson 17 — Los colores 1: rojo, azul, amarillo, verde
- **Duration:** 20 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can say and recognise four colours in Spanish." (CEFR pre-A1 L/S)
- **Hook:** Read *Green Is a Chile Pepper* by Roseanne Greenfield Thong — colours in a Latino neighbourhood.
- **Key activity:** Teach four colours with coloured scarves; "touch something rojo" game.
- **Check for understanding:** "What colour is a banana in Spanish?" → Amarillo.
- **Differentiation:** support: two colours / stretch: name the colours in the book's pictures
- **Materials:** *Green Is a Chile Pepper* by Roseanne Greenfield Thong, coloured scarves
- **Joy:** "Touch the colour" race.

### Lesson 18 — Los colores 2: more colours
- **Duration:** 20 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can say and recognise ten colours." (CEFR pre-A1 L/S)
- **Hook:** A rainbow with the colours missing — "¡Ayúdame! Help me colour it!"
- **Key activity:** Teach naranja, rosa, morado, blanco, negro, marrón, gris; colour-by-word rainbow; colour bingo.
- **Check for understanding:** "What colour do you get if you mix rojo and amarillo?" → Naranja.
- **Differentiation:** support: 6 colours / stretch: colour-mixing sentences ("rojo y azul es morado")
- **Materials:** paints, rainbow sheet, bingo boards
- **Joy:** Colour mixing with paint.

### Lesson 19 — ñ and ll: special Spanish sounds
- **Duration:** 20 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can say words with ñ (España, niño) and ll (amarillo, llama) correctly." (CEFR pre-A1 phonology)
- **Hook:** "Spanish has its own letter — ñ — that English doesn't have!"
- **Key activity:** Teach ñ (like 'ny' in canyon): España, niño, año, piña; ll (like 'y' in yes in most accents; like 'sh' in Argentina): amarillo, llama, pollito. Sing "Los pollitos dicen".
- **Check for understanding:** "What's special about the letter ñ?" → It's an extra letter in Spanish that says 'ny', as in España.
- **Differentiation:** support: echo with actions / stretch: read piña, señor, lluvia aloud
- **Materials:** letter cards, song "Los pollitos dicen"
- **Joy:** "Los pollitos dicen" song with chick puppets.

### Lesson 20 — Las formas: shapes
- **Duration:** 20 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can name shapes and say their colour: 'un círculo rojo'." (CEFR pre-A1 S)
- **Hook:** Read *Round Is a Tortilla* by Roseanne Greenfield Thong: "What shapes can you find?"
- **Key activity:** Teach shapes; note that in Spanish the colour comes after the shape (un triángulo azul); shape hunt around the room.
- **Check for understanding:** "In Spanish, do we say 'azul triángulo' or 'triángulo azul'? Why?" → Triángulo azul — in Spanish the colour usually comes after the thing.
- **Differentiation:** support: name shapes only / stretch: describe a shape picture in a full phrase
- **Materials:** *Round Is a Tortilla*, shape cut-outs
- **Joy:** Shape-hunt game.

### Lesson 21 — Me gusta… no me gusta…
- **Duration:** 20 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can say which colours I like and don't like: 'Me gusta el azul.'" (CEFR pre-A1 S)
- **Hook:** Paco loves verde and hates marrón. "¿Y tú?"
- **Key activity:** Model "Me gusta el…" / "No me gusta el…"; ask a partner "¿Te gusta el rojo?" — "Sí / No"; class colour vote with a block diagram (link: Maths).
- **Check for understanding:** "How would you say you don't like black?" → "No me gusta el negro."
- **Differentiation:** support: "me gusta" + colour / stretch: "Me gusta el azul y el verde, pero no me gusta el gris."
- **Materials:** colour cards, voting stickers
- **Joy:** Class colour vote.

### Lesson 22 — Flags of the Spanish-speaking world
- **Duration:** 20 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can describe the colours of three flags from Spanish-speaking countries." (CEFR pre-A1 S; IC)
- **Hook:** "Which country has a flag with an eagle and a snake?" (Mexico)
- **Key activity:** Look at the flags of Spain, Mexico, Argentina, Colombia and Peru; describe colours in Spanish; colour a flag and present it ("La bandera de Colombia es amarilla, azul y roja").
- **Check for understanding:** "Describe the colours of the flag of Spain in Spanish." → Rojo y amarillo.
- **Differentiation:** support: point and say colours / stretch: describe a flag without naming it for the class to guess
- **Materials:** flag pictures, colouring sheets, world map
- **Joy:** "Guess the flag" game.

### Lesson 23 — ¡Feliz Navidad! Winter festivals and Los Reyes Magos
- **Duration:** 20 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can say festive greetings and describe how Los Reyes Magos is celebrated on 6 January." (CEFR pre-A1 IC)
- **Hook:** "In Spain and much of Latin America, many children get presents from three kings on 6 January!"
- **Key activity:** Learn "¡Feliz Navidad!" and "¡Feliz Año Nuevo!"; the tradition of leaving shoes out for Los Reyes Magos and the Roscón de Reyes cake; compare with festivals children celebrate at home (inclusive of all traditions).
- **Check for understanding:** "What do some children leave out for Los Reyes Magos, and when?" → Their shoes, on the night of 5 January.
- **Differentiation:** support: festive greetings only / stretch: compare with a festival from their own family
- **Materials:** pictures of the Reyes parade (Cabalgata), roscón picture, shoes (paper)
- **Joy:** Decorating paper shoes.

### Lesson 24 — Unit check: colour and shape show-and-tell
- **Duration:** 20 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can describe a picture using colours and shapes and say what I like." (CEFR pre-A1 S — assessment)
- **Hook:** "Make a Kandinsky-style picture of circles — then describe it!"
- **Key activity:** Make a shapes picture; describe it to a partner ("Hay un círculo rojo…"); say a colour you like.
- **Check for understanding:** "Describe one shape in your picture with its colour." → e.g. "un cuadrado azul".
- **Differentiation:** support: two shapes with prompts / stretch: describe three shapes with numbers ("Hay tres círculos verdes")
- **Materials:** paper shapes, glue, can-do sheet
- **Joy:** Art gallery in Spanish.

**End-of-unit check:** Describe a shapes picture using at least three shape-and-colour phrases (in Spanish word order), say which colour you like and don't like, and read aloud España, amarillo and niño correctly.

## Unit 4 — Mi familia

**Essential question:** How can I introduce my family in Spanish?

**Key vocabulary:** la familia, la madre / mamá, el padre / papá, el hermano, la hermana, el abuelo, la abuela, el bebé, el tío, la tía, el primo, la prima, tengo, no tengo, este es, esta es, r, rr

### Lesson 25 — La familia: who's who?
- **Duration:** 20 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can name family members in Spanish." (CEFR pre-A1 L/S)
- **Hook:** Paco shows photos of his big family from Mexico: "¡Esta es mi abuela!"
- **Key activity:** Teach family words with puppet family pictures; "¿Quién es?" (Who is it?) guessing game.
- **Check for understanding:** "Paco points to an old man and says 'mi abuelo'. Who is he?" → Paco's grandfather.
- **Differentiation:** support: 4 words (mamá, papá, hermano, hermana) / stretch: tío, tía, primo, prima
- **Materials:** family picture cards, puppets
- **Joy:** Paco's family album.

### Lesson 26 — Tengo un hermano: talking about my family
- **Duration:** 20 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can say who is in my family: 'Tengo una hermana.' 'No tengo hermanos.'" (CEFR pre-A1 S)
- **Hook:** "Do you have brothers or sisters? Let's find out in Spanish!"
- **Key activity:** Model sentences; children draw their family and talk to a partner; note un hermano / una hermana (un for boys' words, una for girls' words — a first glimpse of gender).
- **Check for understanding:** "How do you say you have two sisters?" → "Tengo dos hermanas."
- **Differentiation:** support: point-and-say with drawings / stretch: add ages ("Mi hermano tiene nueve años")
- **Materials:** drawing paper, sentence cards
- **Joy:** Family portraits.

### Lesson 27 — Este es… esta es…: introducing people
- **Duration:** 20 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can introduce a family member: 'Esta es mi mamá.'" (CEFR pre-A1 S)
- **Hook:** Paco introduces his family puppets one by one.
- **Key activity:** Model "Este es mi…" (for boys/men) and "Esta es mi…" (for girls/women); children introduce their family drawings to the class.
- **Check for understanding:** "Do you say 'este es' or 'esta es' for 'mi abuela'? Why?" → Esta es — abuela is a woman/girl word.
- **Differentiation:** support: "mi mamá / mi papá" with gestures / stretch: add a feeling or age
- **Materials:** family drawings, puppets
- **Joy:** Family introductions show.

### Lesson 28 — The r and the rolled rr
- **Duration:** 20 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can hear and try to say the difference between r (pero) and rr (perro)." (CEFR pre-A1 phonology)
- **Hook:** "'Pero' means 'but' and 'perro' means 'dog'! One little r changes everything."
- **Key activity:** Practise the tapped r (pero, caro) and the rolled rr (perro, carro), tongue-twister "Erre con erre cigarro, erre con erre barril…"; "motorbike" trill warm-ups; minimal-pair listening game.
- **Check for understanding:** "I say a word — is it pero or perro? How can you tell?" → Perro has the long rolled rr sound.
- **Differentiation:** support: listening only — it's fine if the trill takes time / stretch: say the tongue-twister
- **Materials:** picture cards (dog, car), minimal-pair cards
- **Joy:** "Motorbike" rrrr warm-ups.

### Lesson 29 — Family song and story
- **Duration:** 20 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can join in with a family song and understand a short family story." (CEFR pre-A1 L)
- **Hook:** A finger-family song in Spanish (la familia dedo).
- **Key activity:** Sing and act; listen to a short illustrated story about a family; point to people as they are named.
- **Check for understanding:** "In the story, who helps the baby? Answer in Spanish." → e.g. "La abuela."
- **Differentiation:** support: point to pictures / stretch: retell one event with a family word

## Teacher guidance

**How to teach it well.** Speak Spanish for most of the lesson, supported by gesture, pictures and puppets; use English briefly for culture and grammar "noticing". Repetition is the engine: each word should be heard and said dozens of times across games. Praise communication first, accuracy second; model the correct form back rather than correcting bluntly (child: "Yo seis años." Teacher: "¡Sí! Tengo seis años. ¡Muy bien!").

**Common pitfalls.** English vowel sounds in Spanish words (say "o-la", not "oh-lah") → vowel song daily; pronouncing the h → "ninja h"; adjective before noun ("rojo círculo") → "the colour comes after the thing"; *este/esta* confusion → gender is noticed, not drilled, this year; shyness about the rolled rr → it develops with time; never make a child perform alone before they're ready.

**Home-speakers of Spanish** are an asset: give them "expert" roles, extended reading (the Spanish editions in the class library) and early writing tasks.

**Vertical alignment.** This is the first year of the language spine. Year 2 (ages 7–8) extends numbers to 31, adds classroom objects, gender (el/la, un/una) and simple written phrases, building on this year's sound rules and spoken confidence.
