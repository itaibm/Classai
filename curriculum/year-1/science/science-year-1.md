> Part of **the best international primary school** (see [`../../../SCHOOL-CHARTER.md`](../../../SCHOOL-CHARTER.md)).
> Built to [`../../PLANNING-BRIEF.md`](../../PLANNING-BRIEF.md). Standards reference:
> `knowledge-base/subjects/science/year-2.md` (UK Year 2 = our Year 1). Codes: **UK NC Y2** programme
> (LH = living things & habitats, PL = plants, AH = animals including humans, MA = uses of everyday materials,
> WS = working scientifically) and the nearest **NGSS** Grade 1/2 performance expectation for physics and

## Year overview

Year 1 science moves a child from *naming* the world to *explaining* it and *testing* ideas about it. Every unit
runs the working-scientifically cycle at a six-year-old's level — **ask a question → predict → do a simple fair
test or careful observation → measure or count → record in a table or pictogram → say what we found out** —
and every unit has at least one real investigation. Teaching follows an explore-first pattern: children handle,
look, test and talk before the idea is named.

**What this year assumes (from Foundation, ages 5–6):** naming common plants and their parts; deciduous and
evergreen trees; animal groups (fish, amphibians, reptiles, birds, mammals) and what they eat; the human body
and five senses; everyday materials and simple properties; the four seasons and weather observation.

**How the year runs.** All four sciences appear. *Biology:* living things and their habitats, including food
chains (Unit 1, autumn — minibeasts are plentiful); animals including humans — growing up and staying healthy
(Unit 3); plants — how seeds and bulbs grow (Unit 6, timed for spring so seeds grow outside). *Chemistry:* uses of
everyday materials, changing shape, and heating and cooling (Unit 2). *Physics:* sound and vibration (Unit 4).
*Earth & space:* the Sun, Moon and our changing sky, and land and water on Earth (Unit 5). Unit 7 is a child-led
investigation and year review. In the southern hemisphere, swap the order of Units 1 and 6 to match the growing
season.

**What Year 2 relies on:** confidence running a simple fair test with one thing changed; recording in a table;
knowing plants need water, light and warmth; food chains; the idea that materials are chosen for their
properties and that heating/cooling changes some materials; vibration as the cause of sound. Year 2 (ages 7–8)
adds the jobs of plant parts, pollination and seed dispersal, nutrition and skeletons, rocks, fossils and soils,
light and shadows, and forces and magnets.

## Unit 1 — Living things and their habitats

**Essential question:** How do we know if something is living, and how is each living thing suited to where it lives?

**Key vocabulary:** living, dead, never alive, life processes, move, grow, feed, breathe, reproduce, habitat, microhabitat, shelter, suited, survive, food chain, producer, consumer, predator, prey, minibeast

### Lesson 1 — Living, dead, or never alive
- **Duration:** 30 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can sort things into living, dead and never alive and give a reason." (UK NC Y2 LH — compare living, dead, never alive; NGSS K-LS1-1 link)
- **Hook:** "A wooden chair and a tree are both wood — but are they both alive?"
- **Key activity:** Explore: handle a plant, a dead leaf, a pebble, a shell, a toy car and a picture of a butterfly. Notice: living things move, grow, feed and have young. Name the three groups with the test "Does it, or did it ever, grow, feed and have young?" Sort into three hoops and justify each.
- **Check for understanding:** "A toy car moves. Is it alive? Explain using our test." → No — it moves but it has never grown, fed or had young, so it is never alive.
- **Differentiation:** support: sort living vs never alive first, with real objects / stretch: explain why a seashell or a wooden spoon is "dead" (once part of something living)
- **Materials:** potted plant, dead leaf, pebble, seashell, wooden spoon, toy car, butterfly picture, three sorting hoops
- **Joy:** The "gotcha" of busting "a car is alive because it moves".

### Lesson 2 — What do all living things do?
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can name the life processes (move, breathe, feel, grow, reproduce, get rid of waste, feed) and show that plants do them too." (UK NC Y2 LH — life processes)
- **Hook:** "A plant can't walk. Does that mean it isn't alive?"
- **Key activity:** Learn the life processes with actions (MRS GREN as a class chant: Move, Respiration/breathing, Sensitivity, Growth, Reproduction, Excretion, Nutrition). Evidence hunt: sunflower turning to light (moves, senses), time-lapse of a seedling (grows), seeds (reproduces).
- **Check for understanding:** "How do we know a plant moves if we never see it walk?" → It moves slowly — it turns its leaves towards the light and its roots grow down.
- **Differentiation:** support: 4 processes with picture cards / stretch: explain how a fungus or a tree shows the processes
- **Materials:** potted plants (one moved by a window a week ago), time-lapse video of seed growth, action cards
- **Joy:** The MRS GREN action chant.

### Lesson 3 — What is a habitat?
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can explain that a habitat gives a living thing food, water and shelter, and name habitats around the world." (UK NC Y2 LH — habitats provide basic needs; NGSS 2-LS4-1)
- **Hook:** "Could a polar bear live in our school playground? Why not?"
- **Key activity:** Photo sort of world habitats (ocean, desert, rainforest, polar, woodland, pond, savannah). Match animals to habitats and say what each gives them. Record on a habitat grid.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why is the desert a good habitat for a camel but not for a frog?" → A camel can go a long time without water; a frog needs damp places and water to survive and lay eggs.
- **Differentiation:** support: 3 habitats and 6 animals / stretch: choose an animal and explain two features that suit it to its habitat
- **Materials:** habitat photo cards, animal cards, world map, habitat grid
- **Joy:** "Habitat mix-up" — a penguin in the desert; children rescue it.

### Lesson 4 — Suited to where they live
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can describe how an animal or plant is suited (adapted) to its habitat." (UK NC Y2 LH — suited to habitats; NGSS 2-LS4-1)
- **Hook:** "Why does a cactus have spines instead of leaves? Why does a penguin have blubber?"
- **Key activity:** Blubber glove investigation: hands in iced water with and without a "blubber" glove (a bag of fat or shortening) — which feels colder, and for how long? Link to polar animals; then cactus, fish gills, camel humps (fat store, not water).
- **Check for understanding:** "What did the blubber glove show us about penguins?" → A thick layer of fat keeps the cold out, so penguins stay warm in icy water.
- **Differentiation:** support: describe one feature with a picture / stretch: design an animal suited to an imaginary habitat and explain two features
- **Materials:** bowls of iced water, zip bags, vegetable shortening, towels, timer, animal photos
- **Joy:** The blubber glove.

### Lesson 5 — Microhabitats: minibeast hunt
- **Duration:** 30 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can find minibeasts in different microhabitats and say why they live there." (UK NC Y2 LH — microhabitats; WS — observe)
- **Hook:** "Under this log is a whole secret world. Shall we lift it?"
- **Key activity:** Outdoor hunt: lift logs and stones, check leaf litter, bark and grass (return everything as found). Record what lives where in a tally table; compare damp/dark with dry/sunny places.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why did we find woodlice under the log but not on the sunny path?" → Woodlice need damp, dark, cool places; the log gives shelter and food (rotting wood).
- **Differentiation:** support: picture ID sheet and a partner recorder / stretch: suggest a question to test (Do woodlice prefer damp or dry?)
- **Materials:** magnifiers, bug pots, spoons, ID sheets, clipboards, gloves (handwashing afterwards)
- **Joy:** Lifting the log.

### Lesson 6 — Investigation: damp or dry?
- **Duration:** 30 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can carry out a simple fair test to find out whether woodlice prefer damp or dry places." (UK NC Y2 WS — perform simple tests; LH)
- **Hook:** "We think woodlice like damp places. How could we find out for sure — fairly?"
- **Key activity:** A choice chamber: a tray half damp paper, half dry, covered to keep it dark. Put 10 woodlice in the middle; count each side after 10 minutes. Discuss fairness (same light, same tray). Return woodlice kindly.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why did we keep both halves dark?" → So the only thing different was damp vs dry — a fair test changes one thing.
- **Differentiation:** support: count with a partner, one side each / stretch: predict what would happen with light vs dark and plan it
- **Materials:** tray with lid, paper towels, water spray, 10 woodlice (collected and returned), timer, results table
- **Joy:** Real animals making a real choice.

### Lesson 7 — Food chains
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can build a simple food chain starting with a plant and explain what the arrows mean." (UK NC Y2 LH — simple food chains)
- **Hook:** "Where does a fox's energy really come from? Follow it back…"
- **Key activity:** Human food chain with cards (grass → rabbit → fox) and a ball of string showing "is eaten by" arrows. Build chains from different habitats (ocean: seaweed → sea urchin → sea otter; savannah: grass → zebra → lion).
- **Check for understanding:** "Which way does the arrow point in grass → rabbit, and what does it mean?" → From grass to rabbit — it shows the energy/food goes from the grass into the rabbit that eats it.
- **Differentiation:** support: 3-link chains with pictures / stretch: a 4-link chain and "what would happen if the rabbits disappeared?"
- **Materials:** food-chain cards, string, arrow cards, habitat photos
- **Joy:** Human food chain linked by string.

### Lesson 8 — Producers, predators and prey
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can use the words producer, predator and prey to describe animals in a food chain." (UK NC Y2 LH — food chains, vocabulary)
- **Hook:** "Is a bird a predator or prey? Trick question!"
- **Key activity:** Label producers (plants make their own food using sunlight), predators and prey on chains; discover some animals are both (a blackbird eats worms, a cat catches blackbirds). Pond-dipping photos or a video to build a chain.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why does every food chain start with a plant?" → Plants are producers: they make their own food using sunlight, and every other living thing in the chain depends on them.
- **Differentiation:** support: sort pictures into plant / predator / prey / stretch: find an animal that is both predator and prey and explain
- **Materials:** labelled cards, pond or rock-pool photos, habitat video
- **Joy:** "Predator and prey" tag game outdoors.

### Lesson 9 — Caring for habitats
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can explain how people can harm or help habitats and suggest one way to help." (UK NC Y2 LH; link: Life skills sustainability)
- **Hook:** Photo of a sea turtle near a plastic bag: "What's wrong here?"
- **Key activity:** Discuss litter, cutting forests and pollution vs bird boxes, wildflower patches and clean-ups. Plan and do one small action (a bug hotel or a litter pick with gloves).
- **Check for understanding:** "How does a bug hotel help minibeasts?" → It gives them shelter — a microhabitat — so more can live and feed there.
- **Differentiation:** support: sort helpful/harmful picture cards / stretch: explain how harming one living thing can affect a whole food chain
- **Materials:** sticks, pine cones, hollow stems, bricks for a bug hotel; litter pickers and gloves
- **Joy:** Building a class bug hotel.

### Lesson 10 — Scientist spotlight: Jane Goodall and watching animals
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can explain how a scientist learns about animals by watching them patiently and recording what she sees." (UK NC Y2 WS — observe closely; link: History)
- **Hook:** "One young woman sat in a forest in Tanzania for months just watching chimpanzees. What did she discover?"
- **Key activity:** Read *Me… Jane* by Patrick McDonnell. Discuss her discovery that chimps use tools (grass stems to catch termites). Children watch a class animal or birds at a feeder for 5 minutes and record observations.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why did Jane Goodall need to watch for such a long time?" → Animals behave naturally only when they get used to you; you need many observations to be sure of a pattern.
- **Differentiation:** support: tally pictures of what they see / stretch: write one question the observation raised
- **Materials:** *Me… Jane* by Patrick McDonnell, bird feeder or class pet, observation sheets, binoculars
- **Joy:** Silent "field scientist" observation with binoculars.

### Lesson 11 — Retrieval: living things and habitats
- **Duration:** 20 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can recall and use what I've learned about living things, habitats and food chains." (UK NC Y2 LH — retrieval)
- **Hook:** "Habitat hero quiz!"
- **Key activity:** Quick-fire sorting (living/dead/never alive), habitat matching, food-chain building race; "true or false" statements with reasons.
- **Check for understanding:** "True or false: a dead leaf is never alive. Explain." → False — it was once alive (part of a tree), so it is dead.
- **Differentiation:** support: picture cards / stretch: explain a food chain from a new habitat
- **Materials:** sorting cards, food-chain cards, true/false paddles
- **Joy:** True/false paddles.

### Lesson 12 — End-of-unit check: living things and habitats
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can show what I understand about living things, habitats and food chains." (UK NC Y2 LH — assessment)
- **Hook:** "You're a nature guide. Show a visitor around our habitat."
- **Key activity:** Mastery tasks: sort objects and give reasons, explain one animal's suitability to its habitat, build and explain a food chain, describe the woodlice investigation's fair test.
- **Check for understanding:** "Build a food chain for an ocean and explain the arrows." → e.g. seaweed → fish → seal; arrows show who is eaten by whom (the flow of food).
- **Differentiation:** support: oral answers with picture cards / stretch: "What if…" food-chain questions
- **Materials:** sorting objects, cards, assessment sheet
- **Joy:** Nature-guide badges.

**End-of-unit check:** Sort six items into living, dead and never alive with reasons, choose one animal and explain two ways it is suited to its habitat, and draw a three-link food chain with correct arrows.

## Unit 2 — Uses of everyday materials

**Essential question:** Why are things made from the materials they are — and how can we change materials?

**Key vocabulary:** object, material, wood, metal, plastic, glass, fabric, paper, cardboard, rubber, brick, rock, property, hard, soft, bendy, stiff, waterproof, absorbent, transparent, opaque, strong, squash, bend, twist, stretch, melt, freeze, heat, cool, reversible, recycle

### Lesson 13 — Objects and materials (retrieval)
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can say what an object is made of and explain that one object can be made from different materials." (UK NC Y2 MA — identify materials; NGSS 2-PS1-1)
- **Hook:** Three spoons — wooden, metal, plastic: "Same object, three materials. Why?"
- **Key activity:** Feely bag of objects; name the object and the material. Sort a "material museum" (wood, metal, plastic, glass, fabric, paper, rubber). Find objects made of two materials (a pencil: wood and graphite; scissors: metal and plastic).
- **Check for understanding:** "Is 'spoon' a material? Explain." → No — a spoon is an object; it can be made from different materials like wood, metal or plastic.
- **Differentiation:** support: 4 materials with real objects / stretch: find an object made from three materials
- **Materials:** wooden/metal/plastic spoons, feely bag, material museum objects, labels
- **Joy:** Feely-bag guessing.

### Lesson 14 — Properties of materials
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can test and describe materials using property words (hard, bendy, waterproof, transparent…)." (UK NC Y2 MA — properties; NGSS 2-PS1-1)
- **Hook:** "Could you make a window from wood? A towel from glass?"
- **Key activity:** Property stations: bend it, squash it, shine a torch through it, drip water on it, scratch it. Record in a table with ticks.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why is glass good for windows but a bad choice for a football?" → It is transparent (lets light through) but it is hard and breaks — a ball needs to be bouncy and bendy.
- **Differentiation:** support: 3 properties / stretch: explain a property that is both useful and a problem for one material
- **Materials:** samples (fabric, foil, card, plastic, wood, glass tile — with care), torches, pipettes, property table
- **Joy:** Torch test for transparency.

### Lesson 15 — Right material for the job
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can explain why a material is suitable for a particular use, using its properties." (UK NC Y2 MA — suitability; NGSS 2-PS1-2)
- **Hook:** The Three Little Pigs: "Why did the brick house survive? Was it luck?"
- **Key activity:** Match everyday objects to materials and reasons ("a raincoat is plastic because it is waterproof"). Silly-object challenge: chocolate teapot, paper umbrella, glass pillow — explain why they fail.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why would a chocolate teapot be a bad idea?" → Chocolate melts when heated, so hot tea would melt it.
- **Differentiation:** support: sentence frame "___ is made of ___ because it is ___" / stretch: suggest a different suitable material and compare
- **Materials:** everyday objects, silly-object cards, *The Three Little Pigs*
- **Joy:** Silly-object challenge.

### Lesson 16 — Investigation: which fabric is most waterproof?
- **Duration:** 30 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can carry out a fair test to find the most waterproof fabric for a teddy's raincoat." (UK NC Y2 WS — simple test; MA; NGSS 2-PS1-2)
- **Hook:** "Teddy is going out in the rain. Which fabric should we use for his coat?"
- **Key activity:** Predict; stretch each fabric over a cup; pour the same amount of water (use a measuring spoon) on each; measure water that got through (or look at a paper towel underneath). Record and conclude.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why did we use the same amount of water on every fabric?" → So the test is fair — only the fabric changes, so we know it caused the difference.
- **Differentiation:** support: 3 fabrics and pictorial table / stretch: rank all fabrics and suggest a second test (e.g. warmth)
- **Materials:** fabric samples (cotton, wool, plastic, felt, nylon), cups, elastic bands, measuring spoons, water, paper towels, results table
- **Joy:** Dressing teddy in the winning raincoat.

### Lesson 17 — Changing shape: squash, bend, twist, stretch
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can find out how the shapes of some solid objects can be changed by squashing, bending, twisting and stretching." (UK NC Y2 MA — changing shape)
- **Hook:** "Can you change the shape of this spoon? This sponge? This play dough?"
- **Key activity:** Test objects at stations; record which can be squashed, bent, twisted or stretched; notice which spring back (elastic) and which stay changed (dough).
- **Check for understanding:** "What's different about stretching an elastic band and stretching play dough?" → The elastic band springs back to its shape; the dough stays stretched.
- **Differentiation:** support: 2 actions / stretch: find a material that can be changed all four ways
- **Materials:** sponges, pipe cleaners, elastic bands, play dough, foil, spoons, results table
- **Joy:** Play-dough and elastic experiments.

### Lesson 18 — Heating and cooling: melting and freezing
- **Duration:** 30 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can describe how heating melts some materials and cooling freezes them, and that some of these changes can be reversed." (NGSS 2-PS1-4; link: UK NC Y2 MA)
- **Hook:** An ice-cube race: "Which will melt fastest — in your hand, on the windowsill or in the fridge?"
- **Key activity:** Predict and observe ice cubes in three places; melt chocolate buttons in a bag in warm water (adult) and cool them again in a mould. Record changes.
- **Check for understanding:** "Can melted chocolate go back to solid? Can a cooked egg go back to raw?" → Chocolate yes — cooling makes it solid again (reversible); a cooked egg no — cooking is a change that can't be undone.
- **Differentiation:** support: ice melting only / stretch: sort six changes into reversible and not reversible
- **Materials:** ice cubes, chocolate buttons, zip bags, warm water (adult), moulds, timer
- **Joy:** Chocolate shapes made by melting and cooling.

### Lesson 19 — Materials around the world: building homes
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can explain why people in different places build homes from different materials." (UK NC Y2 MA — suitability; link: Geography)
- **Hook:** Photos of an igloo, a stilt house, a mud-brick house, a yurt and a skyscraper: "Why so different?"
- **Key activity:** Match homes to places and materials (snow, wood, mud brick, felt, steel and glass); discuss climate and what's available locally. Build a mini-home from a chosen material.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why are some houses in hot, dry places built from thick mud bricks?" → Mud is easy to find there and thick walls keep homes cool inside.
- **Differentiation:** support: 3 homes / stretch: explain two reasons for one choice (climate and available materials)
- **Materials:** home photo cards, world map, straws, clay, fabric, card
- **Joy:** Mini-home building.

### Lesson 20 — Inventors of new materials
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can explain how an inventor found a new material or a new use for one." (UK NC Y2 MA — people who developed useful materials; link: History)
- **Hook:** "Before rubber tyres, bikes shook your bones! Who fixed it?"
- **Key activity:** Short stories: Charles Macintosh (waterproof fabric), John Dunlop (air-filled rubber tyre), and the ancient Chinese invention of paper. Timeline them. Discuss how each property solved a problem.
- **Check for understanding:** "What problem did the waterproof coat fabric solve, and which property made it work?" → Getting wet in the rain; the rubber layer is waterproof.
- **Differentiation:** support: 2 inventions with pictures / stretch: invent a new material for a job and name its properties
- **Materials:** story cards, timeline strip, rubber samples
- **Joy:** "Invention pitch" — sell your own material.

### Lesson 21 — Recycling: materials can have new lives
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can sort waste by material for recycling and explain why recycling helps." (UK NC Y2 MA; link: Life skills sustainability)
- **Hook:** "Where does a plastic bottle go after you throw it away?"
- **Key activity:** Sort a (clean) waste bag by material; learn what each can become; read *Somebody Swallowed Stanley* by Sarah Roberts. Make a "reuse" invention from junk.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why is it helpful to sort rubbish by material?" → Each material is recycled in a different way, so it can be made into new things instead of being thrown away.
- **Differentiation:** support: 3 materials / stretch: explain reduce, reuse, recycle with examples
- **Materials:** clean sorted waste, bins with material labels, *Somebody Swallowed Stanley* by Sarah Roberts
- **Joy:** Junk-model reuse invention.

### Lesson 22 — Design challenge: a boat that floats
- **Duration:** 30 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can choose materials for a boat, test it and improve it." (UK NC Y2 WS — test and improve; NGSS K-2-ETS1-1)
- **Hook:** "Build a boat that can carry the most marbles without sinking!"
- **Key activity:** Plan (choose from foil, card, plastic, sponge), build, test in water adding marbles one at a time, record, improve once, retest.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why did card work less well than foil or plastic?" → Card absorbs water and goes soggy; foil and plastic are waterproof.
- **Differentiation:** support: foil boats only / stretch: explain how changing the shape (wider) helped
- **Materials:** foil, card, plastic trays, sponges, marbles, water tray, towels, results table
- **Joy:** The marble-sinking countdown.

### Lesson 23 — Retrieval: materials
- **Duration:** 20 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can recall properties, uses and changes of materials." (UK NC Y2 MA — retrieval)
- **Hook:** "Material mystery boxes!"
- **Key activity:** Mystery-box clues ("I am transparent and hard…"); property bingo; reversible/not reversible sort.
- **Check for understanding:** "I'm bendy, waterproof and can be stretched. What could I be, and what could you make from me?" → Rubber — e.g. wellington boots or an elastic band.
- **Differentiation:** support: picture clues / stretch: write your own mystery clue
- **Materials:** mystery boxes, bingo cards
- **Joy:** Mystery boxes.

### Lesson 24 — End-of-unit check: materials
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can explain why materials are chosen for their jobs and describe how some can be changed." (UK NC Y2 MA — assessment)
- **Hook:** "Design Teddy's house — every material must be justified!"
- **Key activity:** Label a house design with materials and reasons; answer questions on the waterproof test; sort changes (squash, melt, freeze, cook).
- **Check for understanding:** "Why should a roof be made from a waterproof, strong material?" → To keep rain out and not break in wind or under weight.
- **Differentiation:** support: word bank and labels / stretch: compare two roof materials
- **Materials:** design sheet, material samples
- **Joy:** Teddy's dream-house display.

**End-of-unit check:** Choose materials for a lunchbox, a window and a raincoat and justify each with a property, describe how you made the fabric test fair, and sort four changes into "can be undone" and "can't be undone".

## Unit 3 — Animals, including humans: growing and staying healthy

**Essential question:** How do animals (including us) grow up, and what do we need to survive and stay healthy?

**Key vocabulary:** offspring, young, adult, life cycle, egg, caterpillar, chrysalis, tadpole, frogspawn, grow, survive, needs, water, food, air, shelter, exercise, hygiene, germs, balanced diet, food group, healthy, heart rate

### Lesson 25 — Animals have offspring
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can match animals to their offspring and notice that some young look like their parents and some don't." (UK NC Y2 AH — animals have offspring which grow into adults; NGSS 1-LS3-1)
- **Hook:** Baby photo of a caterpillar and an adult butterfly: "Same animal?!"
- **Key activity:** Match adult/young cards (kitten–cat, joey–kangaroo, tadpole–frog, caterpillar–butterfly, chick–hen); sort into "looks like parent" and "changes a lot".
- **Check for understanding:** "A tadpole looks nothing like a frog. How do we know it's a young frog?" → It hatches from frogspawn laid by a frog and grows into a frog — it changes shape as it grows.
- **Differentiation:** support: 5 pairs with names / stretch: explain how young animals are like their parents but not exactly the same
- **Materials:** adult/young matching cards, baby-photo guessing board (adults in school)
- **Joy:** Guess-the-baby-photo of school staff.

### Lesson 26 — Life cycles: butterflies and frogs
- **Duration:** 30 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can order the stages of a butterfly and a frog life cycle and describe the changes." (UK NC Y2 AH — growth; NGSS 3-LS1-1 preview)
- **Hook:** "An egg, a very hungry caterpillar… what comes next?"
- **Key activity:** Order life-cycle cards; model with play dough; if possible, raise caterpillars in a class butterfly kit (spring) or watch time-lapse video.
- **Check for understanding:** "What happens inside the chrysalis?" → The caterpillar's body changes into a butterfly.
- **Differentiation:** support: 4 stages with pictures / stretch: compare the two life cycles — what is the same?
- **Materials:** life-cycle cards, play dough, butterfly kit or time-lapse video
- **Joy:** Play-dough life-cycle models.

### Lesson 27 — Human life cycle: how we grow
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can describe the stages of human growth (baby, toddler, child, teenager, adult, older adult) and what changes." (UK NC Y2 AH — growth into adults)
- **Hook:** "What could you do as a baby? What can you do now?"
- **Key activity:** Children bring baby photos or facts; make a class human timeline; measure hand spans and compare with adults.
- **Check for understanding:** "What do human babies need that older children can do for themselves?" → Babies need feeding, carrying and cleaning; children can feed and wash themselves.
- **Differentiation:** support: 4 stages with pictures / stretch: compare how long human young depend on parents with other animals
- **Materials:** photos (optional, with permission), timeline strip, rulers
- **Joy:** "Then and now" photo display.

### Lesson 28 — What do all animals need to survive?
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can explain that animals, including humans, need water, food and air to survive." (UK NC Y2 AH — basic needs)
- **Hook:** "You're going on a space mission to Mars. What must you pack to stay alive?"
- **Key activity:** Sort "needs" vs "wants" cards for a space mission; compare with the needs of a pet, a fish, a bird. Discuss how fish get air from water.

## Unit 4 — Sound and vibration

**Essential question:** What makes a sound, and how does it reach our ears?

**Key vocabulary:** sound, vibrate, vibration, source, travel, ear, loud, quiet, high, low, pitch, volume, instrument, echo, muffle

### Lesson 35 — Sound hunt
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can identify sounds around me and name their sources." (NGSS 1-PS4-1; link: Music)
- **Hook:** "Close your eyes for one minute. How many sounds can you hear?"
- **Key activity:** Listening walk indoors and outdoors; record sounds and their sources on a map; sort into loud and quiet, near and far.
- **Check for understanding:** "What is the 'source' of a sound?" → The thing that makes it — e.g. the bird is the source of birdsong.
- **Differentiation:** support: picture recording sheet / stretch: rank sounds by volume and explain
- **Materials:** clipboards, sound map, blindfolds (optional)
- **Joy:** Silent listening walk.

### Lesson 36 — Sounds are made by vibrations
- **Duration:** 30 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can show that sounds are made when something vibrates." (NGSS 1-PS4-1)
- **Hook:** Rice on a drum: "Watch the rice when I tap… what's happening?"
- **Key activity:** Stations: rice on a drum, a tuning fork touching water, an elastic band stretched over a box, humming with a hand on your throat. Record what vibrates each time.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why did the rice jump when the drum was hit?" → The drum skin vibrated (shook quickly) and made the sound — the rice showed the vibrations.
- **Differentiation:** support: two stations with an adult / stretch: find something that vibrates but you can't see it moving
- **Materials:** drum, rice, tuning fork, bowl of water, elastic bands, boxes
- **Joy:** The splash of the tuning fork in water.

### Lesson 37 — How sound travels to our ears
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can explain that sound travels from the source to our ears and can travel through air, solids and water." (NGSS 1-PS4-1 extension)
- **Hook:** "Put your ear on the table while a friend taps it gently at the other end. What do you notice?"
- **Key activity:** Table-tap test; "slinky" model of vibrations passing along; discuss how whales communicate through water.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why could you hear the tap more clearly through the table?" → Sound travels well through solids like wood.
- **Differentiation:** support: slinky model with an adult / stretch: explain why sound gets quieter the further away you are
- **Materials:** table, slinky spring, whale-song recording
- **Joy:** Listening to whale song.

### Lesson 38 — Make a string telephone
- **Duration:** 30 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can build a string telephone and test how to make it work best." (NGSS 1-PS4-4; WS — test and improve)
- **Hook:** "Can you whisper to a friend across the room without shouting?"
- **Key activity:** Build with cups and string; test tight vs loose string; test different string lengths. Record what works.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why does the string need to be tight?" → The vibrations travel along a tight string; a loose string doesn't carry them well.
- **Differentiation:** support: pre-made telephones to test / stretch: test with different materials (wool, fishing line)
- **Materials:** paper cups, string, wool, fishing line, pencils (to poke holes, adult), paper clips
- **Joy:** Secret messages by string telephone.

### Lesson 39 — Loud and quiet
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can make a sound louder or quieter and explain it using the word vibration." (NGSS 1-PS4-1; link: Music dynamics)
- **Hook:** "Tap the drum gently. Now harder. What changed?"
- **Key activity:** Compare gentle and strong taps on drums; watch rice bounce higher; test ways to muffle sound (hands over ears, cushion on drum).
- **Check for understanding:** "Why does hitting the drum harder make a louder sound?" → Bigger vibrations make a louder sound.
- **Differentiation:** support: loud/quiet sort / stretch: explain how ear defenders protect hearing
- **Materials:** drums, rice, cushions, fabric
- **Joy:** "Quiet challenge" — muffle the loudest sound.

### Lesson 40 — High and low sounds
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can make high and low sounds and notice that shorter or tighter things usually make higher sounds." (link: Music pitch; NGSS 1-PS4-1)
- **Hook:** Bottles with different water levels: "Blow across the tops. Why different notes?"
- **Key activity:** Test: elastic bands of different thickness, chime bars of different lengths, bottles with different water levels (tap them). Record high/low.
- **Check for understanding:** "Which chime bar makes the higher note — the short one or the long one?" → The short one.
- **Differentiation:** support: sort sounds into high/low / stretch: make a 3-note scale with bottles
- **Materials:** glass bottles and water, chime bars, elastic bands, box
- **Joy:** Bottle orchestra.

### Lesson 41 — Design and make a musical instrument
- **Duration:** 30 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can design and make an instrument, explain what vibrates, and change its sound." (NGSS 1-PS4-4, K-2-ETS1-2; link: Music)
- **Hook:** "Every instrument in the world makes sound the same way. Make your own!"
- **Key activity:** Choose shaker, drum, guitar (elastic bands) or pan pipes (straws); build; explain what vibrates; show how to make it louder/quieter or higher/lower.
- **Check for understanding:** "What vibrates in your instrument, and how can you make it louder?" → A named part (the elastic band) — pluck it harder.
- **Differentiation:** support: shaker / stretch: an instrument that can play two pitches
- **Materials:** boxes, elastic bands, straws, tape, pots, rice, lentils
- **Joy:** Class junk-instrument band.

### Lesson 42 — End-of-unit check: sound
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can explain how sounds are made and travel, and change volume and pitch." (NGSS 1-PS4-1 — assessment)
- **Hook:** "Sound detective quiz!"
- **Key activity:** Demonstrate with your instrument; answer questions on vibrations, travel, loud/quiet, high/low; explain the string telephone.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why can't you hear a sound if nothing is vibrating?" → Sounds are made by vibrations; no vibration, no sound.
- **Differentiation:** support: oral answers with demonstrations / stretch: explain why sound gets fainter with distance
- **Materials:** instruments, question cards
- **Joy:** Instrument showcase.

**End-of-unit check:** Show and explain what vibrates to make a sound in two different instruments, make one louder and one higher-pitched, and explain why a tight string telephone works better than a loose one.

## Unit 5 — Sun, Moon and our Earth

**Essential question:** What patterns can we see in the sky, and what is our Earth's surface made of?

**Key vocabulary:** Sun, Moon, star, planet, Earth, day, night, sunrise, sunset, spin, orbit, shadow, phase, full moon, crescent, pattern, daylight, season, land, water, ocean, river, lake, mountain, island, solid, liquid, ice

### Lesson 43 — Day and night
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can explain that day and night happen because the Earth spins." (NGSS 1-ESS1-1)
- **Hook:** "Where does the Sun go at night?"
- **Key activity:** Model: a lamp (Sun) and a globe with a sticker for "home"; spin the globe slowly — watch home move into light (day) and shadow (night). Children act as the spinning Earth.
- **Check for understanding:** "When it's day for us, is it day everywhere on Earth? Explain." → No — the side of Earth facing away from the Sun is having night.
- **Differentiation:** support: guided model with the teacher / stretch: find a country having night when it's our day
- **Materials:** lamp, globe, stickers, torch
- **Joy:** Being the spinning Earth.

### Lesson 44 — Never look at the Sun: shadows during the day
- **Duration:** 30 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can observe how my shadow changes during the day and explain that the Sun appears to move across the sky." (NGSS 1-ESS1-1; safety)
- **Hook:** "Never look directly at the Sun — it can damage your eyes. But we can study it safely using shadows!"
- **Key activity:** Draw around a partner's shadow (or a stick's shadow) in chalk at three times of day; measure its length; notice the change in direction and length.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why is your shadow shortest around midday?" → The Sun is highest in the sky then.
- **Differentiation:** support: describe "longer/shorter" / stretch: predict the next shadow and check
- **Materials:** chalk, metre sticks, a stick in a pot, recording sheet
- **Joy:** Chalk shadow portraits.

### Lesson 45 — The Moon's changing shape
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can describe how the Moon seems to change shape over a month and record it." (NGSS 1-ESS1-1)
- **Hook:** Read *Papa, Please Get the Moon for Me* by Eric Carle: "Does the Moon really get smaller?"
- **Key activity:** Moon diary (4 weeks, at home with families); ball-and-lamp model showing we see different amounts of the lit half. a paper-plate phase wheel.
- **Check for understanding:** "Does the Moon really change shape? What changes?" → No — it's always round; we see different amounts of its sunlit half as it moves around Earth.
- **Differentiation:** support: order 4 phase pictures / stretch: predict the shape a week after full moon
- **Materials:** *Papa, Please Get the Moon for Me* by Eric Carle, polystyrene ball, lamp, moon diary sheets
- **Joy:** Moon diaries with families.

### Lesson 46 — Stars and the night sky
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can explain that stars are suns far away and that people around the world have made pictures (constellations) from them." (NGSS 1-ESS1-1; link: History, cultures)
- **Hook:** "The Sun is a star. Why does it look so much bigger than the others?"
- **Key activity:** Make a constellation viewer (a tube with holes); learn one or two constellations and stories from different cultures (e.g. the Pleiades appear in stories on many continents); discuss light pollution.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why does the Sun look bigger and brighter than other stars?" → It is much closer to Earth.
- **Differentiation:** support: make a viewer with a template / stretch: tell the story behind a constellation
- **Materials:** card tubes, pins (adult), torches, star maps
- **Joy:** Constellation viewers in the dark.

### Lesson 47 — Daylight through the year
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can compare the amount of daylight in different seasons using sunrise and sunset times." (NGSS 1-ESS1-2)
- **Hook:** "Why is it dark at breakfast in winter but light in summer?"
- **Key activity:** Read sunrise and sunset times from a calendar or website for four months; count daylight hours; make a block diagram (link: Maths statistics).
- **Check for understanding:** "Which season has the most daylight, and what does our chart show?" → Summer; its bar is the tallest.
- **Differentiation:** support: compare two months / stretch: compare with a city in the other hemisphere
- **Materials:** sunrise/sunset table, squared paper
- **Joy:** "Daylight detective" chart.

### Lesson 48 — Land and water on Earth
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can identify land and water on a globe and map and name kinds of each (ocean, river, lake; mountain, island, valley)." (NGSS 2-ESS2-2; link: Geography)
- **Hook:** "If you spin a globe and put your finger down, are you more likely to land on land or water?"
- **Key activity:** Globe-toss game: tally land vs water; learn that most of Earth is covered by water; model landforms with sand and water in a tray.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why did we land on water more often in the globe toss?" → Most of the Earth's surface is covered by water (oceans).
- **Differentiation:** support: land/water only / stretch: model three landforms and name them
- **Materials:** inflatable globe, tally sheet, sand tray, water, jugs
- **Joy:** Globe toss.

### Lesson 49 — Water as solid and liquid
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can describe where water is found as a solid (ice) and a liquid on Earth." (NGSS 2-ESS2-3)
- **Hook:** "Is the ice at the North Pole water?"
- **Key activity:** Ice-balloon exploration (melting, floating); photos of glaciers, icebergs, rivers, oceans; sort into solid/liquid water.
- **Check for understanding:** "Is an iceberg made of water? How do you know?" → Yes — it's frozen water; when it melts it becomes liquid water.

### Lesson 50 — Earth changes: fast and slow

## Unit 6 — Plants: how seeds and bulbs grow

**Essential question:** What does a seed need to grow into a healthy plant — and how can we find out?

**Key vocabulary:** seed, bulb, germinate, root, shoot, stem, leaf, flower, seedling, water, light, warmth, soil, healthy, wilt, predict, fair test, measure, record, observe over time

### Lesson 53 — Seeds and bulbs
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can describe seeds and bulbs and explain that they contain a baby plant and a food store." (UK NC Y2 PL — seeds and bulbs grow into mature plants)
- **Hook:** Open a soaked broad bean: "There's a baby plant hiding inside!"
- **Key activity:** Dissect soaked beans with magnifiers; compare seeds (avocado stone, sunflower, bean, rice, coconut picture) and bulbs (onion, tulip); sort by size and shape.
- **Check for understanding:** "What is the food store in a bean for?" → It feeds the baby plant until it grows leaves and can make its own food.
- **Differentiation:** support: label the bean parts with picture cards / stretch: explain why bigger seeds aren't always bigger plants
- **Materials:** soaked broad beans, magnifiers, variety of seeds, onion and tulip bulbs, *A Seed Is Sleepy* by Dianna Hutts Aston
- **Joy:** Finding the baby plant inside the bean.

### Lesson 54 — Planting and predicting
- **Duration:** 30 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can plant a seed and a bulb and predict what will grow first." (UK NC Y2 PL; WS — predict)
- **Hook:** "Bean in a jar: we'll be able to watch the roots grow!"
- **Key activity:** Plant beans in clear jars against damp paper towel (to see roots) and bulbs in pots; set up a class growth diary with photos; predict root or shoot first.
- **Check for understanding:** "Which do you think will grow first, root or shoot, and why?" → A prediction with a reason (e.g. root first, to find water).
- **Differentiation:** support: planting with step cards / stretch: plan how to measure growth each week
- **Materials:** clear jars, paper towels, beans, bulbs, pots, compost, growth diaries
- **Joy:** Planting your own bean.

### Lesson 55 — Root first, then shoot
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can observe and record how a seed germinates over time, with the root appearing before the shoot." (UK NC Y2 PL — observe and describe growth; WS)
- **Hook:** "Was your prediction right? Let's check the jars!"
- **Key activity:** Observe and draw jar beans; measure root and shoot lengths with a ruler (link: Maths length); record in a table each week.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why does the root grow first?" → The seedling needs water from the soil before it can grow up — the root anchors it and takes in water.
- **Differentiation:** support: draw and describe / stretch: make a line of growth drawings (a flick book)
- **Materials:** jar beans, rulers, magnifiers, growth diaries
- **Joy:** Growth flick book.

### Lesson 56 — What do plants need? Planning a fair test
- **Duration:** 30 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can plan a fair test to find out whether plants need water, light and warmth." (UK NC Y2 PL — plants need water, light, suitable temperature; WS; NGSS 2-LS2-1)
- **Hook:** "Do plants really need light? How could we prove it fairly?"
- **Key activity:** Plan three tests with identical seedlings: one without water, one in a dark cupboard, one in the fridge, plus a "control" with everything. Discuss keeping everything else the same.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why do we need a plant that has everything (the control)?" → To compare — so we can see what happens when only one thing is missing.
- **Differentiation:** support: one test (light vs dark) with the teacher / stretch: predict each result with a reason
- **Materials:** identical seedlings (cress or beans), labels, cupboard, fridge, planning sheet
- **Joy:** Setting up a real experiment that runs for two weeks.

### Lesson 57 — Measuring and recording results
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can measure plant height and record observations in a table to compare results." (UK NC Y2 WS — gather and record data; NGSS 2-LS2-1)
- **Hook:** "Our experiment's first check-in: what do you notice?"
- **Key activity:** Measure heights, note leaf colour and droopiness; record in a class table; photograph each plant.
- **Check for understanding:** "The plant in the dark is tall but yellow. What does that tell us?" → It grew tall stretching to find light, but it's unhealthy — it needs light to make its food and stay green.
- **Differentiation:** support: describe with picture scale (healthy → wilting) / stretch: calculate differences in height
- **Materials:** rulers, results table, camera
- **Joy:** Before-and-after photo gallery.

### Lesson 58 — Concluding: what did we find out?
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can use my results to answer our question and explain what plants need." (UK NC Y2 WS — use observations to answer questions)
- **Hook:** "Detectives, the evidence is in!"
- **Key activity:** Compare all plants; write a conclusion using "We found out that… because…"; discuss surprises; rescue the plants if possible.
- **Check for understanding:** "Which plant was healthiest, and what does that show?" → The control plant with water, light and warmth — plants need all three to grow well.
- **Differentiation:** support: conclusion frame / stretch: suggest a new question to test (Does music help?)
- **Materials:** results table, plants, conclusion frames
- **Joy:** Rescuing and reviving the test plants.

### Lesson 59 — How seeds travel
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can explain how seeds travel (wind, water, animals, bursting) and why they need to spread." (NGSS 2-LS2-2; UK NC Y3 PL preview)
- **Hook:** Blow a dandelion clock: "Where will these seeds land?"
- **Key activity:** Seed-dispersal stations: sycamore helicopters, burrs on socks, coconut floating, pea pods; design a seed that travels by wind and test it.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why is it good for seeds to travel away from the parent plant?" → So they have space, light and water to grow and don't compete with the parent.
- **Differentiation:** support: match seeds to ways they travel / stretch: improve the wind-seed design after testing
- **Materials:** sycamore seeds, burrs, old socks, coconut (or photo), paper, paper clips, fan
- **Joy:** Sock walk to collect hitch-hiking seeds.

### Lesson 60 — Scientist spotlight: Wangari Maathai and planting trees
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can explain how Wangari Maathai used tree planting to help people and the land in Kenya." (UK NC Y2 WS — people who helped science; link: Life skills, Geography)
- **Hook:** "One woman helped plant millions of trees. How did she start?"
- **Key activity:** Read *Wangari's Trees of Peace* by Jeanette Winter; discuss why trees matter (shade, soil, fruit, wood, homes for animals); plant a tree or seedling as a class.
- **Check for understanding:** "How did planting trees help the land?" → Tree roots hold the soil together and trees give shade, fruit and firewood, so the land became healthier.
- **Differentiation:** support: picture sequencing / stretch: link to the stream-table model of soil washing away
- **Materials:** *Wangari's Trees of Peace* by Jeanette Winter, seedling, spades
- **Joy:** Planting a class tree.

### Lesson 61 — Retrieval: plants
- **Duration:** 20 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can recall how seeds and bulbs grow and what plants need." (UK NC Y2 PL — retrieval)
- **Hook:** "Plant quiz — grow a class sunflower one petal per right answer!"
- **Key activity:** Quiz; order germination pictures; explain the fair test.
- **Check for understanding:** "What would happen to a plant kept in the dark for two weeks, and why?" → It would grow pale/yellow and weak because it needs light.
- **Differentiation:** support: picture answers / stretch: explain the control
- **Materials:** quiz cards, paper sunflower
- **Joy:** Growing the paper sunflower.

### Lesson 62 — End-of-unit check: plants
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can show what I understand about how plants grow and how we found out." (UK NC Y2 PL — assessment)
- **Hook:** "The garden centre needs a plant expert!"
- **Key activity:** Label germination stages; explain the fair test and results; give advice for growing a healthy plant.
- **Check for understanding:** "Give advice to someone whose plant is wilting." → Water it, give it light and warmth; check the soil.
- **Differentiation:** support: oral with pictures / stretch: explain seed dispersal too
- **Materials:** assessment sheet, seedlings
- **Joy:** Plant-expert badge.

**End-of-unit check:** Order the stages from seed to flowering plant, explain what our fair test showed about light, water and warmth (including why we had a control plant), and give two tips for keeping a plant healthy.

## Unit 7 — Young scientists: our own investigation

**Essential question:** How do scientists answer their own questions?

**Key vocabulary:** question, predict, plan, fair test, change, keep the same, measure, record, table, chart, result, conclusion, evidence, scientist, present

### Lesson 63 — What makes a good science question?
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can sort questions into ones we can test and ones we can't, and write a testable question." (UK NC Y2 WS — ask simple questions)
- **Hook:** "'Why is the sky blue?' vs 'Which ball bounces highest?' — which can we test in class today?"
- **Key activity:** Sort question cards; improve untestable questions; brainstorm class questions from the year (plants, materials, sound, animals).
- **Check for understanding:** "Why is 'Which paper towel soaks up most water?' a good test question?" → We can change one thing (the towel) and measure the result (water soaked up).
- **Differentiation:** support: choose from question cards / stretch: write a question using "Does… affect…?"
- **Materials:** question cards, sticky notes
- **Joy:** The class "Wonder Wall" of questions.

### Lesson 64 — Planning a fair test
- **Duration:** 30 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can plan what to change, what to keep the same and what to measure in my test." (UK NC Y2 WS — perform simple tests)
- **Hook:** "Our question: which ball bounces highest? What would make the test unfair?"
- **Key activity:** In groups, choose a question (e.g. bouncing balls, absorbent paper towels, cars rolling down ramps); fill a planning board: change / keep the same / measure; predict.
- **Check for understanding:** "If you drop one ball from higher up, is it a fair test? Why not?" → No — the height changed too, so we wouldn't know if the ball or the height caused the difference.
- **Differentiation:** support: the teacher's question with a planning board / stretch: choose how many times to repeat and why
- **Materials:** planning boards, balls, paper towels, ramps, toy cars
- **Joy:** Choosing your own investigation.

### Lesson 65 — Doing the investigation
- **Duration:** 30 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can carry out my test carefully, measure and record the results." (UK NC Y2 WS — gather and record data)
- **Hook:** "Scientists, safety goggles on — time to test!"
- **Key activity:** Run the test three times; measure with rulers/metre sticks or count; record in a table.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why did we test three times?" → To check our results — one test might have been a fluke.
- **Differentiation:** support: pre-drawn table / stretch: calculate the difference between highest and lowest
- **Materials:** equipment, rulers, metre sticks, tables, goggles
- **Joy:** Hands-on testing.

### Lesson 66 — Presenting results in a chart
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can show my results in a block diagram or pictogram and say what it shows." (UK NC Y2 WS — record findings; link: Maths statistics)
- **Hook:** "A table is useful, but a chart shows the answer at a glance!"
- **Key activity:** Turn results into a block diagram on squared paper; answer "most/least/how many more" questions.
- **Check for understanding:** "What does the tallest bar show?" → The result with the biggest value — e.g. the ball that bounced highest.
- **Differentiation:** support: pre-drawn axes / stretch: a scale in 2s or 5s
- **Materials:** squared paper, rulers, coloured pencils
- **Joy:** Colourful result charts.

### Lesson 67 — Writing a conclusion
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can write what I found out, compare it with my prediction, and suggest what to test next." (UK NC Y2 WS — use observations to answer questions)
- **Hook:** "Was your prediction right? Scientists love being surprised!"
- **Key activity:** Conclusion frame: "We found out that… This matched/didn't match our prediction because… Next time we could…"
- **Check for understanding:** "Your prediction was wrong. Is that bad?" → No — scientists learn from results that surprise them; the evidence is what counts.
- **Differentiation:** support: frame with word bank / stretch: explain one thing that could have made the test unfair
- **Materials:** conclusion frames, results
- **Joy:** "Surprise of the day" award.

### Lesson 68 — Science fair: presenting our investigation
- **Duration:** 30 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can present my investigation to an audience and answer questions." (UK NC Y2 WS; SL — present)
- **Hook:** "Welcome to the Year 1 Science Fair!"
- **Key activity:** Groups present question, plan, results chart and conclusion to visitors (families or another class); visitors ask questions.
- **Check for understanding:** "What was the fair test in your investigation?" → Named change, kept-the-same factors and measurement.
- **Differentiation:** support: present one part / stretch: answer a "what if" question
- **Materials:** posters, equipment for demonstrations
- **Joy:** The science fair.

### Lesson 69 — Year review: our science year
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can recall the big ideas from each unit and explain the one I found most interesting." (UK NC Y2 — review)
- **Hook:** "Science museum: one exhibit for every unit."
- **Key activity:** Stations revisit each unit with a quick practical (sort, test, model); children explain to partners.
- **Check for understanding:** "Pick one unit and explain its big idea to a younger child." → A clear explanation with an example.
- **Differentiation:** support: picture prompts / stretch: link two units (e.g. sound and materials — muffling)
- **Materials:** station equipment from each unit
- **Joy:** Museum-guide role.

### Lesson 70 — End-of-year science check
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can show my understanding of the year's science and my working-scientifically skills." (UK NC Y2 — end-of-year assessment)
- **Hook:** "Show the scientist you've become!"
- **Key activity:** Low-stakes practical and oral tasks across the units; plan a mini fair test on a new question; teacher records next steps for Year 2.
- **Check for understanding:** "Plan a fair test: does salt water freeze as fast as plain water? What will you change and keep the same?" → Change: salt or no salt; keep the same: amount of water, container, freezer place; measure: time to freeze or check at set times.
- **Differentiation:** support: oral with prompts / stretch: predict with a reason
- **Materials:** assessment cards, simple equipment
- **Joy:** "Young Scientist" certificate.

**End-of-unit check:** Plan, carry out and present a simple fair test on your own question — naming what you changed, what you kept the same and what you measured — and explain whether your result matched your prediction.

1. Sorts things into living, dead and never alive and explains using the life processes. *(Seen: sort and explain.)*
2. Explains how two animals are suited to their habitats and builds a food chain with correct arrows. *(Seen: drawing and talk.)*

## Teacher guidance

**Misconceptions to watch for and how to fix them**
- *"If it moves, it's alive"* (cars, clouds, fire) → use the full life-process test.
- *"Plants get their food from the soil"* → plants make their own food using light; soil gives water and minerals — the dark-cupboard plant is the evidence.
- *"A whale is a fish"* (retrieved from Foundation) → breathes air, feeds milk to young: a mammal.
- *"Heavier/bigger things are made of stronger materials"* → test, don't assume.
- *"The Sun moves around the Earth"* → the Earth spins; model with the globe (it only *looks* like the Sun moves).
- *"The Moon changes shape"* → it is always round; we see different amounts of its lit half.
- *"Sound is only in the air"* → the table-tap test shows it travels through solids.

**Safety.** Never look at the Sun; handwashing after soil, minibeasts and outdoor work; adults handle hot water, pins and knives; check food allergies before tasting; return minibeasts to where they were found.

**How to teach it well.** Let children explore before you explain; ask "How could we find out?" before giving answers; always record results in a table and talk about what they show. Keep the same investigation vocabulary all year (change, keep the same, measure, predict, conclude) so Year 2 can build formal fair tests on it.

**Vertical alignment.** Foundation gives names of plants, animals, body parts, materials and seasons. Year 2 (ages 7–8) builds on this year's growth, needs and materials work with plant organs and pollination, nutrition and skeletons, rocks and soils, light and shadows, and forces and magnets.
