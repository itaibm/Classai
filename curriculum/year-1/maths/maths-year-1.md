# Year 1 Mathematics (ages 6–7) — Scope & Sequence

> Part of **the best international primary school** (see [`../../../SCHOOL-CHARTER.md`](../../../SCHOOL-CHARTER.md)).
> Built to [`../../PLANNING-BRIEF.md`](../../PLANNING-BRIEF.md). Standards reference:
> `knowledge-base/subjects/maths/year-2.md` (UK Year 2 = our Year 1). Codes: **UK NC Y2** strand
> (N = number & place value, AS = addition & subtraction, MD = multiplication & division, F = fractions,
> M = measurement, G = geometry, S = statistics) and the nearest **CCSS** Grade 1/2 standard.

## Year overview

Year 1 is the year a child stops *counting* and starts *reasoning* with numbers to 100. It is taught
in the Singapore / Maths-No-Problem mastery tradition: every lesson moves **concrete → pictorial →
abstract** (base-10 blocks and straws → drawings, part-whole and early bar models → number sentences),
the whole class stays together on one idea until it is secure, and every unit closes with reasoning,
problem solving and a mastery check.

**What this year assumes (from Foundation, ages 5–6):** counting reliably to 100, number bonds to 10,
adding and subtracting within 20 by counting on/back, a first look at tens and ones, halves and quarters
of shapes, coin recognition, o'clock and half past, naming common 2-D and 3-D shapes, and simple position
words. Lesson 1 of each unit retrieves the Foundation idea it builds on, so a child new to the school
(or who skipped Foundation) is caught up inside the unit.

**How the year runs.** *Term 1* builds the number engine: place value to 100 (Unit 1), then addition and
subtraction with two-digit numbers, including exchanging (Unit 2), then multiplication as equal groups
and arrays with the 2, 5 and 10 times tables (Unit 3). *Term 2* completes the four operations with
division (Unit 4) and puts number to work in measurement — length (Unit 5), mass, capacity and
temperature (Unit 6) and money (Unit 7) — before fractions of shapes and quantities (Unit 8), which
need division. *Term 3* covers time to five minutes (Unit 9), statistics (Unit 10), 2-D and 3-D shape
and symmetry (Unit 11), position and direction (Unit 12), and a year-end problem-solving unit (Unit 13).
Retrieval lessons appear in every unit, and each term ends with a review lesson.

**What Year 2 relies on:** instant recall of bonds within 20 and to 100 in tens; secure place value
to 100 (so it can extend to 1000 and to column methods); the 2, 5 and 10 times tables with their
division facts (Year 2 adds the 3, 4 and 8 tables); unit and non-unit fractions of quantities (Year 2
moves to tenths and equivalence); reading scales and telling the time to five minutes (Year 2 goes to
the minute and 24-hour time); and comfort drawing a bar model for a one-step word problem.

**Money note.** Money is taught with *your own* currency. Many currencies are "1 big unit = 100 small
units" (1 dollar = 100 cents, 1 euro = 100 cents, £1 = 100 pence, 1 rupee = 100 paise). The authored
app lessons use pounds and pence as their worked example — the maths is identical in any
100-subunit currency.

## Unit 1 — Numbers to 100

**Essential question:** How do the tens and the ones decide how big a number is?

**Key vocabulary:** ones, tens, hundred, digit, place value, partition, recombine, two-digit, number line, estimate, greater than (>), less than (<), equal to (=), order, ascending, descending, odd, even, count in steps

### Lesson 1 — Tens and ones to 100
- **Duration:** 22 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can build any number to 100 and say how many tens and ones it has." (UK NC Y2 N — place value; CCSS 1.NBT.2)
- **Hook:** "Why are 34 and 43 different if they use the same two digits?"
- **Key activity:** Concrete: build 65 with 6 ten-rods and 5 ones on a place-value mat. Pictorial: draw tens as sticks and ones as dots. Abstract: write 65 = 6 tens and 5 ones. We do 34 and 43 side by side; you do five numbers from a dice roll (tens dice, ones dice).
- **Check for understanding:** "Show me 43 and 34 with rods. Which has more rods, and why?" → 43 has 4 tens (rods), 34 has only 3; the position of the digit decides its value.
- **Differentiation:** support: a tens/ones mat with picture labels, numbers to 50 / stretch: "What is 7 tens and 0 ones? 9 tens and 10 ones?" → 70; 100
- **Materials:** base-10 rods and unit cubes, place-value mat, two 0–9 dice
- **Joy:** Place-value basketball — shoot each digit card into its column hoop (tens basket, ones basket).

### Lesson 2 — Counting big collections by making tens
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can count a big collection quickly by bundling it into tens and explain why that is faster than counting in ones." (UK NC Y2 N — count to 100; CCSS 1.NBT.1)
- **Hook:** Tip out a jar of about 70 lolly sticks: "Guess how many. How could we check without losing count?"
- **Key activity:** I do: count 23 sticks one by one, then bundle into tens with elastic bands and count "10, 20, 23". We do: bundle the jar together, record on a 100 square. You do: bundle and count a second pile (40–90 items), writing the total.
- **Check for understanding:** "Sam counted 6 bundles of ten and 4 loose sticks and said 10. What went wrong?" → He counted the bundles as ones; it is 6 tens and 4 ones = 64.
- **Differentiation:** support: counts under 50, a ten-frame to fill before each bundle is tied / stretch: count a pile past 100 and write it (e.g. 112 = 11 tens and 2 ones)
- **Materials:** lolly sticks or straws, elastic bands, 100 square, ten-frames
- **Joy:** The "guess jar" — everyone's estimate goes on a sticky note and the closest guess wins the job of tying the last bundle.

### Lesson 3 — The value of each digit (and zero)
- **Duration:** 22 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can say what each digit in a two-digit number is worth, including when one digit is zero." (UK NC Y2 N — place value of each digit; CCSS 1.NBT.2c)
- **Hook:** Show 5 and 50 on two cards: "Same digit, but one is worth ten times more. How can a 5 be worth 50?"
- **Key activity:** Build 58, 85, 50 and 5 with base-10; use arrow (place-value) cards that slide together (50 + 8 = 58). We do: "What is the 7 worth in 72? in 27?" You do: circle the digit worth the most in a set of numbers and write its value.
- **Check for understanding:** "Mia says the 0 in 60 doesn't matter so 60 is the same as 6. Is she right? Show me." → No; the 0 holds the ones place so the 6 stays in the tens place — 6 tens is 60, not 6.
- **Differentiation:** support: arrow cards and base-10 always in view / stretch: "I am a number whose tens digit is 3 more than my ones digit and I am less than 50" → 30 or 41
- **Materials:** arrow (place-value) cards, base-10, digit cards
- **Joy:** "Digit detectives" — solve three riddle cards to open a (paper) padlock on a treasure envelope.

### Lesson 4 — Partitioning numbers
- **Duration:** 22 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can partition a two-digit number into tens and ones and put it back together." (UK NC Y2 N — partition; CCSS 1.NBT.2)
- **Hook:** "Can you break a number apart and rebuild it without losing any?"
- **Key activity:** Take 76 in rods and cubes, split it into 70 and 6, then recombine. Record on a part-whole model (whole 76; parts 70 and 6). Repeat with 50 (= 50 + 0) to break the "5 and 0" mistake. Abstract: 43 = 40 + 3 and 40 + 3 = 43.
- **Check for understanding:** "Leo wrote 58 = 5 + 8. Build it and fix it." → 58 = 50 + 8; the 5 is 5 tens.
- **Differentiation:** support: build first, then physically separate the rods from the cubes / stretch: complete 40 + ☐ = 47 and ☐ + 9 = 69
- **Materials:** base-10 rods and cubes, part-whole mats
- **Joy:** "Smash and rebuild" — explode a number into parts, then race to reassemble it.

### Lesson 5 — Flexible partitioning
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can partition a number in more than one way, such as 43 = 30 + 13." (UK NC Y2 N — partition in different ways; CCSS 1.NBT.2a)
- **Hook:** "I have 43 cubes but only 3 ten-rods. Can I still make 43?"
- **Key activity:** Concrete: break one ten-rod into 10 ones — 43 becomes 3 tens and 13 ones, then 2 tens and 23 ones. Pictorial: record every way on part-whole models. Abstract: 43 = 40 + 3 = 30 + 13 = 20 + 23. You do: find three ways to partition 56.
- **Check for understanding:** "Is 64 = 50 + 14 true? Prove it with blocks." → Yes; swap one ten for 10 ones — 5 tens and 14 ones still makes 64.
- **Differentiation:** support: stay with numbers under 40 and physical exchange every time / stretch: partition into three parts (64 = 30 + 30 + 4) and find all tens-and-ones splits for 35
- **Materials:** base-10 blocks (with loose unit cubes that snap into rods, e.g. linking cubes), part-whole mats
- **Joy:** "How many ways?" race — every new way earns a sticker on the class number wall.

### Lesson 6 — Numbers on a number line to 100
- **Duration:** 22 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can place and find numbers on a number line marked in tens and ones." (UK NC Y2 N — number line; CCSS 2.MD.6)
- **Hook:** A washing line across the room with pegs at 0 and 100: "Where does 50 hang? 99?"
- **Key activity:** We do: peg number cards on the washing line, reasoning with tens ("47 is between 40 and 50, nearer 50"). Pictorial: fill missing numbers on lines marked in 1s and in 10s. You do: find the arrowed numbers on four printed lines.
- **Check for understanding:** "An arrow points 3 marks after 60 on a line counting in ones. Tom says 63, Ella says 57. Who is right, and why?" → Tom; counting forward (to the right) makes numbers bigger.
- **Differentiation:** support: a 100-bead string (beads in groups of ten colours) beside the line / stretch: lines counting in 2s or 5s with unmarked arrows
- **Materials:** washing line, pegs, number cards 0–100, 100-bead string, printed number lines
- **Joy:** Human number line — each child is a number and must find their spot without talking.

### Lesson 7 — Estimating on a number line
- **Duration:** 22 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can estimate where a number goes on a 0–100 line and explain my estimate using halfway." (UK NC Y2 N — estimate; CCSS 2.MD.6)
- **Hook:** Show a blank 0–100 line with an arrow: "No marks! Can we still work out roughly where it points?"
- **Key activity:** I do: find halfway (50) by folding a paper strip, then quarter marks (25, 75). We do: estimate arrows ("a bit less than halfway — about 40"). You do: place 10, 48, 90 on a blank line and check with a marked line.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why is 48 close to the middle of a 0–100 line but near the end of a 0–50 line?" → 48 is nearly half of 100 but nearly all of 50; where a number sits depends on the ends of the line.
- **Differentiation:** support: 0–20 blank lines first, with a folded halfway mark / stretch: estimate on a 0–50 and a 50–100 line
- **Materials:** paper strips for folding, blank and marked number lines, sticky dots
- **Joy:** "Close enough!" — score points by how close your sticker lands to the true spot.

### Lesson 8 — 1 more, 1 less, 10 more, 10 less
- **Duration:** 22 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can find 1 more, 1 less, 10 more and 10 less than any number to 100 and say which digit changes." (UK NC Y2 N; CCSS 1.NBT.5)
- **Hook:** On a 100 square, hide 36 under a counter: "What's hiding just below it? Just above it?"
- **Key activity:** Concrete: add or remove a ten-rod or a cube and watch which digit changes. Pictorial: 100-square "plus" shapes (the number, 1 more/less left-right, 10 more/less down-up). Abstract: fill 10 more/10 less tables, including crossing a ten (29 + 1, 30 − 1).
- **Check for understanding:** "What is 10 more than 95, and why does it need 3 digits?" → 105; 9 tens and 1 more ten make 10 tens = 1 hundred.
- **Differentiation:** support: a 100 square and base-10 blocks for every question / stretch: "20 more", "11 less" chains and 10 more than 95
- **Materials:** 100 squares, counters, base-10 blocks, jigsaw pieces cut from a 100 square
- **Joy:** 100-square jigsaw — a missing-piece puzzle where each piece has only one number showing.

### Lesson 9 — Comparing two-digit numbers
- **Duration:** 24 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can compare two two-digit numbers by looking at the tens first." (UK NC Y2 N — compare; CCSS 1.NBT.3)
- **Hook:** "Two house numbers, 38 and 41 — which house comes first along the street?"
- **Key activity:** Build both numbers; compare tens first (4 tens beats 3 tens). Only when the tens are equal compare the ones (47 vs 43). Record with words ("41 is greater than 38"), then symbols (41 > 38).
- **Check for understanding:** "Zara says 19 is bigger than 21 because 9 is bigger than 1. Use rods to show her what to check first." → Compare tens first: 2 tens beats 1 ten, so 21 > 19.
- **Differentiation:** support: line the rods side by side so the taller tens-stack is obvious / stretch: find every number that makes 4☐ > 46 true
- **Materials:** base-10 rods and cubes, >, < and = cards
- **Joy:** "Rod duel" — each player builds a number from dice; the bigger tens tower wins the round.

### Lesson 10 — Comparing with <, > and = (including number sentences)
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can use <, > and = to compare numbers and simple sums such as 30 + 5 and 34." (UK NC Y2 N — use <, > and =; CCSS 1.NBT.3)
- **Hook:** A crocodile puppet "always wants to eat the bigger number" — but what if one side is a sum?
- **Key activity:** Concrete: balance scales with cubes show "=" as balance. We do: build both sides (30 + 5 vs 34) before choosing the sign. You do: a sorting sheet of 10 comparisons, half with sums.
- **Check for understanding:** "Is 40 + 7 = 47 true? Is 47 = 40 + 7 true? Explain." → Both true; = means both sides are worth the same, whichever side the sum is on.
- **Differentiation:** support: compare single numbers only, with the balance and cubes / stretch: 50 + 6 ○ 60 − 4 and "make this true: 3☐ > 3☐"
- **Materials:** pan balance, cubes, <, >, = cards, crocodile puppet
- **Joy:** The "hungry crocodile" puppet chomps the bigger side — children feed it and argue its choices.

### Lesson 11 — Ordering numbers to 100
- **Duration:** 22 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can put a set of two-digit numbers in order, smallest first." (UK NC Y2 N — order; CCSS 1.NBT.3)
- **Hook:** Five jumbled race-bib numbers: "Who finished first? Let's line the runners up."
- **Key activity:** Sort by tens first (group the 20s, 30s…), then order within a tens group by ones. Pictorial: place on a number line. Abstract: order 52, 25, 50, 5 smallest to largest, then largest to smallest.
- **Check for understanding:** "Put 52, 25, 50 in order and explain how you knew which went first." → 25, 50, 52 — 2 tens is fewest; 50 and 52 both have 5 tens so compare ones.
- **Differentiation:** support: order three numbers built from rods / stretch: order six numbers including 100 and a 1-digit number, and write a number that fits between each pair
- **Materials:** number cards, base-10 rods, runner bibs (paper), number lines
- **Joy:** Sports day podium — runners (children holding bibs) must be ordered before the medals are given out.

### Lesson 12 — Counting in 2s, 5s and 10s
- **Duration:** 22 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can skip-count in 2s, 5s and 10s and spot the pattern in the last digits." (UK NC Y2 N — count in steps of 2, 5, 10; CCSS 2.NBT.2)
- **Hook:** "How many socks in 6 pairs? How many fingers in 4 hands? Is there a faster way than counting in ones?"
- **Key activity:** Concrete: pair socks, count hands, count 10-cube towers. Pictorial: jumps on a number line; colour the 2s, 5s and 10s on three 100 squares. Abstract: say and write the sequences; circle the last digits.
- **Check for understanding:** "Counting in 5s from 0, could you ever say 23? How do you know?" → No — counting in 5s from 0 always ends in 0 or 5.
- **Differentiation:** support: count real objects in groups for every step / stretch: count backwards in 5s from 100 and predict whether 85 and 92 are in the 5s pattern
- **Materials:** socks, cubes, 100 squares, coloured pencils, 2p/5p/10p coins (or local equivalents)
- **Joy:** Skip-count clapping rhyme — clap on every 5th number and stamp on every 10th.

### Lesson 13 — Counting in 3s
- **Duration:** 22 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can count forwards and backwards in 3s from 0 to 30." (UK NC Y2 N — count in steps of 3)
- **Hook:** A tricycle picture: "5 tricycles in the park — how many wheels?"
- **Key activity:** Concrete: build towers of 3 cubes and count "3, 6, 9…". Pictorial: jumps of 3 on a number line; shade 3s on a 100 square and notice the diagonal pattern. Abstract: fill gaps in 3s sequences forwards and backwards.
- **Check for understanding:** "Is 20 in the count of 3s from 0? How can you check?" → No; the count goes 18, 21 — it jumps over 20.
- **Differentiation:** support: count cube towers every time; stop at 15 / stretch: count in 3s from a number that isn't 0 (from 2: 2, 5, 8…) and spot the new pattern
- **Materials:** linking cubes, number lines, 100 squares, tricycle picture cards
- **Joy:** "Frog jumps" — a paper frog hops in 3s along a floor number line and the class shouts the landing numbers.

### Lesson 14 — Counting in 10s from any number
- **Duration:** 22 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can count on and back in 10s from any number, such as 7, 17, 27…" (UK NC Y2 N — count in tens from any number; CCSS 2.NBT.8)
- **Hook:** "I start at 4 and count in tens. Will I ever land on 50?"
- **Key activity:** Concrete: start with 4 cubes and add a ten-rod each time. Pictorial: move down a column on the 100 square. Abstract: 4, 14, 24… and back 93, 83, 73… Notice which digit changes and which stays.
- **Check for understanding:** "Counting in 10s from 6, will you say 56? Will you say 65? Explain." → 56 yes (the ones digit stays 6); 65 no (it has 5 ones).
- **Differentiation:** support: base-10 blocks and a 100 square for every step / stretch: count in 10s past 100 (86, 96, 106) and count back from a 3-digit number
- **Materials:** base-10 rods and cubes, 100 squares, counters
- **Joy:** "Lift ride" — a paper lift travels up and down the 100-square columns; the child calls out each floor.

### Lesson 15 — Odd and even numbers
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can tell whether a number is odd or even and prove it by pairing." (UK NC Y2 N; CCSS 2.OA.3)
- **Hook:** "Can everyone in our family (or class) have a partner, or is someone left out?"
- **Key activity:** Concrete: make numbers with cubes in pairs — even numbers pair exactly, odd numbers leave one. Pictorial: draw numbers in pairs. Abstract: notice the ones digit (0, 2, 4, 6, 8 = even) and use it for big numbers like 58.
- **Check for understanding:** "Without building it, is 47 odd or even? Why can you just look at the 7?" → Odd; the tens always pair up, so only the ones decide — 7 leaves one over.
- **Differentiation:** support: numbers to 20 with pairs of cubes / stretch: "Is an odd number + an odd number odd or even? Test three examples."
- **Materials:** linking cubes, pairs of socks or shoes, number cards
- **Joy:** Partner dance — the class tries to pair up for a dance with different group sizes to see who's left out.

### Lesson 16 — Number puzzles and reasoning
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can use place value to solve number puzzles and explain my thinking." (UK NC Y2 N — use place value to solve problems)
- **Hook:** "With the digit cards 3, 7 and 0, what's the biggest two-digit number you can make? The smallest?"
- **Key activity:** We do: digit-card challenges (biggest, smallest, closest to 50). You do: three riddles ("I have 4 tens and I'm odd and more than 46"). Children explain with blocks or a number line.
- **Check for understanding:** "Using 3, 7 and 0, why can't 07 be the smallest two-digit number?" → 07 is really 7, a one-digit number; the smallest two-digit number is 30.
- **Differentiation:** support: two digit cards only, blocks available / stretch: write your own riddle with exactly one answer and test it on a partner
- **Materials:** digit cards 0–9, base-10 blocks, riddle cards
- **Joy:** Make-a-riddle — the child's riddle is posted on the "Puzzle Wall" for others to solve.

### Lesson 17 — Retrieval & fluency: place value
- **Duration:** 22 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can quickly partition, compare, order and count in steps with numbers to 100." (UK NC Y2 N — retrieval)
- **Hook:** "Beat your own score: how many place-value questions can you answer in 3 minutes?"
- **Key activity:** Mixed retrieval stations: partition flashcards, 10 more/less, comparison cards, counting in 2s/3s/5s/10s. Mark together; each child picks their weakest station for a second go.
- **Check for understanding:** "Which is bigger, 10 more than 38 or 10 less than 58? Show why." → They are equal: both are 48.
- **Differentiation:** support: blocks allowed at every station / stretch: bonus cards crossing 100
- **Materials:** flashcards, base-10 blocks, timer, score sheet
- **Joy:** Personal-best chart — children beat their own score, not each other's.

### Lesson 18 — End-of-unit check: Numbers to 100
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can show I understand place value to 100 by building, drawing and explaining numbers." (UK NC Y2 N — assessment)
- **Hook:** "Today you're the teacher: explain place value to a toy who thinks 71 and 17 are the same."
- **Key activity:** Low-stakes mastery task: build three numbers, partition two ways, place numbers on a line, compare and order a set, spot the odd/even numbers. The teacher listens to explanations and notes any gaps for next week's warm-ups.
- **Check for understanding:** "Explain to the toy why 71 and 17 are different." → 71 is 7 tens and 1 one; 17 is 1 ten and 7 ones — the places give the digits different values.
- **Differentiation:** support: blocks for every item and the teacher scribes explanations / stretch: extension items with numbers past 100
- **Materials:** base-10 blocks, number lines, digit cards, a soft toy "pupil"
- **Joy:** Teaching the toy — children love correcting a "silly" learner.

**End-of-unit check:** Build 63 in two different ways, place 63 on a 0–100 line, write two numbers that are greater than 63 but less than 70, and explain which digit changes when you find 10 more.

## Unit 2 — Addition and subtraction

**Essential question:** How can knowing facts to 10 and 20 help us add and subtract bigger numbers, and how do we check we are right?

**Key vocabulary:** add, plus, sum, total, altogether, subtract, minus, take away, difference, fewer, number bond, fact family, part, whole, exchange, regroup, inverse, commutative, bar model, equation, missing number

### Lesson 19 — Number bonds within 20: fact families
- **Duration:** 22 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can use one number bond to write its whole fact family of four facts." (UK NC Y2 AS — recall facts to 20; CCSS 2.OA.2)
- **Hook:** "If I know 7 + 5 = 12, how many other facts do I get for free?"
- **Key activity:** Concrete: a 12-cube tower snapped into 7 and 5. Pictorial: part-whole model and a bar model. Abstract: write 7 + 5 = 12, 5 + 7 = 12, 12 − 5 = 7, 12 − 7 = 5. You do: fact families from dominoes.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why is 5 − 12 = 7 not in the family?" → The whole (12) is the biggest number; you can't take 12 away from 5 and get 7 — subtraction starts with the whole.
- **Differentiation:** support: bonds to 10 with a ten-frame / stretch: fact families with three numbers where one is missing (☐, 8, 17)
- **Materials:** linking cubes, dominoes, part-whole mats, ten-frames
- **Joy:** "Free facts" — every family member earns a counter in the child's fact jar.

### Lesson 20 — Bonds to 100 in tens
- **Duration:** 22 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can use 3 + 7 = 10 to know that 30 + 70 = 100." (UK NC Y2 AS — derive related facts up to 100)
- **Hook:** "You know 3 + 7. Does that help you with 30 + 70? With 300 + 700?"
- **Key activity:** Concrete: 3 cubes + 7 cubes vs 3 ten-rods + 7 ten-rods. Pictorial: a ten-frame of cubes beside a "ten-frame of ten-rods" (100). Abstract: match bonds to 10 with bonds to 100; complete 40 + ☐ = 100.
- **Check for understanding:** "Ava says 60 + 40 = 1000 because 6 + 4 = 10. Where is her mistake?" → 6 tens + 4 tens = 10 tens, which is 100, not 1000.
- **Differentiation:** support: model every fact with rods on a 100 board / stretch: 100 − 35 using 65 + 35 and bonds like 45 + ☐ = 100
- **Materials:** base-10 rods, ten-frames, 100 bead string
- **Joy:** "Snap to 100" — a card game where you win pairs of multiples of ten that make 100.

### Lesson 21 — Adding and subtracting ones (no crossing ten)
- **Duration:** 22 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can add or subtract a one-digit number to or from a two-digit number when the tens don't change, such as 34 + 5 or 47 − 3." (UK NC Y2 AS — TO ± O; CCSS 2.NBT.5)
- **Hook:** "3 + 4 = 7. So what is 23 + 4? 53 + 4? What stays the same?"
- **Key activity:** Concrete: base-10 — only the ones cubes move. Pictorial: jumps on a number line. Abstract: 34 + 5 = 39 because 4 + 5 = 9; 47 − 3 = 44 because 7 − 3 = 4. Spot the pattern across a column of related sums.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why does 62 + 5 have the same ones digit as 2 + 5?" → Only the ones are being added; the 6 tens don't change.
- **Differentiation:** support: blocks on a place-value mat / stretch: write your own set of 4 related sums with a pattern
- **Materials:** base-10 blocks, place-value mats, number lines
- **Joy:** "Pattern spotting" — the child finds a hidden pattern and gets to make a pattern puzzle for the teacher.

### Lesson 22 — Adding across a ten (bridging)
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can add a one-digit number that crosses the next ten, such as 38 + 5, by making the ten first." (UK NC Y2 AS — TO + O; CCSS 2.NBT.5)
- **Hook:** "38 is only 2 away from 40. How can that help with 38 + 5?"
- **Key activity:** Concrete: ten-frames — fill 38's last frame with 2 of the 5 counters, 3 left over. Pictorial: number line jump to the ten, then on (38 → 40 → 43). Abstract: 38 + 5 = 38 + 2 + 3 = 43.
- **Check for understanding:** "Show 47 + 6 on a number line and explain why you split 6 into 3 and 3." → 47 + 3 = 50 (the next ten) and 50 + 3 = 53; 3 is what 47 needs to make 50.
- **Differentiation:** support: numbers within 30, ten-frames for every question / stretch: 68 + 7 + 5 and explain the best route
- **Materials:** ten-frames, counters, number lines, base-10 blocks
- **Joy:** "Ten-hop" — a toy kangaroo must always hop to the ten first before the big leap.

### Lesson 23 — Subtracting across a ten (bridging back)
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can subtract a one-digit number that crosses back through a ten, such as 42 − 5, by going back to the ten first." (UK NC Y2 AS — TO − O; CCSS 2.NBT.5)
- **Hook:** "42 − 5: I only have 2 loose ones. What can I do?"
- **Key activity:** Concrete: take the 2 ones away, then exchange a ten-rod for 10 ones to take 3 more. Pictorial: number line back to the ten, then back more (42 → 40 → 37). Abstract: 42 − 5 = 42 − 2 − 3 = 37.
- **Check for understanding:** "Leo says 42 − 5 = 43 because 5 − 2 = 3. What did he do wrong?" → He took 2 from 5 instead of 5 from 42; going back 5 from 42 lands on 37.
- **Differentiation:** support: numbers within 30 with ten-frames / stretch: 71 − 8 and 50 − 6 (a ten with no loose ones)
- **Materials:** base-10 blocks, ten-frames, number lines
- **Joy:** "Back to base" — the kangaroo hops back to the ten "base camp" before finishing the journey.

### Lesson 24 — Adding and subtracting tens
- **Duration:** 22 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can add or subtract multiples of ten to or from any two-digit number, such as 34 + 20 or 76 − 30." (UK NC Y2 AS — TO ± tens; CCSS 1.NBT.4, 1.NBT.6)
- **Hook:** "On the 100 square, what's a quick way to jump from 34 to 54?"
- **Key activity:** Concrete: add or remove ten-rods; ones cubes don't move. Pictorial: jumps of ten down/up a 100-square column and on a number line. Abstract: 34 + 20 = 54; 76 − 30 = 46.
- **Check for understanding:** "When you add 30 to 45, which digit changes and which stays? Why?" → The tens digit goes from 4 to 7; the ones stay 5 because no ones were added.
- **Differentiation:** support: add one ten at a time with rods / stretch: 85 + 30 crossing 100, and missing numbers 46 + ☐ = 86
- **Materials:** base-10 blocks, 100 squares, number lines
- **Joy:** "Elevator maths" — the lift on the 100 square goes up or down the requested number of floors.

### Lesson 25 — Adding two two-digit numbers (no regrouping)
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can add two two-digit numbers by adding the tens and the ones, such as 34 + 23." (UK NC Y2 AS — TO + TO; CCSS 2.NBT.5)
- **Hook:** "Two bags of marbles, 34 and 23. How many altogether — without counting every one?"
- **Key activity:** Concrete: build both numbers, combine tens, combine ones. Pictorial: draw sticks and dots; number line (+20 then +3). Abstract: 34 + 23 = 30 + 20 + 4 + 3 = 57. Compare the two methods.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why is 34 + 23 the same as 30 + 20 + 4 + 3?" → Partitioning splits each number into tens and ones; adding all the parts in any order gives the same total.
- **Differentiation:** support: blocks on a mat for every calculation / stretch: find three pairs of two-digit numbers with a total of 68
- **Materials:** base-10 blocks, place-value mats, number lines, marbles
- **Joy:** Marble bags — real bags to check the answer by pouring them together.

### Lesson 26 — Subtracting two-digit numbers (no exchange)
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can subtract a two-digit number from another when no exchange is needed, such as 57 − 23." (UK NC Y2 AS — TO − TO; CCSS 2.NBT.5)
- **Hook:** "57 stickers, I give 23 to my friend. How many do I keep?"
- **Key activity:** Concrete: build 57, remove 2 tens and 3 ones. Pictorial: cross out sticks and dots; number line back 20 then back 3. Abstract: 57 − 23 = 34.
- **Check for understanding:** "Do you build 23 as well as 57 when you subtract 57 − 23? Why or why not?" → No; you build 57 only and take 23 away from it — 23 is the part removed.
- **Differentiation:** support: blocks every time, numbers under 50 / stretch: missing-number versions 68 − ☐ = 45
- **Materials:** base-10 blocks, stickers, number lines
- **Joy:** Real sticker sharing — the answer is checked by actually giving stickers away.

### Lesson 27 — Adding two-digit numbers (with regrouping)
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can add two-digit numbers, exchanging ten ones for a ten when needed." (UK NC Y2 AS — TO + TO with regrouping; CCSS 2.NBT.5, 2.NBT.9)
- **Hook:** "36 + 27 — the ones make 13. You can't write 13 in the ones place. What now?"
- **Key activity:** Concrete: base-10 blocks — add the ones (6 + 7 = 13), exchange ten ones for a ten-rod, then add the tens. Pictorial: tens and ones drawing with the exchange circled. Abstract: 36 + 27 = 50 + 13 = 63.
- **Check for understanding:** "Kai says 45 + 38 = 713. Use blocks to explain the mistake." → 5 + 8 = 13 ones must be exchanged: 13 ones = 1 ten and 3 ones, so 7 tens + 1 ten + 3 = 83.
- **Differentiation:** support: exchange with real blocks every time / stretch: 47 + 38 + 5 (two exchanges to think about) and "which sums need an exchange? Sort without calculating."
- **Materials:** base-10 blocks, place-value mats, exchange "bank" tray
- **Joy:** The "exchange bank" — a toy bank clerk swaps ten ones for a rod, stamp and all.

### Lesson 28 — Regrouping on a number line and by partitioning
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can add two-digit numbers that cross a ten using a number line or partitioning, and choose the method I find clearest." (UK NC Y2 AS — mental and written methods; CCSS 2.NBT.5)
- **Hook:** "Two children solved 48 + 25 differently and both got 73. Were they both right?"
- **Key activity:** I do both methods: number line (48 + 20 = 68, + 2 = 70, + 3 = 73) and partitioning (40 + 20 = 60, 8 + 5 = 13, 60 + 13 = 73). We do: compare. You do: 5 sums, first with blocks to check, then in the method of choice.
- **Check for understanding:** "On a number line for 57 + 26, why might you jump +20 then +3 then +3?" → 57 + 20 = 77; +3 makes 80 (a ten); +3 more makes 83 — jumping to the ten makes the step easy.
- **Differentiation:** support: numbers under 50, blocks alongside / stretch: find the missing digit: 4☐ + 2☐ = 73
- **Materials:** number lines, base-10 blocks, whiteboards
- **Joy:** "Method detectives" — children judge two cartoon characters' work and award a gold star.

### Lesson 29 — Subtracting two-digit numbers (with exchange)
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can subtract two-digit numbers, exchanging a ten for ten ones when the ones are too small." (UK NC Y2 AS — TO − TO with exchange; CCSS 2.NBT.5, 2.NBT.9)
- **Hook:** "52 − 27: I have only 2 ones but I need to take away 7. Stuck?"
- **Key activity:** Concrete: build 52; exchange one ten-rod for ten ones (4 tens and 12 ones); take away 7 ones and 2 tens. Pictorial: cross out and exchange on a drawing. Abstract: 52 − 27 = 25.
- **Check for understanding:** "Maya did 7 − 2 in the ones for 52 − 27 and got 35. Explain why that's wrong." → You must take 7 away from the 2 ones, which needs an exchange; flipping the digits gives the wrong answer — it is 25.
- **Differentiation:** support: exchange with real blocks each time / stretch: 60 − 24 (zero ones) and 81 − 57
- **Materials:** base-10 blocks, place-value mats, exchange tray
- **Joy:** "Break the ten" — a satisfying snap as a linking-cube ten is broken into ten ones.

### Lesson 30 — Subtracting on a number line
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can subtract a two-digit number by jumping back in tens and ones on a number line." (UK NC Y2 AS — mental methods)
- **Hook:** "Can we do 63 − 28 without any blocks at all?"
- **Key activity:** I do: 63 − 20 = 43, − 3 = 40, − 5 = 35. We do: two more, talking every jump. You do: 5 subtractions, checking one with blocks.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why is it useful to land on 40 in 63 − 28?" → 40 is a ten, so the rest of the jump (−5) is an easy bond from 10.
- **Differentiation:** support: number lines already marked in ones / stretch: 91 − 46 using the fewest jumps and explain why
- **Materials:** blank and marked number lines, base-10 blocks
- **Joy:** "Jump race" — who can solve it with the fewest jumps?

### Lesson 31 — Finding the difference by counting on
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can find the difference between two numbers by counting on from the smaller one." (UK NC Y2 AS — difference; CCSS 2.OA.1)
- **Hook:** "My tower is 53 cubes, yours is 48. How many more is mine?"
- **Key activity:** Concrete: two cube towers side by side — compare the extra. Pictorial: a comparison bar model and a counting-on number line (48 → 50 → 53 = 5). Abstract: 53 − 48 = 5. Discuss: when is counting on quicker than taking away?
- **Check for understanding:** "For 71 − 68, would you take 68 away or count on? Why?" → Count on — 68 to 71 is only 3; the numbers are close together.
- **Differentiation:** support: towers and numbers within 30 / stretch: "The difference is 7. What could the two numbers be? Find 4 pairs that cross a ten."
- **Materials:** linking cubes, number lines, comparison bar-model strips
- **Joy:** Tower battle — two tall towers, and the child measures the "winning margin".

### Lesson 32 — Adding three one-digit numbers
- **Duration:** 22 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can add three one-digit numbers by looking for a bond to 10 or a double first." (UK NC Y2 AS — add three one-digit numbers; CCSS 1.OA.2)
- **Hook:** Roll three dice: "4 + 7 + 6. Which two would you add first?"
- **Key activity:** Concrete: counters on ten-frames — group 4 + 6 to make 10, then add 7. Pictorial: circle the pair that makes 10 or a double. Abstract: 4 + 7 + 6 = 10 + 7 = 17.
- **Check for understanding:** "Does it matter which order you add 8 + 5 + 2? Show why." → No; addition can be done in any order — 8 + 2 = 10 first is easiest, then 15.
- **Differentiation:** support: ten-frames and counters / stretch: four numbers (7 + 5 + 3 + 5) and three two-digit multiples of ten
- **Materials:** dice, counters, ten-frames
- **Joy:** "Three-dice roll" game — roll, find the best order, score the total.

### Lesson 33 — Order matters? Commutativity
- **Duration:** 22 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can explain that addition can be done in any order but subtraction cannot." (UK NC Y2 AS — commutativity; CCSS 1.OA.3)
- **Hook:** "Is 5 + 3 the same as 3 + 5? Is 5 − 3 the same as 3 − 5?"
- **Key activity:** Concrete: cube trains turned round (addition), cubes taken away (subtraction — you can't take 5 from 3 cubes). Pictorial: bar models. Abstract: sort number sentences into "true" and "false".
- **Check for understanding:** "Josh says 20 − 12 = 12 − 20. Prove whether he's right." → False; 20 − 12 = 8, but you can't take 20 cubes from 12 cubes — subtraction isn't commutative.
- **Differentiation:** support: model every sentence with cubes / stretch: explain whether 15 + 6 − 6 = 15 is always true for any number
- **Materials:** linking cubes, true/false cards, bar-model strips
- **Joy:** "Maths courtroom" — the child is the judge who rules each sentence TRUE or FALSE.

### Lesson 34 — Inverse: addition and subtraction check each other
- **Duration:** 22 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can use an addition fact to check a subtraction (and the reverse)." (UK NC Y2 AS — inverse; CCSS 2.OA.1)
- **Hook:** "How can you be sure your answer is right without asking anyone?"
- **Key activity:** Concrete: part-whole with blocks (whole 63; parts 38 and 25). Pictorial: bar model. Abstract: 38 + 25 = 63, so 63 − 25 = 38 — the inverse undoes it. Check three of last week's calculations with the inverse.
- **Check for understanding:** "If 38 + 25 = 63, what is 63 − 25 — and why don't you need to calculate it?" → 38; subtracting one part from the whole leaves the other part.
- **Differentiation:** support: part-whole mats with blocks / stretch: use inverse to find the missing number in ☐ − 26 = 37
- **Materials:** base-10 blocks, part-whole mats, bar-model strips
- **Joy:** "Answer checker" badge — the child becomes the class's official answer checker.

### Lesson 35 — Missing number problems
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can find a missing number in an addition or subtraction by using the part-whole model." (UK NC Y2 AS — missing numbers; CCSS 1.OA.8)
- **Hook:** "A thief stole a number: 45 − ☐ = 20. Who can find it?"
- **Key activity:** Pictorial first: put the numbers into a part-whole model or bar — is the missing number a part or the whole? Then choose + or −. Concrete: blocks to test. Abstract: ☐ + 7 = 15; 45 − ☐ = 20; ☐ − 12 = 30.
- **Check for understanding:** "In ☐ − 12 = 30, is the missing number a part or the whole? How do you find it?" → The whole; add the parts: 30 + 12 = 42.
- **Differentiation:** support: missing numbers within 20 with cubes / stretch: two missing digits (4☐ − ☐5 = 21) with more than one answer
- **Materials:** part-whole mats, bar strips, base-10 blocks, "wanted poster" sheets
- **Joy:** Number-thief detective cards — each solved case earns a detective stamp.

### Lesson 36 — Comparing number sentences
- **Duration:** 22 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can compare two calculations with <, > or = and sometimes decide without calculating." (UK NC Y2 AS / N — compare expressions)
- **Hook:** "30 + 12 ○ 50 − 7. Can you choose the sign without working both out?"
- **Key activity:** We do: calculate both sides; then reason (40 + 9 ○ 40 + 7 — same tens, compare ones). You do: 10 comparisons, starring the ones you reasoned without calculating.
- **Check for understanding:** "Without calculating, which is bigger: 56 + 10 or 56 + 9? Why?" → 56 + 10; both start at 56 and 10 is more than 9.
- **Differentiation:** support: calculate both sides with blocks / stretch: 63 − 19 ○ 63 − 20 reasoning (taking less leaves more)
- **Materials:** <, >, = cards, pan balance, whiteboards
- **Joy:** "Balance or tip?" — a real pan balance with cubes shows each answer.

### Lesson 37 — Bar models for addition problems
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can draw a bar model for an addition word problem and use it to solve it." (UK NC Y2 AS — solve problems; CCSS 2.OA.1)
- **Hook:** "The farmer has 26 hens and 38 ducks. What drawing would help us see the question?"
- **Key activity:** Concrete: cube trains for hens and ducks placed end to end. Pictorial: turn the trains into a bar (two parts, whole unknown). Abstract: 26 + 38 = 64. Children label every bar with what it stands for.
- **Check for understanding:** "In your bar model, where does the '?' go and why?" → Over the whole bar, because we want both parts together.
- **Differentiation:** support: numbers under 30, cube trains first / stretch: write your own story to fit a bar with parts 45 and 27
- **Materials:** linking cubes, bar-model strips, word-problem cards
- **Joy:** Story-writing — the child invents a problem for the class "problem box".

### Lesson 38 — Bar models for subtraction and comparison problems
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can draw a bar model for a take-away or 'how many more' problem and solve it." (UK NC Y2 AS — solve problems; CCSS 2.OA.1)
- **Hook:** "Ana read 52 pages; Ben read 35. Who read more, and how many more?"
- **Key activity:** Pictorial: two kinds of bar — a part-whole bar (take away) and a comparison bar (two bars side by side, the gap is the difference). Concrete: cube trains to check. Abstract: 52 − 35 = 17.
- **Check for understanding:** "Point to the part of your comparison bar that shows 'how many more'. Why is it a subtraction?" → The gap at the end; it's the whole of the longer bar minus the shorter one.
- **Differentiation:** support: cube trains side by side before drawing / stretch: problems where the bigger number is missing ("Ben read 17 fewer than Ana; Ben read 35")
- **Materials:** cube trains, bar-model strips, word-problem cards, children's books to count pages
- **Joy:** A real reading challenge — pages from two class books are compared.

### Lesson 39 — Two-step problems
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can solve a two-step word problem by drawing a bar model for each step." (UK NC Y2 AS — solve problems; CCSS 2.OA.1)
- **Hook:** "A bus has 23 people. 8 get off and 15 get on. How many now?"
- **Key activity:** Act it out with toy passengers. Pictorial: two bar models, one per step. Abstract: 23 − 8 = 15, 15 + 15 = 30. Discuss: what did we find first, and why?
- **Check for understanding:** "What's the first question hidden inside this two-step problem?" → How many people are on the bus after 8 get off (15).
- **Differentiation:** support: act out every step with toys / stretch: write a two-step problem for the answer 50
- **Materials:** toy bus and figures (or chairs as seats), bar-model strips
- **Joy:** "The class bus" — chairs become a bus and children get on and off.

### Lesson 40 — Mental strategies: near doubles and adding 9 or 11
- **Duration:** 22 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can add 9 or 11 by adding 10 and adjusting, and use near doubles like 6 + 7." (UK NC Y2 AS — mental strategies)
- **Hook:** "Is there a shortcut for 46 + 9 that's quicker than counting?"

### Lesson 41 — Doubles and halves
- **Duration:** 22 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can double and halve numbers to 20 and multiples of 10 to 100." (UK NC Y2 AS/MD — doubles; CCSS 2.OA.2)
- **Hook:** A mirror on a line of counters: "The mirror doubles them! How many now?"

### Lesson 42 — Choosing a strategy
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can choose an efficient strategy for an addition or subtraction and explain why I chose it." (UK NC Y2 AS — mental methods)
- **Hook:** "Would you use blocks, a number line or your head for 50 + 30? For 67 + 28?"

### Lesson 43 — Retrieval & fluency: facts within 20
- **Duration:** 22 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can recall addition and subtraction facts within 20 quickly and accurately." (UK NC Y2 AS — recall facts fluently; CCSS 2.OA.2)

### Lesson 44 — Reasoning: always, sometimes, never
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can test a maths statement with examples and decide if it is always, sometimes or never true." (UK NC Y2 AS — reasoning)
- **Hook:** "'When you add two numbers, the answer is bigger than both.' Always, sometimes or never?"

## Unit 3 — Multiplication: equal groups and the 2, 5, 10 tables

**Essential question:** What does "lots of" mean, and how can equal groups help us count faster?

**Key vocabulary:** equal groups, unequal, lots of, groups of, repeated addition, multiply, times, multiplication (×), array, row, column, commutative, times table, multiple, double

### Lesson 47 — Equal and unequal groups
- **Duration:** 22 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can tell equal groups from unequal groups and describe equal groups as '3 groups of 4'." (UK NC Y2 MD; CCSS 2.OA.3)
- **Hook:** Four plates of biscuits, one plate with fewer: "Is this fair? How do you know?"
- **Key activity:** Concrete: sort plates of counters into equal and unequal. Pictorial: circle equal groups in pictures. Abstract: say and write "4 groups of 3". You do: make 3 groups of 5 with cubes and draw them.
- **Check for understanding:** "Here are 3 groups: 4, 4 and 5. Can we say '3 groups of 4'? Why not?" → No; the groups must all be the same size — one has 5.
- **Differentiation:** support: groups of 2 and 5 only, real objects / stretch: make 12 into equal groups in as many ways as possible
- **Materials:** paper plates, counters, linking cubes, picture cards
- **Joy:** "Fair party" — set up plates for a toy party so every guest gets the same.

### Lesson 48 — Repeated addition
- **Duration:** 22 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can write equal groups as a repeated addition, such as 5 + 5 + 5 = 15." (UK NC Y2 MD — repeated addition)
- **Hook:** "Three bags with 5 apples each. What addition tells us how many?"
- **Key activity:** Concrete: bags of counters. Pictorial: number line jumps of equal size. Abstract: 5 + 5 + 5 = 15. Compare with unequal additions (5 + 4 + 5) — can't be written as "groups of".
- **Check for understanding:** "Can 4 + 4 + 4 + 3 be written as groups of 4? Explain." → No; the last group has 3, so the groups aren't equal.
- **Differentiation:** support: skip-count with real objects / stretch: write 6 + 6 + 6 + 6 two different ways (4 groups of 6; 2 groups of 12)
- **Materials:** bags, counters, number lines
- **Joy:** Fruit bags — the child packs real (or toy) fruit bags and writes the addition label.

### Lesson 49 — Multiplication: equal groups and arrays
- **Duration:** 24 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can show a multiplication as equal groups and as an array." (UK NC Y2 MD — arrays; CCSS 2.OA.4)
- **Hook:** An egg box: "How many eggs? Count them the fastest way."
- **Key activity:** Concrete: build equal groups with counters, then line them up in rows to make an array. Pictorial: draw dots in rows and columns. Abstract: 3 rows of 5 = 15. Find arrays around the house (egg boxes, windows, muffin trays).
- **Check for understanding:** "Draw 2 rows of 6. Now draw 6 rows of 2. What's the same, and why?" → Both have 12 dots; it's the same array turned round.
- **Differentiation:** support: fill an egg box row by row, counting in 2s / stretch: find every array for 12 and 24
- **Materials:** counters, egg boxes, muffin trays, dotted paper
- **Joy:** Array hunt around the classroom or home — photograph or draw each array found.

### Lesson 50 — The multiplication sign
- **Duration:** 22 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can write a multiplication sentence such as 4 × 5 = 20 and say what each number means." (UK NC Y2 MD — write × statements; CCSS 2.OA.4)
- **Hook:** "Writing 5 + 5 + 5 + 5 + 5 + 5 + 5 + 5 takes ages. Mathematicians invented a shortcut."
- **Key activity:** Match repeated additions to × sentences. Concrete: build 4 × 5 as 4 groups of 5. Pictorial: arrays labelled with × sentences. Abstract: read × as "groups of" or "times".
- **Check for understanding:** "In 4 × 5 = 20, what does the 4 tell us and what does the 5 tell us?" → 4 groups (or 4 lots) of 5; each group has 5 (either reading is fine if the child can show it).
- **Differentiation:** support: match cards with pictures / stretch: write a story that fits 3 × 10 = 30
- **Materials:** matching cards, counters, dotted paper
- **Joy:** "Speed writer" — race the teacher writing a long addition while the child writes the × shortcut.

### Lesson 51 — Arrays and commutativity
- **Duration:** 22 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can show with an array that 3 × 5 = 5 × 3 and use this to learn two facts at once." (UK NC Y2 MD — commutativity; CCSS 2.OA.4)
- **Hook:** Turn a tray of 15 cakes round: "Did the number of cakes change?"
- **Key activity:** Concrete: array on a card — rotate a quarter turn. Pictorial: draw and label both ways. Abstract: 3 × 5 = 15, 5 × 3 = 15. Contrast with division, which will not work both ways (preview).
- **Check for understanding:** "Kai says 2 × 10 and 10 × 2 are different because one is 2 tens and one is 10 twos. Are the totals different?" → No; both are 20 — rotate the array and the dots don't change.
- **Differentiation:** support: small arrays of counters to rotate / stretch: "I know 5 × 7 = 35. What else do I know?" (7 × 5 = 35, 35 ÷ 5, 35 ÷ 7)
- **Materials:** counters on card, dotted paper, muffin tray
- **Joy:** Turn-the-tray trick — the child "magically" turns one fact into two.

### Lesson 52 — The 2 times table
- **Duration:** 22 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can recall the 2 times table and explain it as doubling." (UK NC Y2 MD — recall ×2; CCSS 2.OA.3)
- **Hook:** "How many shoes are in the hallway if 8 people come in?"
- **Key activity:** Concrete: pairs of shoes or socks. Pictorial: arrays of 2 rows; a counting stick marked in 2s. Abstract: chant and write 1 × 2 to 12 × 2; connect to doubles and even numbers.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why is every answer in the 2 times table even?" → They are all made of pairs, and pairs never leave one over.
- **Differentiation:** support: counting stick and cubes / stretch: 13 × 2 and 20 × 2 by doubling
- **Materials:** counting stick, socks/shoes, cubes
- **Joy:** "Noah's ark" — animals go into the ark two by two while the class counts.

### Lesson 53 — The 5 times table
- **Duration:** 22 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can recall the 5 times table and use the clock face and 5-value coins to help." (UK NC Y2 MD — recall ×5)
- **Hook:** "Every hand has 5 fingers. How many fingers in the room?"
- **Key activity:** Concrete: hands, 5-value coins (5p/5¢). Pictorial: clock face — the minute marks count in 5s; arrays of 5. Abstract: 1 × 5 to 12 × 5; notice the 0/5 ending pattern.
- **Check for understanding:** "Could 42 be in the 5 times table? How do you know without counting?" → No; answers in the 5 times table end in 0 or 5.
- **Differentiation:** support: hand prints to count / stretch: explain why 5 × 8 is half of 10 × 8
- **Materials:** analogue clock, 5-value coins, hand-print cards, counting stick
- **Joy:** Hand-print poster — the class prints hands and counts them in 5s.

### Lesson 54 — The 10 times table
- **Duration:** 22 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can recall the 10 times table and explain why each answer ends in 0." (UK NC Y2 MD — recall ×10)
- **Hook:** "7 ten-rods — how many cubes, without counting any?"
- **Key activity:** Concrete: ten-rods and 10-value coins. Pictorial: 100-square columns. Abstract: 1 × 10 to 12 × 10; link to place value (7 × 10 = 7 tens = 70).
- **Check for understanding:** "Why does 6 × 10 = 60 have a zero on the end?" → 6 groups of ten is 6 tens and no ones; the zero shows there are no ones.
- **Differentiation:** support: ten-rods for each fact / stretch: 12 × 10 and 10 × 10 = 100 with base-10
- **Materials:** base-10 rods, 10-value coins, 100 squares
- **Joy:** Rocket countdown in tens — 100, 90, 80… blast off!

### Lesson 55 — The 2, 5 and 10 times tables
- **Duration:** 22 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can recall facts from the 2, 5 and 10 times tables." (UK NC Y2 MD — recall and use ×2, ×5, ×10; CCSS 2.OA.2)
- **Hook:** "Which table is faster to say — 2s, 5s or 10s? Let's race!"
- **Key activity:** Mixed practice: counting stick, arrays, coin totals. Notice links: the 10 table is double the 5 table; the 5 table ends in 0 or 5. Quick-fire facts, then a mixed times-table grid.
- **Check for understanding:** "If you forget 7 × 5, how can 7 × 10 help?" → 7 × 10 = 70 and 5 is half of 10, so 7 × 5 is half of 70 = 35.
- **Differentiation:** support: counting stick and arrays / stretch: explain why 6 × 5 = 3 × 10
- **Materials:** counting stick, arrays, coins, times-table grid
- **Joy:** "Hit the button"-style quick-fire game on the whiteboard.

### Lesson 56 — Multiplication word problems
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can solve a multiplication word problem by drawing equal groups or a bar model." (UK NC Y2 MD — solve problems; CCSS 2.OA.1)
- **Hook:** "5 bicycles, 5 starfish and 5 spiders are at the zoo gate. Whose group has the most legs, wheels or arms?"
- **Key activity:** Pictorial: bar model split into equal parts, each labelled. Concrete: counters on the bar. Abstract: 5 × 2 = 10 (bicycle wheels), 5 × 5 = 25 (starfish arms), and 5 × 8 by counting in 8s with support.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why is your bar split into 5 equal parts for 5 starfish?" → Each part is one starfish, and each has the same number of arms (5).
- **Differentiation:** support: act out with toys / stretch: problems with the group size missing ("some bags of 10 make 60 — how many bags?")
- **Materials:** bar-model strips, counters, toy animals, word-problem cards
- **Joy:** Animal legs challenge — illustrated animal cards to multiply.

### Lesson 57 — Using known facts: doubling and halving tables
- **Duration:** 22 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can use one times-table fact to work out another, such as double 5 × 4 to find 10 × 4." (UK NC Y2 MD — reasoning)
- **Hook:** "If you know your 5s, you secretly know your 10s. Here's the trick…"
- **Key activity:** Concrete: build 4 × 5 as an array, then double it to 4 × 10. Pictorial: side-by-side arrays. Abstract: fact webs — from 3 × 5 = 15 derive 3 × 10 = 30, 5 × 3 = 15, 6 × 5 = 30.
- **Check for understanding:** "3 × 5 = 15. What is 6 × 5? How did you know without counting?" → 30; 6 groups is double 3 groups, so double 15.
- **Differentiation:** support: arrays to build each derived fact / stretch: derive the 4 times table from the 2 times table (double it)
- **Materials:** arrays, counters, fact-web sheets
- **Joy:** Fact webs grow like spider webs — each new fact adds a thread.

### Lesson 58 — Retrieval & fluency: times tables
- **Duration:** 22 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can recall mixed 2, 5 and 10 times-table facts quickly." (UK NC Y2 MD — fluency)
- **Hook:** "Times-table tennis: I serve a fact, you return the answer!"
- **Key activity:** Retrieval games: times-table tennis, "loop cards" (I have 20… who has 4 × 5?), counting stick with hidden numbers. Each child identifies two facts to practise at home.
- **Check for understanding:** "What is 8 × 5, and what other fact does that give you?" → 40; 5 × 8 = 40 (or 40 ÷ 5 = 8).
- **Differentiation:** support: counting stick allowed / stretch: 11 × and 12 × facts in all three tables
- **Materials:** loop cards, counting stick, soft ball
- **Joy:** Times-table tennis with a soft ball.

### Lesson 59 — Reasoning with multiplication
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can explain which pictures, arrays and sentences show the same multiplication, and spot the odd one out." (UK NC Y2 MD — reasoning)
- **Hook:** Four cards: an array, a number line, a story and 3 + 3 + 3 + 2. "Which one doesn't belong?"
- **Key activity:** Sort representation cards into families showing the same multiplication; explain each odd one out. Children make their own set of four with one impostor.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why doesn't 3 + 3 + 3 + 2 belong with 4 × 3?" → The groups aren't equal — the last group has 2, so it's 11, not 12.
- **Differentiation:** support: work with 2 × and 10 × only / stretch: create an impostor that is hard to spot
- **Materials:** representation card sets, counters
- **Joy:** "Spot the impostor" — the child's impostor card is used to trick the teacher.

### Lesson 60 — End-of-unit check and Term 1 review
- **Duration:** 30 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can show I understand multiplication as equal groups and recall the 2, 5 and 10 tables, and I can still use place value and addition from this term." (UK NC Y2 MD/N/AS — assessment)
- **Hook:** "Term 1 museum: each station shows something you've mastered."
- **Key activity:** Stations: draw arrays for a fact; write a × sentence for a picture; times-table recall; a word problem; plus retrieval items from Units 1–2 (partition, compare, add with exchange). Teacher conferences with each child.
- **Check for understanding:** "Draw 4 × 5 two different ways and explain why both are right." → e.g. 4 groups of 5 and an array of 4 rows of 5 (or 5 rows of 4) — all show 20.
- **Differentiation:** support: blocks and counters at every station / stretch: challenge cards mixing all three units
- **Materials:** station cards, counters, base-10, whiteboards
- **Joy:** Term celebration — children choose one piece of work for the "Maths Museum" display.

**End-of-unit check:** Draw an array for 5 × 3 and write both multiplication facts it shows; answer six mixed 2, 5 and 10 facts; solve "6 boxes of 10 pencils — how many pencils?" and explain with a drawing.

## Unit 4 — Division

**Essential question:** How do we share fairly, and how is dividing connected to multiplying?

**Key vocabulary:** share, equally, group, divide, division (÷), each, left over, fact family, inverse, halve, half

### Lesson 61 — Sharing equally
- **Duration:** 22 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can share a number of objects equally between groups and say how many each gets." (UK NC Y2 MD — division as sharing; CCSS 2.OA.3)
- **Hook:** "12 strawberries, 3 friends. How do we make it fair?"
- **Key activity:** Concrete: deal one at a time to each plate, like cards. Pictorial: draw circles for groups and share dots. Abstract: "12 shared between 3 is 4 each."
- **Check for understanding:** "Leo shared 12 as 5, 4 and 3. Is that sharing equally? What should he do?" → No; he should deal one at a time until each plate has 4.
- **Differentiation:** support: numbers to 12 with real food/toys / stretch: share 20 among 4 and among 5 — predict first
- **Materials:** paper plates, counters or real fruit, picture cards
- **Joy:** Card-dealing — the child deals like a card dealer.

### Lesson 62 — Grouping
- **Duration:** 22 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can make equal groups of a given size and say how many groups there are." (UK NC Y2 MD — division as grouping; CCSS 2.OA.3)
- **Hook:** "15 eggs go into boxes of 5. How many boxes do we need?"
- **Key activity:** Concrete: put counters into groups of 5. Pictorial: circle groups on a picture; jump back in 5s on a number line. Abstract: "How many 5s in 15? 3."
- **Check for understanding:** "What's the difference between sharing 15 into 5 groups and grouping 15 into 5s?" → Sharing: we know the number of groups (5) and find how many in each (3). Grouping: we know the size (5) and find how many groups (3).
- **Differentiation:** support: groups of 2 and 10 / stretch: 30 socks into pairs and into groups of 5 — compare the results
- **Materials:** counters, egg boxes, number lines
- **Joy:** Packing factory — the child runs an egg-packing line.

### Lesson 63 — Division as sharing and grouping
- **Duration:** 24 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can divide by sharing equally and by making equal groups." (UK NC Y2 MD — division; CCSS 2.OA.3)
- **Hook:** "20 sweets — share between 4 friends, or put into bags of 4. Is the answer the same kind of thing?"
- **Key activity:** Concrete: model both types with counters. Pictorial: sharing circles vs grouping jumps. Abstract: 20 ÷ 4 = 5 in both — but one is "5 each", the other "5 bags".
- **Check for understanding:** "Share 20 equally between 5 people — how many each? Show it." → 4 each, dealt one at a time.
- **Differentiation:** support: counters for every problem / stretch: decide for four stories whether they are sharing or grouping
- **Materials:** counters, bags, plates
- **Joy:** Sweet-shop role play with bags and counters.

### Lesson 64 — The division sign
- **Duration:** 22 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can write a division sentence such as 15 ÷ 5 = 3 and read it in words." (UK NC Y2 MD — write ÷ statements; CCSS 2.OA.3)
- **Hook:** "Here's a new sign: ÷. What do you think the two dots and line might mean?"
- **Key activity:** Match stories and pictures to ÷ sentences. Concrete: model 15 ÷ 5. Pictorial: arrays split into rows. Abstract: read "15 divided by 5 equals 3".
- **Check for understanding:** "Mia wrote 5 ÷ 15 = 3. Why can't you divide 5 counters into 15 equal groups of 3?" → You only have 5 counters; the whole (15) comes first in a division sentence.
- **Differentiation:** support: picture-matching cards / stretch: write a story for 30 ÷ 10 = 3
- **Materials:** matching cards, counters, dotted arrays
- **Joy:** Code-breaking — ÷ sentences unlock letters in a secret message.

### Lesson 65 — Dividing by 2
- **Duration:** 22 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can divide by 2 and explain that it is the same as halving." (UK NC Y2 MD — ÷2)
- **Hook:** "Two twins share 16 marbles. How many each?"
- **Key activity:** Concrete: share into 2 piles; group in pairs. Pictorial: arrays with 2 rows. Abstract: 16 ÷ 2 = 8; connect to half of 16 and 8 × 2 = 16. Notice: odd numbers leave 1 over.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why does 15 ÷ 2 not share exactly?" → 15 is odd; after making pairs, 1 is left over.
- **Differentiation:** support: numbers to 20 / stretch: halve 30, 50 and 70 (e.g. half of 70 = 35) using tens and ones
- **Materials:** counters, 2-row arrays, number lines
- **Joy:** "Twin share" role play with two puppets.

### Lesson 66 — Dividing by 5
- **Duration:** 22 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can divide multiples of 5 by 5 using the 5 times table." (UK NC Y2 MD — ÷5)
- **Hook:** "35 fingers — how many hands?"
- **Key activity:** Concrete: group counters in 5s. Pictorial: jump in 5s on a number line and count the jumps. Abstract: 35 ÷ 5 = 7 because 7 × 5 = 35.
- **Check for understanding:** "How does knowing 7 × 5 = 35 help you with 35 ÷ 5?" → Division is the inverse: 7 fives make 35, so 35 split into 5s gives 7.
- **Differentiation:** support: number line in 5s / stretch: 60 ÷ 5 and "how many 5-cent coins make 45 cents?"
- **Materials:** counters, number lines, 5-value coins
- **Joy:** "How many hands?" — count hand prints in 5s.

### Lesson 67 — Dividing by 10
- **Duration:** 22 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can divide multiples of 10 by 10 using place value." (UK NC Y2 MD — ÷10)
- **Hook:** "80 cubes, how many ten-rods can we make?"
- **Key activity:** Concrete: snap cubes into tens. Pictorial: 100-square columns. Abstract: 80 ÷ 10 = 8 (8 tens). Link to 8 × 10 = 80.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why is 70 ÷ 10 = 7 just 'how many tens'?" → 70 is 7 tens, and dividing by 10 asks how many tens there are.
- **Differentiation:** support: base-10 for every fact / stretch: 100 ÷ 10 and 120 ÷ 10
- **Materials:** linking cubes, base-10 rods, 10-value coins
- **Joy:** Snap-a-ten race.

### Lesson 68 — Multiplication and division fact families
- **Duration:** 22 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can write the four facts in a multiplication and division fact family from one array." (UK NC Y2 MD — inverse; CCSS 2.OA.4)
- **Hook:** "One array — how many facts can you find hiding in it?"
- **Key activity:** Concrete: 4 × 5 array of counters. Pictorial: labelled array. Abstract: 4 × 5 = 20, 5 × 4 = 20, 20 ÷ 4 = 5, 20 ÷ 5 = 4.
- **Check for understanding:** "Is 4 ÷ 20 = 5 in the family? Explain." → No; you start with the total (20) when dividing.
- **Differentiation:** support: 2 × and 10 × arrays / stretch: missing numbers ☐ ÷ 5 = 6 and 40 ÷ ☐ = 4
- **Materials:** counters, arrays, fact-family triangles
- **Joy:** Fact-family "houses" — four facts live in one house built from paper.

### Lesson 69 — Division word problems
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can solve a division word problem using a bar model or counters." (UK NC Y2 MD — solve problems; CCSS 2.OA.1)
- **Hook:** "30 children go on a trip in cars that seat 5. How many cars?"
- **Key activity:** Pictorial: bar model split into equal parts; the question mark on one part (sharing) or on the number of parts (grouping). Concrete: counters to check. Abstract: 30 ÷ 5 = 6.
- **Check for understanding:** "In your bar model for 30 ÷ 5, what does each part stand for?" → One car, holding 5 children.
- **Differentiation:** support: act out with chairs / stretch: "32 children, cars seat 5 — how many cars do we need?" (7, because 2 children still need a car)
- **Materials:** bar-model strips, counters, chairs
- **Joy:** "Car pool" — chairs become cars and children fill them.

### Lesson 70 — Multiply or divide?
- **Duration:** 22 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can decide whether a word problem needs multiplication or division and explain why." (UK NC Y2 MD — solve problems)
- **Hook:** Two stories that look alike: "5 bags of 4 apples" vs "20 apples in bags of 4". Which needs ×?
- **Key activity:** Sort 10 story cards into × and ÷ piles, justifying with a drawing; solve each.
- **Check for understanding:** "How do you know '20 apples in bags of 4' is division?" → We know the total and the group size and want the number of groups.
- **Differentiation:** support: 6 cards with pictures / stretch: write a × story and a ÷ story using the same three numbers
- **Materials:** story cards, sorting hoops, counters
- **Joy:** "Story swap" — each child writes a story for a partner to sort.

### Lesson 71 — Retrieval & fluency: calculation
- **Duration:** 22 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can quickly add/subtract two-digit numbers and recall 2/5/10 facts." (UK NC Y2 AS/MD — retrieval)
- **Hook:** "Warm-up Olympics: four events, one champion — you!"
- **Key activity:** Retrieval events: two-digit add/subtract with exchange, multiplication and division facts, missing numbers, a word problem. Self-mark and choose a focus for next week.
- **Check for understanding:** "If 5 × 6 = 30, what is 30 ÷ 6, and how do you know?" → 5; division undoes multiplication.
- **Differentiation:** support: blocks and counting stick allowed / stretch: bonus two-step problems
- **Materials:** event cards, base-10 blocks, counting stick
- **Joy:** Medal podium for personal bests.

### Lesson 72 — End-of-unit check: Division
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can show I understand division as sharing and grouping and use the 2, 5 and 10 tables to divide." (UK NC Y2 MD — assessment)
- **Hook:** "The fair-share inspector needs your help."
- **Key activity:** Mastery task: share and group with counters, write the ÷ sentence, complete a fact family, solve a word problem, explain a mistake. Teacher notes gaps.
- **Check for understanding:** "Explain the mistake: '20 shared between 5 is 5 each.'" → 5 × 5 = 25, not 20; it's 4 each.
- **Differentiation:** support: counters for every item / stretch: problems with a "left over" to discuss
- **Materials:** counters, fact-family sheets, assessment cards
- **Joy:** Inspector badge.

**End-of-unit check:** Show 20 ÷ 5 as sharing and as grouping, write the whole fact family, and solve "40 pencils are put in pots of 10. How many pots?" with a drawing.

## Unit 5 — Length and height

**Essential question:** Why do we need the same units to measure, and how do we measure accurately?

**Key vocabulary:** length, height, width, long, tall, short, measure, ruler, metre stick, tape measure, centimetre (cm), metre (m), estimate, compare, longer, shorter, total, difference

### Lesson 73 — Why we need standard units
- **Duration:** 22 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can explain why measuring with hand spans can give different answers and why we use centimetres and metres." (UK NC Y2 M — standard units; CCSS 2.MD.1)
- **Hook:** An adult and a child both measure the table in hand spans — and get different answers!
- **Key activity:** Measure the same objects with hand spans, paper clips and cubes; compare results. Discuss fairness. Introduce the centimetre cube and the metre stick as units everyone shares.
- **Check for understanding:** "Mum says the table is 8 spans and you say 12 spans. Who's right, and how could we settle it?" → Both are right in their own spans; spans are different sizes, so use a standard unit (cm) that's the same for everyone.
- **Differentiation:** support: two non-standard units only / stretch: predict whether a longer or shorter unit gives a bigger count, and explain
- **Materials:** paper clips, linking cubes, centimetre cubes, metre stick
- **Joy:** "The king's foot" story — a king orders a bed measured in his feet, and the tiny carpenter builds it too small.

### Lesson 74 — Measuring length with a ruler
- **Duration:** 22 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can measure a length in cm by lining the object up with zero." (UK NC Y2 M — measure length in cm; CCSS 2.MD.1)
- **Hook:** "Two children measured the same pencil and got 12 cm and 13 cm. Why?"
- **Key activity:** Concrete: centimetre cubes lined under a pencil, then a ruler. Show where 0 is (not always at the end of the ruler). Pictorial: read lengths from ruler drawings. Abstract: record "12 cm".
- **Check for understanding:** "Kai started measuring at the 1 and got 13 cm. What's the real length, and why?" → 12 cm; starting at 1 adds a centimetre that isn't there.
- **Differentiation:** support: rulers with a clear 0 and whole-cm objects / stretch: measure a pencil that starts at 3 cm and ends at 15 cm (12 cm, by counting the gap)
- **Materials:** 30 cm rulers, centimetre cubes, pencils, crayons, ribbon
- **Joy:** "Measure the minibeasts" — toy insects to measure and rank.

### Lesson 75 — Drawing and measuring lines in centimetres
- **Duration:** 22 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can draw a line of a given length in cm and check another person's line." (UK NC Y2 M — measure and draw lengths)
- **Hook:** "Draw a road exactly 10 cm long for this toy car."
- **Key activity:** I do: hold the ruler, mark 0 and the end, join. We do: draw 5 cm and 8 cm. You do: draw a set of lines, swap, and check a partner's.
- **Check for understanding:** "Your partner's '9 cm' line starts at the 1 and ends at the 9. Is it 9 cm?" → No; it's only 8 cm long.
- **Differentiation:** support: start dots are printed in place / stretch: draw a line 3 cm longer than a given one and a "staircase" of lines
- **Materials:** rulers, paper, toy cars
- **Joy:** Build a car track of exactly measured road pieces.

### Lesson 76 — Metres: measuring longer things
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can measure longer lengths in metres using a metre stick or tape." (UK NC Y2 M — metres; CCSS 2.MD.1)
- **Hook:** "Is the classroom longer than 10 of me lying down?"
- **Key activity:** Concrete: make a metre from 100 centimetre cubes (or 10 ten-rods) and compare with the metre stick. Measure the room, a corridor and a jump with a metre stick and tape.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why is 1 m the same as 100 cm? Show me with the rods." → 10 ten-rods make 100 cm and they are exactly as long as the metre stick.
- **Differentiation:** support: whole metres only / stretch: "about 3 m and 20 cm" — describe lengths that fall between metres
- **Materials:** metre sticks, measuring tapes, base-10 rods, chalk
- **Joy:** Standing long jump — measure your jump in the playground.

### Lesson 77 — Choosing units and estimating
- **Duration:** 22 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can choose cm or m to measure something and make a sensible estimate first." (UK NC Y2 M — choose units, estimate)
- **Hook:** "Would you measure an ant in metres? A bus in centimetres?"
- **Key activity:** Sort picture cards into "cm" and "m". Estimate then measure 5 objects; record estimate and actual; discuss how estimates improve with a "benchmark" (a finger is about 1 cm wide; a big step is about 1 m).
- **Check for understanding:** "Why is a pencil 'about 15 cm' a better estimate than '15 m'?" → A metre is about a big step long; a pencil is much shorter than that.
- **Differentiation:** support: estimate by comparing with a 10 cm strip / stretch: estimate and measure things taller than yourself
- **Materials:** picture cards, rulers, metre sticks, estimate sheets
- **Joy:** "Estimation champion" — closest guesses score points.

### Lesson 78 — Comparing and ordering lengths
- **Duration:** 22 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can compare and order lengths using <, > and = with cm and m." (UK NC Y2 M — compare and order; CCSS 2.MD.4)
- **Hook:** "Three sunflowers: which is tallest? Measure, don't guess!"
- **Key activity:** Measure three objects, record, order shortest to longest, write comparisons (25 cm < 30 cm). Include a trap: 1 m vs 90 cm.
- **Check for understanding:** "Is 90 cm longer than 1 m? Explain." → No; 1 m = 100 cm and 100 cm is longer than 90 cm.
- **Differentiation:** support: all in cm / stretch: order 1 m, 110 cm, 99 cm and 2 m
- **Materials:** rulers, metre sticks, paper sunflowers or plants, <, > cards
- **Joy:** Paper-sunflower height contest.

### Lesson 79 — Length problems
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can add and subtract lengths to solve problems, using a bar model." (UK NC Y2 M — solve problems; CCSS 2.MD.5)
- **Hook:** "A snake is 45 cm long. It grows 18 cm. How long is it now?"
- **Key activity:** Pictorial: bar models for totals and differences in cm. Concrete: ribbon lengths cut and joined. Abstract: 45 cm + 18 cm = 63 cm; difference between 72 cm and 48 cm.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why do we keep 'cm' in the answer?" → The answer is a length; 63 on its own doesn't tell us what we measured.
- **Differentiation:** support: lengths under 30 cm with ribbons / stretch: two-step problems with m and cm
- **Materials:** ribbon, rulers, bar-model strips, toy snakes
- **Joy:** Grow the snake — add paper segments as the story unfolds.

### Lesson 80 — Measuring project: our growing plants
- **Duration:** 30 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can plan, measure and record lengths in a real investigation." (UK NC Y2 M — apply; link: Science plants unit)
- **Hook:** "How much will our bean plants grow in two weeks?"
- **Key activity:** Measure bean seedlings (or yourself, a toy, shadows) and record in a table; plan to remeasure weekly (linking to Science). Compare heights and find differences.
- **Check for understanding:** "Your bean was 4 cm on Monday and 11 cm today. How much did it grow? How did you work it out?" → 7 cm; 11 − 4, or count on from 4 to 11.
- **Differentiation:** support: measure with centimetre cubes first / stretch: predict next week's height from the pattern
- **Materials:** bean seedlings, rulers, recording tables
- **Joy:** Caring for real growing plants.

### Lesson 81 — End-of-unit check: Length and height
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can show I can measure, estimate, compare and solve problems with lengths in cm and m." (UK NC Y2 M — assessment)
- **Hook:** "The toy workshop needs precise measurers."
- **Key activity:** Mastery task: measure two objects, draw a line, choose units, order lengths, solve a length problem, explain a ruler mistake.
- **Check for understanding:** "Explain why this measurement is wrong: the ruler's end, not the 0, is lined up with the pencil." → Many rulers have a gap before 0, so the reading would be too long; line the object up with 0.
- **Differentiation:** support: whole-cm items only / stretch: m and cm comparison items
- **Materials:** rulers, metre stick, objects, assessment sheet
- **Joy:** Toy workshop certificates.

**End-of-unit check:** Measure a pencil and a book accurately, draw a line 3 cm shorter than the pencil, and decide whether a door is about 2 m or 2 cm tall — with a reason.

## Unit 6 — Mass, capacity and temperature

**Essential question:** How do we measure how heavy, how full and how hot things are — and read the scale correctly?

**Key vocabulary:** mass, heavy, heavier, light, lighter, balance, weigh, gram (g), kilogram (kg), capacity, volume, full, empty, half full, millilitre (ml), litre (l), scale, interval, temperature, degrees Celsius (°C), thermometer

### Lesson 82 — Comparing mass with a balance
- **Duration:** 22 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can compare the mass of two objects with a balance and use heavier, lighter and the same as." (UK NC Y2 M — compare mass; CCSS 1.MD.1 extension)
- **Hook:** "Which is heavier — a big balloon or a small apple? Guess, then test!"
- **Key activity:** Concrete: pan balances — the heavier side goes down. Test surprises (big but light, small but heavy). Measure mass in cubes: "the apple balances 38 cubes". Record with <, >.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why is a big object not always heavier? Give an example from today." → Size isn't mass — the balloon was bigger but lighter than the apple.
- **Differentiation:** support: two very different objects / stretch: order four objects using only the balance (fewest weighings)
- **Materials:** pan balances, linking cubes, balloon, fruit, everyday objects
- **Joy:** "Surprise scale" — hidden objects in paper bags, guess then weigh.

### Lesson 83 — Measuring mass in grams
- **Duration:** 22 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can measure the mass of small objects in grams using gram weights or a scale." (UK NC Y2 M — mass in g; CCSS 2.MD.1)
- **Hook:** "A paper clip weighs about 1 gram. How many paper clips is your pencil case?"
- **Key activity:** Concrete: balance objects with 1 g, 10 g, 50 g, 100 g weights. Pictorial: read simple scale dials marked in 10s. Abstract: record "250 g".
- **Check for understanding:** "You used two 100 g and one 50 g weight to balance a toy. What's its mass, and how did you add it?" → 250 g; 100 + 100 + 50.
- **Differentiation:** support: 10 g weights only, counting in 10s / stretch: find two objects that together weigh about 500 g
- **Materials:** pan balances, gram weights (1 g, 10 g, 50 g, 100 g), kitchen scale, small objects
- **Joy:** "Post office" — weigh parcels and stick on the right stamp.

### Lesson 84 — Measuring mass in kilograms
- **Duration:** 22 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can measure heavier objects in kilograms and know that 1 kg = 1000 g." (UK NC Y2 M — mass in kg)
- **Hook:** "Hold a 1 kg bag of rice. Find three things in the room you think are heavier."
- **Key activity:** Concrete: feel 1 kg (a bag of flour or rice) and compare. Measure with a kitchen or bathroom scale marked in kg. Sort objects into "less than 1 kg", "about 1 kg", "more than 1 kg".
- **Check for understanding:** "Which is heavier: 1 kg of feathers or 1 kg of stones? Why?" → Neither; both are 1 kg — the same mass, even though the feathers take more space.
- **Differentiation:** support: compare with the 1 kg bag by holding / stretch: 1 kg = 1000 g — how many 100 g weights balance 1 kg?
- **Materials:** 1 kg bags of rice/flour, kitchen and bathroom scales, 100 g weights
- **Joy:** "Human balance" — hold two bags and guess which is heavier with eyes closed.

### Lesson 85 — Reading scales: mass and capacity
- **Duration:** 22 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can read a scale and choose the right unit for mass and capacity." (UK NC Y2 M — read scales; CCSS 2.MD.1)
- **Hook:** "The needle is between two numbers. How do we know what it says?"
- **Key activity:** Concrete: real kitchen scales and measuring jugs. Pictorial: scale drawings — count the intervals between numbered marks (in 1s, 2s, 5s or 10s). Abstract: record the reading with its unit.
- **Check for understanding:** "A jug is marked 0, 100, 200… and the water is at the second mark after 0. Ella says 2 ml. What's the real amount?" → 200 ml; each mark is worth 100 ml.
- **Differentiation:** support: scales where every mark is numbered / stretch: scales in 2s and 5s with the pointer between numbers
- **Materials:** kitchen scales, measuring jugs, printed scale pictures
- **Joy:** "Scale spy" — find and photograph (or draw) scales at home or school.

### Lesson 86 — Comparing capacity
- **Duration:** 22 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can compare how much containers hold by pouring, and explain why a taller container doesn't always hold more." (UK NC Y2 M — capacity; CCSS 1.MD.2 extension)
- **Hook:** A tall thin glass and a short wide bowl: "Which holds more water? Vote!"
- **Key activity:** Concrete: fill one container and pour into the other; measure in cups. Record which holds more; words full, half full, nearly empty.
- **Check for understanding:** "The tall glass looked like it held more, but the bowl filled 5 cups and the glass 3. Why?" → The bowl is much wider; how much a container holds depends on width as well as height.
- **Differentiation:** support: pour between two containers only / stretch: order four containers by capacity using the fewest pourings
- **Materials:** containers of different shapes, cups, water tray, towels
- **Joy:** Water play with a purpose — and a "wow" when the short bowl wins.

### Lesson 87 — Millilitres and litres
- **Duration:** 22 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can measure capacity in millilitres and litres and know 1 l = 1000 ml." (UK NC Y2 M — ml and l)
- **Hook:** "A medicine spoon holds 5 ml. How many would fill a 1 litre bottle?" (Hint: a lot!)
- **Key activity:** Concrete: measure water with jugs marked in 100 ml; fill a 1 litre bottle. Choose ml or l for picture cards (a teaspoon, a bath).
- **Check for understanding:** "Why do we measure a bath in litres, not millilitres?" → A bath holds a huge amount; litres are bigger units so the number stays sensible.
- **Differentiation:** support: whole 100 ml readings / stretch: find how many 250 ml cups fill 1 litre
- **Materials:** measuring jugs, 1 l bottles, spoons, cups, water tray
- **Joy:** Potion lab — mix coloured water "potions" to exact recipes.

### Lesson 88 — Reading scales with different intervals
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can work out what each mark on a scale is worth when not all marks are numbered." (UK NC Y2 M — read scales to nearest numbered unit)
- **Hook:** "This scale goes 0, 10, 20 with 4 little marks in between. What's each little mark worth?"
- **Key activity:** Pictorial: count gaps between numbered marks, divide the jump (10 in 5 gaps = 2 each). Use counting in 2s, 5s and 10s. Concrete: real jugs and rulers to check.
- **Check for understanding:** "Between 0 and 10 there are 5 gaps. Why is each mark worth 2, not 1?" → 10 shared across 5 equal gaps is 2 each; count 2, 4, 6, 8, 10.
- **Differentiation:** support: marks every 1 and every 10 / stretch: scales marked every 5 between 0 and 50 and between 100 and 200
- **Materials:** printed scales, measuring jugs, thermometer pictures
- **Joy:** "Mystery scale" cards — find the value, reveal the hidden animal.

### Lesson 89 — Temperature
- **Duration:** 22 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can read a thermometer in °C and say which temperatures are hot, warm, cold or freezing." (UK NC Y2 M — temperature °C; CCSS link to 2.MD.6 number line)
- **Hook:** "Is it hotter today than in Cairo? Than in Oslo? How would we know?"
- **Key activity:** Concrete: a real thermometer in warm water, cold water, sunshine and shade. Pictorial: read thermometer pictures (vertical number lines). Abstract: 0 °C = water freezes; about 37 °C = body temperature. Compare world city temperatures today.
- **Check for understanding:** "A thermometer reads 25 °C in one city and 5 °C in another. Which would need a coat, and why?" → The 5 °C city — it's much colder (lower number).
- **Differentiation:** support: thermometers marked every 1 or 10 / stretch: find the difference between two cities' temperatures
- **Materials:** classroom thermometers, cups of warm and cold water, weather map, printed thermometers
- **Joy:** World weather reporter — present the day's temperatures from four continents.

### Lesson 90 — Measurement problems
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can compare, order and solve problems with mass, capacity and temperature." (UK NC Y2 M — solve problems)
- **Hook:** "A baby elephant drinks 25 litres before lunch and 30 after. How much altogether?"
- **Key activity:** Pictorial: bar models with units. Solve problems: totals, differences, comparisons (a 700 g bag vs a 1 kg bag), temperature rises. Concrete: check one with real jugs.
- **Check for understanding:** "Which is heavier, 700 g or 1 kg? How do you know?" → 1 kg, because 1 kg = 1000 g and 1000 > 700.
- **Differentiation:** support: single-unit problems with equipment / stretch: two-step problems mixing g and kg
- **Materials:** bar-model strips, jugs, scales, problem cards
- **Joy:** Zookeeper problems — feeding and watering zoo animals.

### Lesson 91 — Kitchen project: follow a recipe
- **Duration:** 30 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can measure ingredients by mass and capacity to follow a recipe." (UK NC Y2 M — apply; link: Life skills / healthy food)
- **Hook:** "We're making no-bake energy balls — but only if we measure exactly."
- **Key activity:** Weigh oats and dried fruit, measure milk or juice in ml, follow the recipe steps (adults handle any knives). Record each measurement.
- **Check for understanding:** "The recipe says 200 g oats. Your scale shows 180 g. What do you do, and how much?" → Add 20 g more.
- **Differentiation:** support: pre-marked jugs and a partner reading the scale / stretch: double the recipe
- **Materials:** kitchen scales, measuring jugs, recipe card, ingredients (check allergies), bowls, spoons
- **Joy:** Eating what you measured.

### Lesson 92 — End-of-unit check: Mass, capacity and temperature
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can show I can read scales and compare mass, capacity and temperature." (UK NC Y2 M — assessment)
- **Hook:** "Science lab quality check."
- **Key activity:** Mastery task: read three scales, choose units, order masses, compare capacities, read a thermometer, solve one problem, explain a scale-reading mistake.
- **Check for understanding:** "A scale counts in 5s. The pointer is one mark past 20. Jo says 21. What is it?" → 25; each mark is worth 5.
- **Differentiation:** support: equipment and scales with every mark labelled / stretch: scales with unlabelled intervals
- **Materials:** scales, jugs, thermometer, assessment sheet
- **Joy:** Lab-coat certificates.

## Unit 7 — Money

**Essential question:** How do we use coins and notes to pay, and how do we work out change?

**Key vocabulary:** coin, note, value, amount, total, cost, price, pay, change, spend, save, cents/pence (the small unit), dollars/pounds/euros (the big unit), fewest coins

### Lesson 93 — Recognising coins and notes
- **Duration:** 22 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can recognise the coins and notes of our currency and say their values." (UK NC Y2 M — recognise symbols for money; CCSS 2.MD.8)
- **Hook:** A treasure chest of real (or play) money from several countries: "How can you tell what each coin is worth?"
- **Key activity:** Sort coins by value (not size or colour — a smaller coin can be worth more). Match coins and notes to value labels. Compare with one other currency to see "big unit and small unit" in each.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why isn't the biggest coin always worth the most?" → Value is printed on the coin; size doesn't decide it.
- **Differentiation:** support: 4 coins only / stretch: order all coins and notes by value
- **Materials:** real or play coins and notes (local currency plus one other), sorting trays
- **Joy:** Treasure chest of world coins.

### Lesson 94 — Counting coins of the same value
- **Duration:** 22 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can count amounts made of the same coin by counting in 2s, 5s or 10s." (UK NC Y2 M — count money)
- **Hook:** "Seven 5-cent coins — can you count them without adding one at a time?"
- **Key activity:** Concrete: count piles of 2-, 5- and 10-value coins in steps. Pictorial: coin pictures. Abstract: 7 × 5 = 35 (links to times tables).
- **Check for understanding:** "Why can counting 10-cent coins use the 10 times table?" → Each coin is worth 10, so 6 coins is 6 lots of 10 = 60.
- **Differentiation:** support: 10-value coins only / stretch: 20-value coins (count in 20s)
- **Materials:** coins, counting stick
- **Joy:** Piggy-bank count — tip out a real piggy bank.

### Lesson 95 — Counting mixed coins
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can count a mixed group of coins by starting with the largest value." (UK NC Y2 M — find totals; CCSS 2.MD.8)
- **Hook:** "A purse has 50c, 20c, 20c, 5c and 2c. How much is inside?"
- **Key activity:** Concrete: order coins from largest to smallest value, then count on (50, 70, 90, 95, 97). Pictorial: coin pictures. Abstract: write the total with the unit.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why is it easier to start counting with the 50?" → Big jumps first, then small ones; counting on from a small coin is slower and easier to muddle.
- **Differentiation:** support: two kinds of coin / stretch: totals over 100 (e.g. 1 dollar and 35 cents)
- **Materials:** coins, purses, coin pictures
- **Joy:** Mystery purses — each child empties one and reports the treasure.

### Lesson 96 — Pounds, pence and making amounts
- **Duration:** 22 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can show that £1 = 100p (1 big unit = 100 small units) and make an amount the efficient way." (UK NC Y2 M — £ and p; CCSS 2.MD.8)
- **Hook:** "100 pennies or one coin — which would you rather carry to the shop?"
- **Key activity:** Concrete: count 100 of the smallest coin into a pile and show it equals the big-unit coin; make amounts with the fewest coins, biggest coins first. The app lesson uses pounds and pence; mirror it with your own currency (e.g. $1 = 100¢, €1 = 100c).
- **Check for understanding:** "Make 80p with the fewest coins and explain why you started with the 50p." → 50p + 20p + 10p = 80p (3 coins); the biggest coin that fits first uses fewest coins.
- **Differentiation:** support: amounts under 20 / stretch: make £1.35 two different ways and say which uses fewer coins
- **Materials:** real or play coins (100 small coins, big-unit coin), purses
- **Joy:** "Heavy pocket" — carry 100 small coins vs one big coin and feel the difference.

### Lesson 97 — Same amount, different ways
- **Duration:** 22 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can make the same amount in several different ways and check they are equal." (UK NC Y2 M — combinations of coins)
- **Hook:** "Grandma, Dad and I each have 50 cents in different coins. Who has the most?"
- **Key activity:** Find as many ways as possible to make 20 and 50 (in small units) with coins; record systematically in a table.
- **Check for understanding:** "Kai has one 20 coin; Lin has four 5 coins. Who has more money?" → They have the same — 20 each.
- **Differentiation:** support: make 10 in different ways / stretch: find all ways to make 20 using only 10, 5 and 2 coins
- **Materials:** coins, recording tables
- **Joy:** "Coin detectives" — a family puzzle about who has more.

### Lesson 98 — Comparing amounts
- **Duration:** 22 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can compare two amounts of money and say which is more and by how much." (UK NC Y2 M — compare amounts)
- **Hook:** "Two toy prices: 45 and 54. Which is cheaper, and by how much?"
- **Key activity:** Concrete: make both amounts in coins; compare. Pictorial: comparison bar. Abstract: 54 − 45 = 9.
- **Check for understanding:** "Which is more: three 20-coins or one 50-coin? Explain." → Three 20s = 60, which is more than 50.
- **Differentiation:** support: amounts within 20 / stretch: compare 1 big unit 5 small with 95 small units
- **Materials:** coins, price tags, comparison bars
- **Joy:** "Bargain hunter" — find the cheapest item in a toy catalogue.

### Lesson 99 — Adding amounts
- **Duration:** 22 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can add two or three prices to find the total cost." (UK NC Y2 M — add money; CCSS 2.MD.8)
- **Hook:** "An apple is 25 and a banana is 30. Can I buy both with 50?"
- **Key activity:** Concrete: make each price in coins and combine. Pictorial: bar model. Abstract: 25 + 30 = 55 — so no, 50 isn't enough.
- **Check for understanding:** "Do you have enough money? Explain how you know." → Compare the total (55) with what you have (50) — 55 is more, so not enough.
- **Differentiation:** support: prices in 10s / stretch: three items crossing 100
- **Materials:** coins, price tags, fruit (real or toy)
- **Joy:** A fruit stall where children shop.

### Lesson 100 — Adding amounts and finding change
- **Duration:** 22 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can total a small shopping list and work out change by counting up." (UK NC Y2 M — change; CCSS 2.MD.8)
- **Hook:** "You pay £1 for a 65p toy. Does the shopkeeper owe you anything?"
- **Key activity:** Concrete: pay with a big coin; count up the change from the price (65 → 70 → 100 = 35). Pictorial: number line counting up. Abstract: 100 − 65 = 35.
- **Check for understanding:** "A pen costs 70p; you pay £1. What's your change, and how did you count it?" → 30p; count up from 70 to 100.
- **Differentiation:** support: pay with 20 or 50 for small prices / stretch: pay with £2 (or 2 big units) for items costing over 1 unit
- **Materials:** coins, price tags, number lines, toy till
- **Joy:** Shopkeeper role play with a toy till.

### Lesson 101 — Change by counting up (practice)
- **Duration:** 22 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can find change from 50, 1 big unit or 2 big units by counting up in friendly steps." (UK NC Y2 M — change)
- **Hook:** "Shopkeeper challenge: can you give the right change every time?"
- **Key activity:** Pairs play shop with price tags; each sale records price, paid, change. Model the "count up to the next ten, then to the note" strategy.
- **Check for understanding:** "Price 37, paid 50. Show your counting-up steps." → 37 → 40 (3) → 50 (10) = 13.
- **Differentiation:** support: prices ending in 0 or 5 / stretch: change from 2 big units (200 small) for prices like 1.45
- **Materials:** coins, toy till, price tags, sales log
- **Joy:** Shop role play with customers who try to trick the shopkeeper.

### Lesson 102 — Two-step money problems
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can solve a two-step money problem, such as buying two things and finding the change." (UK NC Y2 M — solve problems)
- **Hook:** "I buy a comic for 45 and a pencil for 20. I pay with 1 big unit (100). What's my change?"
- **Key activity:** Pictorial: bar model with two steps (total, then change). Concrete: coins to check. Abstract: 45 + 20 = 65; 100 − 65 = 35.
- **Check for understanding:** "What did you have to find out before you could work out the change?" → The total cost of both items.
- **Differentiation:** support: act it out with coins / stretch: "I have 1 big unit. Which two items can I buy with the least change?"
- **Materials:** coins, catalogue page, bar-model strips
- **Joy:** "Spend it wisely" — choose what to buy with a budget.

### Lesson 103 — Class shop project
- **Duration:** 30 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can run a shop — pricing items, taking payment and giving the right change." (UK NC Y2 M — apply; link: Life skills money sense)
- **Hook:** "Tomorrow the class shop opens. What should our prices be?"
- **Key activity:** Children price items (junk-modelled or real), make labels, then take turns as shopkeeper and customer with a spending budget. Keep a sales log.
- **Check for understanding:** "You have 60 to spend. You want items costing 25 and 40. Can you buy both? What could you buy instead?" → No (65 > 60); e.g. 25 + another item costing 35 or less.
- **Differentiation:** support: shopkeeper with a counting-up number line / stretch: a "sale" day — everything 10 less
- **Materials:** play coins, price labels, toy till, junk-model items, sales log
- **Joy:** Running a real shop.

### Lesson 104 — End-of-unit check: Money
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can show I can count, make, compare and add money and work out change." (UK NC Y2 M — assessment)
- **Hook:** "The bank needs a new trainee cashier."
- **Key activity:** Mastery task: count a purse, make an amount two ways, compare prices, find a total and change, explain a mistake.
- **Check for understanding:** "Explain the mistake: 'I paid 50 for something costing 38 and got 22 change.'" → Counting up 38 → 40 → 50 is 12, not 22.
- **Differentiation:** support: coins for every item / stretch: amounts with big and small units
- **Materials:** coins, purses, assessment sheet
- **Joy:** Cashier badge.

**End-of-unit check:** Make 75 two different ways, then buy two items costing 32 and 45 with 1 big unit (100) and find the change, showing your counting up.

## Unit 8 — Fractions

**Essential question:** What does each part of a fraction tell us, and how do we find a fraction of a number?

**Key vocabulary:** whole, part, equal, unequal, half (½), halves, third (⅓), thirds, quarter (¼), quarters, three-quarters (¾), fraction, numerator (the top number — how many parts we take), denominator (the bottom number — how many equal parts), unit fraction, equivalent

### Lesson 105 — Equal and unequal parts
- **Duration:** 22 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can tell whether a shape is cut into equal parts and explain why that matters for fractions." (UK NC Y2 F — equal parts; CCSS 2.G.3)
- **Hook:** "I cut the pizza in 2 — one huge bit and one tiny bit. Did I cut it in half?"
- **Key activity:** Concrete: fold and cut paper shapes, some fairly, some not. Pictorial: sort pictures into "equal parts" and "not equal". Retrieval: what a half was in Foundation.
- **Check for understanding:** "Is a shape cut into 2 pieces always cut in halves? Why?" → No; halves must be 2 equal parts.
- **Differentiation:** support: fold strips and squares / stretch: find three different ways to cut a square into 2 equal parts
- **Materials:** paper shapes, scissors, sorting cards, play dough "pizzas"
- **Joy:** Play-dough pizza cutting.

### Lesson 106 — Halves of shapes and objects
- **Duration:** 22 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can find and shade half of a shape and write it as ½." (UK NC Y2 F — ½; CCSS 2.G.3)
- **Hook:** "Can you half a square in a way nobody else has tried?"
- **Key activity:** Concrete: fold squares, rectangles and circles in half. Pictorial: shade ½. Abstract: ½ means 1 of 2 equal parts. Show that two halves make a whole.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why is shading 1 of these 2 unequal parts not ½?" → The two parts aren't equal, so the shaded part isn't one half.
- **Differentiation:** support: pre-drawn fold lines / stretch: halve an irregular shape on squared paper by counting squares
- **Materials:** paper shapes, squared paper, colouring pencils
- **Joy:** "Half-a-face" — draw the missing half of a photo.

### Lesson 107 — Halves of quantities
- **Duration:** 22 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can find half of a number by sharing into 2 equal groups." (UK NC Y2 F — ½ of a quantity; link to ÷2)
- **Hook:** "Half of the cookies are chocolate. There are 14 cookies. How many are chocolate?"
- **Key activity:** Concrete: share counters into 2 equal groups. Pictorial: bar model split into 2. Abstract: ½ of 14 = 7 (= 14 ÷ 2).
- **Check for understanding:** "Why is finding half of 14 the same as 14 ÷ 2?" → Half means 2 equal parts, and dividing by 2 shares into 2 equal parts.
- **Differentiation:** support: even numbers to 20 / stretch: half of 30, 50, 90
- **Materials:** counters, plates, bar-model strips
- **Joy:** Cookie sharing with real (or paper) cookies.

### Lesson 108 — Quarters of shapes and quantities
- **Duration:** 22 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can find ¼ of a shape and ¼ of a number by making 4 equal parts." (UK NC Y2 F — ¼; CCSS 2.G.3)
- **Hook:** "Four friends, one pizza, and 12 olives on top. How do we share both fairly?"
- **Key activity:** Concrete: fold paper in half and half again; share 12 counters into 4 groups. Pictorial: bar split into 4. Abstract: ¼ of 12 = 3.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why is ¼ of 12 found by sharing into 4, not by taking 4 away?" → The 4 tells how many equal parts; ¼ is one of those parts.
- **Differentiation:** support: shapes only, then 8 counters / stretch: ¼ of 20 and ¼ of 40
- **Materials:** paper shapes, counters, plates
- **Joy:** Pizza party with paper pizzas and olive counters.

### Lesson 109 — Thirds and quarters of shapes
- **Duration:** 24 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can find ⅓, ¼ and ¾ of a shape and explain that the parts must be equal." (UK NC Y2 F — ⅓, ¼, ¾; CCSS 2.G.3)
- **Hook:** Three friends, one chocolate bar: mime an unfair break and let the child protest.
- **Key activity:** Concrete: fold a strip into thirds (like a letter), another into quarters; shade 3 of 4 → ¾. Pictorial: fraction bars. Compare the third-strip and quarter-strip — more pieces means smaller pieces.
- **Check for understanding:** "Which is bigger, ½ or ¼? Prove it with your strips." → ½; fold the same strip both ways — halves are bigger because there are fewer pieces.
- **Differentiation:** support: pre-folded strips / stretch: compare ⅓ and ¼ and explain without strips
- **Materials:** paper strips, circles, colouring pencils, scissors, a chocolate bar (or paper one in squares)
- **Joy:** The chocolate bar gets shared fairly in three for real.

### Lesson 110 — Fractions of a quantity
- **Duration:** 24 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can find a unit and non-unit fraction of an amount by sharing." (UK NC Y2 F — ⅓, ¼, ½, ¾ of a quantity)
- **Hook:** "¾ of the 12 cupcakes have sprinkles. How many is that?"
- **Key activity:** Concrete: share 12 counters into 4 equal groups (¼ = 3), then take 3 groups (¾ = 9). Pictorial: bar model. Abstract: divide by the denominator, multiply by the numerator (in words: "find one part, then take that many parts").
- **Check for understanding:** "Why is ¾ of 12 three times as much as ¼ of 12?" → ¾ is 3 of the 4 equal parts, so it's 3 lots of one part (3 × 3 = 9).
- **Differentiation:** support: unit fractions only with counters / stretch: ¾ of 20 and ⅓ of 15, then ⅔ of 15
- **Materials:** counters, plates, bar-model strips, cupcake picture cards
- **Joy:** Cupcake decorating (paper) to show the fraction.

### Lesson 111 — Comparing unit fractions
- **Duration:** 22 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can compare ½, ⅓ and ¼ and explain why more equal parts means smaller parts." (UK NC Y2 F — reasoning)
- **Hook:** "Would you rather have ⅓ of a cake or ¼ of the same cake?"
- **Key activity:** Concrete: fraction strips (same-size wholes) in halves, thirds, quarters lined up. Pictorial: fraction wall. Abstract: ½ > ⅓ > ¼.
- **Check for understanding:** "Leo says ¼ is bigger than ⅓ because 4 is bigger than 3. Use the fraction wall to answer him." → ⅓ is bigger — splitting the same whole into 4 makes smaller pieces than splitting into 3.
- **Differentiation:** support: halves vs quarters only / stretch: "Is ¼ of a big pizza always smaller than ⅓ of a small pizza?" (No — the wholes must be the same size to compare)
- **Materials:** fraction strips, fraction wall, paper cakes
- **Joy:** Cake choice game — defend your choice with the wall.

### Lesson 112 — Non-unit fractions: ¾ and ²⁄₄
- **Duration:** 22 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can name and show non-unit fractions such as ¾ and ²⁄₄ of a shape." (UK NC Y2 F — ¾, ²⁄₄)
- **Hook:** "A quarter is one piece. What would we call 3 of those pieces?"
- **Key activity:** Concrete: shade quarters of strips one at a time, naming each (¼, ²⁄₄, ¾, ⁴⁄₄ = 1 whole). Pictorial: shade given fractions. Abstract: read numerator and denominator.
- **Check for understanding:** "In ¾, what does the 3 tell us and what does the 4 tell us?" → 4 = the whole is split into 4 equal parts; 3 = we take 3 of them.
- **Differentiation:** support: quarters of squares only / stretch: ⅔ of shapes
- **Materials:** fraction strips, squared paper, colouring pencils
- **Joy:** "Fraction flags" — design flags coloured in given fractions.

### Lesson 113 — Equivalence: ²⁄₄ and ½
- **Duration:** 22 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can show that ²⁄₄ is the same as ½." (UK NC Y2 F — equivalence of ²⁄₄ and ½)
- **Hook:** "I ate 2 quarters of a pizza; you ate half. Who ate more?"
- **Key activity:** Concrete: lay 2 quarter-strips on a half-strip. Pictorial: fraction wall; shade ²⁄₄ and ½ of the same shape. Abstract: ²⁄₄ = ½.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why are ½ and ²⁄₄ equal even though the numbers are different?" → They cover exactly the same amount of the whole — 2 quarters fit exactly on 1 half.
- **Differentiation:** support: fraction strips on a wall / stretch: find other fractions equal to ½ on the wall (³⁄₆, ⁴⁄₈)
- **Materials:** fraction strips, fraction wall, paper pizzas
- **Joy:** Pizza "fair or unfair?" debate.

### Lesson 114 — Counting in halves and quarters
- **Duration:** 22 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can count on in halves and quarters, such as ½, 1, 1½, 2." (UK NC Y2 F — count in fractions)
- **Hook:** "A snail crawls half a metre each minute. Where is it after 5 minutes?"
- **Key activity:** Concrete: lay half-strips and quarter-strips end to end along a metre stick. Pictorial: number line with half and quarter marks. Abstract: ½, 1, 1½, 2, 2½; ¼, ½, ¾, 1, 1¼.
- **Check for understanding:** "Counting in quarters, what comes after ¾, and why?" → 1 (four quarters make a whole).
- **Differentiation:** support: count in halves only / stretch: count back in quarters from 3
- **Materials:** fraction strips, metre stick, floor number line
- **Joy:** Snail race along a number line.

### Lesson 115 — Fractions on a number line
- **Duration:** 22 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can place ½, ¼ and ¾ on a number line from 0 to 1." (UK NC Y2 F — number line; CCSS link 3.NF.2 preview)
- **Hook:** "Where does ½ live on a number line?"
- **Key activity:** Concrete: fold a paper strip 0–1 into halves and quarters and label. Pictorial: mark fractions on lines 0–1 and 0–2. Abstract: order ¼, ½, ¾.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why is ½ in the same spot as ²⁄₄ on the line?" → They are equal, so they are the same distance from 0.
- **Differentiation:** support: folded strip on top of the line / stretch: place ⅓ and ⅔
- **Materials:** paper strips, number lines, pencils
- **Joy:** "Fraction washing line" — peg fraction cards in place.

### Lesson 116 — Fraction problems with bar models
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can solve fraction word problems with a bar model." (UK NC Y2 F — solve problems)
- **Hook:** "¼ of the 20 children in a club are wearing hats. How many are not?"
- **Key activity:** Pictorial: bar model split into 4; find one part and three parts. Concrete: counters on the bar. Abstract: ¼ of 20 = 5; 20 − 5 = 15.
- **Check for understanding:** "How did the bar model help you find the children without hats?" → The bar showed 4 equal parts; 1 part had hats, so the other 3 parts (15) didn't.

### Lesson 117 — Fractions in the real world

- **Hook:** "Quarter past, half a cup, a third each — where else do fractions hide?"
- **Key activity:** Fraction hunt: a clock face (quarter past, half past), a measuring cup (½ cup), a recipe to halve, sharing a snack three ways.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why do we say 'quarter past' when the minute hand is on the 3?" → The clock is split into 4 equal quarters; the hand has moved one quarter of the way round.

- **Joy:** Fraction hunt around the room or home.

### Lesson 118 — Retrieval & fluency: fractions

- **Objective:** "By the end, I can quickly find fractions of shapes and amounts and compare unit fractions." (UK NC Y2 F — retrieval)

- **Check for understanding:** "True or false: ¾ of 8 is 6. Prove it." → True; ¼ of 8 = 2, three quarters = 6.

- **Joy:** Fraction bingo.

### Lesson 119 — Reasoning: true or false?
- **Duration:** 22 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can decide if a fraction statement is true or false and prove it." (UK NC Y2 F — reasoning)

### Lesson 120 — End-of-unit check and Term 2 review

## Unit 9 — Time

**Essential question:** How do we read the clock to the nearest 5 minutes, and when do we say "past" or "to"?

**Key vocabulary:** o'clock, half past, quarter past, quarter to, minutes past, minutes to, hour hand, minute hand, clockwise, second, minute, hour, day, week, month, year, calendar, duration, earlier, later

### Lesson 121 — O'clock and half past (retrieval)
- **Duration:** 22 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can read and show o'clock and half past times and explain where the hour hand points at half past." (UK NC Y2 M — time; CCSS 1.MD.3)
- **Hook:** "At half past 3, is the short hand on the 3? Let's look closely."
- **Key activity:** Concrete: a geared teaching clock — turn the minute hand round once and watch the hour hand move. Pictorial: read and draw o'clock and half past. Notice that at half past the hour hand is halfway between two numbers.
- **Check for understanding:** "At half past 3, why is the hour hand between 3 and 4, not on the 3?" → Half the hour has gone, so the hour hand has moved halfway towards 4.
- **Differentiation:** support: geared clock for every question / stretch: "What time will it be in half an hour?" from half past times
- **Materials:** geared teaching clocks, individual clocks, clock stamps
- **Joy:** "What's the time, Mr Wolf?" played with clock times.

### Lesson 122 — Quarter past and quarter to
- **Duration:** 22 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can read and show quarter past and quarter to on an analogue clock." (UK NC Y2 M — quarter past/to)
- **Hook:** Fold a paper clock face into quarters: "Where are the quarters on a clock?"
- **Key activity:** Concrete: paper plate clocks folded into quarters; move the minute hand a quarter turn (quarter past) and three-quarter turn (quarter to). Pictorial: read clocks. Link to fractions (Unit 8).
- **Check for understanding:** "At quarter to 5, which number is the hour hand nearest, and why?" → 5 — it's almost 5 o'clock, so the hour hand is nearly on 5.
- **Differentiation:** support: quarter past only / stretch: "What time is a quarter of an hour after quarter to 5?" (5 o'clock)
- **Materials:** paper plates, split pins, geared clocks
- **Joy:** Make your own paper-plate clock to keep.

### Lesson 123 — Counting minutes in 5s round the clock
- **Duration:** 22 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can count in 5s around the clock face to find how many minutes the minute hand has moved." (UK NC Y2 M — time to 5 minutes; CCSS 2.MD.7)
- **Hook:** "The clock has 12 numbers but there are 60 minutes in an hour. How does that work?"
- **Key activity:** Concrete: stick "5, 10, 15…" labels around a big clock; walk the minute hand round counting. Link to the 5 times table. Abstract: minute hand on 7 = 35 minutes.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why is the minute hand on the 4 worth 20 minutes, not 4?" → Each number is 5 minutes after the one before, so 4 numbers = 4 × 5 = 20 minutes.
- **Differentiation:** support: labelled minutes on the clock / stretch: minute hand on 11 (55 minutes) without labels
- **Materials:** big floor clock (chalk or hoops), geared clocks, minute labels
- **Joy:** Human clock — the child walks the minute hand round a chalk clock.

### Lesson 124 — Minutes past the hour
- **Duration:** 22 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can read 'minutes past' times by counting the minute hand in 5s." (UK NC Y2 M — to 5 minutes; CCSS 2.MD.7)
- **Hook:** "The cake goes in at 5 past 2 — show me!"
- **Key activity:** Concrete: set a geared clock to 5, 10, 20 and 25 past. Pictorial: read clock faces. On the right side (12 to 6) we say "past".
- **Check for understanding:** "Minute hand on 5, hour hand just after 2. Kai says '5 past 2'. What is it?" → 25 past 2; the 5 means 25 minutes.
- **Differentiation:** support: minute labels on the clock / stretch: write each time in digital form (2:25)
- **Materials:** geared clocks, clock-face cards
- **Joy:** "Clock bingo" with past times.

### Lesson 125 — Minutes to the hour
- **Duration:** 22 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can read 'minutes to' times once the minute hand passes the 6." (UK NC Y2 M — to 5 minutes; CCSS 2.MD.7)
- **Hook:** "Why do people say '10 to 3' instead of '50 past 2'?"
- **Key activity:** Concrete: on the left side (6 to 12), count the minutes left to the next hour. Pictorial: shade the "to" half of a clock. Abstract: minute hand on 8 = 20 to.
- **Check for understanding:** "The minute hand is on the 8 and the hour hand is nearly on 6. Say the time as '___ to' and explain which hour you use." → 20 to 6; we count the minutes left until the next hour, 6. (The same time is 5:40 — both names are correct.)
- **Differentiation:** support: quarter to plus 5 and 10 to / stretch: say each time both ways (10 to 3 = 2:50)
- **Materials:** geared clocks, shaded clock cards
- **Joy:** "Countdown to the party" — how many minutes to the hour?

### Lesson 126 — Telling the time to 5 minutes: mixed
- **Duration:** 22 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can read any time to 5 minutes, using past or to correctly." (UK NC Y2 M — to 5 minutes)
- **Hook:** "Timing detectives: which of these clocks is lying?"
- **Key activity:** Mixed reading of clocks; sort into past and to; spot clocks where the hour hand doesn't match the minute hand.
- **Check for understanding:** "This clock shows the minute hand on 9 and the hour hand exactly on 4. Why is it impossible?" → At quarter to, the hour hand should be nearly at the next number, not exactly on 4.
- **Differentiation:** support: past times plus quarter to / stretch: matching analogue to digital times
- **Materials:** clock cards, geared clocks
- **Joy:** Lying-clock detective game.

### Lesson 127 — Drawing hands on the clock
- **Duration:** 22 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can draw the hands on a clock to show a time to 5 minutes, with the hour hand in the right place." (UK NC Y2 M — draw hands)
- **Hook:** "The clock-maker forgot the hands! Can you fix them?"
- **Key activity:** I do: 20 past 7 — minute hand on 4, hour hand a little past 7. We do/you do: blank clock stamps; partner checks with a geared clock.
- **Check for understanding:** "When you draw 20 to 9, where does the hour hand go and why?" → Just before 9 — it's nearly 9 o'clock.

### Lesson 128 — Units of time
- **Duration:** 22 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I know 60 seconds = 1 minute, 60 minutes = 1 hour and 24 hours = 1 day, and I can choose a sensible unit." (UK NC Y2 M — compare intervals)

- **Check for understanding:** "Would brushing your teeth take 2 seconds, 2 minutes or 2 hours? Why?" → About 2 minutes — seconds is too short and hours too long.

### Lesson 129 — Comparing durations

### Lesson 130 — Finding durations

- **Duration:** 22 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can identify the 2-D shapes of the faces on 3-D shapes." (UK NC Y2 G — faces)
- **Hook:** "Print with a 3-D shape. What flat shape does it leave behind?"
- **Key activity:** Printing with paint: dip faces of shapes and print. Record which 2-D shapes appear on each 3-D shape.
- **Check for understanding:** "What shape prints does a triangular prism make?" → 2 triangles and 3 rectangles.
- **Differentiation:** support: cube and cuboid / stretch: predict a shape's prints before printing
- **Materials:** 3-D shapes, paint, paper
- **Joy:** Shape printing (link: Art).

### Lesson 150 — Sorting 3-D shapes
- **Duration:** 22 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can sort 3-D shapes by faces, edges, vertices, or whether they roll or stack." (UK NC Y2 G — compare and sort)
- **Hook:** "Which shapes would make the best tower? The best wheels?"
- **Key activity:** Test shapes on a ramp (roll/slide) and in towers (stack). Sort in a Carroll diagram (curved surface / no curved surface).
- **Check for understanding:** "Why does a cylinder both roll and stack?" → It has a curved surface for rolling and flat circular faces for stacking.
- **Differentiation:** support: roll vs don't roll / stretch: sort by number of vertices
- **Materials:** 3-D shapes, ramp, Carroll diagram mat
- **Joy:** Ramp races.

### Lesson 151 — Symmetry: matching halves
- **Duration:** 22 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can find a line of symmetry by folding so both halves match." (UK NC Y2 G — vertical line symmetry)
- **Hook:** "A butterfly with one wing missing: can you draw it back?"
- **Key activity:** Concrete: fold paper shapes and check if halves match exactly. Pictorial: draw fold lines. Test: a square (4 lines), rectangle (2), circle (many), and a shape with none.
- **Check for understanding:** "Mia folded a rectangle corner to corner and says that's a line of symmetry. Test it — is she right?" → No; the halves don't land on each other when folded along the diagonal.
- **Differentiation:** support: fold pre-cut shapes / stretch: find all lines of symmetry in a regular hexagon (6)
- **Materials:** paper shapes, small mirrors, butterfly outlines
- **Joy:** Paint-blot butterflies.

### Lesson 152 — Completing symmetrical pictures
- **Duration:** 22 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can complete a picture or pattern so that it is symmetrical about a vertical line." (UK NC Y2 G — vertical symmetry)
- **Hook:** Rangoli and Islamic tile patterns: "What do these designs from around the world have in common?"
- **Key activity:** Use mirrors to check; complete half-drawn pictures on squared paper; make a symmetrical pattern with pegs on a pegboard.
- **Check for understanding:** "A square is 2 squares from the mirror line on the left. Where must its partner go?" → 2 squares from the line on the right — the same distance on the other side.
- **Differentiation:** support: mirror to check each square / stretch: complete symmetrical designs with a horizontal line too
- **Materials:** mirrors, squared paper, pegboards, pictures of rangoli and tile patterns
- **Joy:** Design a rangoli-inspired symmetrical pattern.

### Lesson 153 — Shape patterns
- **Duration:** 22 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can make, continue and describe repeating patterns of shapes, including turning shapes." (UK NC Y2 G — patterns and sequences)
- **Hook:** Kente cloth and tiling patterns: "What comes next — and how do you know?"
- **Key activity:** Continue AB, ABC and AAB patterns with shapes; find the 10th shape without drawing all of them; make patterns where a shape rotates.
- **Check for understanding:** "In square, triangle, circle, square, triangle, circle… what is the 10th shape, and how can you work it out?" → Square; the pattern repeats every 3, and 9 is a whole number of repeats, so the 10th starts again.
- **Differentiation:** support: AB patterns with real shapes / stretch: predict the 20th shape
- **Materials:** shape tiles, pattern strips, kente pattern pictures
- **Joy:** Design a pattern border for a class display.

### Lesson 154 — Retrieval & fluency: shape, data and symmetry
- **Duration:** 22 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can quickly recall shape properties, read a chart and spot symmetry." (UK NC Y2 G/S — retrieval)
- **Hook:** "Shape and data quiz show — buzzers ready!"
- **Key activity:** Quick-fire rounds: name the shape, count faces, read a pictogram, is it symmetrical? Mark and set a personal target.
- **Check for understanding:** "Name a 3-D shape with 6 faces that aren't all the same size." → A cuboid.
- **Differentiation:** support: shapes and charts in hand / stretch: bonus rounds on pyramids and keys of 5
- **Materials:** shape set, charts, buzzers (or bells)
- **Joy:** Quiz-show buzzers.

### Lesson 155 — Shape investigation: tangrams
- **Duration:** 30 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can make new shapes from smaller shapes and describe what I made." (UK NC Y2 G — reasoning)
- **Hook:** "The tangram is an old Chinese puzzle: 7 pieces, thousands of pictures."
- **Key activity:** Make a square from 2 triangles, then animals from tangram pieces; name and count the pieces; describe the new shape's sides.
- **Check for understanding:** "Using the two small tangram triangles, can you make a square? A bigger triangle? Explain how the sides fit." → Yes to both — the long sides meet to make a square; the short sides meet to make a bigger triangle.
- **Differentiation:** support: outline cards to fill / stretch: silhouettes with no internal lines
- **Materials:** tangram sets, outline cards
- **Joy:** Tangram animal challenge.

### Lesson 156 — End-of-unit check: Shape and symmetry
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can show I can name, describe, sort and draw shapes and find lines of symmetry." (UK NC Y2 G — assessment)
- **Hook:** "The architect's apprentice test."
- **Key activity:** Mastery task: name shapes in any orientation, count faces/edges/vertices, sort by a rule, draw a line of symmetry, complete a symmetrical picture, explain a mistake.
- **Check for understanding:** "Is a shape with 4 sides always a square? Draw one that isn't." → No; e.g. a rectangle or an irregular quadrilateral.
- **Differentiation:** support: real shapes at hand / stretch: pyramid and prism properties
- **Materials:** shape sets, mirrors, squared paper, assessment sheet
- **Joy:** Architect's certificate.

**End-of-unit check:** Describe a cube and a square-based pyramid by faces, edges and vertices, sort six 2-D shapes by a rule you choose, and draw the line of symmetry on a shape — testing it by folding.

## Unit 12 — Position and direction

**Essential question:** How can we describe exactly where something is and how it moves?

**Key vocabulary:** position, direction, above, below, between, next to, left, right, forwards, backwards, turn, quarter turn, half turn, three-quarter turn, whole turn, clockwise, anticlockwise, route, grid, right angle

### Lesson 157 — Describing position
- **Duration:** 22 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can describe where objects are using position words, including left and right from my point of view." (UK NC Y2 G — position)
- **Hook:** "Hide the teddy — then tell a friend exactly where it is without pointing."
- **Key activity:** Hide-and-find with position clues (above, below, between, left of). Discuss: my left vs your left when facing each other.
- **Check for understanding:** "You and I face each other. The ball is on my left. Is it on your left? Why?" → No, it's on your right — facing each other flips left and right.
- **Differentiation:** support: left/right wristbands / stretch: describe a position with two clues (above the shelf, left of the clock)
- **Materials:** teddy, wristbands, classroom objects
- **Joy:** Hide-and-find the teddy.

### Lesson 158 — Describing movement
- **Duration:** 22 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can give and follow instructions using forwards, backwards, left and right." (UK NC Y2 G — movement in a straight line)
- **Hook:** "Be a robot! Your partner programs you across the room."
- **Key activity:** Pairs give "robot" commands (forward 3 steps, turn right); then on a squared grid move a counter to targets. Link: Computing (algorithms).
- **Check for understanding:** "Why does 'turn right' change which way 'forward' goes?" → Forward is the direction you're facing, and turning changes the way you face.
- **Differentiation:** support: forwards/backwards only / stretch: find two different routes to the same target
- **Materials:** floor grid (tape or chalk), counters, squared paper
- **Joy:** Human robots.

### Lesson 159 — Turns: quarter, half, three-quarter and whole
- **Duration:** 22 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can make and describe quarter, half, three-quarter and whole turns, clockwise and anticlockwise." (UK NC Y2 G — rotation; link: quarter past on the clock)
- **Hook:** "The clock hands only ever turn one way. Which way?"
- **Key activity:** Stand and turn in quarter turns; use a clock to show clockwise; turn objects on a mat; a quarter turn is a right angle.
- **Check for understanding:** "Facing the door, you make a half turn. Which way are you facing? Does it matter if it's clockwise or anticlockwise?" → Away from the door; a half turn ends in the same place either way.
- **Differentiation:** support: whole and half turns / stretch: "three-quarter turn clockwise = quarter turn anticlockwise — why?"
- **Materials:** analogue clock, arrows on the floor, compass-direction labels
- **Joy:** "Turning dance" to music.

### Lesson 160 — Routes on a grid
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can plan and describe a route on a grid using movements and turns." (UK NC Y2 G — combine movements)
- **Hook:** "Guide the pirate to the treasure without hitting the rocks!"
- **Key activity:** Treasure map grid: write the route as instructions; partner tests; debug mistakes. Link: Computing (programming a Bee-Bot).
- **Check for understanding:** "Your route sent the pirate into a rock. How do you find and fix the wrong step?" → Follow the instructions one step at a time until it goes wrong, then change that step.
- **Differentiation:** support: small 4 × 4 grid / stretch: shortest route and a route with exactly 3 turns
- **Materials:** grid maps, counters, floor robot (e.g. Bee-Bot) if available
- **Joy:** Pirate treasure maps.

### Lesson 161 — Patterns with turns
- **Duration:** 22 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can continue a pattern of shapes that turn each time and describe the turn." (UK NC Y2 G — patterns with rotation)
- **Hook:** "The arrow is spinning! Where will it point next?"
- **Key activity:** Continue sequences where an arrow or shape makes a quarter turn clockwise each time; create your own.
- **Check for understanding:** "An arrow points up, then right, then down. What next, and why?" → Left; it's turning a quarter turn clockwise each time.
- **Differentiation:** support: half turns / stretch: patterns alternating clockwise and anticlockwise
- **Materials:** arrow cards, shape tiles
- **Joy:** "Spinning arrow" flipbook.

### Lesson 162 — Treasure hunt project
- **Duration:** 30 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can write clear directions for someone else to follow to a hidden treasure." (UK NC Y2 G — apply)
- **Hook:** "Hide a treasure; write instructions so precise that someone else can find it."
- **Key activity:** In pairs, hide an object indoors or outdoors; write step-by-step directions (steps, turns, position words); swap and follow; improve.
- **Check for understanding:** "Your partner got lost at step 3. What made step 3 unclear?" → e.g. "turn" without saying which way or how far.
- **Differentiation:** support: picture-arrow direction cards / stretch: include quarter and three-quarter turns
- **Materials:** small treasures, direction cards, clipboards
- **Joy:** Real treasure hunt.

### Lesson 163 — End-of-unit check: Position and direction
- **Duration:** 22 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can show I can describe position, movement and turns accurately." (UK NC Y2 G — assessment)
- **Hook:** "Mission control needs a navigator."
- **Key activity:** Mastery task: describe positions, follow and write a grid route, name turns, continue a turning pattern.
- **Check for understanding:** "Describe the turn from facing the window to facing the door." → e.g. a quarter turn clockwise (matching the room).
- **Differentiation:** support: acting out moves / stretch: route with fewest instructions
- **Materials:** grid sheets, arrow cards
- **Joy:** Navigator badge.

**End-of-unit check:** Write directions to move a counter from start to a star on a 5 × 5 grid using forwards, left/right and quarter turns, then describe a three-quarter turn clockwise in another way.

## Unit 13 — Year-end problem solving

**Essential question:** Can I bring all my Year 1 maths together to solve problems in the real world?

**Key vocabulary:** problem, operation, strategy, bar model, estimate, check, reason, explain, prove, investigate, pattern, systematic

### Lesson 164 — Mixed problems: number and calculation
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can choose the right operation to solve a worded number problem." (UK NC Y2 AS/MD — mixed problems)
- **Hook:** "Four problems, four different operations. Can you match them?"
- **Key activity:** Read, draw a bar model, choose +, −, × or ÷, solve and check with the inverse. Discuss key clues without relying only on "key words".
- **Check for understanding:** "Why is 'how many more' a subtraction even though it says 'more'?" → It asks for the difference between two amounts.
- **Differentiation:** support: one-step problems with equipment / stretch: two-step problems mixing operations
- **Materials:** problem cards, bar-model strips, base-10, counters
- **Joy:** Problem-matching race.

### Lesson 165 — Mixed problems: money, measure and time
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can solve everyday problems with money, measurement and time." (UK NC Y2 M — mixed problems)
- **Hook:** "Plan a trip to the zoo: tickets, travel time and snacks."
- **Key activity:** Problem stations: change from a ticket, a duration on the bus, measuring a snack, comparing masses.
- **Check for understanding:** "The bus leaves at quarter past 9 and takes 30 minutes. When does it arrive?" → Quarter to 10.
- **Differentiation:** support: equipment at every station / stretch: two-step problems
- **Materials:** coins, clocks, rulers, scales, station cards
- **Joy:** Zoo-trip role play.

### Lesson 166 — Bar models: which operation?
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can draw a bar model for any one-step problem and use it to decide the operation." (UK NC Y2 — problem solving)
- **Hook:** "One bar model, many stories. Can you write three stories for the same bar?"
- **Key activity:** Match problems to part-whole, comparison and equal-parts bars; write stories for bars.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why does a bar split into 5 equal parts suggest × or ÷?" → Equal parts means equal groups — multiplication or division.
- **Differentiation:** support: two bar types / stretch: write a two-step story for a double bar model
- **Materials:** bar-model cards, strips
- **Joy:** Story-writing for bars.

### Lesson 167 — Investigations: number patterns
- **Duration:** 30 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can explore a number pattern, record it systematically and explain a rule." (UK NC Y2 N — reasoning)
- **Hook:** "Add the corners of any 2 × 2 square on a 100 square — diagonal pairs. What do you notice?"
- **Key activity:** Investigate: on a 100 square, the two diagonal pairs of any 2 × 2 square have equal sums (e.g. 12 + 23 = 13 + 22). Record, test, explain using tens and ones.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why do 12 + 23 and 13 + 22 give the same total?" → Both have 3 tens and 5 ones in total — one ten and one one just move between the numbers.
- **Differentiation:** support: find the pattern with the teacher and blocks / stretch: try 3 × 3 squares
- **Materials:** 100 squares, counters, recording sheets
- **Joy:** Discovering a "magic" rule.

### Lesson 168 — Maths in the world: plan a picnic
- **Duration:** 30 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can use money, measures, time and data to plan a real event." (UK NC Y2 — apply)
- **Hook:** "We have a budget and one afternoon. Let's plan the perfect picnic."
- **Key activity:** Survey food choices (data), cost the shopping (money), measure drinks (capacity), plan a timetable (time), share food fairly (fractions/division).
- **Check for understanding:** "We have 12 sandwiches for 4 people. What fraction does each get, and how many sandwiches?" → ¼ each = 3 sandwiches.
- **Differentiation:** support: one job (e.g. shopping list) / stretch: keep within budget and find the change
- **Materials:** catalogue pages, coins, jugs, clocks, survey sheets
- **Joy:** Planning a real picnic.

### Lesson 169 — End-of-year assessment
- **Duration:** 30 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can show what I know across the year in a calm, low-stakes check." (UK NC Y2 — end-of-year assessment)
- **Hook:** "Show off your maths superpowers — the teacher wants to see how much you've grown."
- **Key activity:** Short mixed tasks across all strands with equipment available; teacher conferences and records next steps for Year 2. Children compare with their September baseline.
- **Check for understanding:** "What's one thing you can do now that you couldn't in September? Show me." → Child demonstrates (e.g. adding with exchange).
- **Differentiation:** support: tasks read aloud and scribed / stretch: extension challenges
- **Materials:** assessment tasks, base-10, counters, clocks, coins
- **Joy:** "Then and now" — compare with September work.

### Lesson 170 — Year 1 maths challenge & review
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can show what I have mastered across the whole year." (UK NC Y2 — review)
- **Hook:** "The great Year 1 maths challenge — every strand, every superpower."
- **Key activity:** A celebratory mixed challenge game across all strands; each child explains one favourite idea to a younger child or family member.
- **Check for understanding:** "Explain one Year 1 idea to someone younger, using a drawing or blocks." → Clear explanation with a representation.
- **Differentiation:** support: team play / stretch: set challenges for others
- **Materials:** challenge cards, all maths equipment
- **Joy:** Maths celebration with certificates.

**End-of-unit check:** Solve three real-world problems of your choice (one money, one time, one calculation), drawing a bar model for at least one and checking one answer with the inverse.

## End-of-year mastery checks

1. **Place value:** builds, partitions (two ways) and compares any number to 100 with base-10, and explains why 71 ≠ 17. *(Seen: build and explain.)*
2. **Facts:** recalls bonds within 20 and to 100 in tens without counting. *(Seen: quick oral round, ~3 seconds per fact.)*
3. **Two-digit add/subtract:** solves 47 + 36 and 72 − 45 with a chosen method, including exchange, and checks with the inverse. *(Seen: written work and explanation.)*
4. **Multiplication and division:** recalls 2, 5 and 10 facts and their division facts; draws an array and a fact family. *(Seen: recall round + drawing.)*
5. **Fractions:** finds ½, ⅓, ¼ and ¾ of shapes and quantities and explains why more equal parts means smaller parts. *(Seen: counters and strips.)*
6. **Measures and money:** measures in cm, reads a scale with unlabelled intervals, and gives correct change by counting up. *(Seen: practical tasks.)*
7. **Time:** reads and draws times to 5 minutes, using past and to correctly. *(Seen: clock tasks.)*
8. **Data and shape:** reads a keyed pictogram, describes a 3-D shape by faces, edges, vertices and finds a line of symmetry. *(Seen: practical tasks.)*
9. **Problem solving:** draws a bar model for a one-step word problem and chooses the operation, explaining why. *(Seen: talk-through.)*

## Teacher guidance

**Common misconceptions and fixes**
- *Reading digits, not values* ("50 is 5 and 0"; "19 > 21") → always build with base-10 first; compare tens first.
- *Not exchanging* (45 + 38 = 713; 52 − 27 = 35 by "smaller from bigger") → exchange with real blocks until the child can say what the exchange means.
- *Multiplication = "adding the numbers"* (3 × 5 = 8) → return to equal groups; say "3 groups of 5".
- *Denominator-as-size* (¼ > ½ because 4 > 2) → fraction strips of the same whole, side by side.
- *Ruler starts at the end* → line up with 0; show why starting at 1 makes it one short.
- *Minute hand number = minutes* ("4 past") → count in 5s round the clock, labelled.
- *Ignoring the key* in pictograms → read the key first, every time.

**How to teach it well.** Keep every lesson CPA: if a child is stuck at abstract, go back to the picture; stuck at pictorial, go back to the blocks. Keep the whole class together; use "support" tasks for extra concrete time and "stretch" for depth (not new content). Retrieval warm-ups are non-negotiable — they're how facts stick. Talk matters: ask "how do you know?" more than "what's the answer?". Use real contexts from many places — markets in Lagos, temperatures in Oslo and Cairo, tangrams from China, rangoli from India.

**Vertical alignment.** Foundation gives counting, bonds to 10, within-20 facts, halves/quarters, coins, o'clock/half past and shape names. Year 2 (ages 7–8) extends place value to 1000, formal column methods, the 3, 4 and 8 tables, tenths and equivalent fractions, time to the minute and 24-hour clock, perimeter, and bar charts. If a child finishes Year 1 without instant bonds and secure exchange, prioritise those in Year 2's first term.
