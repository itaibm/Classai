# Year 1 Physical Education & Health (ages 6–7) — Scope & Sequence

> Part of **the best international primary school** (see [`../../../SCHOOL-CHARTER.md`](../../../SCHOOL-CHARTER.md)).
> Built to [`../../PLANNING-BRIEF.md`](../../PLANNING-BRIEF.md). Codes: **UK NC KS1 PE** (FMS = fundamental movement
> skills, GA = games, DA = dance, GY = gymnastics/balance and agility), **UK Health Education (primary)** statutory
> guidance (PH = physical health and fitness, HE = healthy eating, SL = sleep, HY = health and prevention, MW = mental
> wellbeing, SA = keeping safe), and **SHAPE America** grade 1–2 outcomes (S1 = motor skills, S3 = fitness knowledge,
> S4 = personal and social behaviour).

## Year overview

Year 1 PE & Health grows **confident, capable, happy movers** and teaches the habits that keep bodies and minds
well. PE lessons are **adult-led and active** — a parent, teacher or coach runs them in a hall, garden, park or
playground; the Classai tutor supports only with warm-up ideas, rules and short explanations. Every PE lesson has
the same shape: **warm-up (raise the heart rate, mobilise) → skill teaching (demonstrate, practise, feedback) →
game or sequence that uses the skill → cool-down and reflection**. Children should be moving for most of every
lesson.

**What this year assumes (ages 5–6):** running, jumping and hopping with some control; simple throwing and
catching of a large ball; moving safely in a space; the idea that exercise, food and sleep keep us healthy; washing
hands.

**How the year runs.** Two lessons a week. *Term 1:* fundamental movement skills (Unit 1), sending and receiving
a ball (Unit 2) and Health 1 — my healthy body (Unit 3). *Term 2:* dance from around the world (Unit 4),
gymnastics (Unit 5) and Health 2 — feelings and keeping safe (Unit 6). *Term 3:* team games with simple tactics
(Unit 7), outdoor adventure, water safety and athletics (Unit 8) and a health-and-fitness festival (Unit 9).
Health lessons are shorter and more discussion-based, but always include movement.

**Safety first, every lesson.** Check the space (floor, obstacles, weather), clothing and footwear, water, and
any medical needs (asthma inhalers, allergies) before starting. Safety notes appear in every lesson's Materials.

**What Year 2 relies on:** agile running with changes of direction; two-footed and one-footed jumps with safe
landings; underarm and overarm throws; catching a medium ball; basic dribbling with hands and feet; a simple
gymnastics sequence of balance, roll and jump; simple dance phrases; understanding of fair play; and the health
knowledge to explain why exercise, food, sleep, hygiene and feelings matter. Year 2 (ages 7–8) moves on to
invasion, net and striking games with more tactics, longer sequences and swimming lessons where available.

## Unit 1 — Moving well: fundamental movement skills

**Essential question:** How can I move my body with control, speed and balance?

**Key vocabulary:** warm up, cool down, space, run, jog, sprint, dodge, jump, hop, skip, gallop, leap, land, balance, agility, control, heart rate, stretch

### Lesson 1 — Finding space and warming up
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can move safely in a space without bumping and explain why we warm up." (UK NC KS1 PE FMS; SHAPE S4)
- **Hook:** "Can the whole group move around the space for one minute and nobody touch anyone?"
- **Key activity:** Adult-led. Warm-up game "traffic lights" (green = jog, amber = walk, red = freeze); learn "find a space" with spot markers; heart check before and after; cool-down stretches.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why do we warm up before running games?" → It gets our heart and muscles ready, so we move better and don't get hurt.
- **Differentiation:** support: walk the pattern first with a partner / stretch: change speed and direction on each signal
- **Materials:** cones or spot markers, whistle or drum; safety: clear floor, correct footwear, water available
- **Joy:** "Traffic lights" game.

### Lesson 2 — Running and stopping with control
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can run using my arms, and stop quickly on a signal with bent knees." (UK NC KS1 PE FMS; SHAPE S1)
- **Hook:** "Can you stop as fast as a cheetah — without falling over?"
- **Key activity:** Adult demonstrates running arms (elbows bent, drive back and forth) and "stop and drop" (bend knees, feet apart); relay-free practice runs; "cheetah and tortoise" speed game.
- **Check for understanding:** "What helps you stop without wobbling?" → Bending my knees and putting my feet apart.
- **Differentiation:** support: shorter distance, walk-jog-stop / stretch: stop on a line and change direction
- **Materials:** cones, markers; safety: space to slow down at the end of each run, no walls as finish lines
- **Joy:** Cheetah and tortoise game.

### Lesson 3 — Dodging and changing direction
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can dodge by changing direction quickly, pushing off one foot." (UK NC KS1 PE FMS — agility; SHAPE S1)
- **Hook:** "How do rabbits escape foxes? They zig-zag!"
- **Key activity:** Practise the "push-off" sidestep through a cone zig-zag; tag game "foxes and rabbits" with safe tagging (two fingers on the back or shoulder).
- **Check for understanding:** "Which foot do you push off to dodge to the left?" → The right foot.
- **Differentiation:** support: walk the zig-zag first / stretch: dodge a moving partner
- **Materials:** cones, bibs; safety: agree tagging rules, clear boundaries
- **Joy:** Foxes and rabbits.

### Lesson 4 — Jumping and landing safely
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can jump for distance and height and land safely on two feet with bent knees." (UK NC KS1 PE FMS; SHAPE S1)
- **Hook:** "Frogs jump far; kangaroos jump high. Which are you today?"
- **Key activity:** Teach "swing, bend, jump, land like a spring" (arms swing, knees bend, soft landing); jump over ropes laid on the ground; measure standing long jumps (link: Maths length).
- **Check for understanding:** "Why do we land with bent knees?" → It's softer and safer for our joints, and we don't fall over.
- **Differentiation:** support: jump from a line onto a mat / stretch: jump and turn a quarter turn in the air
- **Materials:** ropes, mats, metre sticks; safety: non-slip surface, soft landing area
- **Joy:** Measuring your personal-best jump.

### Lesson 5 — Hop, skip and gallop
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can hop on each foot, gallop and begin to skip with rhythm." (UK NC KS1 PE FMS; SHAPE S1)
- **Hook:** "Can you hop like a bird with one leg? Gallop like a horse?"
- **Key activity:** Practise each travelling action to music; "step-hop" skipping; follow-the-leader pathways.
- **Check for understanding:** "What's the difference between a hop and a jump?" → A hop takes off and lands on the same one foot; a jump uses two feet (or takes off one and lands on two).
- **Differentiation:** support: step-hop slowly holding an adult's hand / stretch: skip in a pattern and change leading foot
- **Materials:** music, hoops for stepping; safety: enough space between children
- **Joy:** Animal-movement music game.

### Lesson 6 — Balance: still and strong
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can hold a balance on one foot and on different body parts for 5 seconds." (UK NC KS1 PE GY — balance; SHAPE S1)
- **Hook:** "Flamingos stand on one leg for hours! How long can you?"
- **Key activity:** Balance challenges: one foot, knees and hands, "tall and tight" balances; tips (look at a fixed point, tighten tummy); partner counting.
- **Check for understanding:** "What helps you stay balanced?" → Looking at one spot, keeping my tummy tight and my arms out.
- **Differentiation:** support: hold a wall or partner's hand / stretch: balance with eyes closed for 3 seconds
- **Materials:** mats, balance beams (low) or lines on the floor; safety: low equipment only, spotters
- **Joy:** Flamingo challenge.

### Lesson 7 — Agility ladder and obstacle course
- **Duration:** 30 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can move through an obstacle course using running, jumping, hopping and balancing with control." (UK NC KS1 PE FMS)
- **Hook:** "Ninja training course — complete it with control, not just speed!"
- **Key activity:** Circuit of stations (agility ladder, hoop hops, beam walk, zig-zag cones, jump over ropes); children rotate; adult gives one tip per station.
- **Check for understanding:** "At which station do you need to slow down to stay in control, and why?" → e.g. the beam — rushing makes you fall.
- **Differentiation:** support: simpler version of each station / stretch: time yourself and try to improve with control
- **Materials:** agility ladder, hoops, low beam, cones, ropes; safety: stations spaced well apart, one child per station at a time
- **Joy:** Ninja course.

### Lesson 8 — Fitness: what happens to my body?
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can describe what happens to my heart, breathing and skin when I exercise." (UK Health Ed PH; SHAPE S3; link: Science)
- **Hook:** "Detective challenge: find 3 clues that your body is working hard!"
- **Key activity:** One-minute activity bursts (star jumps, running on the spot) with checks between: heart beating faster, breathing faster, feeling warm or sweaty; cool-down and notice return to normal.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why does your heart beat faster when you exercise?" → To pump more blood to the muscles, which need more energy.
- **Differentiation:** support: feel the heart with an adult's help / stretch: count breaths for 15 seconds before and after
- **Materials:** timer, recording sheet; safety: stop if anyone feels dizzy; inhalers available
- **Joy:** Body-detective clues.

### Lesson 9 — Playground games from around the world
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can play running and tag games from other countries, following the rules fairly." (UK NC KS1 PE GA; SHAPE S4)
- **Hook:** "Children everywhere play chasing games — let's try some!"
- **Key activity:** "Kho-kho" style simplified chase (India), "Pilolo" hide-and-find race (Ghana), "Hopscotch" variants (many countries); discuss fair play.
- **Check for understanding:** "What makes a game fair for everyone?" → Everyone follows the same rules, takes turns and is honest when tagged.
- **Differentiation:** support: fewer rules, adult plays / stretch: explain the rules to a new player
- **Materials:** cones, chalk, small objects to hide; safety: clear boundaries, safe tagging
- **Joy:** World games carnival.

### Lesson 10 — Unit check: movement skills circuit
- **Duration:** 30 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can show running, stopping, dodging, jumping, hopping, skipping and balancing with control." (UK NC KS1 PE FMS — assessment)
- **Hook:** "Movement skills passport — earn a stamp at each station!"
- **Key activity:** Adult observes each skill at stations using a simple checklist (e.g. soft landing, arms used in running); children self-assess one strength and one next step.
- **Check for understanding:** "Show me a safe landing and tell me the key points." → Demonstrates bent knees, feet apart, arms forward.
- **Differentiation:** support: skill cues on cards / stretch: combine two skills (hop then balance)
- **Materials:** cones, hoops, mats, checklist; safety: as previous lessons
- **Joy:** Passport stamps.

**End-of-unit check:** Adult observes the child run and stop on a signal, dodge a partner, jump and land safely on two feet, hop on each foot, skip, and hold a one-foot balance for five seconds.

## Unit 2 — Ball skills: sending and receiving

**Essential question:** How can I throw, catch, roll and kick a ball accurately?

**Key vocabulary:** throw, catch, roll, bounce, kick, dribble, aim, target, underarm, overarm, ready position, eyes on the ball, soft hands, follow through, partner, accurate

### Lesson 11 — Rolling to a target
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can roll a ball accurately to a target using a step with the opposite foot." (UK NC KS1 PE FMS — sending; SHAPE S1)
- **Hook:** "Ten-pin bowling challenge — can you knock the skittles down?"
- **Key activity:** Teach: step forward with opposite foot, swing arm low, follow through to the target; roll to skittles and partners.
- **Check for understanding:** "Which foot steps forward when you roll with your right hand?" → The left foot.
- **Differentiation:** support: large ball, close target / stretch: smaller target further away
- **Materials:** balls, skittles (or bottles), cones; safety: children behind the rolling line
- **Joy:** Bowling alley.

### Lesson 12 — Underarm throwing
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can throw a beanbag underarm to a target using the opposite foot step and follow-through." (UK NC KS1 PE FMS; SHAPE S1)
- **Hook:** "Hoop hoopla — who can land a beanbag in the hoop?"
- **Key activity:** "Tick-tock" arm swing; step with opposite foot; point at the target after release; throw at hoops at increasing distances.
- **Check for understanding:** "Where should your hand point after you let go?" → At the target.
- **Differentiation:** support: large target, close / stretch: throw to a partner's hands
- **Materials:** beanbags, hoops; safety: throw in one direction only, collect on a signal
- **Joy:** Hoop hoopla scores.

### Lesson 13 — Catching with soft hands
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can catch a large ball from a gentle throw using 'ready hands' and bringing it into my body." (UK NC KS1 PE FMS — receiving; SHAPE S1)
- **Hook:** "Catch an egg (a balloon first!) without breaking it."
- **Key activity:** Balloon catching → large soft ball; ready position (hands out, fingers spread, eyes on the ball); partner throws from 2 m.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why do we pull the ball in towards our body when we catch?" → It softens the catch so the ball doesn't bounce out.
- **Differentiation:** support: balloons and scarves (slow) / stretch: catch a smaller ball or clap once before catching
- **Materials:** balloons, scarves, large soft balls; safety: soft balls only, space between pairs
- **Joy:** Balloon catch.

### Lesson 14 — Bouncing and dribbling with hands
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can bounce a ball with one hand, pushing with fingertips, while walking." (UK NC KS1 PE FMS; SHAPE S1)
- **Hook:** "Basketball players look at the court, not the ball! Can you?"
- **Key activity:** Stationary bounces (two hands, one hand), then walking dribble through cones; finger pads not slapping palm.
- **Check for understanding:** "Which part of your hand pushes the ball down?" → The fingertips/finger pads.
- **Differentiation:** support: bounce and catch / stretch: dribble around cones switching hands
- **Materials:** bouncy balls, cones; safety: spread out, stop balls on command
- **Joy:** "Basketball star" challenge.

### Lesson 15 — Kicking and stopping a ball
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can kick a ball to a partner with the inside of my foot and stop it when it comes back." (UK NC KS1 PE FMS; SHAPE S1)
- **Hook:** "Goalkeeper challenge: can you score through the cone goal?"
- **Key activity:** Non-kicking foot beside the ball, kick with the inside; trap with the sole or inside of foot; partner passing through gates.
- **Check for understanding:** "Where should your standing foot be when you pass?" → Next to the ball, pointing at the target.
- **Differentiation:** support: stationary ball, big gate / stretch: pass on the move
- **Materials:** footballs (size 3), cones; safety: kick along the ground only, spaced pairs
- **Joy:** Cone-goal scoring.

### Lesson 16 — Dribbling with feet
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can dribble a ball with small touches, keeping it close, around cones." (UK NC KS1 PE FMS; SHAPE S1)
- **Hook:** "Keep the ball on a lead like a puppy!"
- **Key activity:** Small touches with inside/outside of foot; dribble around a cone slalom; "traffic jam" game with stops.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why do we use small touches when dribbling?" → So the ball stays close and under control.
- **Differentiation:** support: walk-dribble with a large soft ball / stretch: dribble and change direction on a signal
- **Materials:** footballs, cones; safety: heads up to avoid collisions
- **Joy:** "Puppy on a lead" game.

### Lesson 17 — Overarm throwing
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can throw overarm for distance, stepping with the opposite foot and following through." (UK NC KS1 PE FMS; SHAPE S1)
- **Hook:** "Throw like a javelin thrower — how far can a beanbag fly?"
- **Key activity:** "Side-on, arm back like a bow, step, throw, point"; throw beanbags or foam javelins for distance; mark landings.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why do we stand side-on for an overarm throw?" → It lets us bring our arm back and use our whole body to throw further.
- **Differentiation:** support: stand side-on with feet markers / stretch: throw at a high target
- **Materials:** beanbags, foam javelins, cones; safety: all throw from one line, collect only on command
- **Joy:** Distance markers with names.

### Lesson 18 — Target games
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can choose underarm or overarm throws to hit different targets and score points." (UK NC KS1 PE GA; SHAPE S2)
- **Hook:** "Target alley: near, far, high, low. Which throw for which?"
- **Key activity:** Stations with targets; children choose throw types and explain; team scoring.
- **Check for understanding:** "Which throw is best for a far target, and which for a close, low one?" → Overarm for far; underarm for close and low.
- **Differentiation:** support: near targets / stretch: moving targets (a rolling hoop)
- **Materials:** hoops, buckets, targets, beanbags; safety: throwing lanes
- **Joy:** Target alley scoring.

### Lesson 19 — Partner and small-group ball games
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can play a small-sided passing game, sending and receiving the ball accurately." (UK NC KS1 PE GA)
- **Hook:** "How many passes can your group make without dropping it?"
- **Key activity:** Passing circles; "keep it off the floor" counts; simple "pass and move" in threes.
- **Check for understanding:** "What helps your partner catch your pass?" → Aiming at their chest/hands and throwing gently.
- **Differentiation:** support: bounce passes / stretch: pass and move to a new space
- **Materials:** balls, cones; safety: spread groups apart
- **Joy:** Pass-count record.

### Lesson 20 — Unit check: ball skills stations
- **Duration:** 30 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can roll, throw underarm and overarm, catch, bounce, kick and dribble with control." (UK NC KS1 PE FMS — assessment)
- **Hook:** "Ball skills Olympics!"
- **Key activity:** Stations observed by an adult with a skill checklist; children set a personal target.
- **Check for understanding:** "Show me ready hands and explain why they help." → Hands out, fingers spread — ready to catch and pull in.
- **Differentiation:** support: large balls / stretch: smaller balls and greater distance
- **Materials:** full ball-skills kit, checklist; safety: station spacing
- **Joy:** Medals.

**End-of-unit check:** Adult observes the child roll to a target, throw underarm and overarm with opposite-foot step, catch a gently thrown ball with soft hands, bounce-dribble while walking and pass a ball with the inside of the foot.

## Unit 3 — Health 1: my healthy body

**Essential question:** What does my body need to be healthy and strong?

**Key vocabulary:** healthy, exercise, active, energy, balanced diet, fruit, vegetables, water, sugar, sleep, rest, hygiene, germs, teeth, sun safety

### Lesson 21 — Active every day
- **Duration:** 20 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can name ways to be active every day and explain why at least an hour of activity a day is good for children." (UK Health Ed PH; SHAPE S3)
- **Hook:** "How many ways did you move yesterday? Let's count!"
- **Key activity:** Activity diary for yesterday; brainstorm fun daily activities (walking to school, dancing, playing); a 5-minute "activity break" routine to use in class.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why is it important to be active every day, not just once a week?" → Our bodies stay strong, our hearts healthy and we feel happier when we move often.
- **Differentiation:** support: picture diary / stretch: plan an active weekend
- **Materials:** diary sheets, music for the activity break; safety: space to move
- **Joy:** The class activity-break dance.

### Lesson 22 — Food for energy
- **Duration:** 20 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can explain that food gives us energy to move and grow and choose foods for a healthy snack." (UK Health Ed HE; link: Science)
- **Hook:** "A car needs fuel. What's your fuel?"
- **Key activity:** Sort snack cards into everyday and sometimes foods; build a healthy snack plate; move-and-sort relay.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why are sugary drinks a 'sometimes' drink?" → Lots of sugar is bad for teeth and doesn't keep us full or give lasting energy.
- **Differentiation:** support: picture sort / stretch: plan a snack from a different country
- **Materials:** food cards, paper plates; safety: check allergies if tasting
- **Joy:** Snack-sort relay race.

### Lesson 23 — Water and staying hydrated
- **Duration:** 20 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can explain why we need to drink water, especially when exercising or when it's hot." (UK Health Ed HE/PH)
- **Hook:** "Your body is more than half water! What happens if you don't drink enough?"
- **Key activity:** Discuss thirst, tiredness and headaches; water bottle routine; a short active game with drink breaks.
- **Check for understanding:** "When should you drink more water?" → When it's hot and when I've been exercising.
- **Differentiation:** support: picture cues / stretch: plan a water schedule for sports day
- **Materials:** water bottles, cups; safety: supervised drinks breaks
- **Joy:** Decorate your water bottle label.

### Lesson 24 — Sleep and rest
- **Duration:** 20 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can explain why sleep matters and describe a calm bedtime routine." (UK Health Ed SL)
- **Hook:** "What do you think your brain does while you sleep?"
- **Key activity:** Discuss that children aged 6–7 need roughly 9–12 hours' sleep; build a calm bedtime routine strip (bath, teeth, story, lights off, no screens before bed); relaxation breathing.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why is it better to stop using screens a while before bed?" → Screens keep our brains awake and make it harder to fall asleep.
- **Differentiation:** support: picture routine cards / stretch: plan a routine for a younger sibling
- **Materials:** routine cards, calm music
- **Joy:** "Floppy starfish" relaxation.

### Lesson 25 — Germs, handwashing and teeth
- **Duration:** 20 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can show how to wash my hands properly and brush my teeth for two minutes, and explain why." (UK Health Ed HY; link: Science)
- **Hook:** "Invisible germs everywhere! How do we get rid of them?"
- **Key activity:** Handwashing steps with a song; tooth-brushing demonstration on a model; 2-minute timer game.
- **Check for understanding:** "You've just stroked a friend's dog and now it's snack time. What should you do first, and why?" → Wash my hands with soap — germs from the dog's fur can get onto my food and into my mouth.
- **Differentiation:** support: step picture cards / stretch: design a hygiene poster
- **Materials:** soap, water, model teeth and toothbrush, sand timer
- **Joy:** Handwashing song.

### Lesson 26 — Sun safety and unit check
- **Duration:** 20 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can explain how to stay safe in the sun and summarise what my body needs to be healthy." (UK Health Ed HY — assessment)
- **Hook:** "Slip, slop, slap, seek, slide! Where does that come from?" (Australia's sun-safety campaign)
- **Key activity:** Learn slip on a shirt, slop on sunscreen, slap on a hat, seek shade, slide on sunglasses; then a quick active quiz: run to the right answer on health questions from the unit.
- **Check for understanding:** "Name three things your body needs every day to be healthy." → e.g. activity, healthy food and water, sleep (and hygiene).
- **Differentiation:** support: picture answers / stretch: explain one habit to a younger child
- **Materials:** hat, sunglasses, shirt, sunscreen bottle (empty), quiz cards; safety: shade and water for outdoor quiz
- **Joy:** Sun-safety dress-up race.

**End-of-unit check:** The child explains in their own words how activity, food and water, sleep and hygiene each keep them healthy, and demonstrates correct handwashing.

## Unit 4 — Dance around the world

**Essential question:** How can I use my body to express ideas and tell stories through dance?

**Key vocabulary:** dance, move, rhythm, beat, level (high, medium, low), direction, speed, shape, mirror, unison, canon, sequence, phrase, perform, audience, character

### Lesson 27 — Moving to the beat
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can move in time to the beat of music, changing action when the music changes." (UK NC KS1 PE DA; link: Music pulse)
- **Hook:** "Can your feet hear the beat?"
- **Key activity:** Warm-up to music; step, clap and march on the beat; freeze when the music stops; change action on a new section.
- **Check for understanding:** "How did you know when to change your movement?" → The music changed (tempo or section).
- **Differentiation:** support: copy a leader / stretch: invent a move that fits the beat
- **Materials:** music player, varied tracks; safety: clear space, bare feet or dance shoes on safe floors
- **Joy:** Freeze dance.

### Lesson 28 — Levels, shapes and directions
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can make shapes and moves at high, medium and low levels and travel in different directions." (UK NC KS1 PE DA)
- **Hook:** "Be a tall tree, a rolling rock, a spiky cactus!"
- **Key activity:** Explore levels and shapes with picture cards; travel forwards, backwards and sideways; combine into a 3-move phrase.
- **Check for understanding:** "Show me a low shape and a high shape. What's different?" → Low is near the floor; high reaches up tall.
- **Differentiation:** support: copy card shapes / stretch: link three shapes smoothly
- **Materials:** shape and level cards, music; safety: space awareness
- **Joy:** Shape-card game.

### Lesson 29 — Mirror and unison
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can mirror a partner and move in unison with a group." (UK NC KS1 PE DA)
- **Hook:** "Are you my reflection? Prove it!"
- **Key activity:** Mirror pairs (slow movements), then small groups moving in unison (same time) to a short phrase.
- **Check for understanding:** "Your group danced in unison except one person who was one move behind. What would the audience notice, and how could you fix it?" → They'd see one dancer out of step, because unison means everyone does the same move at the same time; we could count out loud together or watch a leader.
- **Differentiation:** support: slow simple moves / stretch: switch leader without stopping
- **Materials:** slow music; safety: space between pairs
- **Joy:** Mirror game.

### Lesson 30 — Dance from Ghana: Adowa-inspired movement
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can learn and perform a short movement phrase inspired by a Ghanaian dance, keeping to the drum beat." (UK NC KS1 PE DA; link: Music Unit 4, Art Unit 4)
- **Hook:** Watch a short clip of Adowa dance from Ghana: "What do the dancers' hands and feet do?"
- **Key activity:** Learn simple stepping and gesture moves to a drum rhythm (respecting that Adowa is a traditional Akan dance — we're inspired by it); perform in lines.
- **Check for understanding:** "What keeps all the dancers together?" → The drum beat.
- **Differentiation:** support: feet only / stretch: add hand gestures
- **Materials:** drum or recording, video clip; safety: bare feet on clean floor
- **Joy:** Dancing with live drumming.

### Lesson 31 — Dance from India: telling a story with hands
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can use expressive hand gestures and faces to show a character or feeling, inspired by Indian classical dance." (UK NC KS1 PE DA)
- **Hook:** "In Bharatanatyam, dancers tell whole stories with their hands and eyes. Can you show a bird? A flower?"
- **Key activity:** Learn 3–4 simple hand gestures (e.g. bird, flower, fish) and facial expressions; combine into a mini story; perform to music.
- **Check for understanding:** "How can a dancer tell a story without words?" → With gestures, faces and movements that show what is happening.
- **Differentiation:** support: two gestures / stretch: a 4-gesture story
- **Materials:** Indian classical music, picture cards of gestures; safety: space
- **Joy:** Silent story dance.

### Lesson 32 — Creating a dance story
- **Duration:** 25 min

- **Key activity:** Circuit (wall push-ups, jogging, stretches, balances) in 30-second bursts; discuss which quality each builds.
- **Check for understanding:** "Which activity built your stamina, and how did you know?" → Jogging — I kept going and my heart and breathing were fast.
- **Differentiation:** support: shorter bursts / stretch: longer bursts and explain each quality
- **Materials:** circuit cards, timer, mats; safety: water, rest, inhalers available
- **Joy:** Circuit music.

### Lesson 68 — Personal-best day
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can compare my skills now with the start of the year and set a goal for next year." (UK NC KS1 PE — evaluate; SHAPE S4)
- **Hook:** "Remember your jump in September? Let's see how far you go now!"
- **Key activity:** Repeat baseline tasks (standing long jump, balance time, catches in a row); compare; set a goal.
- **Check for understanding:** "Which skill improved most, and what helped it improve?" → A named skill and practice reason.
- **Differentiation:** support: 2 tasks / stretch: help record others' results
- **Materials:** September results, tape measures, balls, stopwatch
- **Joy:** "Then and now" results.

### Lesson 69 — Healthy habits review
- **Duration:** 20 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can explain my healthy habits for body and mind and choose one to keep over the holidays." (UK Health Ed — review)
- **Hook:** "Healthy-habit bingo!"
- **Key activity:** Active bingo (run to answers) covering activity, food, water, sleep, hygiene, feelings and safety; choose a holiday habit goal.
- **Check for understanding:** "Name one habit for your body and one for your feelings." → e.g. drink water; take deep breaths when upset.
- **Differentiation:** support: picture bingo / stretch: explain why each habit matters
- **Materials:** bingo cards, habit goal cards

### Lesson 70 — Health and fitness festival
- **Duration:** 30 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can show my favourite skills from the year at a festival and encourage others." (UK NC KS1 PE — end-of-year review)
- **Hook:** "Families invited: the Year 1 Health and Fitness Festival!"

## Teacher guidance

**Delivery.** PE is adult-led and active — the tutor is a planning and explanation aid, not a screen activity. Aim for children moving at least 70% of lesson time. Use short, clear demonstrations with one teaching point at a time, then lots of practice and specific feedback ("great bent knees on that landing!").

**Safety.** Before every lesson: check the space and equipment, clothing and footwear, jewellery removed, hair tied back, water available, medical needs known. Teach children to carry equipment safely. Only teach forward rolls, partner balances or swimming with appropriately qualified adults.

**Health education sensitivity.** Body-safety lessons follow the school's safeguarding policy; tell families beforehand and have the safeguarding lead available. Talk about food without labelling foods or bodies as "good" or "bad" — use "everyday" and "sometimes" foods.

**Inclusion.** Adapt every activity (larger or softer balls, shorter distances, seated options) so every child can succeed and be challenged. Celebrate effort and improvement, not just winning.

**Vertical alignment.** This year secures fundamental movement skills and health knowledge (activity, food, water, sleep, hygiene, feelings, body safety with PANTS, road safety, emergency calls, water safety). Year 2 (ages 7–8) develops them into invasion, net and striking games with more tactics, longer dance and gymnastics sequences, swimming towards independent water competence, and health lessons that go deeper (energy release, sugar in drinks, sleep data, activity intensity, consent in everyday situations, first aid) rather than repeating this year's.
