# Lesson 2 — Light hands: build-up lines
**Art & Design · Year 1 · Unit 1 (Sketchbook habits & line revisited)** · 30 min · **Delivery: 🤖 AI-led, with a hands-on drawing beat**
**Objective:** "By the end, I can draw lightly first and darken only the lines I want to keep."
*(Human view of [`lesson-02-light-hands-build-up-lines.json`](./lesson-02-light-hands-build-up-lines.json) — the JSON is the source of truth.)*

**Have ready:** sketchbook or plain paper, a soft pencil, a fine-liner (or dark pencil) for keeper lines, and one chunky simple object to draw (a cup, mug, glue stick or box). An eraser is optional and used sparingly.

## Flow

- **Hook (0–3):** the artist trick of **ghost lines** — why would an artist draw a line they might not keep?
- **Explain (3–8):** a light line is made with a gentle hand; **build-up** means several light tries until the shape looks right; then darken only the best edge.
- **Example (8–13):** the AI models a cup: the big shape first in soft wobbly tries, then only the describing lines darkened. The pale lines helped find the shape.
- **Check (13–16):** "Why do artists draw lightly first?" — trap handled: "pressing hard makes a better drawing" (it makes changes harder).
- **Practice — hands-on (16–26):** the child draws a real object with ghost lines ("tickle the page"), then goes over only the keeper lines in fine-liner, leaving some ghost lines showing.
- **Recap (26–30):** try lightly, look again, then commit — ghost lines show your artist brain working.

**Adaptivity:** 12-item bank (purpose of light lines, order of steps, "build-up" vocabulary, which line to darken, overlapping objects, self-check sentence) · start level 2 · choosing the fine-liner too early → level 1 with a step-by-step re-teach.

**Differentiation:** support — very soft pencil, chunky object, a warm-up page of light lines, adult taps a slow rhythm · stretch — two overlapping objects, darkening only the visible edges.
**Evidence (assessed by looking and talking):** a drawing with pale build-up lines and a darker chosen outline, and the child explains that drawing lightly lets you fix the shape before committing.
