# Lesson 1 — My sketchbook is my workshop
**Art & Design · Year 1 · Unit 1 (Sketchbook habits & line revisited)** · 25 min · **Delivery: 🤖 AI-led, with 🧑‍🏫 a grown-up for the making**
**Objective:** "By the end, I can set up and date a sketchbook page and explain it is for testing, not for perfect pictures."
*(Human view of [`lesson-01-my-sketchbook-is-my-workshop.json`](./lesson-01-my-sketchbook-is-my-workshop.json) — the JSON is the source of truth.)*

**Lay out before the lesson:** a sketchbook (or a few sheets stapled/folded together), pencil, a fine-liner or felt-tip if you have one, crayons or coloured pencils. For a child who needs support, pre-draw a small date box and a 6-square grid. **No rubber today.**

## Flow

- **Hook (0–3):** "Workshop, not gallery" — a slideshow of a real sketchbook's insides: crossings-out, scribbles, tests and notes are *useful*. Today's page does not need to be neat.
- **Explain (3–7):** the one new idea — a sketchbook is where artists **test marks, try ideas and record them, with the date** so they can see themselves improve. Whiteboard: sketchbook → test marks / try ideas / record date.
- **Example (7–11):** the tutor models setting up a first test page: date box, a 6-box warm-up strip (dots, zig-zags, loops, scratches, soft lines, dark lines), and the rule "no rubbing out — a surprising mark is evidence of experimenting".
- **Check (11–14):** "What is a sketchbook really for?" → testing, experimenting and recording ideas. Wrong-answer playbook: "only perfect pictures" (gallery thinking → "sketchbooks help artists get better by trying things first"), "keep pages clean", "one kind of line".
- **Practice — your part (14–22):** the child dates the page and fills it with as many different marks as they can, changing **pressure, speed and tool**. You hand over materials and keep the no-rubbing-out rule warm ("Keep it — artists need evidence of the experiment"). Stuck? Offer the six starter marks. Early finisher: the same mark light, medium and dark. **Hand back when the page is full (≈8 min) by saying "We're ready to recap."**
- **Recap (22–25):** the assessment is the **page itself** — the child holds it up, points to two different marks and says *how* each was made ("pressed hard and went fast for a dark zig-zag; tapped for dots"), then says what a sketchbook is for. Playbook: names marks but not how → one follow-up ("hard or soft? fast or slow?"); "it's messy" → "useful messy — that's a workshop page"; same mark twice → find (or add) a different one.

**Practice bank (12 items, AI):** level 1 — purpose MC, date fillBlank, "rub out strange marks?" true/false, word match; level 2 — what belongs on a test page, setup order, sketchbook-vs-finished-art sort, 6-box count; level 3 — explain in a sentence with *experiment/test*, write an "I tried…" note, choose mark variety, respond to a messy artist page.

**Adaptivity:** start level 2 · two confident level-2 answers → level 3 (explain, choose variety, artist note) · a miss or any worry about neatness → level 1 reassurance items, then back up · mastery = sets the page up in order, dates it, explains testing-not-perfect, and names at least one mark-making test.

**Video:** none — every minute is the child making marks.

**Differentiation:** support — pre-drawn date box + 6-square grid, six named mark prompts, adult scribes the date · stretch — a short written note beside one mark ("pressed hard", "crayon sideways").
**Evidence:** a dated page of several genuinely different marks, and the child describes two of them with how they were made and says a sketchbook is for testing, not perfect pictures.
**Revisit:** next lesson opens on this page — which mark could be built up, outlined or given detail?
