> Part of **the best international primary school** (see [`../../../SCHOOL-CHARTER.md`](../../../SCHOOL-CHARTER.md)).
> Year 1 scope drawn from [`knowledge-base/subjects/art-music/art-ks1-ks2.md`](../../../knowledge-base/subjects/art-music/art-ks1-ks2.md)
> (topics 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 8, 9 — the KS1 / early-KS2 portion). Every lesson honours the 8 design
> principles in [`../README.md`](../README.md).

## Year overview

Year 1 art is a hands-on exploration of the **formal elements** — **line, shape, colour, texture
and pattern** — built up one at a time so that by the end of the year a child has a "toolkit" of
marks, mixes and making methods. The spine runs: first we learn that everything is made of **lines**,
then we discover **colour** (primary → secondary, warm → cool) and learn to **control paint**; then we
bring in real **texture** through collage and **pattern** through printing; and we end by squeezing 3-D
**form** out of dough and junk. Woven through every unit is an **artist focus** — we look at a real
artist or maker (Bridget Riley, Kandinsky, Van Gogh, Romare Bearden, Eric Carle, Yayoi Kusama, Henri
Matisse, Hokusai), describe their work with our new vocabulary, and make something *inspired by* them.
A running **sketchbook** collects every experiment; the rule for every lesson is **make → look →
improve**. Most lessons run 25–35 min; making lessons reach 35–40 min.

> **Continues into Year 2.** Painting (tints/shades), printmaking, collage and sculpture all deepen in
> Year 2, and observational drawing + tone/shading begin properly in lower KS2. Where Year 1 plants a
> seed that Year 2 grows, it is flagged in the lesson.

## Unit 1 — Drawing & line

**Essential question:** What is a line, and how many different ones can I make?
**Key vocabulary:** line, mark, straight, curved, wavy, zig-zag, spiral, dotted, looped, thick, thin, press hard / press soft, sketchbook.

### Lesson 1 — A line goes for a walk
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can make at least four different kinds of line on purpose."
- **Hook:** A line is just a dot that went for a walk — where shall ours go today?
- **Key activity:** Demo straight, wavy, zig-zag, spiral on big paper → children "walk" their pencil to copy each → free explore page filling the paper with as many different lines as they can.
- **Check for understanding:** "Show me three different kinds of line." → child draws any three (e.g. straight, wavy, zig-zag).
- **Differentiation:** support: trace over faint dotted lines drawn for them / stretch: invent and name a brand-new line ("bouncy line", "sneaky line").
- **Materials:** large paper, pencils, crayons, sketchbook.
- **Joy:** filling a whole page with wild scribbly line-creatures, no "wrong" allowed.

### Lesson 2 — Loud lines and whisper lines
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can make a line thick and dark or thin and pale by changing how hard I press."
- **Hook:** Can a pencil shout? Can it whisper?
- **Key activity:** Demo pressing hard ("loud") for a thick dark line and softly ("whisper") for a thin pale one → children make a "loud vs whisper" pair → draw the same simple shape twice, once loud, once whisper.
- **Check for understanding:** "How do you make a line thicker and darker?" → press harder.
- **Differentiation:** support: practise on scrap with hand-over-hand pressure / stretch: shade one object from loud (dark) on one side to whisper (pale) on the other.
- **Materials:** soft pencils (2B), paper, sketchbook.
- **Joy:** discovering they can control the pencil's "voice".

### Lesson 3 — A line zoo (drawing with line only)
- **Duration:** 35 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can draw a whole animal using only different lines."
- **Hook:** Today we build a zoo where every animal is made of nothing but lines.
- **Key activity:** Worked example — a snail: slow **spiral** shell, smooth **curved** body, two straight lines + dots for antennae → children pick an animal and build it from named lines → label the lines used.
- **Check for understanding:** "Which line did you use for the shell / the body?" → child names spiral / curved (or their choices).
- **Differentiation:** support: offer a small "line menu" picture to choose from / stretch: add a line pattern (stripes, spots) on the animal.
- **Materials:** pencils, fine-liners, paper, sketchbook.
- **Joy:** realising the same pencil can make a whole creature.

### Lesson 4 — Lines that feel something (Bridget Riley)
- **Duration:** 30 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can say whether a line feels calm or busy and make both."
- **Hook:** Some lines feel calm and sleepy; some feel fast and dizzy — meet an artist who makes your eyes wobble.
- **Key activity:** Look at **Bridget Riley**'s wavy black-and-white line paintings → talk: do the lines feel calm or busy? → children fill one strip with slow calm curves and one with sharp busy zig-zags, then a Riley-style page of repeating wavy lines.
- **Check for understanding:** "Which line feels calm — a smooth curve or a sharp zig-zag?" → the smooth curve.
- **Differentiation:** support: pre-ruled guide lines to wave between / stretch: make the waves get closer together so the page "moves".
- **Materials:** black felt-tip, white paper, sketchbook; Bridget Riley image.
- **Joy:** an op-art page that seems to shimmer.

> **Continues into Year 2:** observational line drawing from real objects (KB topic 7) begins in lower KS2.

**End-of-unit check:** Child fills a 6-box sketchbook strip with six different named lines (straight, wavy, zig-zag, spiral, dotted, looped) and points to a "loud" line and a "whisper" line.

## Unit 2 — Colour & painting

**Essential question:** Where do new colours come from, and how do I control paint?
**Key vocabulary:** colour, primary (red, yellow, blue), secondary (orange, green, purple), mix, colour wheel, warm, cool, tint, lighter, brush, rinse.

### Lesson 5 — The three colours you can't mix (primaries)
- **Duration:** 30 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can name the three primary colours and explain you can't mix them."
- **Hook:** Three magic colours are the starting point for ALL the others — but you can never make them yourself.
- **Key activity:** Introduce **red, yellow, blue** as primaries → children paint three big bold primary blobs and try (and fail) to mix red from other colours → name them aloud.
- **Check for understanding:** "Name the three primary colours." → red, yellow, blue.
- **Differentiation:** support: pre-labelled pots / stretch: hunt the room for one object of each primary.
- **Materials:** red/yellow/blue paint, big brushes, paper, sketchbook.
- **Joy:** big confident blobs of pure colour.

### Lesson 6 — Mixing magic: making secondary colours
- **Duration:** 35 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can mix two primaries to make orange, green and purple."
- **Hook:** What happens when two magic colours meet on your brush?
- **Key activity:** Worked example — blue + yellow dragged together → **green**; predict and test red+yellow and red+blue → children make all three secondaries, rinsing between mixes.
- **Check for understanding:** "What two colours make green?" → blue and yellow.
- **Differentiation:** support: pre-blobbed pairs to swirl together / stretch: make a lighter and a deeper green by changing how much yellow.
- **Materials:** primary paints, brushes, water pots, paper, sketchbook.
- **Joy:** the gasp when two colours turn into a third.

### Lesson 7 — Building the colour wheel
- **Duration:** 35 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can make and label a colour wheel of primaries and secondaries."
- **Hook:** Let's arrange all six colours into a wheel — like a rainbow bent into a circle.
- **Key activity:** Paint 3 primaries in a triangle around a circle → mix and add each secondary between its two parents → label.
- **Check for understanding:** Point to two colours that mixed to make the one between them → child names the pair (e.g. red + blue → purple).
- **Differentiation:** support: pre-drawn wheel outline with positions marked / stretch: add the colour wheel to the sketchbook with arrows showing the mixes.
- **Materials:** paint, brushes, circle template, paper, sketchbook.
- **Joy:** a finished rainbow wheel they made and understand.

### Lesson 8 — Warm fire, cool ice
- **Duration:** 30 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can sort colours into warm and cool and paint a warm and a cool picture."
- **Hook:** Some colours feel hot like fire; some feel cold like ice — which is which?
- **Key activity:** Sort the wheel into warm (red/orange/yellow) and cool (blue/green/purple) → children paint a tiny "fire" picture (warm) and an "ice" picture (cool).
- **Check for understanding:** "Is blue a warm or cool colour?" → cool.
- **Differentiation:** support: warm/cool colour cards to copy / stretch: paint one picture (e.g. a sunset over sea) using both halves.
- **Materials:** paint, brushes, paper, sketchbook.
- **Joy:** the contrast of a glowing fire next to an icy scene.

### Lesson 9 — Controlling paint: lighter with white (tints)
- **Duration:** 35 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can make a colour lighter by adding white."
- **Hook:** How do you turn red into pink? There's a secret ingredient.
- **Key activity:** Demo red + a little white → pink, more white → paler pink → children make a "tints ladder" of one colour from bright to palest. (Painting control: big brush fills, less water = stronger colour.)
- **Check for understanding:** "How do you make a colour lighter?" → add white.
- **Differentiation:** support: 3-step ladder / stretch: 5-step ladder and name the palest tint.
- **Materials:** paint inc. white, brushes, paper, sketchbook.
- **Joy:** watching a colour get gradually softer step by step.

### Lesson 10 — Painting music (Kandinsky)
- **Duration:** 35 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can use bright shapes and colours to make a Kandinsky-style picture."
- **Hook:** This artist said he could *hear* colours — let's paint music.
- **Key activity:** Look at **Wassily Kandinsky**'s *Concentric Circles* (rings of colour, bold shapes) → children divide a page into squares and fill each with rings of different colours, choosing warm or cool feelings.
- **Check for understanding:** "Name an artist and one thing they painted." → Kandinsky — circles / colourful shapes.
- **Differentiation:** support: pre-drawn squares to fill / stretch: play music and choose colours that match its mood.
- **Materials:** paint or felt-tips, paper, sketchbook; Kandinsky image.
- **Joy:** painting to music with no rules but colour.

> **Continues into Year 2:** *shades* (adding black to darken) and full tints-and-shades control (KB topic 3) are a Year 2 focus.

**End-of-unit check:** Child mixes and names all three secondary colours from primaries, sorts a set of colours into warm/cool, and makes one colour lighter by adding white — all recorded on a sketchbook page.

## Unit 3 — Texture & collage

**Essential question:** How can I build a picture from real materials I can feel?
**Key vocabulary:** collage, stick, glue, tear, cut, texture, rough, smooth, soft, shape, overlap, arrange.

### Lesson 11 — Texture hunt: rough, smooth, soft
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can describe a texture as rough, smooth or soft and sort materials."
- **Hook:** Close your eyes — can you tell what you're touching just by how it feels?
- **Key activity:** Feely-bag of materials (sandpaper, foil, cotton wool, bubble wrap, fabric) → children feel and describe → sort scraps into rough / smooth / soft trays → stick a small "texture sample" strip in the sketchbook.
- **Check for understanding:** "Is sandpaper rough or smooth?" → rough.
- **Differentiation:** support: two categories only (rough/soft) / stretch: find one more texture word (bumpy, fluffy, shiny).
- **Materials:** assorted textured materials, glue, sketchbook.
- **Joy:** the feely-bag guessing game.

### Lesson 12 — Torn-paper picture (collage)
- **Duration:** 35 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can make a picture by tearing and sticking paper shapes."
- **Hook:** No scissors needed today — we make pictures with our fingers by tearing.
- **Key activity:** Worked example — a tree: tear a brown trunk strip, tear many green leaf bits, overlap and glue in a clump → children make their own torn-paper subject; **arrange first, glue last**, thin glue.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why arrange the pieces before gluing?" → so you can change the layout while it's loose.
- **Differentiation:** support: pre-torn pieces to arrange / stretch: mix torn AND cut edges for different effects.
- **Materials:** coloured paper, glue sticks, backing paper, sketchbook.
- **Joy:** ripping paper freely (allowed!) into a real picture.

### Lesson 13 — Texture collage creature (Romare Bearden)
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can use at least three different materials in one collage and name each texture."
- **Hook:** This artist built whole worlds out of glued-down scraps — let's build a creature his way.
- **Key activity:** Look at **Romare Bearden**'s collages (pieced-together figures and scenes) → children design one animal/creature using at least three textures (rough, smooth, soft), arranging then gluing → name each texture.
- **Check for understanding:** "Which formal element does collage really bring out?" → texture.
- **Differentiation:** support: outline shape to fill with materials / stretch: add a textured background.
- **Materials:** fabric, foil, sandpaper, cotton wool, paper, glue, sketchbook; Bearden image.
- **Joy:** a creature you can actually touch and feel.

### Lesson 14 — Caterpillar in the style of Eric Carle
- **Duration:** 35 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can make painted-paper collage in the style of a picture-book artist."
- **Hook:** The Very Hungry Caterpillar isn't drawn — it's collage! Let's make our own.
- **Key activity:** Show **Eric Carle**'s painted-tissue collage → (using pre-painted textured paper from Unit 2) children tear/cut circles and shapes and glue overlapping pieces to build a caterpillar or bug.
- **Check for understanding:** "What is collage?" → making a picture by sticking pieces of material onto a surface.
- **Differentiation:** support: round body templates / stretch: add legs, antennae and a patterned background.
- **Materials:** painted papers, scissors, glue, sketchbook; Eric Carle book.
- **Joy:** recognising a beloved book and making it themselves.

> **Continues into Year 2:** layered collage with more planning and mixed media (KB topic 5) deepens in Year 2.

**End-of-unit check:** Child makes a collage of one subject using at least three materials and correctly describes each as rough, smooth or soft.

## Unit 4 — Pattern & printmaking

**Essential question:** What is a pattern, and how can I print one again and again?
**Key vocabulary:** pattern, repeat, rule, ABAB, alternate, rotate, print, stamp, press, block, row.

### Lesson 15 — Spotting and continuing patterns
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can say what makes a pattern and continue an ABAB pattern."
- **Hook:** Patterns are everywhere — on jumpers, tiles, even zebras. Can you guess what comes next?
- **Key activity:** Pattern hunt (clothes, room, nature pictures) → introduce **ABAB** as a rule you can predict → children continue given patterns then draw their own ABAB strip, saying the rule aloud.
- **Check for understanding:** "Continue: red, blue, red, blue, ___." → red.
- **Differentiation:** support: physical objects (counters) to lay out / stretch: invent an ABBA pattern.
- **Materials:** pattern picture cards, crayons, paper, sketchbook.
- **Joy:** cracking the "what comes next" puzzle.

### Lesson 16 — Stamp it again! (first prints)
- **Duration:** 35 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can make repeated prints of one shape by stamping."
- **Hook:** What's the difference between drawing a star ten times and stamping it ten times?
- **Key activity:** Demo printing with a found object (sponge / halved potato / cork): thin paint, **press straight down, lift straight up** → children stamp a neat row of the same shape, then a second row.
- **Check for understanding:** "What makes printing different from drawing?" → you repeat the same shape by stamping it.
- **Differentiation:** support: chunky easy-grip stampers / stretch: alternate two stamps in an ABAB print.
- **Materials:** sponges/potatoes/corks, paint in shallow trays, paper, sketchbook.
- **Joy:** the satisfying *thunk* of a clean stamp.

### Lesson 17 — Nature prints: leaves and texture
- **Duration:** 35 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can print the texture and veins of a natural object."
- **Hook:** A leaf can leave its fingerprint — let's catch it.
- **Key activity:** Paint the bumpy underside of a leaf → press firmly, peel → veins appear → children build a leaf-print border, turning the leaf each time. Discuss: too much paint blobs; thin layer keeps detail.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why use just a thin layer of paint?" → so the print stays clear and detailed.
- **Differentiation:** support: large flat leaves / stretch: overlap two leaf colours.
- **Materials:** leaves, paint, brushes, paper, sketchbook.
- **Joy:** the surprise of hidden veins printing out.

### Lesson 18 — Rotating-stamp pattern & The Great Wave (Hokusai)
- **Duration:** 35 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can make a repeating print pattern using a rule, and spot pattern in a famous print."
- **Hook:** Long ago an artist carved a giant wave to print over and over — let's look, then print our own pattern.
- **Key activity:** Look at **Katsushika Hokusai**'s *The Great Wave* — bold line, flat colour, foam pattern → children make a repeating print pattern with one stamp, **rotating it each time**, and state their rule.
- **Check for understanding:** "Describe the Great Wave using a formal element." → e.g. curved lines / pattern in the foam / flat colour.
- **Differentiation:** support: grid to stamp into / stretch: combine two rules (rotate + alternate colour).
- **Materials:** stamps, paint, paper, sketchbook; Hokusai image.
- **Joy:** seeing how a 200-year-old print connects to their own stamping.

### Lesson 19 — Polka-dot patterns (Yayoi Kusama)
- **Duration:** 30 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can cover a surface in a repeating dot pattern."
- **Hook:** One artist loves dots so much she covers whole rooms in them — let's go dotty.
- **Key activity:** Look at **Yayoi Kusama**'s polka dots → children fingerprint/stamp a surface or shape (a pumpkin, a flower) all over with a dot pattern, choosing a colour rule.
- **Check for understanding:** "What makes something a pattern?" → it repeats in a predictable way (follows a rule).
- **Differentiation:** support: big dots, big shape / stretch: vary dot size in a planned way.
- **Materials:** paint, fingers/cotton buds/corks, paper, sketchbook; Kusama image.
- **Joy:** the joyful freedom of covering everything in dots.

> **Continues into Year 2:** designing repeating wallpaper/tile patterns and multi-block printing (KB topics 4 & 9) extend in Year 2.

**End-of-unit check:** Child prints a repeating pattern from one block, states the rule (e.g. ABAB or rotate-each-time), and names a place patterns appear in the real world.

## Unit 5 — Form & 3-D making

**Essential question:** How is something solid I can hold different from something flat I draw?
**Key vocabulary:** flat (2-D), solid (3-D), shape, form, sphere, cube, cylinder, roll, squeeze, mould, join, build up.

### Lesson 20 — Flat shape vs solid form
- **Duration:** 30 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can tell the difference between a flat shape and a solid form."
- **Hook:** A drawing of a ball is flat — but a real ball you can hold all the way round. What's the difference?
- **Key activity:** Compare a drawn circle with a real ball → introduce **form**: ball = sphere, box = cube, tin = cylinder → children find and name forms around the room and roll dough into a sphere, cube-ish block, cylinder.
- **Check for understanding:** "What's the difference between a shape and a form?" → shape is flat (2-D), form is solid (3-D).
- **Differentiation:** support: match real objects to form words / stretch: find two of each form in the room.
- **Materials:** play-dough/clay, real objects (ball, box, tin), sketchbook.
- **Joy:** squishing dough into solid shapes you can turn around.

### Lesson 21 — Dough sculpture: a creature in the round
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can mould a small 3-D animal that looks good from all sides."
- **Hook:** Today you're a sculptor — your animal must look great from the front, the side AND the back.
- **Key activity:** Worked example — a snail: roll a fat sausage body, coil a thin sausage into a spiral shell, press on, add eye-stalks → children mould their own creature by rolling and squeezing, turning it as they work.
- **Check for understanding:** "Name two ways to make a sculpture." → moulding (clay/dough) and building up (junk modelling).
- **Differentiation:** support: make a simpler one-lump animal / stretch: add texture (scales, spots) by pressing tools in.
- **Materials:** play-dough/clay, modelling tools, sketchbook.
- **Joy:** making a little creature that stands up on the table.

### Lesson 22 — Junk-model build & Matisse cut-out finish
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can build a 3-D model by joining materials, and decorate with cut-out shapes."
- **Hook:** Today's treasure is the recycling box — what could a cereal box become?
- **Key activity:** Build by **joining** boxes/tubes (tape, glue) into a robot, rocket or animal → check joins are secure → look at **Henri Matisse**'s paper cut-outs and decorate the model with simple bright cut-out shapes.
- **Check for understanding:** "What is collage / cut-out?" and "Why secure the joins?" → sticking shapes; so pieces don't fall off.
- **Differentiation:** support: pre-cut shapes and pre-taped base / stretch: plan the build first in the sketchbook.
- **Materials:** clean recycling, tape, glue, scissors, coloured paper, sketchbook; Matisse image.
- **Joy:** turning rubbish into a robot or rocket.

> **Continues into Year 2:** clay "score-and-slip" joining and larger sculpture (KB topic 6) develop in Year 2.

**End-of-unit check:** Child makes a 3-D model (moulded or built), names the forms used (sphere/cube/cylinder), and shows it looks finished from front, side and back.

## End-of-year outcomes

By the end of Year 1, a child's sketchbook/portfolio should show:

1. **A line collection** — six or more different named lines, with examples of thick/thin (loud/whisper) marks.
2. **A labelled colour wheel** plus evidence of mixing all three secondary colours from primaries.
3. **Warm and cool** sorting and at least one painting using each, plus a tints ladder (lighter with white).
4. **A texture collage** built from three or more materials, with each texture named (rough/smooth/soft).
5. **A repeating print pattern** from a found-object block, with the pattern rule stated (ABAB / rotate).
6. **A nature print** showing real texture (e.g. leaf veins).
7. **A 3-D model** (moulded creature or junk build) using the form words sphere/cube/cylinder.
8. **At least four "inspired-by" artist pieces**, with the child able to name the artist (e.g. Bridget Riley, Kandinsky, Van Gogh, Romare Bearden, Eric Carle, Hokusai, Yayoi Kusama, Matisse) and describe their work using a formal-element word.
