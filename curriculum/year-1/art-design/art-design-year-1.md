# Year 1 Art & Design (ages 6–7) — Scope & Sequence

> Part of **the best international primary school** (see [`../../../SCHOOL-CHARTER.md`](../../../SCHOOL-CHARTER.md)).
> Built to [`../../PLANNING-BRIEF.md`](../../PLANNING-BRIEF.md). Standards reference:
> `knowledge-base/subjects/art-music/art-ks1-ks2.md`. Codes: **UK NC KS1 Art** (MA = use materials creatively,
> DP = drawing, painting, sculpture to share ideas, TE = techniques in colour, pattern, texture, line, shape, form, space,
> AR = learn about the work of a range of artists, craft makers and designers).

## Year overview

Year 1 art turns playful mark-makers into **artists with habits**: they keep a sketchbook, try ideas before
committing, look closely at the work of real artists from around the world, and talk about their own and others'
work with art vocabulary. Every unit follows the same arc — **look at an artist → explore the technique in the
sketchbook → make a final piece → share and talk about it** — and every unit names artists or craft traditions
from different cultures and continents.

**What this year assumes (from Foundation, ages 5–6):** mark-making with loud and quiet lines; the primary and
secondary colours and a colour wheel; tints with white; torn-paper collage; simple stamp printing and patterns;
dough modelling and junk models; artists such as Kandinsky, Bridget Riley, Hokusai, Yayoi Kusama and Matisse.

**How the year runs.** *Unit 1* sets up sketchbook habits and revisits line (Van Gogh, Emily Kame Kngwarreye,
Quentin Blake). *Unit 2* extends colour to **shades**, colour moods and a first taste of digital painting (Monet,
Alma Thomas, Frida Kahlo). *Unit 3* builds layered, mixed-media collage (Romare Bearden, Jeannie Baker, Matisse's
cut-outs). *Unit 4* designs repeating patterns and prints them (Adinkra printing from Ghana, William Morris, Andy
Warhol). *Unit 5* introduces textiles — weaving and simple stitching (Kente cloth, Anni Albers, Faith Ringgold).
*Unit 6* works in clay — pinch pots, coils and joining — and ends in a class exhibition (Maria Martinez, Magdalene
Odundo, Barbara Hepworth).

**Assessment is by looking and talking**, never by quiz: sketchbook pages and final pieces are photographed, and
the child explains what they tried, what they changed and what they'd do next. The end-of-unit checks are short
"artist conversations".

**What Year 2 relies on:** a sketchbook habit; light-first drawing; mixing tints and shades; confidence with
collage, printing, weaving and clay; the language to describe and compare art. Year 2 (ages 7–8) moves to slow,
observational drawing, texture and first tone (shading a form), and a still life.

and printing lessons use the upper end.

## Unit 1 — Sketchbook habits & line revisited

**Essential question:** How does an artist use a sketchbook to get better?

**Key vocabulary:** sketchbook, date, test page, experiment, mark-making, line, light, dark, thick, thin, wavy, zig-zag, dotted, build-up lines, outline, detail, movement, illustrator

### Lesson 1 — My sketchbook is my workshop
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can set up and date a sketchbook page and explain it is for testing, not for perfect pictures." (UK NC KS1 Art MA/DP)
- **Hook:** "Today your sketchbook becomes your artist workshop — crossings-out and wild lines are welcome!"
- **Key activity:** Look at photos of real artists' sketchbook pages (messy, dated, full of tries). Model setting up a date box and a warm-up strip. Children fill a first test page with as many different marks as possible in pencil, fine-liner and crayon — no rubbing out.
- **Check for understanding:** "A friend says a sketchbook is only for your best, neatest pictures. What would you tell them?" → It's a workshop for trying ideas and experimenting — messy tests help you get better.
- **Differentiation:** support: pre-drawn date box and 6-square grid with six named marks / stretch: write a tiny note beside one mark saying what you tried
- **Materials:** sketchbooks, pencils, fine-liners, crayons, photos of artists' sketchbook pages
- **Joy:** Decorating your own sketchbook cover.

### Lesson 2 — Light hands: build-up lines
- **Duration:** 30 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can draw lightly first and darken only the lines I want to keep." (UK NC KS1 Art DP/TE)
- **Hook:** "Artists use 'ghost lines'. Why would you draw a line you might not keep?"
- **Key activity:** Model drawing a cup with soft build-up lines, then darkening the keeper outline in fine-liner. Children draw a real object the same way, leaving some ghost lines visible.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why is it a good idea to draw lightly first?" → You can change and fix the shape before you commit to the dark line.
- **Differentiation:** support: very soft pencil, a chunky simple object / stretch: two overlapping objects, darkening only visible edges
- **Materials:** soft pencils, fine-liners, sketchbooks, simple objects (cups, boxes, glue sticks)
- **Joy:** The "reveal" when the keeper line appears from the ghost lines.

### Lesson 3 — Lines that move: Van Gogh
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can use repeated curving and swirling lines to show movement, like Van Gogh." (UK NC KS1 Art TE/AR)
- **Hook:** Show *The Starry Night* (1889) by Vincent van Gogh: "Does this sky look still or moving? How did he do it?"
- **Key activity:** Look closely and trace the swirls in the air; sketchbook: practise swirls, dashes and waves; then draw a windy sky or sea using only lines, in oil pastel.
- **Check for understanding:** "What kind of lines make a picture look like it's moving? Show me in your drawing." → Repeated curving, swirling or wavy lines going in one direction.
- **Differentiation:** support: trace swirls over a printed template / stretch: use thick and thin lines to show strong and gentle wind
- **Materials:** print of *The Starry Night*, oil pastels, sketchbooks, dark paper
- **Joy:** Swirl dancing — move your arm to the lines before drawing.

### Lesson 4 — Lines and dots from Australia: Emily Kame Kngwarreye
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can describe how an artist uses lines and dots to show the land and make my own line-and-dot artwork." (UK NC KS1 Art AR/TE)
- **Hook:** Show a painting by Emily Kame Kngwarreye, an Aboriginal artist from Utopia in Australia's Northern Territory: "What do you think this painting is about?"
- **Key activity:** Discuss how she painted her country — the land, plants and seasons — with lines and dots. Talk respectfully about Aboriginal art as belonging to its communities (we are inspired by the techniques, not copying sacred symbols). Children make a line-and-dot picture of a place they know (a park, a garden).
- **Check for understanding:** "How did the artist use dots and lines differently in her painting?" → e.g. dots build up colour and texture; lines show movement or paths across the land.
- **Differentiation:** support: cotton buds for dots, thick brush for lines / stretch: plan the layers in the sketchbook first
- **Materials:** images of Emily Kame Kngwarreye's paintings, cotton buds, paint, card
- **Joy:** Dot-painting with cotton buds.

### Lesson 5 — Lively lines: drawing like an illustrator (Quentin Blake)
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can draw a character with quick, lively lines that show how they feel and move." (UK NC KS1 Art DP/AR)
- **Hook:** Show Quentin Blake's illustrations from a Roald Dahl book: "How can a few scratchy lines show someone who is excited?"
- **Key activity:** Watch a short video of Quentin Blake drawing (or model it); practise quick gesture figures in the sketchbook (jumping, running, sad); draw a character from the class text.
- **Check for understanding:** "What did you change in your lines to make your character look happy instead of sad?" → e.g. arms up, mouth curved up, bouncy lines.
- **Differentiation:** support: stick-figure starters to add to / stretch: draw two characters interacting
- **Materials:** Quentin Blake illustrations (e.g. from *Fantastic Mr Fox* or *The Twits*), fine-liners, watercolour
- **Joy:** Adding watercolour washes to your characters.

### Lesson 6 — Unit check: my line sketchbook conversation
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can choose my best line work, explain the techniques I used and say what I'd try next." (UK NC KS1 Art — evaluate)
- **Hook:** "Artists look back through their sketchbooks. What do you notice about yours?"
- **Key activity:** Sketchbook "gallery walk": each child chooses one page, names the artist who inspired it and the kinds of line, and gets a "star and a wish" from a partner. Teacher photographs and records the conversation.
- **Check for understanding:** "Show me a page where you used light lines first. How did it help?" → Points to the page and explains fixing the shape before darkening.
- **Differentiation:** support: sentence starters ("I used… lines to…") / stretch: compare two artists' use of line
- **Materials:** sketchbooks, sticky notes, camera
- **Joy:** Sketchbook gallery walk.

**End-of-unit check:** In an artist conversation, the child shows three sketchbook pages, names two kinds of line and the artist behind one of them, and explains why artists draw lightly first.

## Unit 2 — Colour: tints, shades and moods

**Essential question:** How can changing a colour change how a picture feels?

**Key vocabulary:** primary, secondary, colour wheel, tint, shade, white, black, lighter, darker, warm, cool, mood, palette, mix, digital, brush tool, self-portrait

### Lesson 7 — Tints revisited: lighter with white
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can mix a ladder of tints by adding white a little at a time." (UK NC KS1 Art TE)
- **Hook:** "How many different blues can you make from one blue and some white?"
- **Key activity:** Retrieve primaries and secondaries; model adding white a tiny dab at a time; children paint a 5-step tint ladder in the sketchbook.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why do we add white a little at a time?" → A little white changes the colour a lot; adding slowly lets us control each step.
- **Differentiation:** support: 3-step ladder with pre-squeezed paint / stretch: tint ladders for two colours
- **Materials:** ready-mix paint, white, palettes, brushes, water pots
- **Joy:** "Colour chemist" lab coats.

### Lesson 8 — Shades: darker with black
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can mix shades by adding a tiny amount of black and explain the difference between a tint and a shade." (UK NC KS1 Art TE)
- **Hook:** "Black is powerful — just a speck changes everything!"
- **Key activity:** Model adding black on the tip of a brush; children mix a shade ladder beside their tint ladder, making one colour's full range from light to dark.
- **Check for understanding:** "Here are two new blues you haven't seen me mix. Which is a tint and which is a shade? How do you think each was made?" → The lighter one is a tint (blue + white); the darker one is a shade (blue + a tiny bit of black).
- **Differentiation:** support: 3 shades only / stretch: full 7-step range from lightest tint to darkest shade
- **Materials:** paint, black, white, palettes, brushes
- **Joy:** The full "light to dark" strip.

### Lesson 9 — Colour and light: Monet
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can explain how Monet painted the same place in different light and use tints and shades to paint a scene at two times of day." (UK NC KS1 Art AR/TE)
- **Hook:** Show two of Claude Monet's paintings of the same subject in different light (e.g. his *Water Lilies* or haystack series): "Same place — why so different?"
- **Key activity:** Compare the paintings' colours; children paint a simple landscape twice — morning (tints) and evening (shades).
- **Check for understanding:** "Which of your paintings uses more tints, and why?" → The morning one — it's bright and light, so I added white.
- **Differentiation:** support: pre-drawn outline to paint / stretch: add warm and cool colours to show sun and shadow
- **Materials:** Monet prints, paint, card
- **Joy:** Displaying morning and evening paintings side by side.

### Lesson 10 — Warm and cool colour moods: Alma Thomas
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can choose warm or cool colours to show a mood in an abstract painting." (UK NC KS1 Art TE/AR)
- **Hook:** Show a colourful abstract painting by Alma Thomas: "No people, no objects — but how does it make you feel?"
- **Key activity:** Sort colours into warm and cool; discuss moods; paint an Alma Thomas-inspired picture of short brush dabs in a warm or cool palette to show a feeling.
- **Check for understanding:** "Which colours did you choose to show 'calm', and why?" → Cool colours like blue and green feel calm and quiet.
- **Differentiation:** support: pre-selected warm or cool palette / stretch: paint two halves showing two moods
- **Materials:** Alma Thomas images, paint, short flat brushes, card
- **Joy:** Mood-guessing gallery.

### Lesson 11 — Digital colour: tints and shades on a screen
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can use a drawing app to choose, lighten and darken colours and make a digital colour picture." (UK NC KS1 Art MA; link: Computing)
- **Hook:** "Can you mix colours without any paint at all?"
- **Key activity:** In a simple tablet paint app, find the colour picker and brightness slider; make a tint-and-shade strip; create a digital picture of a sunset; save it.
- **Check for understanding:** "What did you do in the app to make a shade?" → Moved the slider towards black/dark (made the colour darker).
- **Differentiation:** support: step-by-step picture card for the tools / stretch: use the fill and brush tools together
- **Materials:** tablets with a child-safe paint app, stylus (optional), picture tool cards
- **Joy:** Printing digital sunsets.

### Lesson 12 — Colourful self-portraits: Frida Kahlo (unit check)
- **Duration:** 30 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can paint a self-portrait using colours chosen on purpose and explain my choices." (UK NC KS1 Art DP/AR — unit check)
- **Hook:** Show a self-portrait by Frida Kahlo (Mexico): "What does she tell us about herself through colour and the things around her?"
- **Key activity:** Discuss her bold colours and personal symbols (flowers, animals); children paint a self-portrait with a background of colours and objects that show who they are; artist conversation about choices.
- **Check for understanding:** "Point to one colour you chose on purpose and say why." → A named colour and reason (e.g. warm orange because I'm energetic).
- **Differentiation:** support: mirror and face-shape template / stretch: include tints and shades for skin and background
- **Materials:** Frida Kahlo prints, mirrors, paint, card, *Viva Frida* by Yuyi Morales
- **Joy:** "Portrait gallery" with name plaques.

**End-of-unit check:** The child mixes a tint and a shade of one colour on request, explains the difference, and talks about the colours they chose on purpose in their self-portrait.

## Unit 3 — Layered and mixed-media collage

**Essential question:** How can layers of paper, fabric and found materials build a picture?

**Key vocabulary:** collage, layer, background, foreground, overlap, texture, mixed media, cut, tear, arrange, stick, found materials, photograph, cut-out, composition

### Lesson 13 — Background first, then layers
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can make a collage by laying the background first and adding layers on top." (UK NC KS1 Art TE)
- **Hook:** "If you stick the tree first and the sky afterwards, what goes wrong?"
- **Key activity:** Model a landscape collage: sky and ground first (big pieces), then middle, then foreground details (small pieces on top). Children plan in the sketchbook and make a layered scene.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why do we stick the big background pieces first?" → So the smaller foreground pieces can sit on top and be seen.
- **Differentiation:** support: pre-cut background pieces / stretch: three clear layers (back, middle, front)
- **Materials:** coloured and textured papers, glue sticks, scissors, card
- **Joy:** "Peel back the layers" reveal.

### Lesson 14 — Texture collage with found materials
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can choose materials with different textures and arrange them to make an interesting collage." (UK NC KS1 Art TE/MA)
- **Hook:** A texture feely-bag: "Rough, smooth, bumpy, soft — which would make a good tree bark? A cloud?"
- **Key activity:** Sort materials by texture (bubble wrap, fabric, foil, corrugated card, tissue); make a texture creature or landscape.
- **Check for understanding:** "Which material did you choose for the rough part, and why?" → e.g. corrugated card, because its bumps feel and look rough.
- **Differentiation:** support: 4 pre-sorted textures / stretch: add rubbings as another texture layer
- **Materials:** fabric scraps, bubble wrap, foil, corrugated card, tissue, PVA glue
- **Joy:** Feely-bag texture hunt.

### Lesson 15 — Photo-and-paper collage: Romare Bearden
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can combine cut photographs and paper to make a collage of a busy street, inspired by Romare Bearden." (UK NC KS1 Art AR/TE)
- **Hook:** Show Romare Bearden's *The Block* (1971): "What can you find in this street? What is it made of?"
- **Key activity:** Discuss how he cut and layered photos, paper and paint to show life in Harlem, New York; children make a street collage from magazine photos and coloured paper.
- **Check for understanding:** "How does using photographs change the feel of a collage?" → They make parts look real, mixed with painted/paper parts that are more imaginative.
- **Differentiation:** support: pre-cut windows and doors / stretch: add people from magazines at different sizes to show near and far
- **Materials:** Bearden images, magazines, coloured paper, scissors, glue
- **Joy:** "Our street" class collage wall.

### Lesson 16 — Collage illustration: Jeannie Baker
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can use natural and made materials to create a collage picture like a book illustrator." (UK NC KS1 Art AR/MA; link: Geography)
- **Hook:** Show a spread from *Window* or *Mirror* by Jeannie Baker: "Is this a painting or a photo? Look closer!"
- **Key activity:** Notice real leaves, sand, fabric and paper in her collages; children make a "window view" collage of their street or garden using natural materials.
- **Check for understanding:** "What natural material did you use and what does it show in your picture?" → e.g. real leaves for a hedge.
- **Differentiation:** support: window-frame template / stretch: make the view change (half now, half long ago — link: History)
- **Materials:** *Window* by Jeannie Baker, leaves, twigs, sand, fabric, card frames, PVA
- **Joy:** Collecting natural materials outside.

### Lesson 17 — Painting with scissors: Matisse's cut-outs
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can cut bold shapes freely and arrange them into a colourful composition, like Matisse." (UK NC KS1 Art AR/TE)
- **Hook:** Show *The Snail* (1953) by Henri Matisse: "Can you find the snail? He said he was 'drawing with scissors'."
- **Key activity:** Read *Henri's Scissors* by Jeanette Winter; cut organic and geometric shapes from painted paper; try arrangements before gluing (photograph each).
- **Check for understanding:** "Why did you try different arrangements before gluing?" → To see which composition looks best — like testing in a sketchbook.
- **Differentiation:** support: large, simple shapes / stretch: a spiral composition like *The Snail*
- **Materials:** *Henri's Scissors* by Jeanette Winter, painted papers, scissors, glue, camera
- **Joy:** Big floor composition with the whole class.

### Lesson 18 — Unit check: collage artist conversation
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can explain how I built my collage in layers and which artist inspired me." (UK NC KS1 Art — evaluate)
- **Hook:** "Welcome to the collage gallery — you're the guide!"
- **Key activity:** Children choose their best collage, prepare 3 things to say (layers, textures, artist), and present to a partner group. Teacher records.
- **Check for understanding:** "Tell me which layer you made first and why." → The background first so the others could go on top.
- **Differentiation:** support: sentence starters / stretch: compare two artists from the unit
- **Materials:** collages, camera
- **Joy:** Gallery tour.

**End-of-unit check:** In an artist conversation, the child explains the order of layers in their collage, names two textures used and the artist who inspired it.

## Unit 4 — Pattern and printmaking

**Essential question:** How do artists design patterns that repeat — and print them?

**Key vocabulary:** pattern, repeat, motif, tile, symmetry, rotate, print, printing block, relief, press, roller, ink, Adinkra, symbol, wallpaper, pop art

### Lesson 19 — Pattern rules: repeating motifs
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can design a motif and repeat it in different pattern rules (AB, ABBA, rotating)." (UK NC KS1 Art TE; link: Maths patterns)
- **Hook:** Fabric samples and wallpapers from around the world: "What's repeating? What's the rule?"
- **Key activity:** Identify motifs and rules; design a simple motif in the sketchbook; repeat it three ways (straight, alternating, rotating).
- **Check for understanding:** "Look at this new fabric. What is the motif, and what is the rule it follows? How can you tell?" → Names the repeating shape (the motif) and describes the rule (e.g. it flips every other time — I can see it pointing up, down, up, down).
- **Differentiation:** support: stencil motifs / stretch: design a pattern where the motif rotates a quarter turn each time
- **Materials:** fabric and wallpaper samples, stencils, squared paper
- **Joy:** "Guess my rule" game.

### Lesson 20 — Adinkra printing from Ghana
- **Duration:** 30 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can explain what Adinkra symbols are and print a pattern with a stamp I've designed." (UK NC KS1 Art AR/TE)
- **Hook:** Show Adinkra cloth and symbols: "Each of these symbols from Ghana has a meaning — like a message in a pattern."
- **Key activity:** Learn about Adinkra symbols (traditionally stamped on cloth by the Asante people with carved calabash stamps and dye); learn one or two symbols' meanings; design a personal symbol and make a stamp (foam on card); print a grid pattern.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why did Adinkra artists use a stamp rather than painting each symbol?" → A stamp prints the same shape again and again, quickly and evenly, to make a repeating pattern.
- **Differentiation:** support: simple shape stamp / stretch: alternate two stamps in a pattern with a border
- **Materials:** images of Adinkra cloth and symbols, craft foam, card, scissors, glue, printing ink, rollers
- **Joy:** Printing on fabric squares.

### Lesson 21 — Making a relief printing block
- **Duration:** 30 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can make a printing block with raised parts and print it cleanly." (UK NC KS1 Art TE/MA)
- **Hook:** "Why do only the raised parts print?"
- **Key activity:** Make a block with string or foam shapes glued on card; ink with a roller; press and lift; compare prints and improve inking.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why does the print come out as a mirror image of your block?" → The block is pressed face-down, so the design flips.
- **Differentiation:** support: pre-cut foam shapes / stretch: design a letter so it prints the right way round
- **Materials:** card, string, foam, PVA, printing ink, rollers, trays, paper
- **Joy:** The "peel" moment when lifting the print.

### Lesson 22 — Wallpaper design: William Morris
- **Duration:** 30 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can design a nature repeat pattern for wallpaper inspired by William Morris and print it in two colours." (UK NC KS1 Art AR/TE)
- **Hook:** Show *Strawberry Thief* (1883) by William Morris: "Can you find the birds? Where does the pattern repeat?"
- **Key activity:** Find the repeat; draw a leaf or bird motif; print with two blocks in two colours to make a strip of wallpaper.
- **Check for understanding:** "How do you make the pattern line up when you repeat it?" → Use a grid or line up the edge of the block with the last print.
- **Differentiation:** support: one-colour print / stretch: two blocks that fit together
- **Materials:** *Strawberry Thief* image, printing blocks, ink, lining paper, rulers
- **Joy:** A class wallpaper roll.

### Lesson 23 — Pop art repetition: Andy Warhol
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can repeat one image in different colour combinations, like Andy Warhol's pop art." (UK NC KS1 Art AR/TE)
- **Hook:** Show Warhol's *Campbell's Soup Cans* (1962) and a colourful repeated portrait: "Why paint the same thing again and again?"
- **Key activity:** Print or photocopy one simple image (an everyday object the child drew) four times; colour each in a different warm/cool scheme.
- **Check for understanding:** "How does changing the colours change the feel of the same picture?" → Warm colours make it feel bright and loud; cool colours make it calm.
- **Differentiation:** support: 2 repeats / stretch: use complementary colours (opposites on the colour wheel)
- **Materials:** Warhol images, photocopies or prints, felt pens, paint
- **Joy:** Pop-art gallery.

### Lesson 24 — Unit check: pattern and print conversation
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can explain my pattern's motif and rule and how I printed it." (UK NC KS1 Art — evaluate)
- **Hook:** "Design studio pitch: sell your pattern to a fabric company!"
- **Key activity:** Present your best print: motif, rule, printing method, the artist or tradition that inspired it, and one improvement.
- **Check for understanding:** "What would you change to make your print cleaner next time?" → e.g. roll the ink thinner, press more evenly.
- **Differentiation:** support: sentence starters / stretch: compare Adinkra and Morris patterns
- **Materials:** prints, camera
- **Joy:** "Design studio" pitch.

**End-of-unit check:** The child shows a printed pattern, names its motif and repeat rule, explains how a relief block prints, and names the tradition or artist that inspired it.

## Unit 5 — Textiles: weaving and stitching

**Essential question:** How are threads and fabrics turned into art?

**Key vocabulary:** textile, fabric, thread, yarn, weave, warp, weft, over, under, loom, stitch, needle, running stitch, quilt, pattern, Kente

### Lesson 25 — Weaving: over and under
- **Duration:** 30 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can weave paper strips over and under to make a pattern." (UK NC KS1 Art TE/MA; link: Maths patterns)
- **Hook:** Look closely at a piece of fabric with a magnifier: "It's made of threads going over and under!"
- **Key activity:** Cut a paper loom (warp slits); weave contrasting paper strips (weft) over-under; alternate the start each row to make a checkerboard.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why must each new strip start the opposite way to the one before?" → So the strips lock together and make the checkerboard pattern; otherwise they slide out.
- **Differentiation:** support: pre-cut loom and wide strips / stretch: over-two, under-one patterns
- **Materials:** paper looms, paper strips, magnifiers, fabric samples
- **Joy:** Checkerboard reveal.

### Lesson 26 — Kente cloth from Ghana
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can describe Kente cloth and design a Kente-inspired woven pattern with colours chosen for meaning." (UK NC KS1 Art AR/TE)
- **Hook:** Show Kente cloth: "These strips are woven on narrow looms and sewn together. Why so many colours?"
- **Key activity:** Learn that Kente is woven by Asante and Ewe weavers in Ghana in narrow strips; colours carry meanings (e.g. gold for wealth, green for growth); plan and weave a Kente-inspired strip with ribbon or paper.
- **Check for understanding:** "How is Kente made into a big cloth if the loom is narrow?" → Many narrow woven strips are sewn together side by side.
- **Differentiation:** support: paper strips in 3 colours / stretch: explain the meaning of your colour choices
- **Materials:** Kente images, ribbons, paper looms, colour-meaning cards
- **Joy:** Joining everyone's strips into one class "Kente" cloth.

### Lesson 27 — Weaving with yarn: Anni Albers
- **Duration:** 30 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can weave yarn on a card loom and explain how an artist made weaving into modern art." (UK NC KS1 Art AR/MA)
- **Hook:** Show a woven work by Anni Albers: "Is weaving art or craft? Can it be both?"
- **Key activity:** Card loom with a warp; weave yarn with a plastic needle; change colours to make stripes; discuss Albers's bold geometric patterns.
- **Check for understanding:** "Point to the warp and the weft on your loom. Which did you put on first, and why couldn't you do it the other way round?" → The warp is stretched on the loom first; the weft can only go over and under something that is already there, so the warp must come first.
- **Differentiation:** support: chunky yarn and a wide loom / stretch: add a pattern block of a different colour
- **Materials:** card looms, yarn, plastic needles, Anni Albers images
- **Joy:** Colourful woven mini-tapestries.

### Lesson 28 — Running stitch and story quilts: Faith Ringgold
- **Duration:** 30 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can sew a running stitch on binca fabric and plan a story-quilt square." (UK NC KS1 Art AR/TE)
- **Hook:** Read *Tar Beach* by Faith Ringgold: "This book began as a quilt! How can fabric tell a story?"
- **Key activity:** Learn to thread a large blunt needle and sew a running stitch (up, down) on binca; plan a quilt square showing a memory, then stitch its border.
- **Check for understanding:** "What makes a running stitch neat?" → Even stitches and even gaps, going up and down in a straight line.
- **Differentiation:** support: pre-threaded needles, large binca holes / stretch: stitch a simple shape outline
- **Materials:** *Tar Beach* by Faith Ringgold, binca fabric, large blunt needles, yarn, fabric scraps
- **Joy:** A class story quilt of everyone's squares.

### Lesson 29 — Unit check: textiles conversation
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can explain how I wove and stitched and which textile artist or tradition inspired me." (UK NC KS1 Art — evaluate)
- **Hook:** "The textile museum is opening a new room!"
- **Key activity:** Present woven piece and stitched square; explain warp/weft or running stitch; partner feedback.
- **Check for understanding:** "Show me the warp and weft in your weaving." → Points correctly and explains.
- **Differentiation:** support: labels to point with / stretch: compare Kente and Albers
- **Materials:** textile pieces, camera
- **Joy:** Textile museum.

**End-of-unit check:** The child shows their weaving and stitching, points out the warp and weft, demonstrates a running stitch, and names one textile tradition or artist.

## Unit 6 — Clay and 3-D form, and our exhibition

**Essential question:** How do artists turn a lump of clay into a form that stands up and tells a story?

**Key vocabulary:** clay, form, 3-D, sculpture, pinch, roll, coil, slab, score, slip, join, smooth, texture, pot, vessel, burnish, hollow, exhibition, curate

### Lesson 30 — Clay warm-up: roll, pinch, coil
- **Duration:** 30 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can use my hands to roll, pinch and coil clay and describe the shapes each makes." (UK NC KS1 Art MA/TE)
- **Hook:** "Clay is one of the oldest art materials in the world. What can your hands do with it?"
- **Key activity:** Explore techniques: roll a ball, pinch a small pot, roll coils (snakes), press textures; record in the sketchbook with drawings.
- **Check for understanding:** "You want to make a bowl with tall sides. Would you use a pinch or coils, and why?" → Coils — you can stack them round and round to build the walls higher; a pinch pot only stretches as far as one ball of clay goes.
- **Differentiation:** support: softened clay or dough / stretch: coil a small spiral dish
- **Materials:** air-drying clay, boards, texture tools, wet cloths
- **Joy:** Squishing clay.

### Lesson 31 — Pinch pots: Magdalene Odundo
- **Duration:** 30 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can make a pinch pot with even walls and smooth its surface, inspired by Magdalene Odundo's vessels." (UK NC KS1 Art AR/TE)
- **Hook:** Show vessels by Magdalene Odundo, the Kenyan-born British potter: "How do you think she makes them so smooth and shiny?"
- **Key activity:** Discuss her smooth, burnished forms; make a pinch pot by pushing a thumb in and pinching round; smooth with a damp finger; burnish with a spoon when leather-hard (next week).
- **Check for understanding:** "Why should the walls of a pinch pot be the same thickness all round?" → So it's strong and doesn't crack or collapse when drying.
- **Differentiation:** support: a smaller clay ball / stretch: add a foot or a lip to the pot
- **Materials:** clay, spoons for burnishing, images of Odundo's work
- **Joy:** Burnishing to a shine.

### Lesson 32 — Joins that hold: score and slip
- **Duration:** 30 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can join two pieces of clay by scoring and slipping so they don't fall off." (UK NC KS1 Art TE)
- **Hook:** Show a clay model whose ears fell off: "Why did this happen? How can we stop it?"
- **Key activity:** Demonstrate "score, slip, press, smooth"; children add handles, ears or legs to a form; test by gently lifting.
- **Check for understanding:** "Your friend's clay ears fell off when they dried. Which step did they probably skip, and why does it matter?" → Score and slip — scratching both surfaces and adding slip lets the clay grip like glue; just pressing pieces together doesn't hold.
- **Differentiation:** support: one join with help / stretch: three joins on a creature
- **Materials:** clay, plastic forks for scoring, slip (clay + water), sponges
- **Joy:** The "shake test" for joins.

### Lesson 33 — Pots with patterns: Maria Martinez / sculpture with holes: Barbara Hepworth
- **Duration:** 30 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can make a clay form with a decorated or pierced surface inspired by an artist I've studied." (UK NC KS1 Art AR/DP)
- **Hook:** Two artists, two ideas: Maria Martinez's black-on-black pots from San Ildefonso Pueblo (New Mexico, USA) and Barbara Hepworth's sculptures with holes (UK). "Which would you choose?"
- **Key activity:** Choose: a coil or pinch pot with carved patterns (Martinez) or a smooth form pierced with a hole to look through (Hepworth). Plan in sketchbook, then make.
- **Check for understanding:** "What did your artist do with the surface of her work, and how did you use that idea?" → e.g. Martinez used shiny and matt patterns; I carved a pattern band. Or Hepworth made holes to let light through; I pierced my form.
- **Differentiation:** support: stamp-pressed patterns / stretch: plan a symmetrical pattern band
- **Materials:** clay, carving tools, images of Martinez and Hepworth
- **Joy:** Choosing your own artist path.

### Lesson 34 — Curating our exhibition
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can choose my best pieces from the year and write a label explaining them." (UK NC KS1 Art — evaluate)
- **Hook:** "Museums have curators who choose and label the art. Today you are curators!"
- **Key activity:** Review the sketchbook and all units; choose two pieces; write a label (title, materials, artist who inspired me, what I'm proud of); help arrange the exhibition.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why did you choose this piece for the exhibition?" → A reason about technique or idea, not only "it's nice".
- **Differentiation:** support: label frame / stretch: write an "introduction" panel for a unit
- **Materials:** all artwork, label cards, display boards
- **Joy:** Setting up a real exhibition.

### Lesson 35 — Exhibition opening and year reflection
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can talk about my art to visitors and say how I've grown as an artist this year." (UK NC KS1 Art — end-of-year review)
- **Hook:** "The exhibition is open — welcome, visitors!"
- **Key activity:** Children guide families or another class; compare first and last sketchbook pages; share one thing they want to learn next year.
- **Check for understanding:** "Compare your first sketchbook page with your last. What's changed?" → A specific improvement (lighter lines, better colour mixing, more ideas).
- **Differentiation:** support: guide with a partner / stretch: explain one artist's influence across two units
- **Materials:** exhibition, sketchbooks
- **Joy:** The opening party.

**End-of-unit check:** The child makes a clay form with a secure scored-and-slipped join and a decorated surface, and — at the exhibition — explains two pieces, the techniques used and the artists who inspired them.

## End-of-year mastery checks

1. Keeps a dated sketchbook of experiments and explains what it's for. *(Seen: sketchbook review.)*
2. Draws lightly first, then darkens chosen lines; uses different lines to show movement or feeling. *(Seen: drawings.)*
3. Mixes tints and shades and chooses warm or cool colours for a mood. *(Seen: colour strips and paintings.)*
4. Builds a collage in layers with contrasting textures. *(Seen: collage.)*
5. Designs a motif, repeats it in a pattern and makes a relief print. *(Seen: prints.)*
6. Weaves over and under, sews a running stitch, and names warp and weft. *(Seen: textile pieces.)*
7. Pinches, coils and joins clay securely (score and slip). *(Seen: clay form.)*
8. Names artists and craft traditions from several continents and explains how one inspired their work. *(Seen: artist conversations and labels.)*

## Teacher guidance

**Assessment by looking and talking.** Photograph work at each stage; use short artist conversations ("What did you try? What did you change? What would you do next?"). Praise process — experimenting, looking closely, improving — more than the final product.

**Misconceptions:** "Good art is neat and realistic" → show artists who work loosely and abstractly; "black makes a colour darker, so add lots" → a speck at a time; "collage means gluing anything anywhere" → background first, then layers; "clay joins just by pressing" → score and slip.

**Respect for cultures.** When studying Aboriginal Australian art, Adinkra and Kente, teach where the art comes from and what it means; be inspired by techniques and ideas rather than copying sacred or ceremonial designs.

**Safety.** Blunt needles, safety scissors, adult-only craft knives; wash hands after clay; check allergies for materials.

**Vertical alignment.** Foundation gave line, primary/secondary colour, tints, collage, stamp printing and dough. Year 2 (ages 7–8) moves to slow observational drawing, texture by observation, first tone (shading a sphere) and a still life, tertiary colours, multi-colour block printing, slab building and hollow forms, and new weaving traditions — all built on this year's sketchbook habit, light-first drawing, tints and shades, relief printing, weaving and pinch/coil/score-and-slip clay.
