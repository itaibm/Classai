# Year 4 Science (ages 8–9, KS2) — Lesson Outlines

> Part of **the best international primary school** (see [`../../../SCHOOL-CHARTER.md`](../../../SCHOOL-CHARTER.md)).
> Source of truth: [`knowledge-base/subjects/science/year-4.md`](../../../knowledge-base/subjects/science/year-4.md).

## Year overview

Year 4 deepens every strand into proper **systems and methods**. A child learns to **classify** living things with yes/no keys and to see how environments change and put living things in danger (and how humans can help); to trace the **human digestive system** and match teeth to their jobs; to explain the three **states of matter** and the **water cycle**; and to take on two new areas of physics — **sound** (vibrations, pitch and volume) and **electricity** (simple circuits, conductors and insulators). The teaching keeps the inquiry spirit but the **working-scientifically thread** now centres on **measurement**: this year the child takes **accurate measurements in standard units** (a timer, a ruler, a thermometer) and **records data in tables and bar charts**. This builds on Year 3's fair tests by making the *recording* rigorous — the leap from "which was faster?" to "exactly how long, in seconds, and how does the table show it?" Lessons run mostly 30–35 min; the digestion, states-of-matter, sound and circuit lessons use the upper band so there is time to build, measure and chart.

## Unit 1 — Living things and their habitats

**Essential question:** How do we classify living things, and how do changes to the environment affect them?

**Key vocabulary:** classify, group, feature, classification key, yes/no question, observable, environment, change, natural, human-caused, danger, endangered, conservation, help, harm.

### Lesson 1 — Classifying living things
- **Duration:** 30 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can classify living things by observable features and explain there's more than one sensible way."
- **Hook:** "How would you sort every bug in the garden into tidy groups?"
- **Key activity:** Explore — sort a set of living things by features (legs, wings, fur). Notice — we **classify** by **observable features**, and the same set can be grouped sensibly in several ways (by diet, habitat, body). Name good grouping rules (clear, observable) vs poor ones (opinions). Fix "there's only one right way."
- **Check for understanding:** "Is 'Does it have wings?' a good grouping question? Why?" → Yes — it has a clear yes/no answer you can observe.
- **Differentiation:** support: sort by one obvious feature; stretch: group the same set two different ways.
- **Materials:** living-thing picture cards, sorting hoops.
- **Joy:** inventing their own sorting rule and challenging the tutor to guess it.

### Lesson 2 — Making and using a classification key
- **Duration:** 30 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can use a yes/no key to identify a living thing."
- **Hook:** "A game of '20 questions' is secretly a science tool — want to see?"
- **Key activity:** Explore — follow a key to identify a robin (feathers? → bird; red front? → robin). Notice — each **yes/no question** splits the group in two until one is left. Then make a small key for 4 objects/toys. Fix "key questions can be opinions ('is it cute?')."
- **Check for understanding:** "Is 'Is it friendly?' a good key question?" → No — it's an opinion, not an observable feature.
- **Differentiation:** support: follow a ready-made key; stretch: write a 3-question key for four items.
- **Materials:** a sample classification key, 4 toys/objects, blank key template.
- **Joy:** building a key that always guesses the right toy.

### Lesson 3 — Environments change, and the dangers
- **Duration:** 30 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can explain how environmental change can endanger living things, and how humans help."
- **Hook:** "A pond fills with litter — what happens to the frogs?"
- **Key activity:** Explore — the pond story: litter and dirty water (human-caused harm) → frogs and fish lose clean water and food → fewer survive (**danger**) → people clean it up and plant (human help) → wildlife returns. Notice both **natural** and **human-caused** changes, and both **harm and help**. Sort actions into helps/harms. Fix "humans only harm" and "animals just move easily."
- **Check for understanding:** "Name one way humans help the environment." → Nature reserves / recycling / planting trees / cleaning litter.
- **Differentiation:** support: sort three help/harm actions; stretch: choose one helpful action to do this week and explain its effect.
- **Materials:** help/harm action cards, the pond story sequence.
- **Joy:** planning a real "help wildlife" action for home or the neighbourhood.

**End-of-unit 1 check:** Classify a set two sensible ways; use a yes/no key to identify something; explain how a change can endanger living things and name one way humans help.

## Unit 2 — Animals, including humans: digestion and teeth

**Essential question:** What happens to food on its journey through the body, and what do different teeth do?

**Key vocabulary:** digestive system, mouth, oesophagus, stomach, small intestine, large intestine, absorb, nutrient, waste, incisor, canine, molar, cut, tear, grind, decay.

### Lesson 4 — The digestive journey
- **Duration:** 35 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can order the parts of the digestive system and say what each does."
- **Hook:** "Follow a bite of apple — where does it actually go?"
- **Key activity:** Explore — trace food: **mouth** (teeth + saliva) → **oesophagus** (pushes it down) → **stomach** (churns with juices) → **small intestine** (absorbs nutrients into the blood) → **large intestine** (takes back water; rest becomes waste). Order the parts. Fix "food goes to the stomach and digestion's done" — most absorption is in the small intestine.
- **Check for understanding:** "Where are most nutrients absorbed?" → In the small intestine.
- **Differentiation:** support: order the parts with a body outline; stretch: explain the job of each part in turn.
- **Materials:** digestive-system diagram, ordering cards/body outline.
- **Joy:** following an apple bite step-by-step along a giant body outline.

### Lesson 5 — Teeth for different jobs
- **Duration:** 30 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can name the three kinds of teeth and their jobs and say how to keep teeth healthy."
- **Hook:** "Why does a dog have big pointy fangs but a cow has flat grinders?"
- **Key activity:** Explore — in a mirror, find **incisors** (front, **cut/bite**), **canines** (pointy, **tear**), **molars** (back, **grind**). Compare a carnivore's canines to a herbivore's molars. Name how to protect teeth: **brush twice a day, less sugar**. Fix "all teeth do the same job."
- **Check for understanding:** "Which teeth grind your food?" → Molars (the back teeth).
- **Differentiation:** support: match two teeth to jobs; stretch: explain why a lion's and a cow's teeth differ.
- **Materials:** mirror, tooth diagram, carnivore/herbivore skull pictures, tooth–job cards.
- **Joy:** finding their own incisors, canines and molars in a mirror.

**End-of-unit 2 check:** Order the digestive system and say where nutrients are absorbed; name the three teeth and their jobs; give two ways to keep teeth healthy.

## Unit 3 — States of matter and the water cycle

**Essential question:** What are the three states of matter, and how does water move around the world?

**Key vocabulary:** solid, liquid, gas, particle, shape, flow, melt, freeze, evaporate, condense, water vapour, water cycle, precipitation.

### Lesson 6 — Solids, liquids and gases
- **Duration:** 30 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can sort materials into solid, liquid and gas and describe how each behaves."
- **Hook:** "Ice, water and steam are all water — so why are they so different?"
- **Key activity:** Explore — handle/observe a solid (ice, wood), a liquid (water, milk), a gas (a balloon of air). Notice — **solid** keeps its shape (particles packed tight), **liquid** flows and takes its container's shape, **gas** spreads to fill any space. Name the three states and sort examples.
- **Check for understanding:** "How is a liquid different from a solid?" → A liquid flows and takes the shape of its container; a solid keeps its shape.
- **Differentiation:** support: sort solid vs liquid; stretch: explain each state using "particles."
- **Materials:** ice, water, a sealed balloon, mixed examples to sort.
- **Joy:** squashing a balloon to feel that even air (a gas) is "something."

### Lesson 7 — Changing state: melting, freezing, evaporating, condensing (investigation)
- **Duration:** 35 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can name the four changes of state and measure one with a timer."
- **Hook:** "Where does a puddle go when it dries up — does the water just vanish?"
- **Key activity:** Investigate — leave an ice cube in two places (warm vs cool) and **time how long each takes to melt** (a fair test, measured in standard units). Notice the changes: **melt** (solid→liquid), **freeze** (liquid→solid), **evaporate** (liquid→gas), **condense** (gas→liquid, e.g. on a cold window). Record times in a table. Fix "the water vanishes" — it evaporated; and "condensation is air turning to water" — it's vapour cooling.
- **Check for understanding:** "What is it called when a liquid turns into a gas?" → Evaporation.
- **Differentiation:** support: read and report one melt time; stretch: compare both times and explain the difference.
- **Materials:** ice cubes, saucers, timer, warm/cool spots, results table.
- **Joy:** racing two ice cubes and timing the winner.

### Lesson 8 — The water cycle
- **Duration:** 30 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can order the water cycle and explain what drives it."
- **Hook:** "The rain that falls today might have been in a dinosaur's drink — how?"
- **Key activity:** Explore — link the changes of state into a cycle: the **Sun evaporates** water from seas/rivers → vapour rises and **condenses** into clouds → falls as **rain (precipitation)** → flows back to the sea. Order the cycle and label each change of state. Connect to real weather.
- **Check for understanding:** "In the water cycle, what makes water evaporate from the sea?" → Heat from the Sun.
- **Differentiation:** support: order four cycle cards; stretch: name the change of state at each step.
- **Materials:** water-cycle diagram, ordering cards; (optional) a "cycle in a bag" taped to a sunny window.
- **Joy:** watching condensation form in a sealed "water-cycle bag" on the window.

**End-of-unit 3 check:** Sort materials into solid/liquid/gas; name the four changes of state and time a melt; order the water cycle and say what drives it.

## Unit 4 — Sound

**Essential question:** How are sounds made, and what makes a sound high, low, loud or quiet?

**Key vocabulary:** vibration, vibrate, sound wave, medium, ear, pitch, high, low, volume, loud, quiet, distance, vacuum.

### Lesson 9 — Sound is made by vibrations
- **Duration:** 30 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can explain that sounds are made by vibrations that travel to our ears."
- **Hook:** "Put your hand on your throat and hum — what can you feel?"
- **Key activity:** Explore — feel a humming throat, a plucked rubber band, a tapped drum. Notice — every sound comes from something **vibrating**; the vibrations travel through a **medium** (air, water, solid) as a **sound wave** to the **ear**. Try a string telephone (sound through a solid). Fix "sound travels through empty space" — a vacuum has nothing to vibrate.
- **Check for understanding:** "Can sound travel through outer space? Why?" → No — there's no air or medium to carry the vibrations.
- **Differentiation:** support: name what is vibrating in two examples; stretch: explain why a string telephone works.
- **Materials:** rubber band, drum/box, two cups + string for a phone.
- **Joy:** talking down a homemade string telephone.

### Lesson 10 — Pitch and volume (investigation)
- **Duration:** 35 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can change a sound's pitch and volume and explain the difference."
- **Hook:** "Why does a tight guitar string sing high and a loose one growl low?"
- **Key activity:** Investigate — a rubber-band "guitar" over a box. Pluck **harder** → bigger vibration → **louder** (**volume**). **Tighten** the band → faster vibration → **higher** note (**pitch**). Change one thing at a time and record what happens. Also test: sound gets **quieter with distance**. Fix "louder and higher mean the same thing."
- **Check for understanding:** "What changes if vibrations get faster — pitch or volume?" → Pitch (it gets higher).
- **Differentiation:** support: make a louder vs quieter sound; stretch: change pitch and volume separately and explain each.
- **Materials:** box with rubber bands of different thicknesses/tensions, a results sheet.
- **Joy:** composing a tune by tightening and plucking the band.

**End-of-unit 4 check:** Explain that sound is made by vibrations needing a medium; say why there's no sound in space; change and explain pitch vs volume.

## Unit 5 — Electricity

**Essential question:** What makes a circuit work, and which materials let electricity flow?

**Key vocabulary:** circuit, complete loop, cell, battery, wire, component, bulb, buzzer, motor, switch, gap, broken, conductor, insulator, mains safety.

### Lesson 11 — Simple circuits and switches (investigation)
- **Duration:** 35 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can build a circuit that lights a bulb and explain why a gap stops it."
- **Hook:** "Why do all the fairy lights go dark if just one bulb fails?"
- **Key activity:** Investigate — build a **series circuit**: cell → wire → bulb → wire → back to cell. Notice — it lights only when the loop is **complete**. Snip a gap and bridge it with a paperclip **switch**: closed = on, open = off. Fix "electricity only needs one wire to the bulb" — it must be a **complete loop back to the cell**. **Safety: batteries (cells) only — never mains plugs or sockets.**
- **Check for understanding:** "Why won't a bulb light if there's a gap in the circuit?" → The loop is broken, so electricity can't flow round.
- **Differentiation:** support: complete a part-built loop to light the bulb; stretch: add a working switch and explain it.
- **Materials:** cells, wires, bulbs in holders, paperclips, (optional) buzzer/motor.
- **Joy:** the moment the bulb lights when the loop is finally complete.

### Lesson 12 — Conductors and insulators (investigation)
- **Duration:** 30 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can sort materials into conductors and insulators by testing them."
- **Hook:** "Why is a wire metal inside but plastic on the outside?"
- **Key activity:** Investigate — leave a gap in the bulb circuit and bridge it with different objects (coin, paperclip, plastic spoon, pencil lead, wooden peg). Predict first, then test. Notice — **metals conduct** (bulb lights); **plastic, wood, rubber insulate** (bulb stays off). Name **conductor** and **insulator**. Connect to why wires have plastic coats. **Batteries only.**
- **Check for understanding:** "Is plastic a conductor or an insulator?" → An insulator (it doesn't let electricity flow).
- **Differentiation:** support: test three objects and sort them; stretch: predict before testing and explain the wire's plastic coat.
- **Materials:** circuit with a gap, assorted test objects, sorting mats.
- **Joy:** the surprise of which objects light the bulb (and which don't).

**End-of-unit 5 check:** Build a circuit that lights a bulb and explain the complete loop; explain what a switch does; sort materials into conductors and insulators and state the safety rule.

## End-of-year mastery checks

1. **Classification:** Classify a set two sensible ways and use a yes/no key to identify a living thing.
2. **Environments:** Explain how a change can endanger living things and name one way humans help.
3. **Digestion & teeth:** Order the digestive system, say where nutrients are absorbed, and name the three teeth and their jobs.
4. **States of matter:** Sort materials into solid/liquid/gas and name the four changes of state.
5. **Water cycle:** Order the water cycle and say what drives it.
6. **Sound:** Explain that sound is made by vibrations needing a medium, and explain pitch vs volume.
7. **Circuits:** Build a circuit that lights a bulb, explain the complete loop and what a switch does.
8. **Conductors & measurement:** Sort materials into conductors and insulators; and (working scientifically) take a measurement in standard units and record it in a table.
