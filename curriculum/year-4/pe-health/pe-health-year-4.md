# Year 4 PE & Health (ages 9–10) — Scope & Sequence

> Part of **the best international primary school** (see [`../../../SCHOOL-CHARTER.md`](../../../SCHOOL-CHARTER.md)).
> Written to [`../../PLANNING-BRIEF.md`](../../PLANNING-BRIEF.md).
> Codes: **UK NC KS2 PE** (running, jumping, throwing and catching; competitive games; flexibility, strength, technique, control and balance; dance; outdoor and adventurous activity; swimming and water safety) with **health education** (England's statutory Health Education, primary), and **SHAPE America** National PE Standards (Grade 4; S1–S5) and **National Health Education Standards** (Grades 3–5).

## Year overview

Year 4 PE & Health turns **fundamental movement into real sport, dance and adventure** — and teaches children how to look after their bodies and minds. Children learn the *principles* behind games (space, width, support, marking) so they can play any invasion or net game well; they build gymnastic sequences with balance, flight and partner work; they choreograph dance from several cultures; they become safer, stronger swimmers who know how to self-rescue; they run, jump and throw with better technique and measure their improvement; and they navigate outdoors with a map and compass. A dedicated **health unit** covers sleep, food, hygiene, growing up, body safety and mental health, and every lesson includes a warm-up and cool-down that children increasingly lead themselves.

**Delivery.** PE is **human-delivered and active**: a teacher, coach or parent leads every session in a hall, playground, field or pool. An app or tutor can support with explanations, rules, videos, reflection questions and progress tracking, but the doing is physical. Every lesson follows: warm-up (raise heart rate, mobilise) → skill teaching (demonstrate, practise, feedback) → apply in a game or performance → cool-down and reflection.

**What Year 4 assumes (from Year 3):** fundamental movement skills (running, jumping, hopping, throwing, catching, kicking, dodging) with reasonable control; simple team games and rules; basic balances and rolls; simple dance phrases; being able to enter the water and move with confidence (swimming ability varies — see Unit 7).
**What Year 5 relies on:** tactical understanding of attack and defence across game types; sequences combining balance, flight and partner work; secure swimming of 25 m and self-rescue; accurate measurement of personal performance; knowledge of healthy habits and puberty, ready for more independent fitness planning and competitive sport.

**Inclusion.** Adapt every activity for disability, injury, faith and culture (e.g. modest swimwear, fasting periods); use STEP (Space, Task, Equipment, People) to make tasks easier or harder; celebrate personal bests over winning.

## Unit 1 — Fit for life: warm-ups, fitness and the healthy heart

**Essential question:** What does it mean to be fit, and how can I train my body safely?

**Key vocabulary:** warm-up, cool-down, pulse, heart rate, stamina, strength, flexibility, speed, agility, balance, coordination, stretch (dynamic, static), personal best

### Lesson 1 — Why warm up? Heart rate and pulse
- **Duration:** 35 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can find my pulse, measure my heart rate, and explain why we warm up." (UK NC KS2 PE — health-related fitness; SHAPE Standard 3)
- **Hook:** "Put two fingers on your neck. What's that beating — and what will happen to it if we run?"
- **Key activity:** Teacher-led and active: find pulse (wrist or neck) and count for 15 seconds × 4; run a 3-minute pulse-raiser and measure again; dynamic warm-up (leg swings, lunges, arm circles); discuss why warm muscles are less likely to be injured.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why does your heart rate go up when you exercise?" → Muscles need more oxygen, so the heart pumps blood faster to deliver it.
- **Differentiation:** support: partner counts pulse aloud. / stretch: record resting, active and recovery heart rates and graph them (links to maths).
- **Materials:** stopwatches, recording sheets, cones. Safety: check for medical conditions (asthma inhalers available); clear, non-slip space.
- **Joy:** "Pulse detectives" — who can predict their heart rate after each activity?

### Lesson 2 — Components of fitness: circuit training
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can name components of fitness and match exercises to them." (UK NC KS2 PE — strength, flexibility; SHAPE Standard 3)
- **Hook:** "Is a sprinter fit in the same way as a gymnast or a marathon runner?"
- **Key activity:** Teacher-led circuit (6 stations × 45 s): step-ups (stamina), plank (core strength), shuttle run (speed), ladder drills (agility), balance on one leg (balance), sit-and-reach (flexibility); children record effort.
- **Check for understanding:** "Which component of fitness does a plank train, and which sport needs it?" → Core strength; e.g. gymnastics, swimming or football (stability).
- **Differentiation:** support: easier versions (knee plank, lower step). / stretch: design a new station and explain the component.
- **Materials:** benches or low steps, agility ladders, cones, mats, station cards, timer. Safety: stable steps; mats for floor work.
- **Joy:** Circuit with music.

### Lesson 3 — Stamina: pacing a run
- **Duration:** 35 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can pace myself to keep running or jogging for longer." (UK NC KS2 PE — running; SHAPE Standard 1)
- **Hook:** "The tortoise and the hare: why does starting too fast often lose?"
- **Key activity:** Teacher-led: jog–walk intervals; "talk test" (can you still talk?); 6-minute run around a marked course with lap counting; reflect on pacing.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why is it better to run at a steady pace in a long run?" → You don't run out of energy early; your body can keep supplying oxygen.
- **Differentiation:** support: jog–walk intervals. / stretch: aim for even lap times (negative split).
- **Materials:** cones for a course, lap counters, stopwatches. Safety: water available; shade in hot weather.
- **Joy:** "Beat your own laps" challenge.

### Lesson 4 — Flexibility and cool-downs
- **Duration:** 35 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can lead a safe cool-down with static stretches." (UK NC KS2 PE — flexibility; SHAPE Standard 3)
- **Hook:** Yoga-style poses inspired by animals: "Can you hold a stretch for 15 seconds without bouncing?"
- **Key activity:** Teacher models static stretches (hamstring, quad, calf, shoulder, triceps) held for 10–15 s without bouncing; children lead a partner.
- **Check for understanding:** "What is the difference between a warm-up stretch and a cool-down stretch?" → Warm-up stretches are moving (dynamic) to prepare; cool-down stretches are held (static) to relax muscles.
- **Differentiation:** support: picture cards. / stretch: lead the class cool-down.
- **Materials:** mats, stretch cards, calm music.
- **Joy:** Animal-pose yoga flow.

### Lesson 5 — Fitness testing: my personal bests
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can measure my fitness in simple tests and set a personal goal." (UK NC KS2 PE — compare performances to achieve personal best; SHAPE Standard 3)
- **Hook:** "Scientists measure — let's measure ourselves (and only compare with ourselves)."
- **Key activity:** Teacher-led stations: standing long jump (cm), 30 s skipping, sit-and-reach (cm), 20 m sprint (s); record and set one goal for Term 3 retest.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why should you compare your results with your own earlier results rather than other people's?" → Everyone grows and develops differently; improvement is what matters.
- **Differentiation:** support: partner support and shorter distances. / stretch: calculate improvement later in the year.
- **Materials:** tape measures, skipping ropes, sit-and-reach box (or ruler on a bench), stopwatches, record cards. Safety: non-slip surface.
- **Joy:** Personal-best cards.

### Lesson 6 — Design a warm-up
- **Duration:** 35 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can plan and lead a warm-up with a pulse-raiser, mobiliser and dynamic stretches." (UK NC KS2 PE; SHAPE Standard 4 — leadership)
- **Hook:** "Today you are the coach."
- **Key activity:** In groups, plan a 5-minute warm-up with three parts and lead it for another group; feedback.
- **Check for understanding:** "What are the three parts of a good warm-up, and why is each needed?" → Pulse-raiser (warm the body), mobiliser (loosen joints), dynamic stretches (prepare muscles for movement).
- **Differentiation:** support: warm-up card menu. / stretch: tailor the warm-up to a specific sport.
- **Materials:** cones, music, planning cards.
- **Joy:** Coaching the class.

**End-of-unit check:** Lead a three-part warm-up for a small group; explain what happens to heart rate during exercise and name four components of fitness with an exercise for each.

## Unit 2 — Invasion games: football, basketball and tag rugby

**Essential question:** How can a team keep the ball, make space and score — and how do defenders stop them?

**Key vocabulary:** invasion game, attack, defence, possession, space, width, support, marking, intercept, dribble, pass (push, chest, bounce), shoot, tag, onside, fair play

### Lesson 7 — Principles of invasion games
- **Duration:** 35 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can explain and use the principles of creating space in attack." (UK NC KS2 PE — competitive games, attacking and defending; SHAPE Standard 2)
- **Hook:** "Why do professional players spread out instead of all chasing the ball?"
- **Key activity:** Coach-led: 4 v 1 "keep-ball" in a grid, then 3 v 2; freeze play to show width, depth and support; pass and move.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why should you move into space after you pass?" → So you can receive the ball again and give your teammate an easy option.
- **Differentiation:** support: larger grid and more attackers. / stretch: smaller grid, fewer touches.
- **Materials:** bibs, cones, soft balls. Safety: clear boundaries; no contact.
- **Joy:** "Keep-ball" rounds.

### Lesson 8 — Football: passing and receiving
- **Duration:** 35 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can pass accurately with the inside of the foot and control the ball." (UK NC KS2 PE — games skills; SHAPE Standard 1)
- **Hook:** A clip from a women's World Cup match: "How do players pass so accurately?"
- **Key activity:** Coach-led: technique (non-kicking foot beside the ball, inside of foot, follow through); receiving with the sole or inside; passing gates in pairs.
- **Check for understanding:** "Where should your standing foot be when you pass, and why?" → Beside the ball, pointing at the target, for accuracy.
- **Differentiation:** support: stationary passing with bigger gates. / stretch: one-touch passing on the move.
- **Materials:** footballs (size 4), cones for gates.
- **Joy:** "Gate race" — pass through as many gates as possible in 60 s.

### Lesson 9 — Football: dribbling and small-sided game
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can dribble with close control, change direction and use it in a game." (UK NC KS2 PE — games; SHAPE Standard 1)
- **Hook:** "Can you keep the ball glued to your feet?"
- **Key activity:** Coach-led: dribbling with both feet, turns (drag-back, inside hook); 4 v 4 small-sided game with a condition (must pass before shooting).
- **Check for understanding:** "When should you dribble and when should you pass?" → Dribble into open space; pass when a teammate is in a better position or a defender blocks you.
- **Differentiation:** support: bigger ball, larger space. / stretch: use a turn to beat a defender in the game.
- **Materials:** footballs, cones, bibs, small goals.
- **Joy:** 4 v 4 mini-tournament.

### Lesson 10 — Basketball: dribbling and passing
- **Duration:** 35 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can dribble a basketball with either hand and use chest and bounce passes." (UK NC KS2 PE — games; SHAPE Standard 1)
- **Hook:** "Why can't you walk with the ball in basketball?"
- **Key activity:** Coach-led: dribbling with fingertips, eyes up; chest and bounce passes; the travelling rule; pass-and-move drills.
- **Check for understanding:** "When is a bounce pass better than a chest pass?" → When a defender's arms are up or blocking the direct line.
- **Differentiation:** support: stationary dribbling, larger ball. / stretch: dribble with the weaker hand around cones.
- **Materials:** basketballs (size 5), cones.
- **Joy:** "Dribble tag".

### Lesson 11 — Basketball: shooting and the triple-threat position
- **Duration:** 35 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can shoot with correct technique (BEEF) and use triple-threat position." (UK NC KS2 PE — games; SHAPE Standard 1)
- **Hook:** "Balance, Eyes, Elbow, Follow-through — BEEF!"
- **Key activity:** Coach-led: set shot from close range at lowered hoops; triple threat (ready to shoot, pass or dribble); 3 v 3 half-court.
- **Check for understanding:** "What is triple-threat position and why is it useful?" → Holding the ball ready to shoot, pass or dribble, so the defender can't tell what you'll do.
- **Differentiation:** support: lower hoop and closer range. / stretch: shoot after a pass on the move.
- **Materials:** basketballs, adjustable hoops.
- **Joy:** "Around the world" shooting game.

### Lesson 12 — Defending: marking and intercepting
- **Duration:** 35 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can mark an opponent and try to intercept safely." (UK NC KS2 PE — defending; SHAPE Standard 2)
- **Hook:** "Good defenders read the game. How?"
- **Key activity:** Coach-led: stay between opponent and goal, watch the ball and the player, side-on stance; interception games (no contact).
- **Check for understanding:** "Where should you stand when marking someone, and why?" → Between them and the goal, so you can see both the player and the ball.
- **Differentiation:** support: 2 v 1 defending practice. / stretch: zonal vs person-to-person marking discussion.
- **Materials:** bibs, balls, cones.
- **Joy:** "Intercept challenge" scoring.

### Lesson 13 — Tag rugby: running with the ball and passing backwards
- **Duration:** 35 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can run with the ball, pass backwards and play by the tag rules." (UK NC KS2 PE — games; SHAPE Standard 1)
- **Hook:** "In rugby you can only pass backwards — how do teams ever get forward?"
- **Key activity:** Coach-led: holding the ball in two hands, passing backwards, pulling tags (no contact), 4 v 4 game.
- **Check for understanding:** "How does a rugby team move forward if every pass must go backwards?" → By running forward with the ball; passes only help support runners.
- **Differentiation:** support: walking pace first. / stretch: use a dummy pass.
- **Materials:** tag belts, rugby balls (size 4), cones. Safety: no contact; tags only.
- **Joy:** Tag rugby game.

### Lesson 14 — Games: rules, fair play and officiating
- **Duration:** 35 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can officiate a small-sided game fairly and explain why rules matter." (UK NC KS2 PE — fair play; SHAPE Standard 4)
- **Hook:** "What happens to a game without a referee?"
- **Key activity:** Coach-led: children rotate as referee in 4 v 4 games; practise clear signals; discuss respect, handshakes and managing disagreement.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why is it important to accept a referee's decision, even when you disagree?" → It keeps the game fair and safe; everyone agrees to the rules.
- **Differentiation:** support: co-referee with a partner. / stretch: invent a new rule and explain its effect.
- **Materials:** whistles, bibs, balls, rule cards.
- **Joy:** "Ref for a round".

### Lesson 15 — Invent an invasion game
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can design an invasion game with rules that make it fair and fun." (UK NC KS2 PE — games; SHAPE Standard 2)
- **Hook:** "Many sports started as someone's playground invention."
- **Key activity:** Groups design a game (equipment, scoring, boundaries, rules for passing and defending); teach it to another group; refine.
- **Check for understanding:** "Which rule in your game helps attackers make space, and how?" → A specific rule and its tactical effect.
- **Differentiation:** support: a game template. / stretch: include a rule that encourages everyone to be involved.
- **Materials:** mixed equipment, planning cards.
- **Joy:** Playing each other's games.

### Lesson 16 — Unit 2 festival and assessment
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can apply skills and tactics in a game and evaluate my team's performance." (UK NC KS2 PE — evaluate; SHAPE Standard 2)
- **Hook:** "Mini-World Cup."
- **Key activity:** Round-robin 4 v 4 festival; teacher observes use of space, passing, marking; team huddle reflection.
- **Check for understanding:** "What tactic helped your team most today?" → A specific tactic (e.g. spreading wide).
- **Differentiation:** support: conditioned game. / stretch: captain role.
- **Materials:** equipment for chosen sport, bibs, scoreboards.
- **Joy:** Festival with team names from world countries.

**End-of-unit check:** In a 4 v 4 game, the child passes accurately, moves into space after passing and marks an opponent; they explain two attacking principles.

## Unit 3 — Gymnastics: balance, flight and sequences

**Essential question:** How can I link movements with control to create a gymnastic sequence?

**Key vocabulary:** balance, point, patch, base of support, counter-balance, counter-tension, roll (log, egg, forward, backward), jump shapes (straight, tuck, star, pike, straddle), flight, take-off, landing, sequence, canon, unison, symmetry, asymmetry

### Lesson 17 — Balances: points and patches
- **Duration:** 35 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can hold balances on different points and patches with control for 3 seconds." (UK NC KS2 PE — balance and control; SHAPE Standard 1)
- **Hook:** "How many body parts can you balance on — and which is hardest?"
- **Key activity:** Teacher-led: balances on 1–4 points; wide vs narrow bases; tension in the body; hold for 3 s.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why is a balance on two points harder than on four?" → The base of support is smaller, so it's harder to stay steady.
- **Differentiation:** support: wider bases. / stretch: balances on one point with extension.
- **Materials:** mats. Safety: mats spaced apart; no one balances on the head or neck.
- **Joy:** "Balance statues" game.

### Lesson 18 — Rolls
- **Duration:** 35 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can perform log, egg and teddy-bear rolls, and a forward roll (from a crouch) with control." (UK NC KS2 PE — gymnastics; SHAPE Standard 1)
- **Hook:** "How do gymnasts roll without hurting?"
- **Key activity:** Teacher-led progressions with safe technique (chin tucked, rounded back, hands take weight); forward roll down a gentle incline wedge if available.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why must you tuck your chin in a forward roll?" → To roll on your rounded back, not on your head or neck.
- **Differentiation:** support: log and egg rolls only. / stretch: forward roll to standing without hands pushing off knees.
- **Materials:** mats, incline wedge. Safety: teacher supervision and spotting; children with neck or back issues use alternatives.
- **Joy:** Roll sequences to music.

### Lesson 19 — Jumps and landings
- **Duration:** 35 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can perform the five basic jump shapes and land safely." (UK NC KS2 PE — gymnastics; SHAPE Standard 1)
- **Hook:** Slow-motion gymnastics vault clip: "What shapes do you see in the air?"
- **Key activity:** Teacher-led: straight, tuck, star, pike and straddle jumps; safe landing (bent knees, arms forward, feet hip-width).
- **Check for understanding:** "Why do we bend our knees when we land?" → To absorb the force and protect our joints.
- **Differentiation:** support: jumps from the floor only. / stretch: jumps from a low box onto a mat with a half-turn.
- **Materials:** mats, low box or bench. Safety: landing mats, one at a time off apparatus.
- **Joy:** "Shape in the air" photos.

### Lesson 20 — Flight from apparatus
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can jump from low apparatus with a clear shape and controlled landing." (UK NC KS2 PE — gymnastics; SHAPE Standard 1)
- **Hook:** "Take-off, flight, landing — the three parts of every vault."
- **Key activity:** Teacher-led with apparatus: benches, low boxes, springboard (if trained); jump with shapes; station rotation.
- **Check for understanding:** "What are the three phases of a jump from apparatus?" → Take-off, flight, landing.
- **Differentiation:** support: step off rather than jump. / stretch: add a turn.
- **Materials:** benches, boxes, mats. Safety: apparatus checked; children taught to carry and place equipment safely.
- **Joy:** Apparatus circuit.

### Lesson 21 — Partner balances: counter-balance and counter-tension
- **Duration:** 35 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can perform partner balances using counter-balance and counter-tension safely." (UK NC KS2 PE — gymnastics; SHAPE Standard 4)
- **Hook:** "Can two people balance together in ways they couldn't alone?"
- **Key activity:** Teacher-led: push (counter-balance) and pull (counter-tension) balances with partners of similar size; communication cues.
- **Check for understanding:** "What is the difference between counter-balance and counter-tension?" → Counter-balance: partners push against each other; counter-tension: partners pull away from each other.
- **Differentiation:** support: simple pull balances holding wrists. / stretch: a group balance of three.
- **Materials:** mats. Safety: no standing on partners; agree "stop" word.
- **Joy:** Partner balance photo gallery.

### Lesson 22 — Sequences: linking movements
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can create a sequence of 6 movements with smooth links." (UK NC KS2 PE — develop sequences; SHAPE Standard 1)
- **Hook:** "Olympic floor routines are sequences. What makes them flow?"
- **Key activity:** Teacher-led: plan a sequence (balance, roll, jump, travel, balance, finish) with clear start and end positions; practise links.
- **Check for understanding:** "How did you link your roll to your balance smoothly?" → A specific transition (e.g. roll up to standing then step into balance).
- **Differentiation:** support: 4 movements. / stretch: include levels, directions and speed changes.
- **Materials:** mats, sequence cards.
- **Joy:** Performing sequences.

### Lesson 23 — Pair sequences: unison and canon
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can perform a pair sequence using unison and canon." (UK NC KS2 PE — gymnastics; links to music rounds; SHAPE Standard 1)
- **Hook:** "Same moves at the same time, or one after the other — which looks better?"
- **Key activity:** Teacher-led: pairs combine sequences; unison (together) and canon (one after another); include one partner balance.
- **Check for understanding:** "What's the difference between unison and canon, and where did you use each?" → Unison: together; canon: one after another — specific moments named.
- **Differentiation:** support: 4-move pair sequence. / stretch: add mirroring (symmetry).
- **Materials:** mats, apparatus.
- **Joy:** Pair performances.

### Lesson 24 — Gymnastics performance and evaluation
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can perform my sequence and evaluate performances using gymnastics criteria." (UK NC KS2 PE — evaluate; SHAPE Standard 4)
- **Hook:** "Judges' panel."
- **Key activity:** Perform sequences; peers give feedback on control, extension, links and landings; video for self-review.
- **Check for understanding:** "What would you change to improve your sequence's control?" → A specific improvement.
- **Differentiation:** support: feedback frame. / stretch: act as judge using criteria.
- **Materials:** mats, apparatus, tablet for filming (with consent).
- **Joy:** Judges' panel scores (for effort and improvement).

**End-of-unit check:** Perform a pair sequence of 6–8 movements including a balance, a roll, a jump with a clear shape and a partner balance, using unison and canon; evaluate it with one strength and one improvement.

## Unit 4 — Dance around the world and choreography

**Essential question:** How do dancers tell stories and share culture through movement?

**Key vocabulary:** choreography, motif, formation, unison, canon, levels, dynamics, gesture, mirroring, Bollywood, capoeira, ginga, dragon dance, samba, performance

### Lesson 25 — Samba steps (links to music)
- **Duration:** 35 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can perform basic samba steps in time to music." (UK NC KS2 PE — dance; SHAPE Standard 1)
- **Hook:** Rio carnival clip (links to music Unit 4).
- **Key activity:** Teacher-led: basic samba step (ball-change rhythm), arm styling, travelling; perform in lines.
- **Check for understanding:** "How does the music's rhythm tell you how to move?" → The strong beats guide the step changes.
- **Differentiation:** support: slower tempo, marching version. / stretch: add turns.
- **Materials:** samba music, open space.
- **Joy:** Carnival parade.

### Lesson 26 — Bollywood dance: gestures and storytelling
- **Duration:** 35 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can perform a Bollywood-style routine using expressive gestures." (UK NC KS2 PE — dance; SHAPE Standard 1)
- **Hook:** A clip from a Bollywood film dance number: "The dancers tell a story with their hands and faces."
- **Key activity:** Teacher-led: signature moves (e.g. "screw the light bulb", "pat the dog"), hand gestures influenced by Indian classical dance, facial expression; short routine.
- **Check for understanding:** "How did you use gestures to show a feeling or story?" → A specific gesture linked to meaning.
- **Differentiation:** support: repeat a 4-count phrase. / stretch: add your own gesture to the routine.
- **Materials:** music, space.
- **Joy:** Bollywood routine performance.

### Lesson 27 — Capoeira: Afro-Brazilian movement
- **Duration:** 35 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can perform the ginga and simple capoeira movements with control." (UK NC KS2 PE — dance; SHAPE Standard 1)
- **Hook:** A capoeira *roda* (circle) with the berimbau: "Is it a dance, a game or a martial art?"
- **Key activity:** Teacher-led: history (developed by enslaved Africans in Brazil; recognised by UNESCO); the *ginga* (rocking base step), dodges (*esquiva*) and a slow kick with no contact; partner "conversations" of movement.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why is capoeira sometimes called both a dance and a martial art?" → It combines music and flowing movement with the moves of a fighting art, played as a game.
- **Differentiation:** support: ginga only. / stretch: combine ginga, esquiva and a turn smoothly.
- **Materials:** capoeira music (berimbau), space. Safety: no contact; control every kick.
- **Joy:** A class *roda*.

### Lesson 28 — Chinese dragon and ribbon dance
- **Duration:** 35 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can move in unison as part of a group dragon and use ribbons to create shapes." (UK NC KS2 PE — dance; SHAPE Standard 1)
- **Hook:** Lunar New Year dragon dance video.
- **Key activity:** Teacher-led: group travelling in a line (the "dragon") with following and rippling; ribbon circles and figure-eights.
- **Check for understanding:** "What does the group need to do to make the dragon look like one creature?" → Move in unison, keep spacing, follow the leader's pathway.
- **Differentiation:** support: ribbon only. / stretch: lead the dragon.
- **Materials:** ribbons, a cloth "dragon", drum or music.
- **Joy:** Dragon dance around the hall.

### Lesson 29 — Creating a motif
- **Duration:** 35 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can create a movement motif and develop it using levels, speed and direction." (UK NC KS2 PE — dance, composition; SHAPE Standard 1)
- **Hook:** Theme from science: "the Solar System" or "a volcano erupting" (links to other subjects).
- **Key activity:** Teacher-led: create a 4-count motif; develop it (bigger/smaller, faster/slower, different level/direction).
- **Check for understanding:** "How did you change your motif to show a different mood?" → A named change (e.g. slower and lower for calm).
- **Differentiation:** support: choose from motif cards. / stretch: develop the motif three ways.
- **Materials:** music, theme cards.
- **Joy:** Motif sharing.

### Lesson 30 — Group choreography: formations and relationships
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can choreograph a group dance with formations, unison and canon." (UK NC KS2 PE — dance; SHAPE Standard 1)
- **Hook:** Overhead view of a dance formation: "Why do choreographers care about shapes on the floor?"
- **Key activity:** Groups combine motifs; use formations (line, circle, V), unison, canon, mirroring; plan with a floor map.
- **Check for understanding:** "Point to where your group uses canon and explain why it works there." → A reasoned choice.
- **Differentiation:** support: teacher-provided structure. / stretch: add a moment of contrast.
- **Materials:** music, floor-plan sheets.
- **Joy:** Choreographing.

### Lesson 31 — Rehearsal and feedback
- **Duration:** 35 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can improve my group's dance using feedback." (UK NC KS2 PE — evaluate; SHAPE Standard 4)
- **Hook:** Film and watch back.
- **Key activity:** Watch video; "two stars and a wish"; refine timing, spacing and expression.
- **Check for understanding:** "What did you improve after feedback?" → Specific change.
- **Differentiation:** support: one target. / stretch: act as rehearsal director.
- **Materials:** tablet, music.
- **Joy:** Before/after video.

### Lesson 32 — Dance performance
- **Duration:** 40 min

## Unit 7 — Swimming and water safety

**Essential question:** How can I swim confidently and keep myself and others safe in and around water?

**Key vocabulary:** front crawl, backstroke, breaststroke, streamline, glide, float, tread water, self-rescue, cold water shock, lifeguard, flags, "Float to Live", buoyancy aid

### Lesson 49 — Water confidence and floating
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can float on my front and back and recover to standing." (UK NC KS2 PE — swimming and water safety; SHAPE Standard 1)
- **Hook:** "What helps a body float?"
- **Key activity:** Qualified-instructor-led, in groups by ability: star floats, mushroom float, recover to standing; "Float to Live" — lean back, spread arms and legs, float and breathe until calm.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why is floating on your back a good first thing to do if you fall into cold water unexpectedly?" → It helps you control your breathing through cold water shock until you can call for help or swim to safety.
- **Differentiation:** support: floats and noodles; shallow water. / stretch: float for 60 seconds without aids.
- **Materials:** pool with qualified lifeguard and instructors, floats, noodles. Safety: ratios per pool rules; medical forms checked.
- **Joy:** Floating "starfish" circle.

### Lesson 50 — Front crawl
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can swim front crawl with coordinated arms, legs and breathing." (UK NC KS2 PE — swim competently using a range of strokes)
- **Hook:** "Why is front crawl the fastest stroke?"
- **Key activity:** Instructor-led: streamlined push and glide; flutter kick from the hips; arm action; side breathing; widths then lengths.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why should you breathe to the side rather than lifting your head up?" → It keeps the body flat and streamlined, so you swim faster and more easily.
- **Differentiation:** support: kick with a float. / stretch: bilateral breathing every three strokes.
- **Materials:** pool, floats, pull buoys.
- **Joy:** Glide competitions.

### Lesson 51 — Backstroke and breaststroke
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can swim backstroke and breaststroke with basic technique." (UK NC KS2 PE — range of strokes)
- **Hook:** "Which stroke lets you breathe whenever you like?"
- **Key activity:** Instructor-led: backstroke (flat body, ears in water, alternating arms); breaststroke (frog kick, pull–breathe–kick–glide timing).
- **Check for understanding:** "What is the timing of breaststroke?" → Pull, breathe, kick, glide.
- **Differentiation:** support: one stroke with aids. / stretch: swim 25 m of each stroke.
- **Materials:** pool, floats.
- **Joy:** Stroke relay.

### Lesson 52 — Treading water and self-rescue
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can tread water and use self-rescue skills." (UK NC KS2 PE — perform safe self-rescue)
- **Hook:** "What would you do if you fell in wearing clothes?"
- **Key activity:** Instructor-led: tread water (eggbeater or cycling legs, sculling hands); swimming in light clothes; HELP position (Heat Escape Lessening Posture) with a buoyancy aid; signal for help.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why should you keep your clothes on if you fall into cold water with a buoyancy aid?" → Clothes trap a layer of water that helps keep you warmer; the aid keeps you afloat.
- **Differentiation:** support: tread with a noodle. / stretch: tread for 60 seconds, then swim 25 m.
- **Materials:** pool, buoyancy aids, old clothes (pyjamas). Safety: extra adult supervision.
- **Joy:** "Pyjama swim".

### Lesson 53 — Water safety in the real world
- **Duration:** 35 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can explain how to stay safe near rivers, beaches, lakes and pools, and how to help someone without getting in." (UK NC KS2 PE — water safety; Health education — being safe)
- **Hook:** Photos of beach flags used in many countries (red and yellow — swim between them; red — do not swim; check local flag systems), rip currents and frozen lakes.
- **Key activity:** Classroom or poolside: water safety code (stop and think, stay together, float, call for help); beach flags; rip currents (swim parallel to shore, or float and signal); rescue without entering (reach or throw, don't go).
- **Check for understanding:** "If a friend is in trouble in the water, why shouldn't you jump in to help?" → You could get into trouble too; instead call for help and reach or throw something that floats.
- **Differentiation:** support: picture sorting of safe/unsafe. / stretch: plan a family beach safety briefing.
- **Materials:** flag images, rescue throw-bag or float, scenario cards.
- **Joy:** "Throw-bag" rescue practice (on land).

### Lesson 54 — Swimming assessment: 25 metres and safety skills
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can swim 25 m unaided and show self-rescue skills." (UK NC KS2 PE — swim at least 25 m competently, confidently and proficiently)
- **Hook:** "Swim passport stamps."
- **Key activity:** Instructor-led assessment in ability groups: 25 m, strokes, treading water, floating; record for the Year 5 teacher.
- **Check for understanding:** "What would you do first if you fell into cold water?" → Stay calm, float on your back to control breathing, then call for help or swim to safety.
- **Differentiation:** support: assessed on progress (e.g. 10 m, floating). / stretch: 50 m and multiple strokes.
- **Materials:** pool, record cards.
- **Joy:** Swim passport certificates.

**End-of-unit check:** Swim 25 m with a recognised stroke (or show clear progress towards it); float, tread water and explain "Float to Live" and "reach or throw, don't go".

## Teacher guidance

**Delivery.** These lessons are designed to be **delivered by a human adult in a real space** — a teacher, coach or parent. An app can prepare (rules, videos, key points), track personal bests and prompt reflection, but should never replace supervision.

**Safety first.** Warm up every lesson; check medical needs (asthma, allergies, diabetes, epilepsy, heart conditions) and keep medication nearby; set clear boundaries and stop signals; check equipment and surfaces; follow pool and trip regulations and ratios; use qualified instructors for swimming and trained adults for first aid and gymnastics spotting; adapt to heat and cold (water, shade, layers).

**Common misconceptions (and fixes).**
- *"Stretching before exercise means holding stretches."* — use dynamic movement to warm up; save held stretches for the cool-down.
- *"Everyone should chase the ball."* — teach space, width and support with freeze-frames.
- *"In rugby you can throw the ball forward."* — teach and practise backward passing.
- *"Sweets and fizzy drinks are fine if you brush your teeth."* — frequency of sugar matters.
- *"If someone is drowning, jump in."* — "reach or throw, don't go"; call for help.
- *"Fitness means being the fastest."* — fitness has many components; focus on personal progress.

**Inclusion and culture.** Offer alternatives for faith and culture (modest swimwear, fasting adaptations, mixed/single-sex groupings where needed); adapt for disability (e.g. seated volleyball, guide runners, larger or brighter balls); present dances with their cultural origins and respect.

**Health education.** Puberty, body safety and mental health lessons follow the school's policy, with families informed and able to see materials; safeguarding procedures are followed for any disclosure.

**Vertical links.** Year 3: fundamental movements, simple games, basic gymnastics and dance, water confidence. Year 4: tactics across games, sequences with partners, world dance and choreography, 25 m swimming and self-rescue, measured athletics, orienteering, a health unit. Year 5: more competitive sport and personal fitness planning, advanced sequences and choreography, and extended health education.

**Cross-curricular links.** Science (heart and pulse; forces in sport; growing up), maths (measurement, timing, graphs, percentages), geography (maps and compasses), music (samba, rhythm and dance), languages (Spanish healthy advice), life skills (resilience, teamwork, goal setting).
