# Year 4 PE & Health (ages 9–10) — Scope & Sequence

> Part of **the best international primary school** (see [`../../../SCHOOL-CHARTER.md`](../../../SCHOOL-CHARTER.md)).
> Written to [`../../PLANNING-BRIEF.md`](../../PLANNING-BRIEF.md).
> Codes: **UK NC KS2 PE** (running, jumping, throwing and catching; competitive games; flexibility, strength, technique, control and balance; dance; outdoor and adventurous activity; swimming and water safety) with **health education** (England's statutory Health Education, primary), and **SHAPE America** National PE Standards (Grade 4; S1–S5) and **National Health Education Standards** (Grades 3–5).

## Year overview

Year 4 PE & Health turns **fundamental movement into real sport, dance and adventure** — and teaches children how to look after their bodies and minds. Children learn the *principles* behind games (space, width, support, marking) so they can play any invasion or net game well; they build gymnastic sequences with balance, flight and partner work; they choreograph dance from several cultures; they become safer, stronger swimmers who know how to self-rescue; they run, jump and throw with better technique and measure their improvement; and they navigate outdoors with a map and compass. A dedicated **health unit** retrieves the Year 3 basics and goes further — food labels and sugar, the immune system and medicines, sun, heat and cold, puberty, mental wellbeing, staying safe when out and about, and the first-aid primary survey and defibrillators — and every lesson includes a warm-up and cool-down that children increasingly lead themselves.

**Delivery.** PE is **human-delivered and active**: a teacher, coach or parent leads every session in a hall, playground, field or pool. An app or tutor can support with explanations, rules, videos, reflection questions and progress tracking, but the doing is physical. Every lesson follows: warm-up (raise heart rate, mobilise) → skill teaching (demonstrate, practise, feedback) → apply in a game or performance → cool-down and reflection.

**What Year 4 assumes (from Year 3):** fundamental movement skills (running, jumping, hopping, throwing, catching, kicking, dodging) with reasonable control; simple team games and rules; basic balances and rolls; simple dance phrases; health basics (balanced plate, allergies and asthma, road safety, food hygiene, PANTS online and offline, the stress response, first aid for burns and nosebleeds); setting a map and pace counting; spotting rip currents and safe land-based rescue; being able to enter the water and move with confidence (swimming ability varies — see Unit 7).
**What Year 5 relies on:** tactical understanding of attack and defence across game types; sequences combining balance, flight and partner work; secure swimming of 25 m and self-rescue; accurate measurement of personal performance; knowledge of healthy habits and puberty, ready for more independent fitness planning and competitive sport.

**Inclusion.** Adapt every activity for disability, injury, faith and culture (e.g. modest swimwear, fasting periods); use STEP (Space, Task, Equipment, People) to make tasks easier or harder; celebrate personal bests over winning.

## Unit 1 — Fit for life: warm-ups, fitness and the healthy heart

**Essential question:** What does it mean to be fit, and how can I train my body safely?

**Key vocabulary:** warm-up, cool-down, pulse, resting / working / recovery heart rate, stamina, strength, flexibility, speed, agility, balance, coordination, stretch (dynamic, static), progression, specificity, rest and recovery, personal best

### Lesson 1 — Heart rate: resting, working and recovery
- **Duration:** 35 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can measure my resting, working and recovery heart rates and explain what recovery tells us about fitness." (UK NC KS2 PE — health-related fitness; SHAPE Standard 3; links to science)
- **Hook:** "Two runners finish the same race. One runner's heart rate is back to normal in 2 minutes, the other's in 6. Who is fitter?"
- **Key activity:** Teacher-led and active: retrieve taking a pulse (wrist or neck, count for 15 seconds × 4); measure resting heart rate; 3-minute pulse-raiser; measure the working rate; measure again after 1, 2 and 3 minutes of recovery; plot the class's anonymous results as line graphs (maths link); the heart is a muscle that grows stronger with training and pumps more blood with each beat.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why does a fitter person's heart rate usually return to resting faster after exercise?" → their stronger heart pumps more blood with each beat, so it doesn't need to beat as fast, for as long, to deliver the oxygen the muscles need
- **Differentiation:** support: partner counts the pulse aloud. / stretch: graph your three rates and describe the shape of the recovery curve.
- **Materials:** stopwatches, recording sheets, cones. Safety: check for medical conditions (asthma inhalers available); clear, non-slip space; heart rates are personal — no ranking.
- **Joy:** "Pulse detectives" — predict, then measure, your heart rate after each activity.

### Lesson 2 — Components of fitness: circuit training
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can name components of fitness and match exercises to them." (UK NC KS2 PE — strength, flexibility; SHAPE Standard 3)
- **Hook:** "Is a sprinter fit in the same way as a gymnast or a marathon runner?"
- **Key activity:** Teacher-led circuit (6 stations × 45 s): step-ups (stamina), plank (core strength), shuttle run (speed), ladder drills (agility), balance on one leg (balance), sit-and-reach (flexibility); children record effort.
- **Check for understanding:** "Which component of fitness does a plank train, and which sport needs it?" → Core strength; e.g. gymnastics, swimming or football (stability).
- **Differentiation:** support: easier versions (knee plank, lower step). / stretch: design a new station and explain the component.
- **Materials:** benches or low steps, agility ladders, cones, mats, station cards, timer. Safety: stable steps; mats for floor work.
- **Joy:** Circuit with music.

### Lesson 3 — Stamina: pacing a run
- **Duration:** 35 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can pace myself to keep running or jogging for longer." (UK NC KS2 PE — running; SHAPE Standard 1)
- **Hook:** "The tortoise and the hare: why does starting too fast often lose?"
- **Key activity:** Teacher-led: jog–walk intervals; "talk test" (can you still talk?); 6-minute run around a marked course with lap counting; reflect on pacing.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why is it better to run at a steady pace in a long run?" → You don't run out of energy early; your body can keep supplying oxygen.
- **Differentiation:** support: jog–walk intervals. / stretch: aim for even lap times (negative split).
- **Materials:** cones for a course, lap counters, stopwatches. Safety: water available; shade in hot weather.
- **Joy:** "Beat your own laps" challenge.

### Lesson 4 — Flexibility and cool-downs
- **Duration:** 35 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can lead a safe cool-down with static stretches." (UK NC KS2 PE — flexibility; SHAPE Standard 3)
- **Hook:** Yoga-style poses inspired by animals: "Can you hold a stretch for 15 seconds without bouncing?"
- **Key activity:** Teacher models static stretches (hamstring, quad, calf, shoulder, triceps) held for 10–15 s without bouncing; children lead a partner.
- **Check for understanding:** "What is the difference between a warm-up stretch and a cool-down stretch?" → Warm-up stretches are moving (dynamic) to prepare; cool-down stretches are held (static) to relax muscles.
- **Differentiation:** support: picture cards. / stretch: lead the class cool-down.
- **Materials:** mats, stretch cards, calm music.
- **Joy:** Animal-pose yoga flow.

### Lesson 5 — Fitness testing: my personal bests
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can measure my fitness in simple tests and set a personal goal." (UK NC KS2 PE — compare performances to achieve personal best; SHAPE Standard 3)
- **Hook:** "Scientists measure — let's measure ourselves (and only compare with ourselves)."
- **Key activity:** Teacher-led stations: standing long jump (cm), 30 s skipping, sit-and-reach (cm), 20 m sprint (s); record and set one goal for Term 3 retest.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why should you compare your results with your own earlier results rather than other people's?" → Everyone grows and develops differently; improvement is what matters.
- **Differentiation:** support: partner support and shorter distances. / stretch: calculate improvement later in the year.
- **Materials:** tape measures, skipping ropes, sit-and-reach box (or ruler on a bench), stopwatches, record cards. Safety: non-slip surface.
- **Joy:** Personal-best cards.

### Lesson 6 — Training principles: how bodies get fitter
- **Duration:** 35 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can explain how training makes the body fitter and plan a three-week challenge using small steps and rest." (UK NC KS2 PE — develop strength and stamina; SHAPE Standard 3)
- **Hook:** "You want to skip 100 times without stopping. Should you try 100 on day one?"
- **Key activity:** Groups lead the three-part warm-up (retrieval from Year 3). New: training principles — progression (add a little more time, repetitions or difficulty each week), specificity (train the thing you want to improve), rest and recovery (bodies adapt and get stronger while resting), 'use it or lose it' (fitness fades if you stop); plan a three-week skipping or plank challenge with small weekly steps and rest days; complete week 1 and record it.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why does a plan that adds a little each week work better than doing as much as possible on day one?" → the body adapts gradually; small increases with rest let the muscles, heart and lungs get stronger without injury, soreness or giving up
- **Differentiation:** support: planning template with suggested weekly steps. / stretch: explain which principle a footballer ignores if they only ever train by swimming (specificity).
- **Materials:** skipping ropes, mats, challenge planning cards, timer.
- **Joy:** "Challenge cards" with weekly progress stickers.

**End-of-unit check:** Lead a three-part warm-up for a small group; explain what recovery heart rate shows about fitness; name four components of fitness with an exercise for each; plan a three-week challenge using progression and rest.

## Unit 2 — Invasion games: football, basketball and tag rugby

**Essential question:** How can a team keep the ball, make space and score — and how do defenders stop them?

**Key vocabulary:** invasion game, attack, defence, possession, space, width, support, marking, intercept, dribble, pass (push, chest, bounce), shoot, tag, onside, fair play

### Lesson 7 — Principles of invasion games
- **Duration:** 35 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can explain and use the principles of creating space in attack." (UK NC KS2 PE — competitive games, attacking and defending; SHAPE Standard 2)
- **Hook:** "Why do professional players spread out instead of all chasing the ball?"
- **Key activity:** Coach-led: 4 v 1 "keep-ball" in a grid, then 3 v 2; freeze play to show width, depth and support; pass and move.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why should you move into space after you pass?" → So you can receive the ball again and give your teammate an easy option.
- **Differentiation:** support: larger grid and more attackers. / stretch: smaller grid, fewer touches.
- **Materials:** bibs, cones, soft balls. Safety: clear boundaries; no contact.
- **Joy:** "Keep-ball" rounds.

### Lesson 8 — Football: passing and receiving
- **Duration:** 35 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can pass accurately with the inside of the foot and control the ball." (UK NC KS2 PE — games skills; SHAPE Standard 1)
- **Hook:** A clip from a women's World Cup match: "How do players pass so accurately?"
- **Key activity:** Coach-led: technique (non-kicking foot beside the ball, inside of foot, follow through); receiving with the sole or inside; passing gates in pairs.
- **Check for understanding:** "Where should your standing foot be when you pass, and why?" → Beside the ball, pointing at the target, for accuracy.
- **Differentiation:** support: stationary passing with bigger gates. / stretch: one-touch passing on the move.
- **Materials:** footballs (size 4), cones for gates.
- **Joy:** "Gate race" — pass through as many gates as possible in 60 s.

### Lesson 9 — Football: dribbling and small-sided game
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can dribble with close control, change direction and use it in a game." (UK NC KS2 PE — games; SHAPE Standard 1)
- **Hook:** "Can you keep the ball glued to your feet?"
- **Key activity:** Coach-led: dribbling with both feet, turns (drag-back, inside hook); 4 v 4 small-sided game with a condition (must pass before shooting).
- **Check for understanding:** "When should you dribble and when should you pass?" → Dribble into open space; pass when a teammate is in a better position or a defender blocks you.
- **Differentiation:** support: bigger ball, larger space. / stretch: use a turn to beat a defender in the game.
- **Materials:** footballs, cones, bibs, small goals.
- **Joy:** 4 v 4 mini-tournament.

### Lesson 10 — Basketball: dribbling and passing
- **Duration:** 35 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can dribble a basketball with either hand and use chest and bounce passes." (UK NC KS2 PE — games; SHAPE Standard 1)
- **Hook:** "Why can't you walk with the ball in basketball?"
- **Key activity:** Coach-led: dribbling with fingertips, eyes up; chest and bounce passes; the travelling rule; pass-and-move drills.
- **Check for understanding:** "When is a bounce pass better than a chest pass?" → When a defender's arms are up or blocking the direct line.
- **Differentiation:** support: stationary dribbling, larger ball. / stretch: dribble with the weaker hand around cones.
- **Materials:** basketballs (size 5), cones.
- **Joy:** "Dribble tag".

### Lesson 11 — Basketball: shooting and the triple-threat position
- **Duration:** 35 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can shoot with correct technique (BEEF) and use triple-threat position." (UK NC KS2 PE — games; SHAPE Standard 1)
- **Hook:** "Balance, Eyes, Elbow, Follow-through — BEEF!"
- **Key activity:** Coach-led: set shot from close range at lowered hoops; triple threat (ready to shoot, pass or dribble); 3 v 3 half-court.
- **Check for understanding:** "What is triple-threat position and why is it useful?" → Holding the ball ready to shoot, pass or dribble, so the defender can't tell what you'll do.
- **Differentiation:** support: lower hoop and closer range. / stretch: shoot after a pass on the move.
- **Materials:** basketballs, adjustable hoops.
- **Joy:** "Around the world" shooting game.

### Lesson 12 — Defending: marking and intercepting
- **Duration:** 35 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can mark an opponent and try to intercept safely." (UK NC KS2 PE — defending; SHAPE Standard 2)
- **Hook:** "Good defenders read the game. How?"
- **Key activity:** Coach-led: stay between opponent and goal, watch the ball and the player, side-on stance; interception games (no contact).
- **Check for understanding:** "Where should you stand when marking someone, and why?" → Between them and the goal, so you can see both the player and the ball.
- **Differentiation:** support: 2 v 1 defending practice. / stretch: zonal vs person-to-person marking discussion.
- **Materials:** bibs, balls, cones.
- **Joy:** "Intercept challenge" scoring.

### Lesson 13 — Tag rugby: running with the ball and passing backwards
- **Duration:** 35 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can run with the ball, pass backwards and play by the tag rules." (UK NC KS2 PE — games; SHAPE Standard 1)
- **Hook:** "In rugby you can only pass backwards — how do teams ever get forward?"
- **Key activity:** Coach-led: holding the ball in two hands, passing backwards, pulling tags (no contact), 4 v 4 game.
- **Check for understanding:** "How does a rugby team move forward if every pass must go backwards?" → By running forward with the ball; passes only help support runners.
- **Differentiation:** support: walking pace first. / stretch: use a dummy pass.
- **Materials:** tag belts, rugby balls (size 4), cones. Safety: no contact; tags only.
- **Joy:** Tag rugby game.

### Lesson 14 — Games: rules, fair play and officiating
- **Duration:** 35 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can officiate a small-sided game fairly and explain why rules matter." (UK NC KS2 PE — fair play; SHAPE Standard 4)
- **Hook:** "What happens to a game without a referee?"
- **Key activity:** Coach-led: children rotate as referee in 4 v 4 games; practise clear signals; discuss respect, handshakes and managing disagreement.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why is it important to accept a referee's decision, even when you disagree?" → It keeps the game fair and safe; everyone agrees to the rules.
- **Differentiation:** support: co-referee with a partner. / stretch: invent a new rule and explain its effect.
- **Materials:** whistles, bibs, balls, rule cards.
- **Joy:** "Ref for a round".

### Lesson 15 — Invent an invasion game
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can design an invasion game with rules that make it fair and fun." (UK NC KS2 PE — games; SHAPE Standard 2)
- **Hook:** "Many sports started as someone's playground invention."
- **Key activity:** Groups design a game (equipment, scoring, boundaries, rules for passing and defending); teach it to another group; refine.
- **Check for understanding:** "Which rule in your game helps attackers make space, and how?" → A specific rule and its tactical effect.
- **Differentiation:** support: a game template. / stretch: include a rule that encourages everyone to be involved.
- **Materials:** mixed equipment, planning cards.
- **Joy:** Playing each other's games.

### Lesson 16 — Unit 2 festival and assessment
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can apply skills and tactics in a game and evaluate my team's performance." (UK NC KS2 PE — evaluate; SHAPE Standard 2)
- **Hook:** "Mini-World Cup."
- **Key activity:** Round-robin 4 v 4 festival; teacher observes use of space, passing, marking; team huddle reflection.
- **Check for understanding:** "What tactic helped your team most today?" → A specific tactic (e.g. spreading wide).
- **Differentiation:** support: conditioned game. / stretch: captain role.
- **Materials:** equipment for chosen sport, bibs, scoreboards.
- **Joy:** Festival with team names from world countries.

**End-of-unit check:** In a 4 v 4 game, the child passes accurately, moves into space after passing and marks an opponent; they explain two attacking principles.

## Unit 3 — Gymnastics: balance, flight and sequences

**Essential question:** How can I link movements with control to create a gymnastic sequence?

**Key vocabulary:** balance, point, patch, base of support, counter-balance, counter-tension, roll (log, egg, forward, backward), jump shapes (straight, tuck, star, pike, straddle), flight, take-off, landing, sequence, canon, unison, symmetry, asymmetry

### Lesson 17 — Balances: points and patches
- **Duration:** 35 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can hold balances on different points and patches with control for 3 seconds." (UK NC KS2 PE — balance and control; SHAPE Standard 1)
- **Hook:** "How many body parts can you balance on — and which is hardest?"
- **Key activity:** Teacher-led: balances on 1–4 points; wide vs narrow bases; tension in the body; hold for 3 s.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why is a balance on two points harder than on four?" → The base of support is smaller, so it's harder to stay steady.
- **Differentiation:** support: wider bases. / stretch: balances on one point with extension.
- **Materials:** mats. Safety: mats spaced apart; no one balances on the head or neck.
- **Joy:** "Balance statues" game.

### Lesson 18 — Rolls
- **Duration:** 35 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can perform log, egg and teddy-bear rolls, and a forward roll (from a crouch) with control." (UK NC KS2 PE — gymnastics; SHAPE Standard 1)
- **Hook:** "How do gymnasts roll without hurting?"
- **Key activity:** Teacher-led progressions with safe technique (chin tucked, rounded back, hands take weight); forward roll down a gentle incline wedge if available.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why must you tuck your chin in a forward roll?" → To roll on your rounded back, not on your head or neck.
- **Differentiation:** support: log and egg rolls only. / stretch: forward roll to standing without hands pushing off knees.
- **Materials:** mats, incline wedge. Safety: teacher supervision and spotting; children with neck or back issues use alternatives.
- **Joy:** Roll sequences to music.

### Lesson 19 — Jumps and landings
- **Duration:** 35 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can perform the five basic jump shapes and land safely." (UK NC KS2 PE — gymnastics; SHAPE Standard 1)
- **Hook:** Slow-motion gymnastics vault clip: "What shapes do you see in the air?"
- **Key activity:** Teacher-led: straight, tuck, star, pike and straddle jumps; safe landing (bent knees, arms forward, feet hip-width).
- **Check for understanding:** "Why do we bend our knees when we land?" → To absorb the force and protect our joints.
- **Differentiation:** support: jumps from the floor only. / stretch: jumps from a low box onto a mat with a half-turn.
- **Materials:** mats, low box or bench. Safety: landing mats, one at a time off apparatus.
- **Joy:** "Shape in the air" photos.

### Lesson 20 — Flight from apparatus
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can jump from low apparatus with a clear shape and controlled landing." (UK NC KS2 PE — gymnastics; SHAPE Standard 1)
- **Hook:** "Take-off, flight, landing — the three parts of every vault."
- **Key activity:** Teacher-led with apparatus: benches, low boxes, springboard (if trained); jump with shapes; station rotation.
- **Check for understanding:** "What are the three phases of a jump from apparatus?" → Take-off, flight, landing.
- **Differentiation:** support: step off rather than jump. / stretch: add a turn.
- **Materials:** benches, boxes, mats. Safety: apparatus checked; children taught to carry and place equipment safely.
- **Joy:** Apparatus circuit.

### Lesson 21 — Partner balances: counter-balance and counter-tension
- **Duration:** 35 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can perform partner balances using counter-balance and counter-tension safely." (UK NC KS2 PE — gymnastics; SHAPE Standard 4)
- **Hook:** "Can two people balance together in ways they couldn't alone?"
- **Key activity:** Teacher-led: push (counter-balance) and pull (counter-tension) balances with partners of similar size; communication cues.
- **Check for understanding:** "What is the difference between counter-balance and counter-tension?" → Counter-balance: partners push against each other; counter-tension: partners pull away from each other.
- **Differentiation:** support: simple pull balances holding wrists. / stretch: a group balance of three.
- **Materials:** mats. Safety: no standing on partners; agree "stop" word.
- **Joy:** Partner balance photo gallery.

### Lesson 22 — Sequences: linking movements
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can create a sequence of 6 movements with smooth links." (UK NC KS2 PE — develop sequences; SHAPE Standard 1)
- **Hook:** "Olympic floor routines are sequences. What makes them flow?"
- **Key activity:** Teacher-led: plan a sequence (balance, roll, jump, travel, balance, finish) with clear start and end positions; practise links.
- **Check for understanding:** "How did you link your roll to your balance smoothly?" → A specific transition (e.g. roll up to standing then step into balance).
- **Differentiation:** support: 4 movements. / stretch: include levels, directions and speed changes.
- **Materials:** mats, sequence cards.
- **Joy:** Performing sequences.

### Lesson 23 — Pair sequences: unison and canon
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can perform a pair sequence using unison and canon." (UK NC KS2 PE — gymnastics; links to music rounds; SHAPE Standard 1)
- **Hook:** "Same moves at the same time, or one after the other — which looks better?"
- **Key activity:** Teacher-led: pairs combine sequences; unison (together) and canon (one after another); include one partner balance.
- **Check for understanding:** "What's the difference between unison and canon, and where did you use each?" → Unison: together; canon: one after another — specific moments named.
- **Differentiation:** support: 4-move pair sequence. / stretch: add mirroring (symmetry).
- **Materials:** mats, apparatus.
- **Joy:** Pair performances.

### Lesson 24 — Gymnastics performance and evaluation
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can perform my sequence and evaluate performances using gymnastics criteria." (UK NC KS2 PE — evaluate; SHAPE Standard 4)
- **Hook:** "Judges' panel."
- **Key activity:** Perform sequences; peers give feedback on control, extension, links and landings; video for self-review.
- **Check for understanding:** "What would you change to improve your sequence's control?" → A specific improvement.
- **Differentiation:** support: feedback frame. / stretch: act as judge using criteria.
- **Materials:** mats, apparatus, tablet for filming (with consent).
- **Joy:** Judges' panel scores (for effort and improvement).

**End-of-unit check:** Perform a pair sequence of 6–8 movements including a balance, a roll, a jump with a clear shape and a partner balance, using unison and canon; evaluate it with one strength and one improvement.

## Unit 4 — Dance around the world and choreography

**Essential question:** How do dancers tell stories and share culture through movement?

**Key vocabulary:** choreography, motif, formation, unison, canon, levels, dynamics, gesture, mirroring, flamenco, compás, palmas, Bharatanatyam, mudra, breaking, toprock, freeze, cypher, jig, performance

### Lesson 25 — Flamenco: footwork and the 12-beat compás (links to music)
- **Duration:** 35 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can perform flamenco-style footwork, palmas and arm movements to a 12-beat rhythm." (UK NC KS2 PE — dance; SHAPE Standard 1; links to music Unit 4)
- **Hook:** "Flamenco comes from Andalusia in southern Spain, with deep roots in Gitano (Roma) communities and many other influences; UNESCO recognised it in 2010. Listen — can you count to 12?"
- **Key activity:** Teacher-led: retrieve the 12-beat compás from music (accents on 3, 6, 8, 10 and 12) with palmas; upright posture; simple footwork (flat-foot stamp, ball, heel) in soft shoes; *braceo* (slow, curving arms); combine into a short phrase that places strong stamps on the accents, with a turn to finish.
- **Check for understanding:** "How does knowing where the accents fall in the 12-beat cycle help a flamenco dancer?" → they can place strong stamps or turns on the accented beats, so the footwork fits the guitar and palmas and the group stays together
- **Differentiation:** support: palmas plus one stamp on each accent. / stretch: add a turn that lands on beat 12.
- **Materials:** flamenco recordings, space. Safety: footwork on a wooden or sprung floor in soft shoes — no hard stamping on concrete.
- **Joy:** Palmas circle with solo turns in the middle.

### Lesson 26 — Bharatanatyam: telling stories with mudras
- **Duration:** 35 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can use Bharatanatyam hand gestures (mudras), stance and facial expression to tell part of a story." (UK NC KS2 PE — dance; SHAPE Standard 1)
- **Hook:** "In classical Indian dance one hand shape can be a cloud, a forest or a blessing. How many meanings can two hands hold?"
- **Key activity:** Teacher-led: Bharatanatyam, a classical dance from Tamil Nadu in southern India; the *aramandi* (half-sitting stance, knees turned out); a basic step (*adavu*) to spoken rhythm syllables; mudras such as *pataka* (flat hand — e.g. a cloud or a blessing), *alapadma* (open lotus — a flower or the full moon) and *shikhara* (fist with the thumb up — e.g. a bow or a pillar); facial expression (*abhinaya*); groups tell a four-line nature story. Compare with Year 2's Bollywood dance, which borrows gestures from classical forms.
- **Check for understanding:** "How is Bharatanatyam different from the Bollywood dance you learned in Year 2?" → Bharatanatyam is a classical form with set stances, steps and hand gestures that carry particular meanings; Bollywood is a modern film style that mixes many influences, including gestures borrowed from classical dance
- **Differentiation:** support: two mudras and the stance. / stretch: create a new four-count phrase that tells a story with three mudras.
- **Materials:** Bharatanatyam recordings, mudra picture cards. Safety: build up the aramandi gradually, knees over toes.
- **Joy:** "Mudra story" performances.

### Lesson 27 — Breaking: from the Bronx to the Olympics
- **Duration:** 35 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can perform toprock, simple footwork and a freeze with control and explain where breaking began." (UK NC KS2 PE — dance; SHAPE Standard 1)
- **Hook:** "Breaking began at block parties in the Bronx, New York, in the 1970s — and in 2024 it appeared at the Paris Olympics. How did a street dance get there?"
- **Key activity:** Teacher-led: history (African American and Puerto Rican young people in the Bronx; DJs such as DJ Kool Herc looping the instrumental 'break' of funk records for dancers); *toprock* (standing steps on the beat), simple floor footwork on hands and feet, and a freeze supported on hands and feet; the *cypher* (circle) where dancers take turns and answer each other's moves.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why is a breaking 'battle' in a cypher more like a conversation than a fight?" → dancers take turns, respond to each other's moves and the music, and show respect — the aim is creativity and style, not hurting anyone
- **Differentiation:** support: toprock only. / stretch: link toprock → footwork → freeze to end on the phrase.
- **Materials:** instrumental hip-hop or funk music, mats. Safety: warm up wrists; mats for floor work; no head-supported moves or spins.
- **Joy:** A class cypher.

### Lesson 41 — Reading food labels: sugar and portions
- **Duration:** 35 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can read a food label and work out how much sugar is in a drink or snack." (Health education — healthy eating; NHES Standard 1; maths link)
- **Hook:** "How many teaspoons of sugar are in a can of fizzy drink? Guess — then let's calculate."
- **Key activity:** Retrieve the balanced plate from Year 3 using photos of family meals from around the world (*Hungry Planet*); read labels 'per 100 g / 100 ml' and 'per portion'; 4 g of sugar is about one teaspoon; calculate the sugar in real drinks and snacks and build the teaspoon piles; the WHO advises keeping 'free sugars' (sugar added to food, plus honey, syrups and fruit juice) low; water and milk as everyday drinks.
- **Check for understanding:** "A drink has 10 g of sugar per 100 ml. How much sugar is in a 330 ml can, and about how many teaspoons is that?" → 3.3 × 10 = 33 g; 33 ÷ 4 ≈ 8 teaspoons
- **Differentiation:** support: labels with the per-portion sugar highlighted, and a calculator. / stretch: compare the sugar in a whole orange and a glass of orange juice and explain why juice counts as free sugar.

### Lesson 42 — Germs, the immune system and medicines
- **Duration:** 35 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can explain how my body defends itself against germs and why antibiotics don't cure colds." (Health education — health and prevention; NHES Standard 1; prepares for Year 6 science (microorganisms))
- **Hook:** "You catch a cold and get better in a week without any medicine. What did the fighting?"
- **Key activity:** Retrieve handwashing and food hygiene (Year 3); the body's defences — skin and mucus as barriers, white blood cells that find and destroy germs and 'remember' them; vaccines train the immune system safely (Edward Jenner's smallpox vaccine, 1796; smallpox was declared eradicated in 1980); bacteria and viruses are different — antibiotics work on bacteria, not viruses; medicine safety: only take medicines given by a trusted adult.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why won't antibiotics help you get over a cold?" → colds are caused by viruses, and antibiotics only work against bacteria; your immune system fights off the cold virus
- **Differentiation:** support: a picture 'body defence' strip to sequence. / stretch: explain how a vaccine prepares the body without making you ill.
- **Materials:** body-defence diagrams, scenario cards. Sensitivity: respect families' medical circumstances; medicines are only given by trusted adults.
- **Joy:** "Immune system tag" — white blood cells tag the germs.

### Lesson 43 — Sun, heat and cold: protecting my body outdoors
- **Duration:** 35 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can use the UV index and weather forecast to protect my body from sun, heat and cold." (Health education — health and prevention; NHES Standard 7; links to geography)
- **Hook:** "Today's UV index is 8. What does that number mean for playtime?"
- **Key activity:** Retrieve sun basics from Year 2; the UV index — at 3 or above, protect your skin (shade, hat, clothing, broad-spectrum sunscreen of SPF 30 or more, reapplied, sunglasses), even on cloudy days; heat — signs of heat exhaustion (headache, dizziness, feeling sick, heavy sweating) mean stop, find shade, drink water and tell an adult; cold — layers trap warm air, hats and gloves, and wet clothes lose heat fast; plan kit for a hot day and a cold day.

- **Differentiation:** support: kit picture cards. / stretch: explain why several thin layers keep you warmer than one thick jumper (trapped air between layers insulates, and you can take one off if you get hot).
- **Materials:** weather forecasts showing the UV index, clothes for a layering demonstration, empty sunscreen bottles.
- **Joy:** "Dress the explorer" relay.

### Lesson 47 — First aid: the primary survey and defibrillators
- **Duration:** 40 min

- **Hook:** "Hop, step, jump — which leg do you use for each?"
- **Key activity:** Coach-led: same foot for hop and landing of hop, other foot for step, both feet land; rhythm drills; measure.
- **Check for understanding:** "Which foot do you land on after the hop, and then after the step?" → The same foot you took off with; then the other foot.
- **Differentiation:** support: hoops to show foot placement. / stretch: a short run-up.
- **Materials:** hoops, mats, tape measures.
- **Joy:** "Hop, step, jump" rhythm chant.

### Lesson 59 — Throwing: push, pull and sling
- **Duration:** 35 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can use push, pull and sling throws with good technique." (UK NC KS2 PE — throwing; SHAPE Standard 1)
- **Hook:** "Shot put, javelin, discus — three different ways to throw."
- **Key activity:** Coach-led with safe equipment: push throw (soft shot), pull throw (foam javelin/vortex), sling throw (quoit or soft discus); release angle; measure.
- **Check for understanding:** "What angle of release helps a throw travel furthest in our lessons, and why?" → Somewhere between flat and halfway up (roughly 35–45°); too low and it hits the ground early, too high and it goes up rather than along.
- **Differentiation:** support: shorter distances, larger targets. / stretch: compare distances at different release angles.
- **Materials:** soft shots, foam javelins or vortex howlers, quoits, tape measures, cones. Safety: throwing lines, everyone behind the thrower, retrieve only on command.
- **Joy:** "Vortex howler" throws.

### Lesson 60 — Middle distance: pacing 600 m

- **Check for understanding:** "What happened to your lap times, and what does that tell you about your pacing?" → Evidence-based reflection (e.g. first lap too fast).
- **Differentiation:** support: 400 m. / stretch: aim for even splits.

- **Joy:** Beating a personal plan.

- **Check for understanding:** "By how much did you improve in one test, and what training helped?" → A calculated difference and a reason.
- **Differentiation:** support: calculator for differences. / stretch: calculate percentage improvement.

### Lesson 62 — Athletics competition practice
- **Duration:** 40 min

## Unit 9 — Outdoor and adventurous activity: orienteering and teamwork

**Essential question:** How can a map, a compass and a good team get us anywhere?

**Key vocabulary:** orienteering, map, key, orientate, control point, handrail, catching feature, attack point, compass, north, bearing, route, teamwork, trust, problem-solving, leadership

### Lesson 63 — Route planning: handrails, catching features and attack points
- **Duration:** 35 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can use handrails, catching features and attack points to plan and follow a route to a control." (UK NC KS2 PE — outdoor and adventurous; links to geography)
- **Hook:** "Expert orienteers rarely run in a straight line to a control. Why not?"
- **Key activity:** Teacher-led outdoors: quick retrieval of setting the map, thumbing and pace counting (Year 3). New: *handrails* (line features such as paths, fences, walls or streams to follow), *catching features* (a feature just beyond the control that tells you you've gone too far) and *attack points* (an easy-to-find feature near the control, from which you go carefully); a map-memory relay (memorise one leg, run it without the map).
- **Check for understanding:** "What is a catching feature, and how does it stop you getting lost?" → a feature just beyond the control, such as a fence or path — if you reach it, you know you've overshot and can turn back
- **Differentiation:** support: pairs with a picture map. / stretch: plan two routes to one control and justify which is safer or faster.
- **Materials:** school-grounds maps, control markers, punch cards. Safety: boundaries set; buddy system.
- **Joy:** Map-memory relay.

### Lesson 64 — Compass skills
- **Duration:** 35 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can use a compass to find north and follow simple directions." (UK NC KS2 PE — outdoor; geography link)
- **Hook:** "Which way is north — and how does a compass know?"
- **Key activity:** Teacher-led: parts of a compass; the needle points to magnetic north (links to science magnets); follow 8-point compass directions and simple bearings (0°, 90°, 180°, 270°).
- **Check for understanding:** "Why should you keep a compass away from metal objects when using it?" → Iron and steel attract the magnetic needle and give a false reading.
- **Differentiation:** support: 4 compass points. / stretch: follow bearings to a hidden object.
- **Materials:** baseplate compasses, cones.
- **Joy:** Compass treasure trail.

### Lesson 65 — Score orienteering
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can plan an efficient route to collect controls in a time limit." (UK NC KS2 PE — outdoor; SHAPE Standard 2)
- **Hook:** "Controls are worth different points — plan the best route!"
- **Key activity:** Teacher-led: controls with point values; pairs plan routes; 20-minute time limit; review routes.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why might the shortest route not be the best?" → Higher-value controls might be worth the extra distance; obstacles may slow you.
- **Differentiation:** support: fewer controls, adult checkpoint. / stretch: add penalties for being late.
- **Materials:** maps, controls, stopwatches.
- **Joy:** Score orienteering race.

### Lesson 66 — Team challenges: communication and trust
- **Duration:** 35 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can solve team problems by communicating and listening." (UK NC KS2 PE — outdoor, team challenges; SHAPE Standard 4)
- **Hook:** "Cross the 'river' using only three mats — without touching the 'water'."
- **Key activity:** Teacher-led: problem-solving challenges (river crossing, blind-fold guide with verbal directions, human knot); reflect on roles.
- **Check for understanding:** "What made your team successful or unsuccessful?" → Specific behaviour (listening, planning, including everyone).
- **Differentiation:** support: simpler challenge. / stretch: silent challenge (non-verbal communication).
- **Materials:** mats, blindfolds, ropes, cones. Safety: blindfolded partners walk slowly; clear space.
- **Joy:** Team challenges.

### Lesson 67 — Outdoor adventure day (planning)
- **Duration:** 35 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can plan safely for an outdoor activity, including kit, weather and risks." (UK NC KS2 PE — outdoor; Health education — being safe)
- **Hook:** "What should an explorer pack?"
- **Key activity:** Plan kit (water, layers, sun protection, first-aid kit); check weather; identify risks and how to reduce them.
- **Check for understanding:** "Name two risks on our adventure and how we'll reduce them." → e.g. getting lost — stay in pairs with a map; sunburn — hats and sunscreen.
- **Differentiation:** support: kit checklist. / stretch: write a group risk assessment.
- **Materials:** kit checklist, weather forecast.
- **Joy:** Packing challenge.

### Lesson 68 — Outdoor adventure: orienteering in a park or woodland
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can navigate a new area with a map and compass in a team." (UK NC KS2 PE — outdoor and adventurous)
- **Hook:** "Adventure time."
- **Key activity:** Teacher- and adult-led trip (or extended school-grounds course): orienteering course in teams; debrief.
- **Check for understanding:** "What was your team's biggest navigation decision and how did you make it?" → A specific decision using map/compass.
- **Differentiation:** support: adult-accompanied team. / stretch: navigate without adult help (adults at controls).
- **Materials:** maps, compasses, control markers, first-aid kit, radios/phones. Safety: full risk assessment; adult ratios; meeting point.
- **Joy:** The adventure.

**End-of-unit check:** Plan a route using handrails, catching features and attack points and navigate a course with a partner; explain how to use a compass to find north; describe one way the team solved a problem together.

## Teacher guidance

**Delivery.** These lessons are designed to be **delivered by a human adult in a real space** — a teacher, coach or parent. An app can prepare (rules, videos, key points), track personal bests and prompt reflection, but should never replace supervision.

**Safety first.** Warm up every lesson; check medical needs (asthma, allergies, diabetes, epilepsy, heart conditions) and keep medication nearby; set clear boundaries and stop signals; check equipment and surfaces; follow pool and trip regulations and ratios; use qualified instructors for swimming and trained adults for first aid and gymnastics spotting; adapt to heat and cold (water, shade, layers).

**Common misconceptions (and fixes).**
- *"Stretching before exercise means holding stretches."* — use dynamic movement to warm up; save held stretches for the cool-down.
- *"Everyone should chase the ball."* — teach space, width and support with freeze-frames.
- *"In rugby you can throw the ball forward."* — teach and practise backward passing.
- *"Sweets and fizzy drinks are fine if you brush your teeth."* — frequency of sugar matters.
- *"If someone is drowning, jump in."* — "reach or throw, don't go"; call for help.
- *"Antibiotics cure colds and flu."* — they work only on bacteria; colds and flu are viruses.
- *"You can't get sunburnt on a cloudy day."* — check the UV index; UV passes through cloud.
- *"Fitness means being the fastest."* — fitness has many components; focus on personal progress.

**Inclusion and culture.** Offer alternatives for faith and culture (modest swimwear, fasting adaptations, mixed/single-sex groupings where needed); adapt for disability (e.g. seated volleyball, guide runners, larger or brighter balls); present dances with their cultural origins and respect.

**Health education.** Puberty, body safety and mental health lessons follow the school's policy, with families informed and able to see materials; safeguarding procedures are followed for any disclosure.

**Vertical links.** Year 3: fundamental movements, simple games, basic gymnastics and dance (bhangra, kpanlogo, capoeira, ribbon dance), health (allergies, road safety, food hygiene, stress response, burns and nosebleeds), map scale and pacing, rip currents and land-based rescue — Year 4 lessons open with a brief retrieval of these and go further rather than repeating them. Year 4: tactics across games, sequences with partners, world dance and choreography, 25 m swimming and self-rescue, measured athletics, orienteering, a health unit. Year 5: more competitive sport and personal fitness planning, advanced sequences and choreography, and extended health education.

**Cross-curricular links.** Science (heart and pulse; forces in sport; growing up), maths (measurement, timing, graphs, percentages), geography (maps and compasses), music (flamenco compás, rhythm and dance), languages (Spanish healthy advice), life skills (resilience, teamwork, goal setting).
