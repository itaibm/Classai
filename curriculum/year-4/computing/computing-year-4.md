# Year 4 Computing (ages 9–10) — Scope & Sequence

> Part of **the best international primary school** (see [`../../../SCHOOL-CHARTER.md`](../../../SCHOOL-CHARTER.md)).
> Written to [`../../PLANNING-BRIEF.md`](../../PLANNING-BRIEF.md). Standards reference:
> `knowledge-base/subjects/computing-life-skills/computing-ks1-ks2.md` and `online-safety.md`.
> Codes: **UK NC KS2 Computing** (design, write and debug programs; sequence, selection, repetition; variables; networks and the internet; search; evaluate digital content; use technology safely and respectfully) and **CSTA** (US Computer Science Teachers Association K–12 Standards, Level 1B, Grades 3–5).

## Year overview

Year 4 computing is the year children write **programs that make decisions and remember things**, and look **inside the computer itself**. In Scratch they move from loops (Year 3) to **selection** (*if…then…else*, conditions, sensing and operators) and **variables** (score, timer, lives), combining all three to design, test and improve their own games. Unplugged activities reveal **how a computer works** — input, processing, memory, storage — and how everything it handles, from numbers to pictures, is stored in **binary**; later, children learn how **search engines** find and rank pages. (Networks, packets and the web were taught in Years 2 and 3; Year 5 adds IP addresses, DNS and encryption.) A **spreadsheets** unit uses real data from science and PE, and a **media literacy** unit teaches children to question what they find online — including answers from AI chatbots. **Online safety and wellbeing** (digital footprint, strong passwords, kindness, balance and getting help) run through the year and have their own unit.

Every programming lesson follows **Use → Modify → Create**, with **debugging** taught as a normal, positive part of programming ("bugs are clues"). Unplugged activities come first when a concept is new.

**What Year 4 assumes (from Years 2–3):** sequences, events and broadcasts; loops (*repeat*, *forever*, *repeat until*, nested loops, parallel scripts) in Scratch; drawing with the pen; debugging by tracing; branching databases; networks, packets, servers and URLs; making audio and video; reliable sources and user-written content; passwords, privacy settings, kindness and sleep-friendly screen habits.
**What Year 5 relies on from Year 4:** confident use of selection and variables (for complete programs and physical computing); understanding of how computers store data in binary and how search works (for IP addresses, encryption and evaluating information); spreadsheet formulas and charts (for a full data investigation); the habit of planning, testing and evaluating code (for the move to text-based programming in Python).

## Unit 1 — Inside the computer: input, processing, storage and binary

**Essential question:** What actually happens inside a computer — and how can it store words, numbers and pictures using only 0s and 1s?

**Key vocabulary:** input, output, process, processor (CPU), memory (RAM), storage, sensor, data, binary, bit, byte, pixel, resolution, computer system

### Lesson 1 — Input, process, output: computers all around us
- **Duration:** 35 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can identify the input, processing and output in everyday computer systems." (UK NC KS2 understand computer systems; CSTA 1B-CS-01)
- **Hook:** "A microwave, a traffic light, a supermarket self-checkout and a games console — which of these are computers?"
- **Key activity:** Retrieve inputs and outputs from earlier years in two minutes. New: the input–process–output model. Groups sort photo cards of devices, then draw an input → process → output diagram for three of them (including one with a sensor, such as automatic doors).
- **Check for understanding:** "A self-checkout scans a barcode and shows a price. Name the input, the processing and the output." → input: the scanner reads the barcode; processing: the computer looks up the price and adds it to the total; output: the price on the screen and a beep
- **Differentiation:** support: picture cards with the three boxes already drawn. / stretch: find a system with more than one input and explain how the processing combines them.
- **Materials:** device photo cards, IPO diagram sheets.
- **Joy:** "Is it a computer?" debate.

### Lesson 2 — Inside the box: processor, memory and storage
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can name the main parts inside a computer and explain the jobs of the processor, memory and storage." (UK NC KS2 understand computer systems; CSTA 1B-CS-02)
- **Hook:** An old desktop computer with its side panel removed: "What's in here — and which part is the 'brain'?"
- **Key activity:** An adult opens an old, unplugged desktop computer (power supply left sealed, any batteries removed); children identify the processor (CPU), memory (RAM), storage (hard drive or SSD), motherboard and fan. Unplugged role-play: a 'processor' child follows instructions from a 'memory' whiteboard that is wiped when the 'power' goes off, while a 'storage' notebook keeps its notes.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why does a computer need both memory (RAM) and storage?" → memory holds what the computer is working on right now and is very fast, but it forgets everything when the power goes off; storage keeps files even when the computer is switched off
- **Differentiation:** support: a labelled photo of the inside of a computer to match. / stretch: explain why a computer with more memory can run more apps at once.
- **Materials:** an old, unplugged desktop computer (adult-opened; do not open the power supply or batteries), labels, whiteboards and notebooks for the role-play.
- **Joy:** "Computer surgeons" labelling the parts.

### Lesson 3 — Binary: everything is 0s and 1s (unplugged)
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can represent numbers up to 31 in binary and explain why computers use only two digits." (UK NC KS2 understand how data is represented; CSTA 1B-DA-06)
- **Hook:** "Five cards, each with dots on one side. Can you show any number from 0 to 31 just by turning them face up or face down?"
- **Key activity:** CS Unplugged binary cards (16, 8, 4, 2, 1 dots): face up = 1, face down = 0. Children make numbers, write them in binary, and link to the on/off signals of Year 3 (a switch, a light, a current). Send a secret number in binary with torches.
- **Check for understanding:** "Using the cards 16, 8, 4, 2 and 1, show 13 in binary and explain why each card is either face up or face down." → 01101 (8 + 4 + 1); each card is either on (1) or off (0) — just like a switch in a computer, which can only be on or off
- **Differentiation:** support: numbers to 15 with four cards. / stretch: work out the largest number six cards could show, and explain the doubling pattern.
- **Materials:** binary dot cards (one set per pair), whiteboards, torches.
- **Joy:** "Binary birthday" — write your age and birthday in binary.

### Lesson 4 — Pictures as numbers: pixels
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can explain how a picture is stored as a grid of pixels, each saved as numbers." (UK NC KS2 understand how data is represented; CSTA 1B-DA-06)
- **Hook:** Zoom right into a digital photo on the board: "Where did the smooth picture go?"
- **Key activity:** Colour in a black-and-white picture on squared paper from a list of 0s and 1s (1 = black, 0 = white); then send a partner a picture as a list of numbers; discuss how colour pictures use three numbers per pixel (red, green, blue) and how more pixels give a sharper image.
- **Check for understanding:** "A 5 × 5 black-and-white picture is sent as 25 zeros and ones. Why does a bigger, sharper picture take longer to send?" → it has more pixels, so there are more numbers (bits) to store and send across the network
- **Differentiation:** support: a 5 × 5 grid with the first row done. / stretch: design an 8 × 8 icon and work out how many bits it needs; explain how colour needs even more.
- **Materials:** squared paper, pixel-grid sheets, a zoomable digital photo.
- **Joy:** "Pixel postcard" — send a hidden picture as numbers.

### Lesson 5 — Unit 1 review: design a smart device
- **Duration:** 35 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can design a simple computer system and explain its inputs, processing, storage and how its data is stored in binary." (UK NC KS2 understand computer systems; CSTA 1B-CS-01)
- **Hook:** "Invent a device that would make our school better — a plant-watering robot, a noise meter, a smart bin."
- **Key activity:** Groups draw their device as an input → process → output diagram, say what it must store and remember, write a simple algorithm for it, and explain one piece of its data in binary.
- **Check for understanding:** "Point to your device's input, processing and output, and explain one thing it must store and why." → a correct diagram and a sensible stored item (e.g. the plant robot stores the last time it watered, so it doesn't water twice)
- **Differentiation:** support: a template with the three boxes and picture prompts. / stretch: add a condition to the algorithm ('if the soil is dry then…') — a preview of Unit 2.
- **Materials:** design sheets, coloured pens.
- **Joy:** "Inventors' fair" pitch.

**End-of-unit check:** Draw an input → process → output diagram for a device; explain why a computer needs both memory and storage; write a number to 31 in binary and explain how a picture is stored as pixels.

## Unit 2 — Selection: programs that decide

**Essential question:** How can a program make choices and react to what happens?

**Key vocabulary:** algorithm, condition, selection, if…then, if…then…else, Boolean (true/false), sensing, operator, greater than, less than, equals, and, or, not, event, debug

### Lesson 6 — Conditions in everyday life (unplugged)
- **Duration:** 35 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can write everyday algorithms that use conditions." (UK NC KS2 use selection; CSTA 1B-AP-10)
- **Hook:** "If it's raining, take an umbrella; otherwise, wear sunglasses."
- **Key activity:** Unplugged: "if…then…else" flowcharts for everyday choices; play "Simon says" as an if-statement; children follow flowchart cards.
- **Check for understanding:** "In 'If it is raining then take an umbrella', what is the condition and what is the action?" → Condition: it is raining; action: take an umbrella.
- **Differentiation:** support: flowchart templates with pictures. / stretch: a flowchart with two conditions joined by 'and'.
- **Materials:** flowchart cards, sticky notes.
- **Joy:** "Flowchart Simon says".

### Lesson 7 — If…then in Scratch with sensing
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can use an if…then block with a sensing condition inside a loop." (UK NC KS2 selection; CSTA 1B-AP-10)
- **Hook:** "Make a cat say 'Ouch!' when it touches a cactus."
- **Key activity:** Use: run a starter program; Modify: change the condition (touching colour, key pressed); Create: add a new reaction. Explain why the *if* must be inside a *forever* loop so it keeps checking.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why doesn't the 'if touching' work when it isn't inside a forever loop?" → Without the loop, the program checks only once at the start, then stops checking.
- **Differentiation:** support: starter project with blocks provided. / stretch: two different if-blocks for two different events.
- **Materials:** computers or tablets with Scratch, starter projects.
- **Joy:** Sprite reactions.

### Lesson 8 — If…then…else
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can use if…then…else to choose between two actions." (UK NC KS2 selection; CSTA 1B-AP-10)
- **Hook:** "A traffic light robot: go or stop?"
- **Key activity:** Program a sprite to change costume or speech depending on a condition (e.g. if touching the road then say 'Beep!' else say 'Safe'); trace the program with a partner.
- **Check for understanding:** "When does the 'else' part of the block run?" → Only when the condition is false.
- **Differentiation:** support: fill-in-the-gap blocks. / stretch: nested if-blocks.
- **Materials:** Scratch, printed block cards.
- **Joy:** Traffic-light robots.

### Lesson 9 — Operators: comparing numbers
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can use <, > and = operators in conditions." (UK NC KS2 selection, logical reasoning; CSTA 1B-AP-10; links to maths)
- **Hook:** "Guess my number — too high or too low?"
- **Key activity:** *pick random 1 to 100*; *ask and wait*; if answer > number say 'Too high' else if answer < number say 'Too low' else say 'Correct!'. Link to Scratch's coordinate stage (x from −240 to 240; y from −180 to 180) and use x/y comparisons.
- **Check for understanding:** "What will the program say if I guess 50 and the number is 37?" → "Too high."
- **Differentiation:** support: numbers 1–10. / stretch: count guesses and give a hint.
- **Materials:** Scratch.
- **Joy:** Guess-my-number game.

### Lesson 10 — And, or, not
- **Duration:** 35 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can combine conditions with and, or and not." (UK NC KS2 logical reasoning; CSTA 1B-AP-10)
- **Hook:** Unplugged: "Stand up if you have a pet AND like maths… OR… NOT…"
- **Key activity:** Unplugged standing game; then in Scratch: a door that opens only if the player has a key AND presses the space bar.
- **Check for understanding:** "When is 'A and B' true? When is 'A or B' true?" → 'And' only when both are true; 'or' when at least one is true.
- **Differentiation:** support: 'and' only. / stretch: 'not' in a game rule.
- **Materials:** Scratch, condition cards.
- **Joy:** "Stand up if…" logic game.

### Lesson 11 — Build a quiz with selection
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can design and program a quiz that checks answers and responds." (UK NC KS2 design and write programs; CSTA 1B-AP-11)
- **Hook:** "Make a quiz for the Year 2 class about something you know well."
- **Key activity:** Plan questions and answers on paper; program with *ask*, *answer* and *if…then…else*; test with a partner; handle capital letters (Scratch's = is not case-sensitive, but spelling matters).
- **Check for understanding:** "What happens if someone spells the answer differently, and how could you fix it?" → The program marks it wrong; accept alternative answers with 'or' or use multiple choice.
- **Differentiation:** support: 3-question template. / stretch: accept several correct spellings.
- **Materials:** Scratch, planning sheets.
- **Joy:** Quiz swap with a partner.

### Lesson 12 — Debugging selection
- **Duration:** 35 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can find and fix bugs in programs that use selection." (UK NC KS2 detect and correct errors; CSTA 1B-AP-15)
- **Hook:** "Bug hunt: five programs, five bugs."
- **Key activity:** Predict what each program should do; run; compare; locate and fix bugs (condition backwards, if outside loop, wrong operator); record the fix.
- **Check for understanding:** "Explain one bug you found and how you knew it was the cause." → A specific bug and the evidence (e.g. it only checked once, so the if was outside the loop).
- **Differentiation:** support: bug hints. / stretch: create a bugged program for a partner.
- **Materials:** Scratch bug-hunt projects.
- **Joy:** Bug-hunt bingo.

**End-of-unit check:** Program a sprite that reacts differently in two situations using *if…then…else* inside a loop, and explain why the loop is needed; debug a given program with a faulty condition.

## Unit 3 — Variables and games

**Essential question:** How can a program remember and change information — like a score, a timer or lives?

**Key vocabulary:** variable, value, set, change, score, timer, lives, initialise (reset), increment, broadcast, message, costume, clone, design, test, iterate

### Lesson 13 — What is a variable? (unplugged)
- **Duration:** 35 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can explain a variable as a named box that stores a value that can change." (UK NC KS2 work with variables; CSTA 1B-AP-09)
- **Hook:** A labelled box "score" with a number card inside: "What happens when I score a goal?"
- **Key activity:** Unplugged: labelled boxes for score, lives and timer; act out 'set to', 'change by'; trace short algorithms.
- **Check for understanding:** "If score is 5 and then 'change score by 3' runs, what is score now?" → 8.
- **Differentiation:** support: one variable. / stretch: two variables changing in the same algorithm.
- **Materials:** labelled boxes, number cards.
- **Joy:** "Variable boxes" game.

### Lesson 14 — Score variables in Scratch
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can create a score variable, reset it at the start and change it when something happens." (UK NC KS2 variables; CSTA 1B-AP-09)
- **Hook:** "A game without a score — would you play it?"
- **Key activity:** Make a variable; *set score to 0* when green flag clicked; *change score by 1* when the player catches an object.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why must you set the score to 0 at the start?" → Otherwise the score keeps the value from the last game.
- **Differentiation:** support: starter project. / stretch: different points for different objects.
- **Materials:** Scratch.
- **Joy:** Catch game with scores.

### Lesson 15 — Timers and lives
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can use a timer variable and a lives variable to end a game." (UK NC KS2 variables; selection; CSTA 1B-AP-09)
- **Hook:** "Game over — but how does the computer know?"
- **Key activity:** Timer counts down with *wait 1 second* and *change timer by −1*; lives decrease on a hit; *if lives = 0 then stop all*.
- **Check for understanding:** "What code makes the game end when lives reach zero?" → *if lives = 0 then* say "Game over" and *stop all*, checked inside a loop.
- **Differentiation:** support: timer only. / stretch: both timer and lives, with a 'You win!' condition.
- **Materials:** Scratch.
- **Joy:** Beat-the-clock game.

### Lesson 16 — Designing a game: plan and decompose
- **Duration:** 35 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can plan a game by breaking it into smaller parts (decomposition)." (UK NC KS2 decompose problems; CSTA 1B-AP-11)
- **Hook:** Play a classic simple game (e.g. a maze or catch game): "What are its parts?"
- **Key activity:** Plan own game: sprites, controls, rules, variables, win/lose conditions; storyboard; algorithm for each sprite.
- **Check for understanding:** "What are the three most important parts of your game, and which variable does each need?" → A decomposed plan with variables identified.
- **Differentiation:** support: choose from three game templates. / stretch: include a level-up.
- **Materials:** planning sheets.
- **Joy:** Game-designer notebooks.

### Lesson 17 — Building the game
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can build my game's main features using loops, selection and variables." (UK NC KS2 design and write programs; CSTA 1B-AP-12)
- **Hook:** "Studio time."
- **Key activity:** Build in stages, testing after each small change; save versions.
- **Check for understanding:** "Show me where your program uses a loop, a condition and a variable." → All three identified.
- **Differentiation:** support: partner programming (driver and navigator). / stretch: use broadcast messages between sprites.
- **Materials:** Scratch, plans.
- **Joy:** Seeing your own game run.

### Lesson 18 — User testing and feedback
- **Duration:** 35 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can test my game with users and collect useful feedback." (UK NC KS2 evaluate; CSTA 1B-AP-13)
- **Hook:** "Real game studios test with real players."
- **Key activity:** Swap games; testers complete a feedback form (fun, difficulty, bugs, instructions); designers observe without helping.
- **Check for understanding:** "What was the most useful feedback you received and why?" → Specific feedback that suggests an improvement.
- **Differentiation:** support: tick-box feedback form. / stretch: watch and note where players get stuck.
- **Materials:** feedback forms.
- **Joy:** Playtesting.

### Lesson 19 — Improving the game (iterate)
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can improve my game in response to feedback and explain the change." (UK NC KS2 debug and improve; CSTA 1B-AP-15)
- **Hook:** "Version 2.0!"
- **Key activity:** Choose two improvements from feedback; implement; retest; write a "changelog".
- **Check for understanding:** "What did you change and how does it make the game better?" → A specific change linked to feedback.
- **Differentiation:** support: one improvement. / stretch: add difficulty levels.
- **Materials:** Scratch, changelog template.
- **Joy:** Changelog writing.

### Lesson 20 — Unit 3 assessment: game arcade
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can explain how my game uses variables, selection and loops." (UK NC KS2 — assessment; CSTA 1B-AP-17)
- **Hook:** "The Year 4 Arcade opens."
- **Key activity:** Class plays games; each designer explains their code to the teacher or a visitor.
- **Check for understanding:** "Show me the code that changes the score and explain when it runs." → Correct code and trigger.
- **Differentiation:** support: explanation frame. / stretch: explain a design decision and its trade-off.
- **Materials:** games, arcade signs.
- **Joy:** Arcade.

**End-of-unit check:** A working game with at least one variable that is reset and changed, a condition that ends or changes the game, and a loop; the child explains each part.

## Unit 4 — Spreadsheets and data

**Essential question:** How can a spreadsheet help us organise, calculate and see patterns in data?

**Key vocabulary:** spreadsheet, cell, row, column, cell reference, data, formula, SUM, MAX, MIN, sort, filter, chart, bar chart, line graph, axis, label

### Lesson 21 — Inside a spreadsheet
- **Duration:** 35 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can navigate a spreadsheet and enter data using cell references." (UK NC KS2 collect and present data; CSTA 1B-DA-06)
- **Hook:** "Battleships" on a spreadsheet grid.
- **Key activity:** Rows (numbers), columns (letters), cell references (B3); enter class data (e.g. pets or favourite fruit); format headings.
- **Check for understanding:** "What is in cell C4 of this sheet, and how did you find it?" → Correct content; column C, row 4.
- **Differentiation:** support: pre-labelled sheet. / stretch: use formatting to highlight a column.
- **Materials:** spreadsheet software (any), class data.
- **Joy:** Spreadsheet battleships.

### Lesson 22 — Formulas: the computer does the maths
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can write formulas using cell references and SUM." (UK NC KS2 use software to accomplish goals; CSTA 1B-DA-06; maths link)
- **Hook:** "Change one number — watch the total update itself!"
- **Key activity:** =B2+B3; =SUM(B2:B10); explain why formulas use cell references (they update automatically); check with mental maths.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why is =SUM(B2:B5) better than typing the total yourself?" → It updates automatically if any number changes and avoids calculation errors.
- **Differentiation:** support: + formulas only. / stretch: MAX and MIN.
- **Materials:** spreadsheets with sample data.
- **Joy:** "Magic total" demonstration.

### Lesson 23 — Budgets and planning (links to maths and life skills)
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can use a spreadsheet to plan a budget and check it stays within a limit." (UK NC KS2 use software; CSTA 1B-DA-06)
- **Hook:** "Plan a class party with 50.00 — every item costs something."
- **Key activity:** Items × quantity × price with formulas (=B2*C2); total with SUM; check if total ≤ budget; adjust.
- **Check for understanding:** "How does your spreadsheet show whether you are over budget?" → The total cell is compared with the budget (and optionally a formula shows the difference).
- **Differentiation:** support: prices already entered. / stretch: add a formula that shows money left (=budget−total).
- **Materials:** price lists, spreadsheet template.
- **Joy:** Party planning.

### Lesson 24 — Charts from data
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can create a chart from spreadsheet data and choose a suitable type." (UK NC KS2 present data; CSTA 1B-DA-07)
- **Hook:** Same data as a table and a chart: "Which shows the pattern faster?"
- **Key activity:** Make a bar chart (categories) and a line graph (change over time, e.g. the science cooling experiment); add titles and axis labels.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why is a line graph better for temperature over time than for favourite fruits?" → Temperature changes continuously over time; favourite fruits are separate categories.
- **Differentiation:** support: guided steps. / stretch: compare two data sets on one chart.
- **Materials:** spreadsheets with science/PE data.
- **Joy:** "Chart makeover" — turn a messy chart into a clear one.

### Lesson 25 — Sorting and answering questions
- **Duration:** 35 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can sort data and use it to answer questions." (UK NC KS2 analyse data; CSTA 1B-DA-07)
- **Hook:** PE fitness data (anonymous): "Who improved most?"
- **Key activity:** Sort by column (ascending/descending); MAX/MIN; answer questions; discuss privacy (anonymous data).
- **Check for understanding:** "Why should we use anonymous data for fitness results?" → Personal information should be kept private and respected.
- **Differentiation:** support: one-column sort. / stretch: calculate improvement with a formula.
- **Materials:** anonymised data sets.
- **Joy:** Data detectives.

### Lesson 26 — Unit 4 project: class data investigation
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can collect data, analyse it with formulas and present it with a chart." (UK NC KS2 collect, analyse, present data; CSTA 1B-DA-07)
- **Hook:** "Ask a question about our class and answer it with data."
- **Key activity:** Choose a question (e.g. How do we travel to school? How much water do we drink?); collect data; spreadsheet with formulas and a chart; one-sentence conclusion.
- **Check for understanding:** "What does your chart show and how confident are you in the answer?" → A conclusion and a reason for confidence or doubt (sample size, accuracy).
- **Differentiation:** support: provided question. / stretch: two charts compared.
- **Materials:** survey sheets, spreadsheets.
- **Joy:** Data posters.

**End-of-unit check:** Build a spreadsheet with a SUM formula and a correctly labelled chart, and explain why formulas use cell references.

## Unit 5 — Searching and media literacy

**Essential question:** How do search engines work, and how can I tell if what I find online is true?

**Key vocabulary:** search engine, index, keyword, ranking, advert, sponsored, reliable, source, bias, fact, opinion, misinformation, artificial intelligence (AI), chatbot, cross-check

### Lesson 27 — How search engines work
- **Duration:** 35 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can explain how a search engine finds and ranks web pages." (UK NC KS2 understand how results are selected and ranked; CSTA 1B-NI-04)
- **Hook:** Unplugged: a "library" of cards — find all pages about volcanoes as fast as possible.
- **Key activity:** Crawlers visit pages; the index stores words and where they appear; ranking uses many signals (relevance, links, freshness); sponsored results are adverts.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why is the first result not always the best or most reliable?" → Results can be adverts or ranked by popularity, not truth.
- **Differentiation:** support: picture flow of crawl → index → rank. / stretch: explain how a website might try to rank higher.
- **Materials:** index cards, sample search result pages.
- **Joy:** "Human search engine" race.

### Lesson 28 — Better searching
- **Duration:** 35 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can choose effective keywords and refine a search." (UK NC KS2 use search technologies effectively)
- **Hook:** "Search: 'animals' vs 'how do Arctic foxes survive winter'."
- **Key activity:** Choose key words; add or remove words; use quotation marks for an exact phrase; use child-friendly search tools; research a question from another subject.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why does 'Arctic fox winter adaptations' give better results than 'animals'?" → It is specific, so the search engine returns pages about exactly that topic.
- **Differentiation:** support: keyword menu. / stretch: compare results from two search tools.
- **Materials:** devices with child-safe search.
- **Joy:** "Search challenge" treasure hunt.

### Lesson 29 — Misinformation: why false stories spread
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can explain why false information spreads online and check a claim before sharing it." (UK NC KS2 be discerning in evaluating digital content)
- **Hook:** "'We only use 10% of our brains.' Millions of people have shared this. Is it true?"
- **Key activity:** Retrieve Year 3's reliability checklist, edited images and wikis in two minutes. New: misinformation — why surprising, scary or funny stories get shared faster than careful ones; how 'likes' and shares are not evidence; tracing a claim back to where it started; checking with fact-checking and expert sources. Children investigate two viral claims (one true, one false) and present their verdicts.
- **Check for understanding:** "A shocking post has thousands of shares. Why doesn't that make it true, and what would you check before sharing it?" → shares only show that it is popular, not that it is accurate; check who first posted it, whether trusted experts or news organisations report it, and whether the picture appears elsewhere with a different story
- **Differentiation:** support: a three-question checking card with picture prompts. / stretch: explain why people create misinformation (money from clicks, jokes, trying to change what people think).
- **Materials:** printed examples of viral claims (teacher-prepared), a checking card.
- **Joy:** "Myth-busters" verdict board.

### Lesson 30 — AI chatbots: helpful but not always right
- **Duration:** 35 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can explain that AI tools make predictions from data and can be wrong, and how to check their answers." (UK NC KS2 evaluate digital content; CSTA 1B-IC-18)
- **Hook:** The teacher asks an AI tool a question and it gives a confident but wrong answer (prepared example).
- **Key activity:** Discuss simply: AI chatbots learned patterns from huge amounts of text and predict likely answers; they can make mistakes or make things up; always check important facts with reliable sources; follow family and school rules about using AI (age limits apply to many tools).
- **Check for understanding:** "If an AI tool tells you a fact for your homework, what should you do?" → Check it with a reliable source (a book, a trusted website, a teacher).
- **Differentiation:** support: discussion with picture prompts. / stretch: compare an AI answer with an encyclopaedia entry.
- **Materials:** prepared examples (teacher-run; children do not need accounts).
- **Joy:** "Catch the robot's mistake."

**End-of-unit check:** Explain how search engines index and rank pages; check a viral claim before sharing it and explain why shares are not evidence; explain why AI answers need checking.

## Unit 6 — Safe, kind and balanced online

**Essential question:** How can I protect myself and be a good digital citizen?

**Key vocabulary:** digital footprint, privacy, personal information, passphrase, phishing, scam, two-step verification, viral challenge, in-game purchase, pressure, report, trusted adult

### Lesson 31 — Digital footprint and privacy
- **Duration:** 35 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can explain what a digital footprint is and decide what is safe to share." (UK NC KS2 use technology safely and respectfully; CSTA 1B-NI-05)
- **Hook:** Footprints in sand vs footprints in cement: "Which lasts?"
- **Key activity:** Sort information: safe to share / ask an adult / never share (full name with address, school, passwords, location); discuss how posts can be copied and shared.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why should you think carefully before posting a photo, even to friends?" → It can be copied and shared beyond your control and may stay online for a long time.
- **Differentiation:** support: picture sorting. / stretch: consider how location data in photos can reveal where you are.
- **Materials:** sorting cards.
- **Joy:** "Footprint" sorting game.

### Lesson 32 — Phishing, scams and two-step verification
- **Duration:** 35 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can spot the signs of a phishing message and explain how two-step verification protects an account." (UK NC KS2 security; CSTA 1B-NI-05)
- **Hook:** "'Congratulations! You've won a free tablet! Click here and enter your password to claim it.' Would you click?"
- **Key activity:** Retrieve Years 2–3 password habits (three random words; never share) in one minute. New: phishing — messages that pretend to be from someone trusted to steal passwords or money; the warning signs (urgency, prizes, spelling mistakes, odd addresses, requests for passwords); what two-step verification adds (a code sent to a second device); what to do (don't click, tell an adult, report, delete).
- **Check for understanding:** "A message says 'You've won a new tablet! Click here and enter your password to claim it.' Give two clues that it's phishing, and say what you should do." → it's too good to be true, it asks for your password, it is urgent or comes from an odd address; don't click, don't reply, tell a trusted adult and delete or report it
- **Differentiation:** support: word-picker cards and a picture checklist of warning signs. / stretch: explain why two-step verification still protects you even if someone learns your password.
- **Materials:** example messages (teacher-prepared; children don't use real passwords in class).
- **Joy:** "Scam spotter" challenge.

### Lesson 33 — Online pressure: dares, challenges and in-game spending
- **Duration:** 35 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can recognise online pressure (viral dares, in-game purchases, group-chat pressure) and know how to say no and get help." (UK NC KS2 recognise acceptable/unacceptable behaviour; identify ways to report concerns; CSTA 1B-NI-05)
- **Hook:** Scenario cards: a video dares children to try a risky 'challenge'; a game offers a '50% off — today only!' skin.
- **Key activity:** Retrieve Year 3's upstander actions and sleep plan in two minutes. New: why challenges and limited-time offers spread and pressure us; role-play saying no in a group chat; who to tell (parents, teachers, national helplines such as Childline in the UK or your country's equivalent); agree a class 'think before you tap' rule.
- **Check for understanding:** "An online 'challenge' dares children to do something risky and post a video. Why do challenges like this spread, and what should you do?" → they get views, likes and attention, and people feel they must join in; don't do it or share it, talk to a trusted adult and report the video
- **Differentiation:** support: action cards with picture prompts. / stretch: design a poster explaining one persuasive trick games use to make you spend money.
- **Materials:** scenario cards, helpline information for your country.
- **Joy:** Class "think before you tap" charter.

**End-of-unit check:** Explain what a digital footprint is; spot two signs of a phishing message; describe what to do if you feel pressured online.

## Teacher guidance

**Common misconceptions (and fixes).**
- *"The if-block keeps checking by itself."* — it only checks when it runs; put it inside a loop.
- *"Variables remember the right value automatically."* — always initialise (reset) at the start.
- *"Memory and storage are the same."* — memory is fast and forgets when the power is off; storage keeps files.
- *"Computers understand words and pictures directly."* — everything is stored as numbers in binary.
- *"The first search result is the best."* — rankings include adverts and popularity.
- *"AI always knows the answer."* — AI predicts likely text and can be wrong; check facts.
- *"Deleting a post removes it forever."* — it may have been copied or saved.

**Pedagogy.** Unplugged first for new concepts; then Use → Modify → Create; pair programming (driver/navigator swapping every 10 minutes); celebrate bugs as learning; ask children to predict before running code.

**Online safety.** Follow the school's acceptable-use and safeguarding policies; use child-safe search; children never share real passwords or personal data in class; provide helpline details relevant to your country.

**International notes.** Scratch is available in many languages; spreadsheet formulas may use semicolons instead of commas in some locales (e.g. =SUM(B2;B5)).

**Vertical links.** Years 2–3: loops (to nested and parallel), pen drawing, branching databases, networks, packets, servers and URLs, multimedia, reliable sources. Year 4: selection, variables, games, how computers work and binary, spreadsheets, search, misinformation and AI literacy. Year 5: complete programs, physical computing, a first look at text-based code (Python), full data investigations and online reputation.

**Cross-curricular links.** Maths (coordinates and negative numbers on the Scratch stage; operators; spreadsheets), science (data from investigations), design and technology (smart-device design; circuits and switches as on/off), English (evaluating sources, persuasive writing about online safety), art (digital pattern design), life skills (budgets, kindness).
