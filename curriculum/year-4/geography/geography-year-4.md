# Year 4 Geography (ages 9–10) — Scope & Sequence

> Part of **the best international primary school** (see [`../../../SCHOOL-CHARTER.md`](../../../SCHOOL-CHARTER.md)).
> Written to [`../../PLANNING-BRIEF.md`](../../PLANNING-BRIEF.md). Standards reference:
> `knowledge-base/subjects/history-geography/geography-ks2.md`, `misconceptions.md` and `_lesson-banks-humanities.md`.
> Codes: **UK NC KS2 Geography** (locational knowledge, place knowledge, human and physical geography, skills and fieldwork) and **C3** (US College, Career and Civic Life Framework, D2.Geo, Grades 3–5).

## Year overview

Year 4 geography is about **a moving, working planet**. Children first learn to locate anywhere on Earth precisely (latitude, longitude, hemispheres, the tropics and time zones). They then study the **restless Earth** — tectonic plates, mountains, volcanoes and earthquakes — and **rivers**, from source to sea. A **region study in South America** (the Amazon basin and the Andes of Peru) is compared with the children's own region, so they learn to describe places fairly and avoid stereotypes. The year ends with **settlements, land use and natural resources** and an independent **fieldwork investigation** in the local area, using maps with four- and six-figure grid references.

Every unit uses **maps** (atlases, globes, digital maps, local-area maps), includes **fieldwork** or real data, connects **physical and human geography**, and asks about **sustainability** — how people can live well with the planet.

**Continents across the spine:** Year 4 focuses on **South America** (region study), with case studies from **Asia** (Japan, the Himalaya, the Mekong), **Europe** (Iceland, Italy), **Africa** (the Nile, the East African Rift) and **North America** (Mount St Helens), plus the Pacific Ring of Fire.

**What Year 4 assumes (from Year 3):** the water cycle; weather vs climate; climate zones and biomes; the fieldwork cycle (question → collect → present → conclude); continents and oceans; the eight compass points.
**What Year 5 relies on:** latitude/longitude and grid references (for detailed map reading with contours and scale); rivers and tectonic processes (for explaining regions); comparing regions fairly (for the Year 5 regional comparison); resources and land use (for trade and economic geography).

## Unit 1 — Where in the world? Latitude, longitude and time zones

**Essential question:** How can we describe the exact location of any place on Earth?

**Key vocabulary:** globe, atlas, Equator, hemisphere, latitude, longitude, Prime Meridian, Tropic of Cancer, Tropic of Capricorn, Arctic Circle, Antarctic Circle, time zone, coordinates, compass

### Lesson 1 — Hemispheres and the Equator
- **Duration:** 35 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can locate the Equator and the Northern, Southern, Eastern and Western Hemispheres on a globe and map." (UK NC KS2 locational knowledge — hemispheres; C3 D2.Geo.1.3-5)
- **Hook:** An orange as a globe: "Cut it in half around the middle — what have you made?"
- **Key activity:** Draw the Equator and Prime Meridian on oranges or balls; find the four hemispheres on a globe; list countries in each; notice most land is in the Northern Hemisphere.
- **Check for understanding:** "Brazil is crossed by the Equator. Which hemispheres is it in?" → Both the Northern and Southern (and the Western) Hemispheres.
- **Differentiation:** support: a labelled world map to colour. / stretch: find a country in all four hemispheres (e.g. Kiribati is often cited because it spans both the Equator and the 180° meridian).
- **Materials:** globes, oranges or balls, marker pens, world maps.
- **Joy:** "Orange Earth" models.

### Lesson 2 — Latitude: lines around the world
- **Duration:** 35 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can use latitude to describe how far north or south a place is." (UK NC KS2 latitude; C3 D2.Geo.1.3-5)
- **Hook:** "Why is it hot in Singapore and cold in Oslo all year round?"
- **Key activity:** Lines of latitude measured in degrees north or south of the Equator (0° to 90°); the Tropics of Cancer and Capricorn (about 23.4° N and S) and the Arctic and Antarctic Circles (about 66.5° N and S); link to climate zones from Year 3.
- **Check for understanding:** "Singapore is about 1° N and Oslo about 60° N. Why is Singapore hotter?" → It is near the Equator, where the Sun's rays are more direct all year.
- **Differentiation:** support: a map with main lines labelled. / stretch: find the latitude of your own town in an atlas.
- **Materials:** atlases, globes, world maps.
- **Joy:** "Latitude line-up" — children holding city cards stand in order from north to south.

### Lesson 3 — Longitude and the Prime Meridian
- **Duration:** 35 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can use longitude to describe how far east or west a place is." (UK NC KS2 longitude, Prime Meridian; C3 D2.Geo.2.3-5)
- **Hook:** "Why does 0° longitude run through Greenwich in London?"
- **Key activity:** Lines of longitude run from pole to pole; 0° is the Prime Meridian (agreed at an international conference in 1884); up to 180° east or west. Plot places on a large world grid.
- **Check for understanding:** "How is longitude different from latitude?" → Latitude measures north–south from the Equator; longitude measures east–west from the Prime Meridian.
- **Differentiation:** support: a simplified grid with 30° lines. / stretch: explain why the Prime Meridian's position was a human decision, not a natural fact.
- **Materials:** globes, atlases, a floor world grid.
- **Joy:** Floor-map "coordinates hop".

### Lesson 4 — Finding places with coordinates
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can give and use latitude and longitude coordinates to find places." (UK NC KS2 locational knowledge; C3 D2.Geo.2.3-5)
- **Hook:** A treasure message: "0° 20' S, 91° W — where are the giant tortoises?" (the Galápagos Islands, roughly)
- **Key activity:** Practise finding cities from coordinates using atlases and digital maps; write coordinates for landmarks; link to coordinates in maths.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why do we always need both latitude and longitude to find an exact place?" → One line alone only gives a band around the Earth; the crossing point of two lines pinpoints the place.
- **Differentiation:** support: whole-degree coordinates and a gridded map. / stretch: use a digital map to find the coordinates of your school.
- **Materials:** atlases, tablets with a digital map, coordinate cards.
- **Joy:** "World treasure hunt" with coordinates.

### Lesson 5 — Time zones
- **Duration:** 35 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can explain why the world has time zones and calculate simple time differences." (UK NC KS2 time zones, day and night; C3 D2.Geo.1.3-5)
- **Hook:** "It's New Year's Eve — which country celebrates first?"
- **Key activity:** Earth turns 360° in 24 hours (15° per hour); time-zone map; calculate times in other cities (whole-hour differences); the International Date Line near 180°. Links to science and maths.
- **Check for understanding:** "When it is 12:00 in Nairobi (UTC+3), what time is it in Lima, Peru (UTC−5)?" → 04:00 (8 hours earlier).
- **Differentiation:** support: two-city time lines. / stretch: explain why some countries (like China) use one time zone across a very wide area.
- **Materials:** time-zone maps, clocks, a globe and lamp.
- **Joy:** "Midnight around the world" New Year countdown simulation.

### Lesson 6 — Unit 1 assessment: world location challenge
- **Duration:** 35 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can use latitude, longitude, hemispheres and time zones to locate and describe places." (UK NC KS2 — assessment)
- **Hook:** "Air-traffic controller challenge."
- **Key activity:** Map task: locate places from coordinates, name hemispheres, find the tropics, calculate a time difference.
- **Check for understanding:** "Describe the location of your country using hemispheres, a continent and approximate latitude." → An accurate multi-part description.
- **Differentiation:** support: atlas page references. / stretch: describe a place using coordinates and neighbouring countries.
- **Materials:** assessment map, atlases.
- **Joy:** Pilot's licence certificate.

**End-of-unit check:** Locate five places from coordinates; describe your own country's location precisely; calculate a whole-hour time difference between two cities.

## Unit 2 — The restless Earth: plates, mountains, volcanoes and earthquakes

**Essential question:** Why do earthquakes and volcanoes happen where they do — and how do people live with them?

**Key vocabulary:** crust, mantle, core, tectonic plate, plate boundary, fold mountain, volcano, magma, lava, crater, vent, active, dormant, extinct, earthquake, epicentre, tsunami, Ring of Fire, hazard, preparedness

### Lesson 7 — Inside the Earth
- **Duration:** 35 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can describe the layers of the Earth and explain that the crust is broken into plates." (UK NC KS2 physical geography — volcanoes and earthquakes; C3 D2.Geo.7.3-5)
- **Hook:** Cut a boiled egg (shell = crust): "What happens if you crack the shell?"
- **Key activity:** Label crust, mantle, outer core and inner core; tectonic plates move a few centimetres a year (about as fast as fingernails grow); map the major plates.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why is a cracked egg a good (but imperfect) model of the Earth?" → The shell is thin and broken into pieces like plates; but Earth's mantle is mostly solid rock that moves very slowly, not liquid like egg white.
- **Differentiation:** support: labelled diagram. / stretch: explain what evidence suggested plates move (matching coastlines and fossils of South America and Africa — Alfred Wegener's idea of continental drift).
- **Materials:** boiled eggs, plate map, layer diagram.
- **Joy:** "Jigsaw continents" — fit South America and Africa together.

### Lesson 8 — Plate boundaries and fold mountains
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can explain how fold mountains form when plates push together." (UK NC KS2 mountains; C3 D2.Geo.7.3-5)
- **Hook:** Push layers of coloured towels or modelling dough together: "Watch mountains grow!"
- **Key activity:** Plate boundaries (moving apart, pushing together, sliding past); the Himalaya formed as the Indian Plate pushed into the Eurasian Plate, and are still rising; the Andes form where the Nazca Plate sinks under the South American Plate. Locate on maps.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why are fossils of sea creatures found near the top of Mount Everest?" → The rock formed under an ancient sea and was pushed up when the plates collided.
- **Differentiation:** support: modelling with guided commentary. / stretch: explain why the Himalaya are still growing.
- **Materials:** layered modelling dough or towels, plate maps, photos of the Himalaya and Andes.
- **Joy:** Make mountain ranges from dough layers.

### Lesson 9 — Volcanoes: how they form and erupt
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can label a volcano and explain why it erupts." (UK NC KS2 volcanoes; C3 D2.Geo.7.3-5)
- **Hook:** Time-lapse footage of an Icelandic eruption (e.g. Fagradalsfjall, 2021).
- **Key activity:** Magma rises through the crust where plates move apart or where one sinks beneath another; label magma chamber, vent, crater, lava, ash cloud; active, dormant and extinct; Iceland sits on the Mid-Atlantic Ridge where plates move apart.
- **Check for understanding:** "What is the difference between magma and lava?" → Magma is molten rock underground; lava is molten rock that has reached the surface.
- **Differentiation:** support: labelling cut-out diagram. / stretch: compare a gentle Icelandic eruption with an explosive one (e.g. Mount St Helens, USA, 1980).
- **Materials:** cross-section diagrams, video, a model volcano (bicarbonate of soda and vinegar — links to science irreversible changes).
- **Joy:** Erupting model volcano.

### Lesson 10 — Living with volcanoes: Pompeii to Iceland
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can explain why people live near volcanoes and how they reduce the risks." (UK NC KS2 human and physical interaction; C3 D2.Geo.8.3-5)
- **Hook:** Images of Pompeii, buried by Mount Vesuvius in AD 79: "Why did people live there — and why do people live near Vesuvius today?"
- **Key activity:** Benefits (fertile soil, geothermal energy — Iceland heats most homes with it, tourism, minerals) vs risks; monitoring and evacuation plans; the 2010 Eyjafjallajökull ash cloud that stopped flights across Europe.
- **Check for understanding:** "Give one reason people choose to live near a volcano and one way they stay safer." → Fertile soil/geothermal energy; monitoring and evacuation plans.
- **Differentiation:** support: benefits/risks sorting cards. / stretch: weigh up whether a family should stay or leave, from different viewpoints.
- **Materials:** images, sorting cards, a map of Italy and Iceland.
- **Joy:** "Volcano town council" role-play.

### Lesson 11 — Earthquakes
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can explain what causes earthquakes and how they are measured." (UK NC KS2 earthquakes; C3 D2.Geo.7.3-5)
- **Hook:** Two tables pushed together then released suddenly: "Feel the jolt?"
- **Key activity:** Plates catch and slip; epicentre and focus; seismometers record shaking; magnitude scales (each whole number is a much bigger quake). Make a simple seismometer with a pen, paper strip and box.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why do most earthquakes happen near plate boundaries?" → That's where plates push, pull or slide past each other and stress builds up and is released.
- **Differentiation:** support: step-by-step seismometer build. / stretch: compare earthquake maps with plate boundary maps and describe the pattern.
- **Materials:** boxes, pens, paper strips, string, earthquake map, plate map.
- **Joy:** Test home-made seismometers with "desk quakes".

### Lesson 12 — The Ring of Fire and tsunamis
- **Duration:** 35 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can describe the Ring of Fire and explain how earthquakes can cause tsunamis." (UK NC KS2 physical geography; C3 D2.Geo.7.3-5)
- **Hook:** A world map of volcanoes and earthquakes: "What shape do you see around the Pacific?"
- **Key activity:** Plot volcanoes and earthquakes around the Pacific; tsunamis caused by undersea earthquakes; the 2011 Tōhoku earthquake and tsunami in Japan; warning systems.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why do Pacific countries like Japan, Chile and Indonesia share warning systems?" → They are all on the Ring of Fire, and a tsunami can cross the whole ocean.
- **Differentiation:** support: guided plotting. / stretch: explain why tsunami waves grow taller near the coast.
- **Materials:** world maps, stickers, a water tray for wave modelling.
- **Joy:** Wave tray demonstration.

### Lesson 13 — Preparing for hazards: building for earthquakes
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can design and test a structure that resists shaking and explain how countries prepare for earthquakes." (UK NC KS2 human geography — hazards; C3 D2.Geo.8.3-5)
- **Hook:** "Tokyo has many skyscrapers in an earthquake zone. How do they stay up?"
- **Key activity:** Earthquake-resistant design (wide bases, flexible materials, cross-bracing, base isolators); build towers from spaghetti and marshmallows or straws; test on a jelly or shake table. Discuss earthquake drills ("drop, cover, hold on").
- **Check for understanding:** "Why might the same size earthquake cause more damage in one country than another?" → Building quality, preparation, warning systems and emergency services differ.
- **Differentiation:** support: template design. / stretch: improve the design after testing and explain why.
- **Materials:** spaghetti or straws, marshmallows or tape, a tray of jelly or a shake table.
- **Joy:** Shake-table tower test.

### Lesson 14 — Unit 2 assessment
- **Duration:** 35 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can explain tectonic hazards and how people live with them." (UK NC KS2 — assessment)
- **Hook:** "Geology news report."
- **Key activity:** Label a volcano; explain a plate map; write a short report on a hazard and how people respond.
- **Check for understanding:** "Using the map, explain why Iceland has volcanoes." → It sits on the Mid-Atlantic Ridge where two plates move apart and magma rises.
- **Differentiation:** support: word bank. / stretch: compare two hazard events.
- **Materials:** assessment sheet, maps.
- **Joy:** Present as a TV "geology news" report.

**End-of-unit check:** Label a volcano cross-section; explain with a map why the Ring of Fire has so many earthquakes; describe two ways people reduce the risk from a hazard.

## Unit 3 — Rivers: from source to sea

**Essential question:** How do rivers shape the land — and how do people depend on them?

**Key vocabulary:** source, mouth, tributary, confluence, channel, meander, oxbow lake, delta, estuary, erosion, transportation, deposition, flood plain, drainage basin, upper/middle/lower course, dam

### Lesson 15 — The journey of a river
- **Duration:** 35 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can describe the course of a river from source to mouth using key vocabulary." (UK NC KS2 rivers; C3 D2.Geo.2.3-5)
- **Hook:** A photo of a mountain stream and a wide river delta: "Could these be the same river?"
- **Key activity:** Retrieve the water cycle; upper, middle and lower course; tributaries and confluences; label a river diagram; trace real rivers in atlases (the Amazon, the Nile, the Mekong).
- **Check for understanding:** "Why does a river usually get wider and slower towards its mouth?" → Tributaries add more water and the land becomes flatter.
- **Differentiation:** support: labelling cards. / stretch: explain the difference between an estuary and a delta.
- **Materials:** atlases, river diagrams, photos.
- **Joy:** "River in a tray" — pour water down a sand slope to watch a river form.

### Lesson 16 — Erosion, transportation and deposition
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can explain how rivers erode, carry and drop material." (UK NC KS2 rivers — processes; C3 D2.Geo.7.3-5)
- **Hook:** Shake pebbles in a jar of water for two minutes: "What happened to the sharp edges?"
- **Key activity:** Stream-table experiment: vary slope; observe erosion on the outside of bends and deposition on the inside; link to meanders and oxbow lakes.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why does a meander get bigger over time?" → The water flows faster on the outside of the bend and erodes it, while slower water on the inside drops sediment.
- **Differentiation:** support: guided observation sheet. / stretch: predict and test how slope affects erosion.
- **Materials:** stream table or long tray, sand, gravel, jugs of water, pebbles in jars.
- **Joy:** Stream-table landscapes.

### Lesson 17 — Great rivers of the world
- **Duration:** 35 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can compare major world rivers using data." (UK NC KS2 place knowledge; C3 D2.Geo.2.3-5)
- **Hook:** "The Nile or the Amazon — which is longer? Even experts argue!"
- **Key activity:** Compare the Nile, Amazon, Yangtze, Mississippi and Mekong using length, countries crossed and discharge; bar charts (links to maths); the Amazon carries the most water of any river.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why might scientists disagree about which river is the longest?" → It depends where you decide the source and mouth are, and measurements differ.
- **Differentiation:** support: data cards with rounded numbers. / stretch: explain what 'discharge' means and why the Amazon's is so large.
- **Materials:** atlases, river fact cards, graph paper.
- **Joy:** "River Top Trumps"-style card game (a trumps game with river data).

### Lesson 18 — People and rivers
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can explain how people use rivers and the benefits and problems this brings." (UK NC KS2 human geography — water, settlements; C3 D2.Geo.8.3-5)
- **Hook:** "Egypt has been called 'the gift of the Nile' (a phrase from the ancient Greek writer Herodotus). Why?"
- **Key activity:** Uses: water supply, farming, transport, energy (hydroelectric dams), fishing, recreation; problems: flooding, pollution, disputes between countries sharing a river (e.g. the Mekong or the Nile). Sort and discuss.
- **Check for understanding:** "Give one benefit and one problem of building a big dam." → Benefit: electricity, water storage; problem: flooding land and homes upstream, less sediment and fish downstream.
- **Differentiation:** support: picture sorting. / stretch: consider the views of people upstream and downstream.
- **Materials:** picture cards, maps of the Nile and Mekong.
- **Joy:** "River council" role-play.

### Lesson 19 — Floods and how we manage them
- **Duration:** 35 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can explain what causes river floods and how communities reduce flooding." (UK NC KS2 physical and human geography; C3 D2.Geo.8.3-5)
- **Hook:** Pour water onto a sponge and onto a plastic board: "Which lets water run off faster?"
- **Key activity:** Causes (heavy rain, snowmelt, paved surfaces, deforestation); management (flood walls, planting trees, wetlands, flood warnings); natural flood management vs hard engineering.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why can planting trees upstream reduce flooding downstream?" → Trees and their roots slow rainwater and soak some up, so less rushes into the river at once.
- **Differentiation:** support: sponge/board model with guided questions. / stretch: evaluate two flood-management options for a town.
- **Materials:** sponges, plastic boards, watering cans, trays.
- **Joy:** Build and test a model flood defence.

### Lesson 20 — Local river or stream fieldwork (or virtual fieldwork)
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can collect and record data about a local stream or river and draw conclusions." (UK NC KS2 fieldwork; C3 D2.Geo.3.3-5)
- **Hook:** "Is the stream faster in the middle or at the edge?"
- **Key activity:** Measure width, depth and surface speed (time a float over a measured distance) at safe points; sketch map; record; conclude. If no safe site, use virtual fieldwork data and video.
- **Check for understanding:** "What did your results show, and how could you make them more reliable?" → A pattern (e.g. faster in the middle) and an improvement (repeat readings, more sites).
- **Differentiation:** support: a partner measurement role and recording sheet. / stretch: calculate average speed from repeats.
- **Materials:** metre sticks, tape measures, stopwatches, oranges or dog biscuits as floats, recording sheets, waterproof clothing. Safety: risk assessment; shallow, slow water only; adult supervision; life jackets where required; wash hands after.
- **Joy:** Real outdoor fieldwork.

**End-of-unit check:** Label a river from source to mouth with processes (erosion, transportation, deposition); explain one benefit and one problem of a dam; present stream fieldwork results with a conclusion.

## Unit 4 — Region study: the Amazon and the Andes compared with our region

**Essential question:** How are the lives of people in the Amazon and the Andes similar to and different from ours — and how can we describe a place fairly?

**Key vocabulary:** region, rainforest, canopy, Andes, altitude, climate, Indigenous, settlement, economy, tourism, deforestation, sustainability, stereotype, compare

### Lesson 21 — South America: countries, features and people
- **Duration:** 35 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can locate key countries, cities and physical features of South America." (UK NC KS2 locational knowledge — South America; C3 D2.Geo.2.3-5)
- **Hook:** An empty outline of South America: "What do you already know — and what might be a stereotype?"
- **Key activity:** Map countries, capital cities, the Andes, the Amazon River and rainforest, the Atacama Desert, Patagonia; languages (Spanish, Portuguese, Quechua, Guaraní and many more).
- **Check for understanding:** "Why is it wrong to say 'everyone in South America lives in the rainforest'?" → Most people live in large cities (e.g. São Paulo, Buenos Aires, Lima); the continent has deserts, mountains, grasslands and coasts too.
- **Differentiation:** support: labelled map to complete. / stretch: explain why Brazil speaks Portuguese and most other countries Spanish (history of colonisation).
- **Materials:** atlases, blank maps, photos of South American cities and landscapes.
- **Joy:** "Myth or fact?" stereotype-busting quiz.

### Lesson 22 — The Amazon rainforest: layers and life
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can describe the climate and layers of the Amazon rainforest and the life it supports." (UK NC KS2 biomes, vegetation belts; C3 D2.Geo.7.3-5)
- **Hook:** Rainforest soundscape in a darkened room.
- **Key activity:** Climate graph of Manaus (hot and wet all year); layers (emergent, canopy, understorey, forest floor); animals and plants in each layer; biodiversity.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why is the rainforest so hot and wet all year?" → It is near the Equator (strong sun all year) and warm air rises and forms clouds and rain almost daily.
- **Differentiation:** support: layer diagram with picture cards. / stretch: read a climate graph and describe the pattern.
- **Materials:** climate graph, layer diagram, animal cards, soundscape.
- **Joy:** Build a four-layer rainforest in the classroom corner.

### Lesson 23 — Living in the Amazon: Indigenous communities and cities
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can describe different ways people live in the Amazon region and why their knowledge matters." (UK NC KS2 human geography; C3 D2.Geo.6.3-5)
- **Hook:** "Manaus is a city of over two million people in the middle of the rainforest. How?"
- **Key activity:** Compare life in Manaus (industry, port, tourism) and in Indigenous communities (e.g. the Yanomami, Kayapó) — farming, fishing, forest knowledge, rights to land; use first-person voices and photos from reputable organisations; avoid "primitive" framing.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why is Indigenous knowledge important for protecting the rainforest?" → Indigenous peoples have managed the forest sustainably for thousands of years and know its plants, animals and seasons; research shows forests on Indigenous lands often suffer less deforestation.
- **Differentiation:** support: picture comparison grid. / stretch: consider how a road or dam might affect both groups.
- **Materials:** photos and short testimonies (from organisations such as Survival International or national museum sites), comparison grids.
- **Joy:** "A day in the life" storyboard.

### Lesson 24 — Deforestation and sustainability
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can explain causes and effects of deforestation and evaluate solutions." (UK NC KS2 human-environment interaction; C3 D2.Geo.10.3-5)
- **Hook:** Satellite images of the same area before and after clearing (e.g. from a space agency's public imagery).
- **Key activity:** Causes (cattle ranching, soy, logging, mining, roads); effects (loss of species, climate, Indigenous land); solutions (protected areas, Indigenous land rights, sustainable products, monitoring by satellite). Diamond-rank solutions.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why might a farmer clear forest, and what could help them earn a living without doing so?" → To grow crops/graze cattle to feed their family; sustainable farming, fair prices for forest products, or payment to protect the forest.
- **Differentiation:** support: cause–effect cards. / stretch: consider the viewpoints of a farmer, a government and an Indigenous leader.
- **Materials:** satellite images, cause–effect cards, diamond-ranking sheets.
- **Joy:** "Save the forest" solutions summit.

### Lesson 25 — The Andes: life at altitude
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can describe how altitude affects climate and farming in the Andes of Peru." (UK NC KS2 physical and human geography — mountains; C3 D2.Geo.4.3-5)
- **Hook:** "Cusco is about 3,400 m above sea level. Why do visitors sometimes feel breathless?"
- **Key activity:** Temperature falls with altitude; farming zones by height (maize lower, potatoes higher, llama and alpaca herding highest); Inca terraces (e.g. at Moray) and the thousands of potato varieties grown in Peru; Machu Picchu and tourism.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why do farmers grow different crops at different heights on the same mountain?" → Temperature and conditions change with altitude, so each crop suits a different zone.
- **Differentiation:** support: a mountain diagram with zones. / stretch: explain the benefits and problems of tourism at Machu Picchu.
- **Materials:** photos, altitude diagram, potato varieties (real or pictures).
- **Joy:** Potato tasting (varieties) or build a terraced hillside from clay.

### Lesson 26 — Comparing with our region
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can compare a region of South America with my own region using physical and human geography." (UK NC KS2 understand similarities and differences; C3 D2.Geo.6.3-5)
- **Hook:** "What would a child in Cusco find surprising about where we live — and familiar?"
- **Key activity:** Comparison grid (climate, landscape, jobs, homes, food, transport, languages); use data (climate graphs, population) for both; worked example modelled for the teacher's own region.
- **Check for understanding:** "Give one similarity and one difference between your region and the Andes of Peru, using evidence." → Specific, evidenced comparison.
- **Differentiation:** support: partly filled grid. / stretch: explain what causes one of the differences.
- **Materials:** comparison grids, local and Peruvian climate data, photos.
- **Joy:** Write a postcard to a child in Cusco.

### Lesson 27 — Unit 4 assessment: a fair description
- **Duration:** 35 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can write a fair, evidence-based description of a region and avoid stereotypes." (UK NC KS2 — assessment)
- **Hook:** "Travel-guide editors need accurate writers."
- **Key activity:** Write a short travel-guide entry for the Amazon or Andes using facts, data and balanced language.
- **Check for understanding:** "Point to a sentence where you avoided a stereotype. How?" → A sentence using specific evidence instead of a generalisation.
- **Differentiation:** support: guide frame. / stretch: include a climate graph and its interpretation.
- **Materials:** assessment frame, data.
- **Joy:** Travel-guide booklet.

**End-of-unit check:** A balanced travel-guide entry with a map, climate data and one comparison with the home region; explain two causes and two solutions for deforestation.

## Unit 5 — Settlements, land use and natural resources

**Essential question:** Why are settlements where they are — and how do we share the Earth's resources fairly?

**Key vocabulary:** settlement, hamlet, village, town, city, megacity, site, situation, land use, residential, commercial, industrial, agricultural, recreational, grid reference, natural resource, renewable, non-renewable, sustainable

### Lesson 28 — Why settlements grow where they do
- **Duration:** 35 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can explain the site factors that made early settlements grow." (UK NC KS2 settlements; C3 D2.Geo.5.3-5)
- **Hook:** An imaginary island map: "Where would you build your first village, and why?"
- **Key activity:** Site factors (water, defence, flat land, fertile soil, shelter, crossing points, trade routes); apply to real cities (Cairo by the Nile, Baghdad on the Tigris — links to history, your own town).
- **Check for understanding:** "Why were so many cities built beside rivers?" → Water for drinking and farming, transport and trade, and often flat, fertile land.
- **Differentiation:** support: site-factor picture cards. / stretch: explain why some cities grew even without good site factors (e.g. for trade or government).
- **Materials:** imaginary island maps, atlases.
- **Joy:** "Found a village" island game.

### Lesson 29 — Four- and six-figure grid references
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can use four- and six-figure grid references to locate features on a map." (UK NC KS2 map skills — grid references; C3 D2.Geo.1.3-5)
- **Hook:** "Along the corridor and up the stairs" — find the treasure square.
- **Key activity:** Four-figure references for squares (eastings first, then northings); six-figure references by imagining each square divided into tenths; practise on local maps and an island map.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why do we read eastings before northings?" → It's the agreed convention so everyone finds the same place ("along the corridor, then up the stairs").
- **Differentiation:** support: four-figure only with a large grid. / stretch: give six-figure references to a partner who must find the feature.
- **Materials:** local maps with grids, island maps, rulers, tracing paper.
- **Joy:** Grid-reference treasure hunt.

### Lesson 30 — Land use in our local area
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can identify and map land use in my local area." (UK NC KS2 land use; fieldwork; C3 D2.Geo.3.3-5)
- **Hook:** Aerial photo of the school area: "What is each patch of land used for?"
- **Key activity:** Land-use categories; colour-code a local map from aerial photos; a short walk to check (fieldwork); tally land uses.
- **Check for understanding:** "Which land use is most common near our school, and why do you think that is?" → Evidence-based answer (e.g. residential, because many people live near schools).
- **Differentiation:** support: pre-marked map areas. / stretch: suggest how land use might change in 20 years and why.
- **Materials:** local maps, aerial photos (e.g. from free online mapping), coloured pencils, clipboards.
- **Joy:** Local walk with clipboards.

### Lesson 31 — Natural resources: energy, food, water and minerals
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can describe how natural resources are distributed around the world and explain renewable and non-renewable resources." (UK NC KS2 distribution of natural resources; C3 D2.Geo.9.3-5)
- **Hook:** "Where did everything in your pencil case come from?"
- **Key activity:** Sort resources as renewable/non-renewable; map where some come from (e.g. cocoa from West Africa, copper from Chile, oil from the Middle East, solar potential in deserts); discuss fair access to water.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why is wind a renewable resource but oil is not?" → Wind is constantly replaced by nature; oil took millions of years to form and will run out.
- **Differentiation:** support: picture sorting cards. / stretch: explain how a country with few resources can still become wealthy (trade, skills).
- **Materials:** resource cards, world map, pencil-case objects.
- **Joy:** "Pencil-case world map" — trace where things come from.

### Lesson 32 — Sustainable settlements
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can design a more sustainable neighbourhood and justify my choices." (UK NC KS2 human geography; C3 D2.Geo.10.3-5)
- **Hook:** Photos of green cities (e.g. Singapore's gardens, Copenhagen's cycle lanes, Curitiba's bus system in Brazil).
- **Key activity:** Design a neighbourhood plan (homes, green space, transport, energy, water, waste); use a grid and key; justify choices using sustainability ideas.
- **Check for understanding:** "Explain one feature of your design that makes it more sustainable." → A specific feature and why (e.g. cycle lanes reduce car pollution).
- **Differentiation:** support: a template plan with feature stickers. / stretch: include a trade-off and explain it.
- **Materials:** grid paper, coloured pens, feature stickers, city photos.
- **Joy:** "Future neighbourhood" showcase.

**End-of-unit check:** Give four- and six-figure grid references for features on a local map; explain three site factors of a settlement; classify resources as renewable or non-renewable with reasons.

## Unit 6 — Fieldwork investigation and assessment

**Essential question:** Can I plan and carry out my own geographical investigation of the local area?

**Key vocabulary:** enquiry question, hypothesis, data, tally, survey, sketch map, bar chart, conclusion, evaluation

### Lesson 33 — Planning the investigation
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can plan a local fieldwork investigation with a question, method and data sheet." (UK NC KS2 fieldwork; C3 D2.Geo.3.3-5)
- **Hook:** "What would make our local area better?"
- **Key activity:** Choose a question (e.g. Where is traffic busiest near school? Where is litter worst? How do people travel to school?); plan method, locations (grid references) and data sheets; risk assessment.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why is it important to collect data at the same time at each site?" → To make the comparison fair.
- **Differentiation:** support: choose from three prepared investigations. / stretch: write a hypothesis and plan to test it.
- **Materials:** local maps, planning sheets.
- **Joy:** Children choose their investigation.

### Lesson 34 — Collecting and presenting data
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can collect data in the field and present it on maps and graphs." (UK NC KS2 fieldwork — record and present)
- **Hook:** Clipboards out: "Geographers go outside."
- **Key activity:** Collect data (tallies, counts, photos, sketch maps); present with bar charts and annotated maps.
- **Check for understanding:** "Which graph or map best shows your data, and why?" → A justified choice.
- **Differentiation:** support: roles in a group. / stretch: add digital mapping or photos with annotations.
- **Materials:** clipboards, tally sheets, cameras/tablets, graph paper. Safety: adult supervision, high-visibility vests near roads.
- **Joy:** Outdoor fieldwork.

### Lesson 35 — Conclusions, recommendations and end-of-year assessment
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can draw conclusions, make a recommendation and show my geography learning from the year." (UK NC KS2 — assessment)
- **Hook:** "Present your findings to the 'town council' (headteacher or a local councillor)."
- **Key activity:** Write a conclusion and a recommendation for improving the area; short end-of-year map and knowledge check.
- **Check for understanding:** "What does your data show, and what would you recommend? How confident are you in your results?" → A conclusion, a recommendation and an honest evaluation.
- **Differentiation:** support: conclusion frame. / stretch: suggest a follow-up investigation.
- **Materials:** fieldwork results, assessment sheet.
- **Joy:** Presenting to a real decision-maker.

**End-of-unit check:** A completed fieldwork report (question, method, data presentation, conclusion, recommendation, evaluation) and the end-of-year map-skills check.

## Teacher guidance

**Common misconceptions (and fixes).**
- *"The Earth's inside is all liquid lava."* — The mantle is mostly solid rock that flows very slowly; only small parts melt to form magma.
- *"Volcanoes are just mountains that explode."* — Explain plate boundaries and gentle vs explosive eruptions.
- *"Rivers always flow south (down the page)."* — Rivers flow downhill, whichever direction that is; show the Nile flowing north.
- *"Latitude and longitude are the same kind of line."* — use an orange model: lines of latitude are like the rungs of a ladder around the globe; lines of longitude are long and meet at the poles.
- *"Everyone in South America lives in the rainforest."* — show cities, deserts and mountains; most people live in cities.
- *"Hot countries are poor."* — challenge with data (e.g. Singapore, Qatar) and discuss the real causes of wealth and poverty.
- *"Deforestation is only farmers' fault."* — show global demand for beef, soy and timber.

**Fieldwork and safety.** Carry out risk assessments for every trip; river fieldwork only at shallow, slow, supervised sites (or virtually); high-visibility vests near roads.

**International notes.** Every lesson on "our region" or "local area" adapts to any country; the teacher models a worked example (e.g. "Our town is at 51° N, beside the river…"). If your school *is* in South America, choose a region on another continent (e.g. the Himalaya of Nepal or the Mekong delta of Vietnam) for Unit 4 and compare it with your own.

**Vertical links.** Year 3: water cycle, climate zones and biomes, fieldwork basics. Year 5: detailed map skills (contours, scale), trade and economic geography, climate change and comparing regions — building on this year's locational skills, tectonics and rivers.

**Cross-curricular links.** Science (Earth and space; rocks; time zones; forces in earthquakes), maths (coordinates, grid references, graphs, time differences), history (Baghdad on the Tigris; the Maya of Central America), English (*The Explorer*, *Esperanza Rising*'s Mexican and Californian landscapes), Spanish (South American places and languages), art (Andean textiles).
