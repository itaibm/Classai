# Spanish — Year 4 (ages 8–9) — Full Tutor Scripts

> Deliver-it-now scripts for **every** lesson in [`../spanish-year-4.md`](../spanish-year-4.md).
> Format per [`../../../SCRIPTING-GUIDE.md`](../../../SCRIPTING-GUIDE.md); content from
> `knowledge-base/subjects/languages/spanish-ks2.md` and `spanish-extension.md`.
>
> **Read-aloud conventions.** Instructions to the child are in **English**; the language to
> practise is in **Spanish**. Always present new language **by ear first, spelling second**.
> Pronunciation hints are plain-English approximations with the **STRESSED** syllable in CAPITALS.
> Spanish *h* is silent; *ll* = "y"; *rr* is a rolled/tapped r; *ñ* = "ny"; *z*/soft *c* = "th"
> in Spain or "s" in Latin America (both fine). Keep it warm, fast and forgiving — **praise the
> communication first**, then gently model the correct form.
>
> 14 lessons across 5 units. Each is fully timed; the ⏱ ranges sum to the stated duration.

## Unit 1 — La familia: my family

---

## Lesson 1 — Los miembros de la familia
**Spanish · Year 4 · Unit 1 (Family)** | **Duration:** 30 min | **Objective:** "By the end, I can name family members in Spanish."

**You will need:** a blank family-tree sheet, family-photo cards (or a cartoon family picture), a whiteboard.
**Prior knowledge to retrieve:** Year-3 greetings (*hola, me llamo*); the idea that *-o*/*-a* endings can mark boy/girl.
**Key vocabulary:** *madre* (MA-dre) mother · *padre* (PA-dre) father · *hermano* (er-MA-no) brother · *hermana* (er-MA-na) sister · *abuelo/abuela* (ah-BWEH-lo/-la) grandfather/grandmother · *tío/tía* (TEE-o/TEE-a) uncle/aunt · *primo/prima* (PREE-mo/-ma) cousin · *bebé* (beh-BEH) baby.
**What to emphasize (the heart of the lesson):** the **-o = boy / -a = girl** pattern runs through the whole family (herman**o**/herman**a**, prim**o**/prim**a**, tí**o**/tí**a**). Say each word *by ear first*. Pre-empt the slip of using *hermano* for a sister — the ending tells you who.

---

### ⏱ 0–4 min · HOOK
> **Say:** "Today we're building a family tree — but every label is in Spanish! Let's warm up first. *¡Hola! ¿Cómo estás?*"
**Show:** a half-drawn family tree on the board with blank labels.
**Do:** let the child answer the greeting; sketch yourself at the top of the tree.
**Ask (retrieve prior knowledge):** "In Spanish, which ending often means a boy, and which means a girl?" → *listen for:* "-o for boy, -a for girl."
*(If shaky: remind them — *niñ**o*** boy / *niñ**a*** girl, then move on.)*

### ⏱ 4–11 min · EXPLAIN (I do)
> **Say:** "Listen first, then say each one back to me. *madre* (MA-dre) — mother. *padre* (PA-dre) — father. *hermano* (er-MA-no) — brother. *hermana* (er-MA-na) — sister. Hear the change? Herman-**O** for a boy, herman-**A** for a girl."
**Show / Do:** point to each family-tree slot as you say the word; write the word *after* they've heard it.
> **Say:** "More: *abuelo* (ah-BWEH-lo) grandfather, *abuela* grandmother. *tío* (TEE-o) uncle, *tía* aunt. *primo* (PREE-mo) cousin-boy, *prima* cousin-girl. *bebé* (beh-BEH) baby."
**Emphasize:** "Same trick every time — **-o for the boy, -a for the girl.**"

### ⏱ 11–17 min · EXAMPLE (we do)
> **Say:** "Let's label this cartoon family together. Here's the dad — what's the Spanish?"
**Do:** point to figures one at a time; the child supplies *padre, madre, hermano…*; you write each label on the tree.
**Ask:** "This one's the grandmother — boy ending or girl ending?" → *listen for:* *abuel**a*** (girl, -a).

### ⏱ 17–22 min · CHECK
**Ask:** "Say 'brother' and then 'sister' in Spanish."
- ✅ **Expected:** *hermano* / *hermana* — then probe: *"How did you know which ending to use?"*
- ❌ **If "hermano" for both:** the -o/-a contrast hasn't landed → **fix:** say them as a pair, stretching the last sound — "herman-OHH (boy)… herman-AHH (girl)" — and have the child point to a boy then a girl as they say each.
- ❌ **If "hermana" is pronounced 'her-MA-na' with English h:** Spanish *h* is silent → **fix:** model "er-MA-na", pair with *hola* ("OH-la") so the silent *h* clicks.

### ⏱ 22–27 min · PRACTICE (you do)
**Do:** the child labels their **own** (real or pretend) family tree on the blank sheet — at least five labels said aloud as they write each.
**Watch for:** wrong-ending slips (writing *primo* for a girl cousin); silent-*h* pronunciation.
> **If the child is stuck (missed twice):** 1) don't say the word for them; 2) name *why* — "the ending tells us boy or girl"; 3) shrink it — point to just one picture, a clear boy or girl; 4) give ONE hint: "boy ends in…?"; 5) re-ask that single word, then add another; 6) keep it safe — "Mistakes show us what to practise — let's do one small one."

### ⏱ 27–30 min · RECAP
> **Say:** "Teach me back — name three people in your family in Spanish."
**Process praise:** "You listened first, then matched the right ending every time — that's exactly how to learn the words."
**Revisit later:** quick *¿quién es?* picture-flash retrieval at the start of Lessons 2 and 3.

---

**Differentiation**
- **Support (gentle):** match a word card to a face card before producing it; give just four core words (*madre, padre, hermano, hermana*) with a guaranteed early win.
- **Stretch (challenge):** add *tíos* and *primos*; ask "why does *abuela* end in -a?" and have them explain the rule.

**Extension (if time allows):** make a tree for a favourite cartoon family (e.g. the Simpsons) and label every member in Spanish, choosing correct -o/-a endings.
**Assessment evidence:** the child's labelled tree shows ≥5 correct family words with correct -o/-a endings, said aloud.

## Lesson 2 — Mi y mis: saying "my"
**Spanish · Year 4 · Unit 1 (Family)** | **Duration:** 25 min | **Objective:** "By the end, I can say 'my…' for one person (*mi*) and more than one (*mis*)."

**You will need:** finger-cue cards (1 finger = *mi*, 2 fingers = *mis*); the family words from Lesson 1.
**Prior knowledge to retrieve:** family vocabulary (*madre, abuelos, primos*).
**Key vocabulary:** *mi* (mee) my (one) · *mis* (mees) my (more than one) · *mis abuelos* (mees ah-BWEH-los) my grandparents · *mis primos* my cousins.
**What to emphasize (the heart of the lesson):** **one person → *mi*; more than one → *mis*** (the extra *s* matches the extra people). Pre-empt the slip *mi hermanos* — if there are several, the "my" word also needs its *s*.

---

### ⏱ 0–3 min · HOOK
> **Say:** "Quick question: one brother, or two? Watch — in Spanish the word for 'my' actually *changes*! One finger…" *(hold up one finger)* "…or two fingers." *(hold up two.)*
**Show:** finger-cue cards: ☝️ = *mi*, ✌️ = *mis*.
**Ask (retrieve prior knowledge):** "Tell me three family words from last time." → *listen for:* any three (*madre, abuelo, primo…*).

### ⏱ 3–9 min · EXPLAIN (I do)
> **Say:** "Listen. One person: *mi madre* (mee MA-dre) — my mother. *mi hermano* — my brother. More than one person: *mis abuelos* (mees ah-BWEH-los) — my grandparents. *mis primos* — my cousins. Hear it? *mi* for one… *mi-S* for many."
**Show / Do:** raise one finger on every *mi*, two fingers on every *mis*. Write *mi* / *mis* with the extra *s* underlined.
**Emphasize:** "**More people, more letters — *mi* becomes *mis*.**"

### ⏱ 9–14 min · EXAMPLE (we do)
> **Say:** "My turn to say the family word, your turn to put 'my' in front — and show me the fingers. *…padre.*"
**Do:** you say *padre* → child says *mi padre* (one finger); you say *abuelos* → child says *mis abuelos* (two fingers).
**Ask:** "*…primos* — one finger or two?" → *listen for:* two fingers, *mis primos*.

### ⏱ 14–18 min · CHECK
**Ask:** "Say 'my mother' and then 'my grandparents.'"
- ✅ **Expected:** *mi madre* / *mis abuelos* — then probe: *"Why does the second one get an extra s?"*
- ❌ **If "mi abuelos":** the plural cue was missed → **fix:** "How many grandparents? More than one — so 'my' needs its *s* too: *mi-s*." Show two fingers.
- ❌ **If "mis madre":** over-applied the *s* to a single person → **fix:** "Just one mum — one finger — *mi madre*."

### ⏱ 18–22 min · PRACTICE (you do)
**Do:** "one or many?" clap game — you say a family word; the child says it with *mi* or *mis* and **claps once** for *mi*, **twice** for *mis*. Run 8–10 words, mixing singular and plural.
**Watch for:** the *mi hermanos* slip (several brothers but singular "my").
> **If the child is stuck (missed twice):** 1) don't supply it; 2) name *why* — "we're counting the people, not the word"; 3) hold up the matching fingers as a silent cue; 4) one hint: "more than one person needs…?"; 5) re-ask the same item, then a new one; 6) keep it safe — "Good thinking — let's take one small step."

### ⏱ 22–25 min · RECAP
> **Say:** "Tell me the rule in your own words — when do we use *mis*?"
**Process praise:** "You used the finger cue to check yourself — that self-check is what good linguists do."
**Revisit later:** fold into Lesson 3 (full introductions) and the Unit-1 check.

---

**Differentiation**
- **Support (gentle):** choose *mi/mis* using only the finger cards; keep to four familiar words.
- **Stretch (challenge):** build a whole sentence — *Estos son mis primos* (these are my cousins) — and explain why it's *mis*.

**Extension (if time allows):** sort a pile of word cards (*madre / abuelos / tío / primos…*) into a *mi* pile and a *mis* pile, saying each aloud.
**Assessment evidence:** the child chooses *mi* vs *mis* correctly for ≥4 of 5 mixed singular/plural prompts.

## Lesson 3 — Este es mi… + age
**Spanish · Year 4 · Unit 1 (Family)** | **Duration:** 30 min | **Objective:** "By the end, I can introduce a family member with their name and age."

**You will need:** family photos or drawings; sentence-frame strips (*Este/Esta es mi ___. Se llama ___. Tiene ___ años.*); number cards 1–12.
**Prior knowledge to retrieve:** numbers (Year 3), family words, *mi/mis*; that age in Spanish uses *tener* ("to have").
**Key vocabulary:** *Este es mi…* (ES-te es mee) this is my… (male) · *Esta es mi…* (ES-ta) this is my… (female) · *Se llama…* (se YA-ma) he/she is called… · *Tiene … años* (tee-EH-ne … AN-yos) is … years old.
**What to emphasize (the heart of the lesson):** **age uses *tener*, not "to be"** — *Tiene siete años* = "has seven years", never "*es siete*". And **Este** for a male, **Esta** for a female. String three short sentences into one smooth introduction.

---

### ⏱ 0–4 min · HOOK
> **Say:** "Show me a photo and I'll tell you *who* it is and *how old* — all in Spanish. *Este es mi padre. Se llama David. Tiene cuarenta años.* Your turn to learn the trick!"
**Show:** one of your own photo cards held up like a museum guide.
**Ask (retrieve prior knowledge):** "How do we say 'has' / 'have' — the word we used for age? *Tengo nueve años* — I have nine years." → *listen for:* *tengo/tiene* (the *tener* family).
*(If shaky: re-say *Tengo ___ años* about yourself, child copies with their own age.)*

### ⏱ 4–11 min · EXPLAIN (I do)
> **Say:** "Three little steps. Step one — point and say who: *Este es mi padre* (a man) or *Esta es mi madre* (a woman). *Este* for a he, *Esta* for a she. Step two — the name: *Se llama Tom* (se YA-ma) — 'he's called Tom'. Step three — the age: *Tiene siete años* (tee-EH-ne … AN-yos) — 'he has seven years'."
**Show / Do:** lay the three sentence-frame strips in a row; touch each as you say its part.
**Emphasize:** "Age is **has**, not **is** — *Tiene* siete años. We never say '*es* siete'."

### ⏱ 11–18 min · EXAMPLE (we do)
> **Say:** "Let's introduce my brother together. He's a 'he', so… *Este es mi…?*"
**Do:** child completes *hermano*; you prompt the name slot (*Se llama ___*) and the age slot; child fills each using the strips and a number card.
**Ask:** "Now a sister, age ten — start it." → *listen for:* *Esta es mi hermana. Se llama… Tiene diez años.*

### ⏱ 18–23 min · CHECK
**Ask:** "Introduce your brother Sam, who is 8."
- ✅ **Expected:** *Este es mi hermano. Se llama Sam. Tiene ocho años.* — then probe: *"Why *Este* and not *Esta*?"*
- ❌ **If "Mi hermano es ocho":** age translated as "is" → **fix:** "Age uses *tener* — he **has** eight years: *Tiene ocho años.*" Mime holding 8 in your hands.
- ❌ **If "Esta es mi hermano":** wrong demonstrative for a male → **fix:** "A brother is a 'he' — *Este* es mi hermano." Pair *Este* with a boy photo, *Esta* with a girl photo.

### ⏱ 23–28 min · PRACTICE (you do)
**Do:** "family museum" — the child is a tour guide introducing **three** family members from their photos/drawings, each with all three sentences. You play a visitor and ask back *¿Cómo se llama?* and *¿Cuántos años tiene?*
**Watch for:** the *es* age slip; *Este/Esta* mix-ups; dropping *mi*.
> **If the child is stuck (missed twice):** 1) don't model the whole line; 2) name *why* — "which step are we on?"; 3) hand them the matching sentence strip to lean on; 4) one starter: "*Tiene…*"; 5) re-ask just that one sentence, then rebuild the set of three; 6) keep it safe — "Three little steps — let's nail one, then add the next."

### ⏱ 28–30 min · RECAP
> **Say:** "Say back the three steps of a Spanish introduction."
**Process praise:** "You kept the three steps in order and remembered age uses *tener* — that's a real conversation now."
**Revisit later:** **End-of-unit check** — introduce three family members; revisit ages in Lesson 9 (dates also use numbers).

---

**Differentiation**
- **Support (gentle):** keep the three sentence strips in front; pre-fill the name; child supplies only the age.
- **Stretch (challenge):** ask the tutor about their family — *¿Cuántos años tiene tu hermano?* — and report the answer (*Tiene…*).

**Extension (if time allows):** present the whole tree as a guided "tour", linking members with *y* (*Este es mi padre, y esta es mi madre…*).
**Assessment evidence (Unit 1 check):** the child introduces three family members with *Este/Esta es mi… Se llama… Tiene … años*, using *mi/mis* correctly and *tener* for age.

## Lesson 4 — Tengo un perro: animals & *tener*
**Spanish · Year 4 · Unit 2 (Animals)** | **Duration:** 30 min | **Objective:** "By the end, I can name pets and say which I have (*Tengo un…*)."

**You will need:** animal flashcards, toy animals if available, a whiteboard.
**Prior knowledge to retrieve:** *un/una* (Year 3 school objects); *tengo* from age (*Tengo … años*).
**Key vocabulary:** *un perro* (PEH-rro, roll the rr) dog · *un gato* (GA-to) cat · *un pez* (peth) fish · *un conejo* (ko-NEH-ho) rabbit · *un pájaro* (PA-ha-ro) bird · *una tortuga* (tor-TOO-ga) tortoise · *Tengo un…* (TEN-go) I have a… · *No tengo un animal* I don't have a pet.
**What to emphasize (the heart of the lesson):** the **rolled/tapped *rr*** in *perro* — without it, *perro* (dog) becomes *pero* (but)! And **keep *un/una*** after *tengo* — *Tengo **un** perro*, never "tengo perro".

---

### ⏱ 0–4 min · HOOK
> **Say:** "Does a Spanish dog say 'woof'? No — it says *¡guau!* (gwow). Today we meet the animals — and the trickiest sound in Spanish: the rolling *rr* in *perro*. Listen: *pe-RRRR-o.*"
**Show:** an animal flashcard fan; the word *perro* on the board.
**Ask (retrieve prior knowledge):** "What are the two little words for 'a' in Spanish?" → *listen for:* *un* and *una*.

### ⏱ 4–11 min · EXPLAIN (I do)
> **Say:** "Listen, then repeat. *un perro* (PEH-rro — buzz that *rr*!), *un gato* (GA-to), *un pez* (peth), *un conejo* (ko-NEH-ho), *un pájaro* (PA-ha-ro), *una tortuga* (tor-TOO-ga)."
**Show / Do:** flash each card as you say it; have the child echo. Practise *rr* alone first — "make your tongue flutter: rr-rr-rr."
> **Say:** "Now say what you have: *Tengo un perro* (TEN-go) — 'I have a dog'. Don't have a pet? *No tengo un animal.*"
**Emphasize:** "Roll the *rr* — *pe**rr**o* is a dog; *pero* with one *r* means 'but'! And always keep the *un*: *Tengo **un** gato.*"

### ⏱ 11–17 min · EXAMPLE (we do)
> **Say:** "I'll show a card; you tell me the animal with *un/una* in front."
**Do:** flash *gato* → child *un gato*; flash *tortuga* → child *una tortuga* (point out: tortuga takes *una*).
**Ask:** "Now put it in a sentence — you 'have' the dog." → *listen for:* *Tengo un perro* (with a tapped *rr*).

### ⏱ 17–22 min · CHECK
**Ask:** "Say 'I have a dog.'"
- ✅ **Expected:** *Tengo un perro* — then probe: *"Can you make that *rr* roll a little more?"*
- ❌ **If "Tengo pero" (flat r, no article):** sounds like "I have but", and *un* dropped → **fix:** isolate the *rr* ("rr-rr"), rebuild *pe-rro*, then add *un* back: *Tengo **un** perro.*
- ❌ **If "Tengo perro" (no un):** article dropped → **fix:** "We keep the little 'a' — *Tengo **un** perro*", tap the *un* card.

### ⏱ 22–27 min · PRACTICE (you do)
**Do:** animal-sound charades — the child makes an animal's sound/mime; you guess; then they say *Tengo un…* for their **real or dream** pet. Run several animals.
**Watch for:** missing *rr*; dropped *un/una*; *una tortuga* vs *un perro* article choice.
> **If the child is stuck (missed twice):** 1) don't say it for them; 2) name *why* — "let's check that *rr*" or "where's the little 'a'?"; 3) shrink to just the word, no sentence; 4) one hint — flutter your tongue as a model; 5) re-ask the word, then rebuild *Tengo un…*; 6) keep it safe — "That *rr* is hard for everyone — small steps."

### ⏱ 27–30 min · RECAP
> **Say:** "Tell me which pet you'd love — in a full Spanish sentence."
**Process praise:** "You kept practising that rolling *rr* and remembered the little *un* — that's careful Spanish."
**Revisit later:** retrieve animal words at the start of Lessons 5 and 6; *rr* practice each lesson warm-up.

---

**Differentiation**
- **Support (gentle):** *Tengo un ___* with a picture to point at; accept a light tongue-tap for *rr*.
- **Stretch (challenge):** list two pets with *y* — *Tengo un perro y un gato.*

**Extension (if time allows):** add *un caballo, un ratón, una vaca, un león, un elefante* and a sound for each; say *Tengo un…* for a zoo animal.
**Assessment evidence:** the child names ≥4 animals with correct *un/una* and produces *Tengo un perro* with an audible *rr*.

## Lesson 5 — ¿Tienes un animal? (asking)
**Spanish · Year 4 · Unit 2 (Animals)** | **Duration:** 25 min | **Objective:** "By the end, I can ask someone if they have a pet and answer back."

**You will need:** a survey grid with animal pictures; animal flashcards.
**Prior knowledge to retrieve:** *Tengo un… / No tengo un animal* (Lesson 4); animal words.
**Key vocabulary:** *¿Tienes un animal?* (tee-EH-nes) Do you have a pet? · *Sí, tengo un…* Yes, I have a… · *No, no tengo un animal* No, I don't have a pet · *Tiene un…* (tee-EH-ne) he/she has a…
**What to emphasize (the heart of the lesson):** **ask = *tienes*, answer = *tengo*** (same verb *tener*, different endings — *you* vs *I*). Keep the *un/una* in both question and answer. Pre-empt dropping the article under the pressure of a real exchange.

---

### ⏱ 0–3 min · HOOK
> **Say:** "Let's find out who at home has a pet — but the rule is: you may **only** ask in Spanish! The magic question is *¿Tienes un animal?* (tee-EH-nes). Listen and copy."
**Show:** the survey grid with pet pictures.
**Ask (retrieve prior knowledge):** "How do you say 'I have a cat'?" → *listen for:* *Tengo un gato.*

### ⏱ 3–9 min · EXPLAIN (I do)
> **Say:** "When *I* speak about myself: *tengo*. When I *ask you*: *tienes*. Listen — *¿Tienes un animal?* — 'Do **you** have a pet?' Answer two ways: *Sí, tengo un perro.* / *No, no tengo un animal.*"
**Show / Do:** write *tengo* (I) and *tienes* (you?) side by side; underline the endings *-o* / *-es*.
**Emphasize:** "Ask with *tienes*, answer with *tengo* — and keep the little *un* both times."

### ⏱ 9–14 min · EXAMPLE (we do)
> **Say:** "I'll be the asker, you answer; then we swap. *¿Tienes un animal?*"
**Do:** child answers *Sí, tengo un…* / *No, no tengo un animal*; then the child asks **you** the question and you answer.
**Ask:** "Now you ask me." → *listen for:* a clear *¿Tienes un animal?* with rising question tone.

### ⏱ 14–18 min · CHECK
**Ask:** "Ask someone if they have a pet."
- ✅ **Expected:** *¿Tienes un animal?* — then probe: *"And how would you answer it yourself?"*
- ❌ **If "¿Tengo un animal?":** used the "I" form to ask → **fix:** "To ask *you*, change the ending — *tien**es***. *Tengo* is only for me." Point to the two-column board.
- ❌ **If "¿Tienes animal?" (no un):** article dropped → **fix:** "Keep the little 'a' — *¿Tienes **un** animal?*"

### ⏱ 18–22 min · PRACTICE (you do)
**Do:** "pet survey" — the child interviews you first, then any family members at home (or toy "people" you each speak for), asking *¿Tienes un animal?*, recording each answer on the grid, then reports one: *Tiene un gato.*
**Watch for:** *tengo/tienes* mix-ups; dropped articles; flat (non-question) intonation.
> **If the child is stuck (missed twice):** 1) don't supply the line; 2) name *why* — "asking or answering?"; 3) point to *tienes* vs *tengo* on the board; 4) one starter: "*¿Tien…?*"; 5) re-ask the question alone, then add an answer; 6) keep it safe — "You're really talking in Spanish now — one small fix."

### ⏱ 22–25 min · RECAP
> **Say:** "What's the difference between *tengo* and *tienes*?"
**Process praise:** "You stayed in Spanish for the whole survey and switched between *ask* and *answer* — brilliant."
**Revisit later:** the reporting form *Tiene un…* feeds Lesson 6 (describing) and the Unit-2 check.

---

**Differentiation**
- **Support (gentle):** child gives a yes/no answer only (*Sí / No*) before building the full sentence.
- **Stretch (challenge):** report the tutor's answer in the third person — *Tiene un gato* — and ask a follow-up.

**Extension (if time allows):** tally the household's pets (real and dream) on a chart and announce the most popular in Spanish (*El animal favorito es el perro*).
**Assessment evidence:** the child asks *¿Tienes un animal?* correctly and gives both a *Sí* and a *No* answer with the article kept.

## Lesson 6 — Un gato negro: colours that agree
**Spanish · Year 4 · Unit 2 (Animals)** | **Duration:** 30 min | **Objective:** "By the end, I can describe an animal's colour, making the colour agree (*-o/-a*)."

**You will need:** coloured animal cards with **both** masculine and feminine nouns (e.g. *gato, perro, caballo* / *vaca, tortuga*); colours from Year 3.
**Prior knowledge to retrieve:** colours (*negro, blanco, rojo…*); noun gender (*un/una*); colour goes *after* the noun.
**Key vocabulary:** *negro/negra* (NEH-gro/-gra) black · *blanco/blanca* (BLAN-ko/-ka) white · *un gato negro* a black cat · *una vaca negra* a black cow · *una vaca blanca* a white cow.
**What to emphasize (the heart of the lesson):** **the colour's ending must match the animal's gender** — masculine noun → colour ends **-o** (*gato negr**o***); feminine noun → colour ends **-a** (*vaca negr**a***). The colour comes **after** the noun. Pre-empt the classic slip *un gato negra*.

---

### ⏱ 0–4 min · HOOK
> **Say:** "Here's a puzzle. A black *cat* is *un gato negr**o***. But a black *cow* is *una vaca negr**a***. The word for 'black' just changed its ending! Why? Let's crack it."
**Show:** a black-cat card next to a black-cow card, with *negro* / *negra* under them.
**Ask (retrieve prior knowledge):** "Which little word goes with *gato* — *un* or *una*? And with *vaca*?" → *listen for:* *un gato*, *una vaca*.

### ⏱ 4–11 min · EXPLAIN (I do)
> **Say:** "The colour has to *agree* — to match — with the animal. A masculine animal (the *un* ones) takes a colour ending in **-o**: *un gato negr**o***, *un perro blanc**o***. A feminine animal (the *una* ones) takes **-a**: *una vaca negr**a***, *una tortuga blanc**a***. And the colour always comes **after** the animal — never before."
**Show / Do:** sort cards into an *un* (-o) column and an *una* (-a) column; write the matching colour ending in each.
**Emphasize:** "**Match the endings: *un … -o*, *una … -a*.** The colour follows the animal."

### ⏱ 11–18 min · EXAMPLE (we do)
> **Say:** "Let's describe together. Black cat — *un gato…?*"
**Do:** child supplies *negro*; you give a white cow → child supplies *una vaca blanca*; mix a few, you naming the animal, child agreeing the colour.
**Ask:** "A white cat?" → *listen for:* *un gato blanco* (masc. -o).

### ⏱ 18–23 min · CHECK
**Ask:** "Describe a black cat, then a black cow."
- ✅ **Expected:** *un gato negro* / *una vaca negra* — then probe: *"How did you decide the ending each time?"*
- ❌ **If "un gato negra":** colour didn't agree with a masculine noun → **fix:** "*gato* is an *un* word — so the colour ends like *un*: negr**o**." Point to the *-o* column.
- ❌ **If "un gato negro / un vaca negro" (vaca kept masc.):** gender of *vaca* missed → **fix:** "*vaca* is an *una* word — so the colour ends in **-a**: negr**a**."

### ⏱ 23–28 min · PRACTICE (you do)
**Do:** "colour-the-zoo" — the child colours/labels several animal cards (mixing masculine and feminine) and describes each aloud with an agreeing colour: *un caballo blanco, una tortuga negra…*
**Watch for:** the *-o/-a* agreement slip; colour placed before the noun.
> **If the child is stuck (missed twice):** 1) don't reveal the ending; 2) name *why* — "is it an *un* word or an *una* word?"; 3) put the card in the right column as a visual cue; 4) one hint: "*una* → colour ends in…?"; 5) re-ask that animal, then a fresh one; 6) keep it safe — "Agreement is tricky — let's match just one pair."

### ⏱ 28–30 min · RECAP
> **Say:** "Tell me the rule — what makes a colour end in -o or in -a?"
**Process praise:** "You used the *un/una* of the animal to choose the colour ending — that's the whole secret of agreement."
**Revisit later:** **Unit-2 check** (one masculine + one feminine animal with agreeing colour); agreement returns in Year 6 descriptions.

---

**Differentiation**
- **Support (gentle):** give the colour with the ending blanked (*negr_*) and a cue card showing *un→o / una→a* to choose from.
- **Stretch (challenge):** describe three animals, deliberately mixing genders, and add *y* — *Tengo un gato negro y una tortuga blanca.*

**Extension (if time allows):** describe a whole picture-zoo, every animal with an agreeing colour; spot and fix a deliberate error you plant (*una vaca blanco*).
**Assessment evidence (Unit 2 check):** the child says which pet they have, asks *¿Tienes un animal?*, and describes one masculine and one feminine animal with a correctly agreeing colour.

## Unit 3 — El calendario: days, months & birthdays

## Lesson 7 — Los días de la semana
**Spanish · Year 4 · Unit 3 (Calendar)** | **Duration:** 25 min | **Objective:** "By the end, I can say the days of the week (starting Monday) and today's day."

**You will need:** day cards (one per day), a weekly calendar showing Monday first.
**Prior knowledge to retrieve:** the English days; that Spanish doesn't capitalise like English.
**Key vocabulary:** *lunes* (LOO-nes) Monday · *martes* (MAR-tes) Tuesday · *miércoles* (mee-AIR-ko-les) Wednesday · *jueves* (HWEH-bes) Thursday · *viernes* (vee-AIR-nes) Friday · *sábado* (SA-ba-do) Saturday · *domingo* (do-MEEN-go) Sunday · *Hoy es…* (oy es) Today is…
**What to emphasize (the heart of the lesson):** **the Spanish week starts on Monday**, and the days are **lower-case** (not capital like in English). The accent on *miércoles* and *sábado* marks the stressed syllable.

---

### ⏱ 0–3 min · HOOK
> **Say:** "Spot the difference! In English we write 'Monday' with a big M. In Spanish? *lunes* — small *l*. And the Spanish week starts on **Monday**, not Sunday. Let's learn the seven days as a chant."
**Show:** day cards laid Monday→Sunday; point out the lower-case first letters.
**Ask (retrieve prior knowledge):** "How many days in a week, and which day does ours start on at home?" → *listen for:* seven; (varies) — note Spanish starts Monday.

### ⏱ 3–9 min · EXPLAIN (I do)
> **Say:** "Listen, then chant with me to a clap: *lunes* (LOO-nes), *martes* (MAR-tes), *miércoles* (mee-AIR-ko-les), *jueves* (HWEH-bes), *viernes* (vee-AIR-nes), *sábado* (SA-ba-do), *domingo* (do-MEEN-go)."
**Show / Do:** tap each card on the beat; repeat the chant twice, faster the second time.
**Emphasize:** "Lower-case, Monday-first — and *miércoles* has its stress marked by the accent: mee-**AIR**-ko-les."

### ⏱ 9–14 min · EXAMPLE (we do)
> **Say:** "Let's put the cards in order together — hand me Monday first."
**Do:** child orders the seven cards, saying each as they place it; you fix only the spot, not the word.
**Ask:** "Today — *Hoy es…?*" → *listen for:* the correct day (e.g. *Hoy es martes*).

### ⏱ 14–18 min · CHECK
**Ask:** "Say the days of the week in order, starting from Monday."
- ✅ **Expected:** *lunes, martes, miércoles, jueves, viernes, sábado, domingo* — then probe: *"Which two have an accent, and why?"*
- ❌ **If they start at Sunday:** English habit → **fix:** "Spanish weeks open on Monday — start with *lunes*." Slide the cards so *lunes* is first.
- ❌ **If "miércoles" is flat/mis-stressed:** accent ignored → **fix:** "The little mark shows where to push: mee-**AIR**-ko-les." Clap on AIR.

### ⏱ 18–22 min · PRACTICE (you do)
**Do:** scramble the day cards; the child re-orders them against the clock, chanting; then answers *¿Qué día es hoy?* with *Hoy es…* and (stretch) *mañana es…* (tomorrow).
**Watch for:** capitalising when they "read it back"; Sunday-first ordering; *miércoles* stress.
> **If the child is stuck (missed twice):** 1) don't say the missing day; 2) name *why* — "what comes after *martes*?"; 3) give the first sound only; 4) one hint — hum the chant up to the gap; 5) re-ask from the previous day; 6) keep it safe — "Seven new words is a lot — let's chant the first three."

### ⏱ 22–25 min · RECAP
> **Say:** "Tell me two things that are different about Spanish days versus English ones."
**Process praise:** "You kept the Monday-first order and remembered the lower-case rule — careful noticing."
**Revisit later:** open every lesson with *¿Qué día es hoy?*; days feed dates in Lesson 9 and the Unit-3 check.

---

**Differentiation**
- **Support (gentle):** order just three days; sing the chant with the cards visible.
- **Stretch (challenge):** say yesterday's and tomorrow's day too (*Ayer fue lunes, hoy es martes, mañana es miércoles*).

**Extension (if time allows):** invent a days-of-the-week clapping song or actions for each day.
**Assessment evidence:** the child says all seven days in order (Monday-first, lower-case) and gives today's day with *Hoy es…*.

## Lesson 8 — Los meses del año
**Spanish · Year 4 · Unit 3 (Calendar)** | **Duration:** 25 min | **Objective:** "By the end, I can name the months of the year in Spanish."

**You will need:** month cards (12), seasons pictures, a whiteboard.
**Prior knowledge to retrieve:** English months; that Spanish months are lower-case; days from Lesson 7.
**Key vocabulary:** *enero* (eh-NEH-ro) January · *febrero* February · *marzo* (MAR-tho) March · *abril* (ah-BREEL) April · *mayo* (MA-yo) May · *junio* (HOO-nee-o) June · *julio* (HOO-lee-o) July · *agosto* (ah-GOS-to) August · *septiembre* September · *octubre* October · *noviembre* November · *diciembre* (dee-thee-EM-bre) December.
**What to emphasize (the heart of the lesson):** many months are **near-cognates** of English — hunt the "cousins" (*marzo, abril, mayo, septiembre, octubre*) to learn fast. All **lower-case**, like the days.

---

### ⏱ 0–4 min · HOOK
> **Say:** "Twelve new words today — but here's a secret: lots of them look almost exactly like English! Listen for the 'cousins'. *septiembre… octubre… abril.* Hear English hiding inside? Let's hunt them all."
**Show:** the 12 month cards in a fan.
**Ask (retrieve prior knowledge):** "Are Spanish months capitalised like English ones?" → *listen for:* "no, lower-case" (same as days).

### ⏱ 4–11 min · EXPLAIN (I do)
> **Say:** "Listen and repeat in batches of four. *enero* (eh-NEH-ro), *febrero*, *marzo* (MAR-tho), *abril* (ah-BREEL). Next four: *mayo* (MA-yo), *junio* (HOO-nee-o), *julio* (HOO-lee-o), *agosto* (ah-GOS-to). Last four: *septiembre, octubre, noviembre, diciembre* (dee-thee-EM-bre)."
**Show / Do:** lay the cards in order; circle the near-cognates as the child spots them.
**Emphasize:** "Find the cousins — *marzo/March, abril/April, septiembre/September* — they almost say themselves."

### ⏱ 11–17 min · EXAMPLE (we do)
> **Say:** "Match each month card to a season picture with me — and say the month."
**Do:** child places months under spring/summer/autumn/winter pictures, saying each aloud; you confirm placement.
**Ask:** "Which month is your birthday? *Mi cumpleaños es en…*" → *listen for:* a correctly named month.

### ⏱ 17–21 min · CHECK
**Ask:** "Name three months in Spanish."
- ✅ **Expected:** any three, e.g. *enero, mayo, agosto* — then probe: *"Which one is a 'cousin' of its English name?"*
- ❌ **If they say an English month with a Spanish accent (e.g. "Marchzo"):** mixing the two → **fix:** model the true Spanish (*marzo*, MAR-tho) and have them echo it twice.
- ❌ **If a month is capitalised when written:** English habit → **fix:** "Lower-case, like the days — *enero*."

### ⏱ 21–25 min · PRACTICE (you do)
**Do:** "birthday line-up" — scramble the month cards; the child orders them, calling each out, then says *Mi mes es…* for their birthday month.
**Watch for:** order slips around the trickier middle months (*junio/julio* confusion); capitalisation.
> **If the child is stuck (missed twice):** 1) don't supply the month; 2) name *why* — "what comes after *abril*?"; 3) offer the first sound; 4) one hint — "it sounds like the English…"; 5) re-ask from the month before; 6) keep it safe — "Twelve is a lot — let's lock in the first four."

### ⏱ 25–25 min · RECAP
> **Say:** "Quick — say your birthday month and one month either side of it."
**Process praise:** "You used the English 'cousins' as clues — smart way to learn twelve words fast."
**Revisit later:** months power dates and birthdays in Lesson 9 and festivals in Lesson 13.

---

**Differentiation**
- **Support (gentle):** match each month to a season picture before ordering; learn cognate months first.
- **Stretch (challenge):** say all twelve in order, unprompted, against the clock.

**Extension (if time allows):** sort the 12 months into the four seasons and label each season in Spanish (*primavera, verano, otoño, invierno*) if confident.
**Assessment evidence:** the child names ≥3 months correctly (lower-case) and identifies their own birthday month.

## Lesson 9 — La fecha & mi cumpleaños
**Spanish · Year 4 · Unit 3 (Calendar)** | **Duration:** 30 min | **Objective:** "By the end, I can give a date (day-month) and say when my birthday is."

**You will need:** number cards, month cards, a blank calendar to mark on.
**Prior knowledge to retrieve:** numbers 0–31 (Year 3); months (Lesson 8); the *el … de …* structure.
**Key vocabulary:** *el … de …* (el … de) the … of … · *¿Cuándo es tu cumpleaños?* (KWAN-do es too koom-ple-AN-yos) When's your birthday? · *Mi cumpleaños es el…* My birthday is the… · *el diez de julio* the 10th of July.
**What to emphasize (the heart of the lesson):** **the day comes BEFORE the month** — *el 4 de mayo*, never "mayo 4". Build the date as *el* + number + *de* + month. Numbers come straight back from Year 3.

---

### ⏱ 0–4 min · HOOK
> **Say:** "In Spanish the date flips! The **day comes first**, then the month. *El cuatro de mayo* — 'the four of May'. You would never say 'mayo four'. Star Wars fans know this date! Let's learn to say any date."
**Show:** *el 4 de mayo* written big, with arrows: day → *de* → month.
**Ask (retrieve prior knowledge):** "Count me from one to ten in Spanish." → *listen for:* *uno… diez.*

### ⏱ 4–11 min · EXPLAIN (I do)
> **Say:** "A date is three pieces: *el* + the **number** + *de* + the **month**. Watch me build the tenth of July: *el* … *diez* … *de* … *julio* → *el diez de julio.* And the big question: *¿Cuándo es tu cumpleaños?* (KWAN-do es too koom-ple-AN-yos) — 'When's your birthday?' Answer: *Mi cumpleaños es el doce de junio.*"
**Show / Do:** physically slot a number card and a month card into an *el ___ de ___* frame on the board.
**Emphasize:** "**Day first, then *de*, then month.** *el [number] de [month].*"

### ⏱ 11–18 min · EXAMPLE (we do)
> **Say:** "Let's build a date together. Hand me the number for 23, and the month March."
**Do:** child places *veintitrés* and *marzo* → reads *el veintitrés de marzo*; build two or three more dates this way.
**Ask:** "Now ask me my birthday in Spanish." → *listen for:* *¿Cuándo es tu cumpleaños?*

### ⏱ 18–23 min · CHECK
**Ask:** "Say 'the 10th of July.'"
- ✅ **Expected:** *el diez de julio* — then probe: *"Which comes first, the day or the month?"*
- ❌ **If "julio diez" / "el julio de diez":** English order or pieces swapped → **fix:** "Day first: *el* + the number, then *de* + the month — *el diez de julio.*" Re-slot the cards in order.
- ❌ **If they forget *de*:** dropped the joining word → **fix:** "We need *de* to glue them: *el diez **de** julio.*"

### ⏱ 23–28 min · PRACTICE (you do)
**Do:** the child answers *¿Cuándo es tu cumpleaños?* with a full *el … de …* date, then asks **you**, and then gives family members' birthdays (or speaks for toys); the child marks each on the big calendar.
**Watch for:** reversed order; missing *el* or *de*; number errors (recycle from Year 3 as needed).
> **If the child is stuck (missed twice):** 1) don't give the date; 2) name *why* — "is the day or month first?"; 3) hand them the *el ___ de ___* frame; 4) one starter: "*el…*"; 5) re-ask with just a number + month, then add the frame; 6) keep it safe — "The flip is the tricky bit — let's do one slow date."

### ⏱ 28–30 min · RECAP
> **Say:** "Say your own birthday in Spanish — full sentence."
**Process praise:** "You kept the day-first order and glued it with *de* every time — that's the Spanish way."
**Revisit later:** **Unit-3 check**; dates resurface in Lesson 13 (festival dates).

---

**Differentiation**
- **Support (gentle):** fill the *el ___ de ___* frame with cards; pre-give the month, child supplies only the number.
- **Stretch (challenge):** give a family member's birthday (*Su cumpleaños es el…*) and a festival date (*el seis de enero*).

**Extension (if time allows):** make a family birthday calendar, writing each date correctly in the day-de-month order.
**Assessment evidence (Unit 3 check):** the child says the days in order, three months, and answers *¿Cuándo es tu cumpleaños?* with a correct *el [number] de [month]* date.

## Unit 4 — El tiempo: weather

## Lesson 10 — ¿Qué tiempo hace? (hace…)
**Spanish · Year 4 · Unit 4 (Weather)** | **Duration:** 25 min | **Objective:** "By the end, I can say sunny/hot/cold/windy weather with *hace…*"

**You will need:** weather-icon flashcards (sun, hot, cold, wind), a pretend weather map.
**Prior knowledge to retrieve:** the greeting routine; the idea of fixed phrase "chunks".
**Key vocabulary:** *¿Qué tiempo hace?* (keh tee-EM-po AH-the) What's the weather like? · *Hace sol* (AH-the sol) it's sunny · *Hace calor* (ka-LOR) it's hot · *Hace frío* (FREE-oh) it's cold · *Hace viento* (vee-EN-to) it's windy · *Hace buen/mal tiempo* the weather's good/bad.
**What to emphasize (the heart of the lesson):** Spanish says the weather **"makes"** — *hace sol* = "it makes sun". Sun, heat, cold and wind all use ***hace***. Learn each as a whole chunk. *frío* has its accent on the *í* — FREE-oh.

---

### ⏱ 0–3 min · HOOK
> **Say:** "Funny one: Spanish doesn't say 'it **is** sunny' — it says the weather **makes** sun! *Hace sol.* Be the forecaster — *¿Qué tiempo hace?* — what's the weather like today?"
**Show:** four weather-icon cards and a map.
**Ask (retrieve prior knowledge):** "How do we greet and ask how someone is in Spanish?" → *listen for:* *Hola, ¿cómo estás?* (quick warm-up).

### ⏱ 3–9 min · EXPLAIN (I do)
> **Say:** "Listen and repeat — every one starts with *Hace* (AH-the). *Hace sol* — sunny. *Hace calor* (ka-LOR) — hot. *Hace frío* (FREE-oh) — cold. *Hace viento* (vee-EN-to) — windy. And to judge the day: *Hace buen tiempo* (good) / *Hace mal tiempo* (bad)."
**Show / Do:** hold up each icon as you say its phrase; child echoes; write *Hace ___* on the board.
**Emphasize:** "Sun, heat, cold, wind all use ***hace*** — 'the weather makes it'. And *frío* = FREE-oh, push on the *í*."

### ⏱ 9–14 min · EXAMPLE (we do)
> **Say:** "I'll flash an icon; you give me the *Hace…* phrase."
**Do:** flash sun → child *Hace sol*; flash cold → child *Hace frío*; mix four icons.
**Ask:** "Now ask me the weather question." → *listen for:* *¿Qué tiempo hace?*

### ⏱ 14–18 min · CHECK
**Ask:** "Say 'it's sunny.'"
- ✅ **Expected:** *Hace sol* — then probe: *"Now 'it's cold' — what changes?"*
- ❌ **If "es sol" / "está sol":** wrong verb chosen → **fix:** "Sun uses *hace* — the weather **makes** sun: *Hace sol.*" Tap the *Hace ___* board.
- ❌ **If "hace caliente" for hot:** literal translation → **fix:** "Weather-hot is *hace calor*, a fixed chunk — not *caliente*."

### ⏱ 18–22 min · PRACTICE (you do)
**Do:** the child is a **TV weather presenter** pointing at the map; you flash icons; they announce each with the right *Hace…* phrase, then add a judgement (*Hace buen tiempo*).
**Watch for:** wrong verb (*es/está* sneaking in); *calor* vs *caliente*; flat *frío*.
> **If the child is stuck (missed twice):** 1) don't say the phrase; 2) name *why* — "all four use the same first word — which?"; 3) point to *Hace ___*; 4) one hint — give the icon's English; 5) re-ask just that phrase, then string two; 6) keep it safe — "Forecasters practise lots — let's do one icon."

### ⏱ 22–25 min · RECAP
> **Say:** "Tell me the four weather phrases that all start with *Hace*."
**Process praise:** "You remembered weather 'makes' it — *hace* every time — and your forecaster voice was great."
**Revisit later:** open lessons with *¿Qué tiempo hace?*; Lesson 11 adds the *está…* weather.

---

**Differentiation**
- **Support (gentle):** match an icon to its phrase card before saying it; start with two phrases (*sol, frío*).
- **Stretch (challenge):** add *Hace buen/mal tiempo* and judge today's real weather.

**Extension (if time allows):** give a four-icon "forecast" for the week (Monday *hace sol*, Tuesday *hace viento*…), recycling days.
**Assessment evidence:** the child answers *¿Qué tiempo hace?* with ≥3 correct *Hace…* phrases.

## Lesson 11 — Está lloviendo (está…) and joining with *y*
**Spanish · Year 4 · Unit 4 (Weather)** | **Duration:** 30 min | **Objective:** "By the end, I can use *está…* for rain/snow/cloud and join two weather facts with *y*."

**You will need:** a *hace / está* sorting mat (two columns); weather icons (sun, hot, cold, wind, rain, snow, cloud).
**Prior knowledge to retrieve:** the *Hace…* phrases (Lesson 10); *y* = "and" (Year 3).
**Key vocabulary:** *Está lloviendo* (es-TA yo-vee-EN-do) it's raining · *Está nevando* (neh-VAN-do) it's snowing · *Está nublado* (noo-BLA-do) it's cloudy · *y* (ee) and.
**What to emphasize (the heart of the lesson):** **rain, snow and cloud break the *hace* rule — they use *está***. Sort the world into *hace* (sun/heat/cold/wind) vs *está* (rain/snow/cloud). Join two facts with *y* to make a real forecast.

---

### ⏱ 0–4 min · HOOK
> **Say:** "Yesterday everything used *hace*. But three sneaky ones break the rule! Rain, snow and cloud use *está*, not *hace*. *Está lloviendo* — it's raining. Let's learn the rule-breakers."
**Show:** rain/snow/cloud icons placed apart from the sunny ones.
**Ask (retrieve prior knowledge):** "Give me two *Hace…* weather phrases." → *listen for:* e.g. *Hace sol, hace frío.*

### ⏱ 4–11 min · EXPLAIN (I do)
> **Say:** "Listen and repeat the three rule-breakers: *Está lloviendo* (es-TA yo-vee-EN-do) — raining. *Está nevando* (neh-VAN-do) — snowing. *Está nublado* (noo-BLA-do) — cloudy. So we have two families: *hace* for sun, heat, cold, wind; *está* for rain, snow, cloud. And to join two facts, use *y* (ee) — 'and': *Hace frío **y** está lloviendo.*"
**Show / Do:** sort each icon onto the *hace* or *está* column of the mat as you name it.
**Emphasize:** "**Rain, snow, cloud → *está*.** Everything else → *hace*. Join with *y*."

### ⏱ 11–18 min · EXAMPLE (we do)
> **Say:** "Let's sort together — which column for snow?"
**Do:** child places each remaining icon in the right column, saying the phrase; you confirm.
**Ask:** "Now make a two-part forecast: cold AND raining." → *listen for:* *Hace frío y está lloviendo.*

### ⏱ 18–23 min · CHECK
**Ask:** "Say 'it's raining.'"
- ✅ **Expected:** *Está lloviendo* — then probe: *"Why *está* and not *hace*?"*
- ❌ **If "Hace lloviendo":** wrong family → **fix:** "Rain is a rule-breaker — it uses *está*: *Está lloviendo.*" Move the rain icon to the *está* column.
- ❌ **If "Está sol":** over-applied *está* to the sun → **fix:** "Sun stays with *hace* — *Hace sol*. Only rain, snow, cloud take *está*."

### ⏱ 23–28 min · PRACTICE (you do)
**Do:** "forecast for a faraway place" — the child invents the weather on a made-up island and gives a **two-part** forecast joined with *y*, choosing *hace*/*está* correctly. Run two or three forecasts.
**Watch for:** wrong verb family; missing *y*; mixing the two verbs in one phrase.
> **If the child is stuck (missed twice):** 1) don't supply it; 2) name *why* — "is this a *hace* one or an *está* one?"; 3) point to the sorted mat; 4) one hint — "rain, snow, cloud use…?"; 5) re-ask one phrase, then join two with *y*; 6) keep it safe — "Two families to remember — let's sort one icon."

### ⏱ 28–30 min · RECAP
> **Say:** "Which three weathers break the rule and use *está*?"
**Process praise:** "You sorted every icon into the right verb family and joined two facts with *y* — that's a proper forecast."
**Revisit later:** **Unit-4 check**; weather features in the Lesson 14 personal profile (*Hoy hace sol*).

---

**Differentiation**
- **Support (gentle):** pick the right verb from two cards (*hace*/*está*) before producing the phrase; one-part forecast first.
- **Stretch (challenge):** give a three-part forecast with *y* (*Hace frío, hace viento y está nevando*).

**Extension (if time allows):** present a week's forecast (a day + its two-part weather for several days), recycling days and *y*.
**Assessment evidence (Unit 4 check):** the child answers *¿Qué tiempo hace?* with a two-part forecast, choosing *hace* and *está* correctly and joining with *y*.

## Unit 5 — En clase en español: survival language & review

---

## Lesson 12 — Survival phrases (staying in Spanish)
**Spanish · Year 4 · Unit 5 (Survival & review)** | **Duration:** 25 min | **Objective:** "By the end, I can ask for help in Spanish instead of switching to English."

**You will need:** situation cards (English "stuck" moments), phrase posters around the learning space.
**Prior knowledge to retrieve:** the idea that good learners stay in the language; *gracias* (Year 3).
**Key vocabulary:** *No entiendo* (no en-tee-EN-do) I don't understand · *¿Cómo se dice…?* (KOH-mo se DEE-the) How do you say…? · *¿Puedes repetir, por favor?* (PWEH-des) Can you repeat, please? · *Otra vez, por favor* (O-tra veth) Again, please · *No sé* (no seh) I don't know.
**What to emphasize (the heart of the lesson):** these are **fixed chunks** — learn and reuse them whole, don't translate word by word. Using them keeps the whole lesson **in Spanish** even when you're stuck, which is exactly what good language learners do.

---

### ⏱ 0–3 min · HOOK
> **Say:** "Here's a secret of brilliant language learners: when they get stuck, they don't switch to English — they ask for help **in Spanish**! Today you get the magic phrases to do that."
**Show:** phrase posters on the wall.
**Ask (retrieve prior knowledge):** "What do you usually do when you don't understand a word?" → *listen for:* "switch to English / ask" — pivot to "let's ask in Spanish instead."

### ⏱ 3–10 min · EXPLAIN (I do)
> **Say:** "Learn these as whole chunks — don't pull them apart. *No entiendo* (no en-tee-EN-do) — I don't understand. *¿Cómo se dice…?* (KOH-mo se DEE-the) — How do you say…? *¿Puedes repetir, por favor?* — Can you repeat, please? *Otra vez, por favor* — Again, please. *No sé* — I don't know."
**Show / Do:** point to each poster as you say it; child echoes each chunk twice.
**Emphasize:** "Whole chunks — say them like one word. These keep you **in Spanish** when it's hard."

### ⏱ 10–15 min · EXAMPLE (we do)
> **Say:** "I'll describe a stuck moment in English; you fire back the right Spanish chunk. 'You didn't hear what I said…'"
**Do:** child responds *¿Puedes repetir, por favor?*; give another ("You don't know the word for 'fish'") → child *¿Cómo se dice 'fish'?*
**Ask:** "And if you simply don't understand at all?" → *listen for:* *No entiendo.*

### ⏱ 15–19 min · CHECK
**Ask:** "Say 'I don't understand.'"
- ✅ **Expected:** *No entiendo* — then probe: *"And how would you ask me to say it again?"*
- ❌ **If they translate word-by-word and tangle it:** treating it as separate words → **fix:** "It's one ready-made chunk — *No entiendo* — say it as a single piece." Model, child echoes.
- ❌ **If they switch to English to ask:** old habit → **fix:** "Try the Spanish chunk instead — *¿Puedes repetir?*" Praise the attempt over accuracy.

### ⏱ 19–23 min · PRACTICE (you do)
**Do:** "stuck challenge" — run 5–6 situation cards; the child earns a point for each correct Spanish chunk **and** for staying in Spanish. Include one where they must actually learn a word: *¿Cómo se dice 'fish'?* → you answer *pez* → child says *¡Gracias!*
**Watch for:** reverting to English; word-by-word translation; mispronounced *¿Cómo se dice?* (KOH-mo se DEE-the).
> **If the child is stuck (missed twice):** 1) don't say the chunk; 2) name *why* — "which phrase fits this situation?"; 3) point to the matching poster; 4) one starter — first word only; 5) re-ask with a simpler situation; 6) keep it safe — "Even saying half in Spanish wins a point — try it."

### ⏱ 23–25 min · RECAP
> **Say:** "Which phrase will you use next time you're stuck?"
**Process praise:** "You stayed in Spanish even when it was tricky — that's exactly what strong learners do."
**Revisit later:** require at least one survival phrase per lesson from now on; reused in the Unit-5 check and Lesson 14.

---

**Differentiation**
- **Support (gentle):** match each situation card to its phrase poster before saying it; start with two chunks.
- **Stretch (challenge):** use a survival phrase to genuinely learn a new word, then use that word in a sentence.

**Extension (if time allows):** invent two more "stuck" situations and the phrase that solves each.
**Assessment evidence:** the child produces ≥4 survival chunks correctly and uses at least one spontaneously when stuck.

## Lesson 13 — Las fiestas: a Spanish festival (culture)
**Spanish · Year 4 · Unit 5 (Survival & review)** | **Duration:** 30 min | **Objective:** "By the end, I can name a Spanish-speaking festival and say its date in Spanish."

**You will need:** festival pictures (Navidad, los Reyes Magos, La Tomatina, optionally el Día de los Muertos), a calendar, craft materials (crown template or collage scraps).
**Prior knowledge to retrieve:** dates in day-de-month order (Lesson 9); months (Lesson 8).
**Key vocabulary:** *las fiestas* (FYES-tas) festivals · *Navidad* (na-vee-DAD) Christmas · *los Reyes Magos* (RAY-es MA-gos) the Three Kings · *el seis de enero* the 6th of January · *La Tomatina* the tomato fight · *el Día de los Muertos* the Day of the Dead.
**What to emphasize (the heart of the lesson):** language carries **culture**. On *el 6 de enero*, Spanish children often get presents from *los Reyes Magos*. Saying a festival's date **recycles your months and the day-de-month order**. (If covering *Día de los Muertos*: it is **joyful and respectful, remembering loved ones — not scary**.)

---

### ⏱ 0–4 min · HOOK
> **Say:** "Imagine waking up to presents — not on Christmas Day, but on the 6th of January! In Spain, children often get gifts from *los Reyes Magos*, the Three Kings, on *el seis de enero*. Let's explore Spanish festivals."
**Show:** the Reyes Magos picture beside a calendar pointing at 6 January.
**Ask (retrieve prior knowledge):** "How do we say a date — day first or month first?" → *listen for:* "day first" (*el [number] de [month]*).

### ⏱ 4–12 min · EXPLAIN (I do)
> **Say:** "*Las fiestas* — festivals. *Navidad* (na-vee-DAD) is Christmas. On *el seis de enero* come *los Reyes Magos* (RAY-es MA-gos) — the Three Kings — when many Spanish children get their presents. In August in the town of Buñol there's *La Tomatina* — a giant, friendly **tomato fight**! And in Mexico, in early November, *el Día de los Muertos* — the Day of the Dead — a **happy, respectful** festival remembering loved ones with flowers, music and sugar skulls. It looks spooky but it's joyful, not scary."
**Show / Do:** point to each festival picture; locate Spain and Mexico on a map.
**Emphasize:** "Each festival has a **date** — and we say it in Spanish, day first: *el seis de enero.*"

### ⏱ 12–18 min · EXAMPLE (we do)
> **Say:** "Let's match festival to date together. The Three Kings — which date?"
**Do:** child says *el seis de enero*; match *La Tomatina* → August, *Día de los Muertos* → early November.
**Ask:** "Say the Reyes Magos date fully in Spanish." → *listen for:* *el seis de enero.*

### ⏱ 18–22 min · CHECK
**Ask:** "What happens on *el 6 de enero* in Spain?"
- ✅ **Expected:** *Los Reyes Magos* — children often get presents — then probe: *"Say that date in Spanish."* (*el seis de enero*).
- ❌ **If "Día de los Muertos is like Halloween / scary":** misreading the skulls → **fix:** "It's a **joyful, respectful** festival remembering loved ones — a celebration, not a fright."
- ❌ **If they say the date month-first ("enero seis"):** English order → **fix:** "Day first: *el seis de enero.*"

### ⏱ 22–28 min · PRACTICE (you do)
**Do:** festival fact-share **plus** a craft — the child makes a Three Kings crown (or a tomato-fight collage), then names a festival, says its date in Spanish, and tells one fact in English.
**Watch for:** month-first dates; the "scary" misconception; mispronounced *Reyes* (RAY-es).
> **If the child is stuck (missed twice):** 1) don't supply it; 2) name *why* — "which festival, and what's its date?"; 3) point to the picture + calendar; 4) one starter: "*el…*"; 5) re-ask just the date, then add the festival name; 6) keep it safe — "Lots of new facts — let's pick one festival you liked."

### ⏱ 28–30 min · RECAP
> **Say:** "Name one Spanish festival and say its date in Spanish."
**Process praise:** "You connected a real celebration to its Spanish date — language and culture together."
**Revisit later:** dates and festivals can feature in the Lesson 14 profile; revisit *Navidad* near December.

---

**Differentiation**
- **Support (gentle):** match each festival to its picture and a season before giving dates; date with the frame in front.
- **Stretch (challenge):** say two festivals with full Spanish dates and name a country other than Spain where Spanish is spoken (e.g. Mexico).

**Extension (if time allows):** make a mini festival fact-card with the festival, its date in Spanish, and a drawing.
**Assessment evidence:** the child names a Spanish-speaking festival and gives its date in correct day-de-month Spanish.

## Lesson 14 — Year-4 review: all about me and mine
**Spanish · Year 4 · Unit 5 (Survival & review)** | **Duration:** 35 min | **Objective:** "By the end, I can give a short spoken profile: family, a pet, my birthday and the weather."

**You will need:** prompt cards (one per sentence type), the child's photos/drawings, optional device to film a "Spanish vlog".
**Prior knowledge to retrieve:** **everything** — family + *mi/mis* + *Este es…* (Unit 1); *Tengo un… (negro/a)* (Unit 2); days/months/dates (Unit 3); weather *hace/está… y* (Unit 4); survival phrases (Lesson 12).
**Key vocabulary (recombined):** *Este es mi hermano. Se llama… Tiene … años. · Tengo un gato negro. · Mi cumpleaños es el … de … · Hoy hace sol / está lloviendo.*
**What to emphasize (the heart of the lesson):** **recombine the year into 4–5 connected sentences** about *me and mine*. This isn't new grammar — it's putting the pieces together fluently, using *y* to join and a survival phrase if stuck.

---

### ⏱ 0–4 min · HOOK
> **Say:** "Today you give me a 'this is my world' mini-presentation — all in Spanish! Family, your pet, your birthday, the weather. Watch mine: *Esta es mi madre. Se llama Ana. Tengo un perro negro. Mi cumpleaños es el diez de mayo. Hoy hace sol.* Now we build yours."
**Show:** five prompt cards laid in order (family / pet / birthday / weather / + a survival phrase).
**Ask (retrieve prior knowledge):** "Quick — how do you introduce a family member?" → *listen for:* *Este/Esta es mi… Se llama… Tiene … años.*

### ⏱ 4–11 min · EXPLAIN (I do)
> **Say:** "Five pieces, in order. One — family: *Este es mi hermano. Se llama Sam. Tiene ocho años.* Two — pet, with an agreeing colour: *Tengo un gato negro.* Three — birthday: *Mi cumpleaños es el doce de junio.* Four — weather today: *Hoy hace sol y está nublado.* Five — if you get stuck, you say *No entiendo* or *¿Cómo se dice…?* — and carry on in Spanish."
**Show / Do:** touch each prompt card as you model its sentence.
**Emphasize:** "It's all things you already know — just **connect** them, and use *y* to join."

### ⏱ 11–19 min · EXAMPLE (we do)
> **Say:** "Let's build yours together, one card at a time. Card one — introduce a family member."
**Do:** child produces each sentence with you prompting the next card; jot reminders, don't supply words.
**Ask:** "Now your pet, with its colour agreeing." → *listen for:* e.g. *Tengo una tortuga blanca* (correct agreement).

### ⏱ 19–24 min · CHECK
**Ask:** "Tell me one family member, your pet, your birthday and today's weather."
- ✅ **Expected:** four correct sentences (e.g. *Este es mi padre… Tengo un perro negro… Mi cumpleaños es el… Hoy hace frío.*) — then probe: *"Which part felt easiest, which trickiest?"*
- ❌ **If colour doesn't agree ("una vaca negro"):** Unit-2 slip → **fix:** "Is it an *un* or *una* animal? Match the colour — *vaca* → negr**a**."
- ❌ **If the date is month-first:** Unit-3 slip → **fix:** "Day first — *el [number] de [month].*"

### ⏱ 24–32 min · PRACTICE (you do)
**Do:** the child delivers the **full 4–5 sentence profile** start to finish (optionally filmed as a "Spanish vlog" or performed for the family). Then deliver it once more, smoother, with one *y* join and one survival phrase if a word slips.
**Watch for:** agreement, date order, *Este/Esta*, *tener* for age, *hace/está* choice; reverting to English (cue a survival phrase).
> **If the child is stuck (missed twice):** 1) don't supply the sentence; 2) name *why* — point to which prompt card; 3) drop back to one sentence in isolation; 4) one starter for that card; 5) rebuild the sequence one card at a time; 6) keep it safe — "Five sentences is a lot — let's nail one card, then add the next. Or say *No entiendo* and keep going in Spanish!"

### ⏱ 32–35 min · RECAP
> **Say:** "Give me your favourite sentence from your profile, with your best pronunciation."
**Process praise:** "You connected a whole year of Spanish into one smooth presentation — and stayed in Spanish when it got hard. That's a real speaker."
**Revisit later:** this profile is the spine for Year-5 work; revisit as a warm-up, swapping in new vocabulary.

---

**Differentiation**
- **Support (gentle):** one prompt card per sentence kept in view; deliver three sentences before stretching to five.
- **Stretch (challenge):** add an agreeing colour to the pet, join two weather facts with *y*, and use a survival phrase unprompted.

**Extension (if time allows):** add a sentence with an opinion (*Me gusta mi perro*) or a second family member, linking with *y*.
**Assessment evidence (Unit 5 check):** the child uses at least two survival phrases when prompted and gives a four-sentence personal profile spanning family, pet, birthday and weather, with correct agreement and date order.

## End-of-year "I can…" statements (Year 4 Spanish)

By the end of Year 4, I can…
1. **Introduce** my family with *mi/mis*, names and ages (*Este es mi… Se llama… Tiene … años*).
2. **Say** which pets I have, ask someone (*¿Tienes un animal?*), and answer.
3. **Describe** an animal's colour with correct agreement (*un gato negro / una vaca blanca*).
4. **Say** the days of the week (Monday-first, lower-case) and the months of the year.
5. **Give** a date in day-de-month order and say when my birthday is.
6. **Describe** the weather using *hace…* and *está…* and join two facts with *y*.
7. **Use** survival phrases (*No entiendo, ¿Cómo se dice…?, ¿Puedes repetir?*) to stay in Spanish.
8. **Name** a Spanish festival, say its date, and give a short spoken profile about myself.

---

### Closing note for the tutor

Year 4 is where single words become **real sentences**. Keep the lessons **speaking-heavy** — listen and repeat *by ear first*, then practise, then produce; new spelling comes after the sound. Praise **communication before accuracy**, then gently model the correct form. **Recycle relentlessly:** numbers power ages and dates, colours now *agree* with the animals they describe, and survival phrases let more and more of each lesson run in Spanish. The whole year converges on Lesson 14's "all about me and mine" profile — return to it often as a warm-up, swapping in fresh vocabulary, so the child leaves Year 4 able to talk about *their world* in Spanish.
