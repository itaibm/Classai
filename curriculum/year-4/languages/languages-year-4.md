# Year 4 Languages — Spanish (ages 9–10) — Scope & Sequence

> Part of **the best international primary school** (see [`../../../SCHOOL-CHARTER.md`](../../../SCHOOL-CHARTER.md)).
> Written to [`../../PLANNING-BRIEF.md`](../../PLANNING-BRIEF.md). Standards reference:
> `knowledge-base/subjects/languages/spanish-ks2.md` and `spanish-extension.md`.
> Codes: **UK NC KS2 Languages** (listening, speaking, reading, writing, grammar, culture) and **ACTFL** (US World-Readiness Standards; target proficiency Novice Mid → Novice High).

## Year overview

Year 4 Spanish is the year children move from **single sentences to connected speech**. They give opinions *with reasons* (*porque*), join ideas with connectives (*y, pero, también, o, sin embargo*), and talk about real topics that matter to them — food, hobbies and sport, the body and health, time and their school day, and their town. Every unit follows the same rhythm: **hear it → say it → play with it → read it → write it**, with songs, games and role-plays so children speak far more than they write. Instructions stay in English where needed; Spanish is used for routines (greetings, the date, the weather, classroom requests) every lesson.

**Culture every term:** Mexico and *el Día de los Muertos* (timed for early November), sport and music from across the Spanish-speaking world, and a final unit on stories and songs (including the Spanish edition of Eric Carle's *La oruga muy hambrienta*). Children learn that Spanish is spoken by around 500 million people as a first language, and it is an official language in about 20 countries, with regional differences (*zumo/jugo, patata/papa, coche/carro*) that are all correct.

**What Year 4 assumes (from Year 3):** greetings and introductions; numbers to 31; colours; family (*mi/mis*); pets with *tengo*; days, months and the date; weather (*hace/está*); survival classroom phrases; simple noun–adjective agreement for colours.
**What Year 5 relies on from Year 4:** confident opinions with reasons and connectives; *me gusta/me gustan*; the *yo* form of common verbs as chunks (*juego, toco, como, voy*); telling the time; places and directions; reading short paragraphs. Year 5 then teaches full present-tense verb patterns, adjective agreement, the past (*pretérito*) and near future (*voy a…*) and paragraph-length storytelling.

**Swapping the language.** This plan can be taught in another language with the same unit structure, topics and lesson rhythm — for example **French** (see `knowledge-base/subjects/french/`) or **Hebrew** (see `knowledge-base/subjects/hebrew/`). Replace the target-language examples and the culture lessons (e.g. *la Chandeleur* or Hanukkah in place of *Día de los Muertos*), keeping the grammar progression (opinions with reasons, connectives, plurals, time, directions).

**Pronunciation key.** New words are taught by ear first, spelling second. Key sound–spelling links: **j** and **g** before e/i = a throaty "h"; **h** is silent; **ll** usually sounds like "y"; **ñ** = "ny"; **z** and **c** before e/i = "th" in much of Spain and "s" in Latin America (both correct); **r** is tapped, **rr** is rolled; **b** and **v** sound the same. Stress: words ending in a vowel, *n* or *s* are stressed on the second-to-last syllable; others on the last; an accent mark shows an exception (*plátano, miércoles*).

## Unit 1 — ¡Hola otra vez! Retrieval, numbers to 100 and phonics

**Essential question:** How much Spanish do I already know — and how do Spanish letters sound?

**Key vocabulary:** hola, buenos días, ¿cómo estás?, me llamo, tengo … años, treinta, cuarenta, cincuenta, sesenta, setenta, ochenta, noventa, cien, la jota, la eñe, el acento

### Lesson 1 — ¡Hola otra vez! Greetings and introductions
- **Duration:** 30 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can greet people and introduce myself in three or more Spanish sentences." (UK NC KS2 languages — speaking; ACTFL Interpersonal Novice Mid)
- **Hook:** A mystery Spanish speaker (video or visiting speaker) introduces themselves: "How much can you understand?"
- **Key activity:** Retrieve greetings, name, age, where you live (*vivo en…*), feelings (*estoy bien/mal/cansado*); speed-meeting in pairs using three sentences.
- **Check for understanding:** "What is the difference between *¿Cómo estás?* and *¿Cómo te llamas?*" → The first asks how you are; the second asks your name.
- **Differentiation:** support: sentence builder mat with pictures. / stretch: add a question back to the partner and a connective (*y*, *pero*).
- **Materials:** sentence builder mats, name badges, video clip of a native speaker.
- **Joy:** "Speed-meeting" with a bell every 60 seconds.

### Lesson 2 — Numbers 31–100
- **Duration:** 30 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can say, recognise and write numbers from 31 to 100." (UK NC KS2 languages — listening and speaking)
- **Hook:** Bingo card with numbers 31–100: "¿Qué número es?"
- **Key activity:** Tens (*treinta, cuarenta … noventa, cien*); from 31 numbers are three words (*treinta y uno*), unlike 16–29 which are one word (*dieciséis, veintiuno*); counting chants in tens; number bingo.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why is 25 written as one word (*veinticinco*) but 35 as three (*treinta y cinco*)?" → Numbers from 16 to 29 are joined into one word; from 31 onwards they are written as three words with *y*.
- **Differentiation:** support: tens only, then add ones. / stretch: say prices (e.g. *setenta y cinco céntimos*) and phone numbers in pairs.
- **Materials:** number cards, bingo cards, mini-whiteboards.
- **Joy:** Spanish number bingo.

### Lesson 3 — Phonics: j, g, h and ll
- **Duration:** 30 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can pronounce j, g (before e/i), silent h and ll correctly in new words." (UK NC KS2 languages — phonics)
- **Hook:** Tongue-twister: *"Juan juega en el jardín"*.
- **Key activity:** Sound–action for each letter pattern; read unfamiliar words aloud (*jirafa, gente, helado, llave*); sort words by sound.
- **Check for understanding:** "How would you say *gigante*, and why?" → "hee-GAN-teh": the first g is before i (throaty h sound), the second g is before a (hard g).
- **Differentiation:** support: picture-word cards with sound buttons. / stretch: read a short unseen poem aloud accurately.
- **Materials:** phonics cards, audio recordings.
- **Joy:** Tongue-twister challenge.

### Lesson 4 — Phonics: ñ, r and rr, z and c
- **Duration:** 30 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can pronounce ñ, r/rr and z/c accurately and know that 'th' and 's' are both correct for z." (UK NC KS2 languages — phonics)
- **Hook:** "*Perro* or *pero*? One is a dog and one means 'but'!"
- **Key activity:** Practise tapped r vs rolled rr (*pero/perro, caro/carro*); ñ (*España, niño*); z/c before e/i as "th" (Spain) or "s" (Latin America) with audio of both.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why is *pero* different from *perro*?" → One r is tapped (*pero* = but); rr is rolled (*perro* = dog) — the sound changes the meaning.
- **Differentiation:** support: minimal pairs with pictures. / stretch: read words aloud in both Spain and Latin American accents.
- **Materials:** minimal-pair cards, audio clips.
- **Joy:** "Rolling r" competition.

### Lesson 5 — Stress and accents
- **Duration:** 30 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can use the stress rules and accent marks to pronounce new words." (UK NC KS2 languages — phonics)
- **Hook:** "*Papa* (potato in Latin America) or *papá* (dad)? The accent changes everything!"
- **Key activity:** Stress rules (vowel, n or s ending → stress the second-to-last syllable; others → last syllable; accent mark = exception); clap syllables; read *plátano, miércoles, jamón, lápiz*.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why does *jamón* need an accent?" → It ends in n, so it would be stressed on 'ja'; the accent shows it's actually stressed on 'món'.
- **Differentiation:** support: clap-and-say with teacher. / stretch: predict the stress of five unseen words and check with audio.
- **Materials:** word cards, clapping cards, audio.
- **Joy:** "Stress conductor" — children conduct the class with a baton on the stressed syllable.

### Lesson 6 — Unit 1 review: all about me
- **Duration:** 30 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can give a short spoken introduction and read a short text with good pronunciation." (UK NC KS2 languages — assessment)
- **Hook:** "Record a 30-second video message for a Spanish-speaking pen pal class."
- **Key activity:** Practise and record introductions; read aloud a short paragraph; peer feedback on pronunciation.
- **Check for understanding:** "Read this sentence aloud: *Me llamo Juan y tengo nueve años.*" → Accurate j, ll, ñ and stress.
- **Differentiation:** support: sentence builder. / stretch: add opinions or a question.
- **Materials:** tablets for recording, sentence builders.
- **Joy:** Video messages (shared only with parent consent).

**End-of-unit check:** Give a 4-sentence introduction including age and where you live; say six numbers between 31 and 100; read aloud five unseen words with correct pronunciation.

## Unit 2 — La comida y las bebidas: food, opinions and the café

**Essential question:** How can I say what I like to eat — and why — and order food in a Spanish café?

**Key vocabulary:** el pan, el queso, el jamón, el pollo, el pescado, la fruta, la manzana, el plátano, la naranja, las fresas, los tomates, las patatas/las papas, el agua, la leche, el zumo/el jugo, el chocolate, el helado; me gusta, me gustan, me encanta, no me gusta, odio; porque es / son; delicioso, rico, sano, dulce, asqueroso; quiero, ¿cuánto es?

### Lesson 7 — Food vocabulary
- **Duration:** 30 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can name 12 foods and drinks with the right article (el/la/los/las)." (UK NC KS2 languages — vocabulary)
- **Hook:** A shopping bag of real (or plastic) food: "¿Qué es?"
- **Key activity:** Present by ear with real items; articles show gender and number; flashcard games (kim's game, what's missing?).
- **Check for understanding:** "Why is it *la manzana* but *el plátano*?" → Nouns have gender; many ending in -a are feminine (la), many ending in -o are masculine (el) — learn each with its article.
- **Differentiation:** support: 8 words with pictures. / stretch: 16 words including regional variants (*zumo/jugo*).
- **Materials:** real or plastic food, flashcards.
- **Joy:** "What's missing?" memory game.

### Lesson 8 — Me gusta / no me gusta
- **Duration:** 30 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can say which foods I like and dislike." (UK NC KS2 languages — opinions)
- **Hook:** Thumbs up/down vote on real foods.
- **Key activity:** *Me gusta el chocolate / No me gusta el queso*; survey classmates; tally results.
- **Check for understanding:** "How do you say you don't like fish?" → *No me gusta el pescado.*
- **Differentiation:** support: sentence builder. / stretch: add *me encanta* and *odio* for strength.
- **Materials:** survey sheets, food flashcards.
- **Joy:** Class survey with a bar chart (links to maths).

### Lesson 9 — Me gusta vs me gustan
- **Duration:** 30 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can use me gustan for plural foods and explain why." (UK NC KS2 languages — grammar)
- **Hook:** "*Me gusta la fresa* or *me gustan las fresas*? What's the difference?"
- **Key activity:** *Gusta* for one thing, *gustan* for more than one; sort cards; build sentences.
- **Check for understanding:** "Complete: *Me ___ los tomates.*" → *gustan*, because *los tomates* is plural.
- **Differentiation:** support: colour-coded singular/plural cards. / stretch: explain *me gusta* + plural as a mistake in a partner's work.
- **Materials:** singular/plural sort cards.
- **Joy:** "Gusta or gustan?" jump-left/jump-right game.

### Lesson 10 — Opinions with reasons: porque
- **Duration:** 30 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can give a reason for my opinion using porque and an adjective." (UK NC KS2 languages — opinions with reasons)
- **Hook:** "*Me gusta el helado porque es delicioso.* How is this better than just *me gusta*?"
- **Key activity:** *Porque es…* (one thing) / *porque son…* (plural); adjectives *delicioso, rico, sano, dulce, asqueroso*; notice adjectives can change ending (*es deliciosa* for feminine nouns) as a chunk.
- **Check for understanding:** "Give an opinion and a reason about apples." → e.g. *Me gustan las manzanas porque son sanas.*
- **Differentiation:** support: a two-column builder. / stretch: agree adjective endings correctly (*la pizza es deliciosa*).
- **Materials:** sentence builders, adjective cards.
- **Joy:** "Food critic" — rate foods with reasons.

### Lesson 11 — Connectives: y, pero, también
- **Duration:** 30 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can join two opinions using y, pero and también." (UK NC KS2 languages — connected sentences)
- **Hook:** Two short sentences vs one long one: "Which sounds more natural?"
- **Key activity:** Join: *Me gusta el pollo y también me gusta el pescado, pero no me gustan las patatas.* Sentence-building chains.
- **Check for understanding:** "Join these: *Me gusta el pan. No me gusta el queso.*" → *Me gusta el pan pero no me gusta el queso.*
- **Differentiation:** support: two-connective builder. / stretch: use *sin embargo* (however) and *también*.
- **Materials:** connective cards.
- **Joy:** "Sentence snake" — each child adds a connective and a clause.

### Lesson 12 — Reading a menu
- **Duration:** 30 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can read a café menu and understand prices." (UK NC KS2 languages — reading)
- **Hook:** A real café menu from Spain or Mexico (printed).
- **Key activity:** Scan for known words and cognates (*chocolate, tomate*); read prices in euros or pesos; answer questions.
- **Check for understanding:** "How did you work out *bocadillo de jamón* without a dictionary?" → Used known word *jamón* and the picture/context to guess a ham sandwich.
- **Differentiation:** support: picture menu. / stretch: find three regional words on menus from Spain and Mexico.
- **Materials:** printed menus, question sheets.
- **Joy:** "Menu detectives".

### Lesson 13 — En el café: ordering
- **Duration:** 35 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can order food and drink politely and ask the price." (UK NC KS2 languages — speaking)
- **Hook:** Turn the classroom into *Café Classai*.
- **Key activity:** Model dialogue: *¿Qué quieres? — Quiero un bocadillo de queso y un zumo de naranja, por favor. — ¿Algo más? — No, gracias. ¿Cuánto es? — Son cuatro euros.* Role-play with play money.
- **Check for understanding:** "How do you ask the price, and how might the waiter answer?" → *¿Cuánto es?* — *Son … euros.*
- **Differentiation:** support: dialogue cards with gaps. / stretch: improvise a problem (*No hay…* — there's no…).
- **Materials:** play money, menus, aprons, a bell.
- **Joy:** Café role-play.

### Lesson 14 — Food from the Spanish-speaking world
- **Duration:** 30 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can name and describe foods from different Spanish-speaking countries." (UK NC KS2 languages — culture)
- **Hook:** Photos: *tortilla española* (Spain), *arepas* (Venezuela and Colombia), *tacos* (Mexico), *empanadas* (Argentina and many others), *ceviche* (Peru).
- **Key activity:** Match foods to countries on a map; describe with *es de…* and opinions; discuss regional words.
- **Check for understanding:** "Where do arepas come from, and how would you give your opinion of them?" → Venezuela and Colombia; e.g. *Me gustan las arepas porque son ricas.*
- **Differentiation:** support: map with flags. / stretch: explain in English why the same food has different names in different countries.
- **Materials:** photos, world map, (allergy-checked) tasting samples if possible.
- **Joy:** Tasting (e.g. a simple tortilla or arepa), with allergies checked.

### Lesson 15 — Writing: my food opinions
- **Duration:** 30 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can write 4 connected sentences about food with opinions and reasons." (UK NC KS2 languages — writing)
- **Hook:** "Write a food review for a Spanish-speaking pen pal."
- **Key activity:** Plan with a builder; write 4 sentences using *me gusta(n)*, *porque*, and two connectives; check spelling and accents.
- **Check for understanding:** "Point to a connective and a reason in your writing." → Correctly used examples.
- **Differentiation:** support: sentence builder and word bank. / stretch: 6 sentences including *me encanta* and *sin embargo*.
- **Materials:** writing frames, word banks.
- **Joy:** Pen-pal food reviews posted.

### Lesson 16 — Listening: at the market
- **Duration:** 30 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can understand prices and quantities in a market conversation." (UK NC KS2 languages — listening)
- **Hook:** Market sounds; a recorded conversation: "What did she buy and how much did it cost?"
- **Key activity:** Listen for key details (items, *un kilo de…*, prices); note-taking grid; role-play market stalls.
- **Check for understanding:** "What did the customer buy, and how much did it cost?" → Correct items and price from the recording.
- **Differentiation:** support: picture-choice answers. / stretch: note extra details (why they bought it).
- **Materials:** recordings, grids, market props.
- **Joy:** Market-stall role-play.

### Lesson 17 — Song and retrieval: food
- **Duration:** 30 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can recall food words and phrases quickly through songs and games." (UK NC KS2 languages — retrieval)
- **Hook:** A Spanish food song (e.g. from a reputable children's Spanish music resource).
- **Key activity:** Sing, then play "Pasa la pelota" (pass the ball, say a food and opinion).
- **Check for understanding:** "Give me a sentence with a food, an opinion, a reason and a connective." → A correct connected sentence.
- **Differentiation:** support: picture prompts. / stretch: no prompts.
- **Materials:** song audio, soft ball.
- **Joy:** Singing and ball game.

### Lesson 18 — Unit 2 assessment
- **Duration:** 30 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can show my food language in listening, speaking, reading and writing." (UK NC KS2 languages — assessment)
- **Hook:** "Café inspector."
- **Key activity:** Four short tasks: listen to an order; perform a café role-play; read a menu; write 3 sentences.
- **Check for understanding:** "Order a meal and give an opinion of it." → Accurate order with an opinion and reason.
- **Differentiation:** support: picture prompts. / stretch: extended role-play.
- **Materials:** assessment tasks, props.
- **Joy:** Café inspector badge.

**End-of-unit check:** Perform a café role-play (order, ask the price, pay); write 4 sentences of food opinions with *porque* and two connectives, using *me gusta/me gustan* correctly.

## Unit 3 — México y el Día de los Muertos

**Essential question:** How do families in Mexico remember loved ones — and what can we learn from how other cultures celebrate?

**Key vocabulary:** México, la Ciudad de México, el Día de los Muertos, la ofrenda, la calavera, el pan de muerto, el cempasúchil, el papel picado, la vela, la familia, recordar

### Lesson 19 — Mexico: a country of many regions
- **Duration:** 30 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can locate Mexico and describe some of its regions, cities and features in Spanish." (UK NC KS2 languages — culture; ACTFL Cultures)
- **Hook:** Photos: Mexico City, the Yucatán beaches, Maya pyramids (links to history), deserts, volcanoes.
- **Key activity:** Map work; simple sentences *En México hay…* (There is/are…); Mexico has more Spanish speakers than any other country.
- **Check for understanding:** "Say one sentence in Spanish about Mexico using *hay*." → e.g. *En México hay volcanes.*
- **Differentiation:** support: *hay* + picture cards. / stretch: add an opinion and reason.
- **Materials:** map of Mexico, photos.
- **Joy:** Virtual "tour" slide show.

### Lesson 20 — What is el Día de los Muertos?
- **Duration:** 30 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can explain the meaning of the Day of the Dead and name its symbols in Spanish." (UK NC KS2 languages — culture)
- **Hook:** A decorated sugar skull (*calavera*) and marigolds: "Is this scary or joyful?"
- **Key activity:** Celebrated on 1 and 2 November; families remember loved ones with an *ofrenda* (altar), photos, *cempasúchil* (marigolds), candles, *pan de muerto*, *papel picado*; listed by UNESCO as Intangible Cultural Heritage. It is a joyful celebration of memory, not Halloween.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why is it wrong to call el Día de los Muertos 'Mexican Halloween'?" → It is about remembering and honouring loved ones with joy, with its own history and customs, not about scaring.
- **Differentiation:** support: symbol picture cards. / stretch: explain the roots mixing Indigenous (Aztec) traditions and Catholic All Saints' and All Souls' Days.
- **Materials:** images, a sugar skull (or picture), marigolds (or paper ones).
- **Joy:** Learn the symbols with a matching game.

### Lesson 21 — Making papel picado
- **Duration:** 35 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can follow instructions in Spanish to make papel picado." (UK NC KS2 languages — listening to instructions)
- **Hook:** Colourful paper banners across the classroom.
- **Key activity:** Instructions in Spanish (*dobla el papel, corta, abre*); make banners; describe colours (*rojo, amarillo, morado*).
- **Check for understanding:** "What does *dobla el papel* mean? Show me." → Fold the paper — child folds it.
- **Differentiation:** support: picture instruction cards. / stretch: give instructions to a partner in Spanish.
- **Materials:** tissue paper, scissors, string.
- **Joy:** Classroom decorated with *papel picado*.

### Lesson 22 — Calaveras: describing faces
- **Duration:** 30 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can describe a decorated calavera using colours and features." (UK NC KS2 languages — speaking; links to Unit 5 body words)
- **Hook:** Duncan Tonatiuh's *Funny Bones* — pictures of José Guadalupe Posada's famous calaveras.
- **Key activity:** Design a calavera; describe it: *Tiene los ojos azules y la nariz roja*; present.
- **Check for understanding:** "Describe your calavera in two sentences." → Correct *tiene* + feature + colour.
- **Differentiation:** support: sentence builder. / stretch: add opinions and connectives.
- **Materials:** calavera templates, coloured pens, *Funny Bones* (Duncan Tonatiuh).
- **Joy:** Calavera gallery.

### Lesson 23 — A class ofrenda of memories
- **Duration:** 35 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can write a short Spanish caption remembering someone or something special." (UK NC KS2 languages — writing; culture)
- **Hook:** "Who or what do you want to remember? (a grandparent, a pet, a famous person you admire)"
- **Key activity:** Create a respectful class *ofrenda* display; captions: *Recuerdo a mi abuela. Le gustaba el chocolate.* (taught as a chunk); discuss sensitively.
- **Check for understanding:** "What does *Recuerdo a…* mean, and how did you use it?" → "I remember…" — used correctly in a caption.
- **Differentiation:** support: caption frame. / stretch: add a sentence about why they are special.
- **Materials:** card, photos or drawings, paper marigolds. Sensitivity: optional for families; children can choose a famous person or pet.
- **Joy:** The class ofrenda.

### Lesson 24 — Term 1 review
- **Duration:** 30 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can use Term 1 language (introductions, numbers, food, culture) in a conversation." (UK NC KS2 languages — review)
- **Hook:** "*Pasapalabra*" — a quiz game inspired by the Spanish TV show.
- **Key activity:** Team quiz; pair conversations with prompt cards.
- **Check for understanding:** "Have a 5-exchange conversation about yourself and food." → Five correct turns.
- **Differentiation:** support: prompt cards. / stretch: no cards.
- **Materials:** quiz, prompt cards.
- **Joy:** Quiz show.

**End-of-unit check:** Explain in English three customs of *el Día de los Muertos* and name them in Spanish; describe a *calavera* in two Spanish sentences.

## Unit 4 — Los pasatiempos y los deportes: hobbies and sport

**Essential question:** How can I talk about what I do in my free time, how often, and why?

**Key vocabulary:** juego al fútbol, juego al baloncesto, nado, bailo, leo, toco la guitarra/el piano, veo la tele, escucho música, monto en bici, dibujo; me gusta + infinitivo (jugar, nadar, bailar, leer); siempre, a menudo, a veces, nunca; divertido, aburrido, emocionante, fácil, difícil

### Lesson 25 — Hobbies vocabulary
- **Duration:** 30 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can say 10 hobbies in the 'I' form." (UK NC KS2 languages — vocabulary)
- **Hook:** Charades: act out a hobby, the class guesses in Spanish.
- **Key activity:** Present hobbies by ear with actions; *juego al…* (games and sports), *toco…* (instruments); games.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why do we say *juego al fútbol* but *toco el piano*?" → *Jugar* is for playing games and sports; *tocar* is for playing instruments.
- **Differentiation:** support: 6 hobbies with actions. / stretch: 12 hobbies with *juego al* / *toco el/la* correctly.
- **Materials:** flashcards, action cards.
- **Joy:** Charades.

### Lesson 26 — Me gusta + infinitive
- **Duration:** 30 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can say what I like doing using me gusta + a verb." (UK NC KS2 languages — grammar)
- **Hook:** "*Me gusta nadar* — why not *me gusta nado*?"
- **Key activity:** After *me gusta*, use the infinitive (the dictionary form, ending in -ar, -er, -ir): *nadar, leer, escribir*. Match infinitives to the 'I' forms.
- **Check for understanding:** "Correct this: *Me gusta juego al fútbol.*" → *Me gusta jugar al fútbol* (infinitive after *me gusta*).
- **Differentiation:** support: infinitive/'I'-form matching cards. / stretch: notice patterns of -ar, -er, -ir endings.
- **Materials:** verb cards.
- **Joy:** "Verb match" race.

### Lesson 27 — Frequency: how often?
- **Duration:** 30 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can say how often I do activities." (UK NC KS2 languages — adverbs of frequency)
- **Hook:** "Do you always, often, sometimes or never…?"
- **Key activity:** *siempre, a menudo, a veces, nunca*; with *nunca*, the verb needs *no* when *nunca* comes after it (*no nado nunca*) or *nunca* can go first (*nunca nado*) — teach *nunca* first for simplicity; class survey.
- **Check for understanding:** "Say how often you read." → e.g. *A veces leo.*
- **Differentiation:** support: a frequency line with pictures. / stretch: use *nunca* both ways correctly.
- **Materials:** frequency line, survey sheets.
- **Joy:** "Frequency line-up" — stand on a line to show how often.

### Lesson 28 — Opinions and reasons about hobbies
- **Duration:** 30 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can give opinions about activities with reasons." (UK NC KS2 languages — opinions)
- **Hook:** "Is chess boring or exciting?"

## End-of-year mastery checks

1. **Connected speaking** — speaks for about a minute on familiar topics with opinions, reasons (*porque*) and connectives. *See it:* the "Mi mundo" showcase talk.
2. **Opinions and grammar** — uses *me gusta/me gustan*, *me gusta* + infinitive and *me duele/me duelen* correctly. *See it:* corrects a partner's "*me gusta las fresas*".
3. **Listening** — picks out key details (items, times, opinions, reasons) from recordings of connected speech. *See it:* market and radio listening tasks.
4. **Reading** — understands short paragraphs, menus, leaflets and a picture book, using cognates and context. *See it:* tourist-leaflet task.
5. **Writing** — writes 6–8 connected sentences with accurate spelling and accents. *See it:* the pen-pal letter.
6. **Pronunciation** — reads unfamiliar words aloud applying sound–spelling rules and stress. *See it:* unseen reading aloud.
7. **Time and directions** — tells the time (including quarter past/to) and gives directions. *See it:* tourist role-play.
8. **Culture** — explains customs such as *el Día de los Muertos* and recognises regional variety in the Spanish-speaking world. *See it:* ofrenda captions and explanation.

## Teacher guidance

**Common misconceptions (and fixes).**
- *"Me gusta las fresas."* — link *gusta/gustan* (and *duele/duelen*) to one thing vs more than one.
- *"Me gusta juego."* — after *me gusta*, use the infinitive: *me gusta jugar*.
- *"Juego el piano."* — *tocar* for instruments, *jugar a* for games and sports.
- *Pronouncing Spanish with English sounds* (h, j, ll, z) — short, daily phonics warm-ups; praise good accents.
- *"Only Spain's Spanish is correct."* — Latin American and Spanish forms are equally correct; most Spanish speakers live in the Americas.
- *"Día de los Muertos is Mexican Halloween."* — teach its meaning and history respectfully.
- *Leaving off accents* — accents change meaning (*papa/papá*) and show stress.

**Pedagogy.** Keep lessons 70% spoken; use songs, games and routines; present by ear before spelling; recycle numbers, colours and days constantly; every lesson ends with a quick "say it to a partner" check.

**International notes.** Use local currency or euros/pesos for prices; if children speak Spanish at home, make them "language ambassadors" and extend with reading and writing challenges; welcome all home languages in the classroom.

**Swapping languages.** See the overview: the same structure works for French or Hebrew — keep the grammar progression (opinions with reasons, plurals, *I-form* verbs as chunks, time, directions) and replace cultural lessons.

**Vertical links.** Year 3: family, animals, calendar, weather, survival phrases. Year 4: connected opinions, food, hobbies, body, time, town. Year 5: full present-tense patterns, adjectives with agreement, past and near future, retelling stories and a Spanish-speaking-world project.

**Cross-curricular links.** Geography (Mexico, the Andes, South American cities), history (the Maya), English (*Esperanza Rising*), music ("La Bamba"), art (*calaveras*, *papel picado*, Frida Kahlo), PE & Health (healthy advice), maths (numbers, time, prices).
