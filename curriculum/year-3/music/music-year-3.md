# Year 3 Music (ages 7–8, KS2) — Lesson Outlines

> Part of **the best international primary school** (see [`../../../SCHOOL-CHARTER.md`](../../../SCHOOL-CHARTER.md)).
> Scope drawn from [`knowledge-base/subjects/art-music/music-ks1-ks2.md`](../../../knowledge-base/subjects/art-music/music-ks1-ks2.md)
> (Year-3 slice: begin a real **instrument pathway — the recorder**; turn ta/ti-ti into **standard rhythmic notation** (Topic 2 + Topic 8 rhythm half); first **improvising**; consolidate dynamics/tempo/timbre through playing; appraise a widening range (Topic 10).)

## Year overview

Year 3 is the year the child **picks up a real instrument and reads real notation**. The dimensions spine continues, but everything is now channelled through the **recorder** (the classic KS2 first wind instrument — cheap, melodic, reads on the stave next year): breath control, the notes **B–A–G** then **high E–D and low C**, and playing songs in time and in tune. Rhythm graduates from "ta / ti-ti" to **standard rhythmic notation on a single line** — crotchets, paired quavers, minims and crotchet rests — which the child now *reads to play* on the recorder, not just claps. **Improvising** appears for the first time: making up a short answer within set notes/rhythms, the safe doorway to composing. Dynamics, tempo and timbre are consolidated *through* the instrument (play this phrase forte, then piano; fast, then slow). The listening thread widens to world wind/percussion traditions and keeps the **notice → feel → judge** appraising sharp. Pitch *on the five-line stave* and full melody-reading are deliberately deferred **→ Year 4**; part-singing and written composition **→ Year 5**.

## Unit 1 — Meet the recorder (breath, B–A–G)

**Essential question:** How do I make a clean, in-tune sound on the recorder and play the first three notes?

**Key vocabulary:** recorder, woodwind, breath, "doo" tonguing, B, A, G, posture

### Lesson 1 — Hold it, breathe it, "doo" it
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can make a clean, gentle recorder sound using soft breath and 'doo' tonguing."
- **Hook:** The recorder is a 700-year-old woodwind — blow too hard and it SQUEAKS; whisper into it and it sings.
- **Key activity:** Listen → imitate → practise. Posture (tall, left hand on top); gentle warm breath, never a hard blow; tongue each note "doo" to start it cleanly. Make long, steady, squeak-free sounds.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why do we tongue 'doo' and use soft breath?" → "to start the note cleanly and not squeak."
- **Differentiation:** support: just one long steady note / stretch: four even "doo" notes in a steady pulse.
- **Materials:** recorders (one each); a recording of a recorder consort.
- **Joy:** taming the squeak and making the instrument actually sing.

### Lesson 2 — The note B (and good fingers)
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can play the note B cleanly and in a steady pulse."
- **Hook:** One finger and a thumb make your very first real note — B!
- **Key activity:** Imitate → practise → perform. Cover thumb-hole + first finger for **B**; check no leaks. Play B on every pulse beat of a steady backing. Echo short B-rhythms the tutor plays (ta, ta, ti-ti, ta).
- **Check for understanding:** "Play me four steady B's, tongued." → child plays clean, even B's in time.
- **Differentiation:** support: play B on every other beat / stretch: echo a 4-beat B rhythm with ti-ti.
- **Materials:** recorders; fingering chart for B; steady backing track.
- **Joy:** your first recognisable note played in time.

### Lesson 3 — Add A and G (your first tunes)
- **Duration:** 30 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can play B, A and G and a simple 3-note tune in time."
- **Hook:** Three notes is all "Hot Cross Buns" needs — and you'll have all three today!
- **Key activity:** Imitate → practise → perform. Add **A** (two fingers) and **G** (three fingers); move smoothly B→A→G. Learn **"Hot Cross Buns"** (B-A-G) and **"Merrily We Roll Along"** (B-A-G), played in time over a backing.
- **Check for understanding:** "Play 'Hot Cross Buns' in time." → child plays the B-A-G tune steadily and roughly in tune.
- **Differentiation:** support: play just the first phrase / stretch: play the whole tune from memory.
- **Materials:** recorders; B-A-G fingering chart; backing track.
- **Joy:** playing a tune anyone can recognise.

**Listening thread (Unit 1):** A **recorder consort** (Renaissance) and an Andean **panpipes / quena** piece — *notice → feel → judge*: compare two woodwind timbres and say which mood each creates and why.

**End-of-unit 1 check:** Child produces a clean tongued sound, plays B, A and G cleanly, and performs a 3-note tune in time and roughly in tune.

## Unit 2 — Reading rhythm to play (standard notation, rhythm)

**Essential question:** How do I read written crotchets, quavers, minims and rests and play them on the recorder?

**Key vocabulary:** crotchet (1 beat), quavers (½ each), minim (2 beats), crotchet rest, stem, beam, bar

### Lesson 4 — From ta/ti-ti to real notes
- **Duration:** 30 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can read a crotchet, paired quavers and a rest in standard notation and clap them."
- **Hook:** Your old friends ta and ti-ti have grown-up names and proper shapes — meet them on the page!
- **Key activity:** Read → clap → play. Crotchet = filled note + stem = ta (1 beat); paired quavers = beamed = ti-ti; crotchet rest = silent beat. Clap notated 4-beat lines, then play them all on **B**.
- **Check for understanding:** "How many beats is a crotchet?" → "one"; "what's a beamed pair?" → "two quavers, two quick sounds in one beat."
- **Differentiation:** support: read crotchets and rests only / stretch: read a line mixing all three.
- **Materials:** rhythm flashcards; recorders.
- **Joy:** realising the "secret code" of real sheet music is just ta and ti-ti.

### Lesson 5 — The minim and the bar
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can hold a minim for two beats and group notes into bars of four."
- **Hook:** A new note that lasts *twice* as long — a hollow note called a minim. Don't cut it short!
- **Key activity:** Read → play. Minim = hollow note + stem = 2 beats. Play minims on A (hold "doo——" for two beats) among crotchets. Group 4 beats into a **bar** with bar-lines; read 2-bar lines.
- **Check for understanding:** "How many beats is a minim?" → "two."
- **Differentiation:** support: alternate crotchet–minim / stretch: read a 2-bar line of crotchets, quavers and a minim.
- **Materials:** notated rhythm cards with bar-lines; recorders.
- **Joy:** the smooth, long minim note "hum."

### Lesson 6 — Read a written tune
- **Duration:** 30 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can play a short notated B-A-G tune reading its rhythm from the page."
- **Hook:** Now the page tells you both *which* note (letter) and *how long* (shape) — play it like a musician!
- **Key activity:** Read → practise → perform. Read a short B-A-G piece written with note-letters above the rhythm; clap the rhythm, finger silently, then play. Keep a steady pulse with a backing.
- **Check for understanding:** "Play this written line in time." → child plays the notated rhythm and notes steadily.
- **Differentiation:** support: clap-then-play one bar at a time / stretch: sight-read a new 2-bar line.
- **Materials:** notated B-A-G tune; recorders; backing track.
- **Joy:** "sight-reading" a tune you've never heard.

**Listening thread (Unit 2):** **"Mars" from Holst's *The Planets*** (revisited) — clap the relentless repeated rhythm, then *notice → feel → judge* the driving pulse and heavy dynamics.

**End-of-unit 2 check:** Child reads and claps crotchets, quavers, minims and rests, and plays a short notated B-A-G tune in time.

## Unit 3 — Improvising (making it up, safely)

**Essential question:** How do I make up my own short musical "answer" using set notes and rhythms?

**Key vocabulary:** improvise, question and answer, phrase, pentatonic, in time

### Lesson 7 — Musical question and answer
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can improvise a 4-beat 'answer' to a played 'question' using B, A and G."
- **Hook:** Music can have a conversation — I play a question, you make up the answer on the spot!
- **Key activity:** Imitate → improvise → perform. Tutor plays a 4-beat "question" on B-A-G; child improvises a different 4-beat "answer" using only B, A, G, ending on G (feels finished). Keep the pulse; never stop.
- **Check for understanding:** "Improvise an answer to my question." → child plays a fresh, in-time 4-beat phrase ending on G.
- **Differentiation:** support: improvise rhythm on one note (B) / stretch: answer using all three notes with a ti-ti.
- **Materials:** recorders; steady backing.
- **Joy:** inventing music live with nobody knowing what comes next.

### Lesson 8 — Pentatonic playground
- **Duration:** 30 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can improvise a short tune on pentatonic notes that always sounds good."
- **Hook:** A magic set of notes where *every* note sounds right together — the pentatonic scale!
- **Key activity:** Explore → improvise → perform. On chime bars/glockenspiel use C-D-E-G-A (pentatonic); improvise a 8-beat tune over a drone or backing — it can't sound wrong. Take turns soloing while others keep a pulse.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why does a pentatonic improvisation sound good?" → "those five notes always fit together."
- **Differentiation:** support: improvise on three pentatonic notes / stretch: shape an idea with a repeat.
- **Materials:** pentatonic chime bars/glockenspiel; drone backing.
- **Joy:** the freedom of soloing with no wrong notes.

### Lesson 9 — Dynamics & tempo in your solo
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can improvise a phrase and shape it with deliberate dynamics and tempo."
- **Hook:** A great solo isn't just notes — it gets louder, softer, faster, slower to tell a story.
- **Key activity:** Improvise → perform. Improvise a pentatonic phrase that starts *piano and slow*, grows *forte and faster*, then fades. Name the dynamics/tempo choices afterwards. (Consolidates Y2 Topic 5.)
- **Check for understanding:** "What did you change in your solo?" → child names a dynamics change and a tempo change.
- **Differentiation:** support: change just dynamics / stretch: a smooth crescendo and accelerando together.
- **Materials:** pentatonic instruments; drone.
- **Joy:** making a tiny solo feel dramatic.

**Listening thread (Unit 3):** A **jazz improvisation** (e.g. a Louis Armstrong trumpet solo) and an **Indian raga** improvisation — *notice → feel → judge*: hear how a player makes it up live, and say what you noticed and liked.

**End-of-unit 3 check:** Child improvises a 4-beat answer to a question, improvises a pentatonic tune that sounds good, and shapes a solo with deliberate dynamics and tempo.

## Unit 4 — Play, perform & appraise (bringing it together)

**Essential question:** How do I rehearse and perform a recorder piece well, and appraise music with real musical reasons?

**Key vocabulary:** rehearse, ensemble, perform, appraise, notice → feel → judge, timbre, texture

### Lesson 10 — Add low and high notes (extend the range)
- **Duration:** 30 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can play high E, high D and low C, widening the tunes I can perform."
- **Hook:** Three new notes unlock dozens of real songs — let's reach higher and lower!
- **Key activity:** Imitate → practise → perform. Add **high E, high D** (fewer fingers, gentle breath) and **low C** (all fingers, soft breath). Learn a 5-note tune such as **"Au Clair de la Lune"** or **"Frère Jacques"** opening.
- **Check for understanding:** "Play high E and low C cleanly." → child plays both without squeaking, with correct breath.
- **Differentiation:** support: add just high E / stretch: play a full simple song using the new notes.
- **Materials:** recorders; extended fingering chart; song.
- **Joy:** playing a "proper" tune people sing along to.

### Lesson 11 — Rehearse and perform a piece
- **Duration:** 30 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can rehearse a recorder piece and perform it in time, in tune, with chosen dynamics."
- **Hook:** Real musicians rehearse the tricky bar over and over — then nail the whole thing.
- **Key activity:** Practise → perform. Pick the hardest bar, loop it; then play the whole piece with a backing, adding a forte and a piano. Perform for an audience; start and stop together (ensemble basics).
- **Check for understanding:** "Should you shout/blow harder to get louder?" → "no — control breath; loud isn't squeak."
- **Differentiation:** support: perform with the tutor playing along / stretch: perform from memory with dynamics.
- **Materials:** recorders; the chosen piece; backing track.
- **Joy:** the buzz of a performance you rehearsed and pulled off.

### Lesson 12 — Appraise a range of music
- **Duration:** 30 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can appraise two contrasting pieces using notice → feel → judge with dimension words."
- **Hook:** Be the music critic: two very different pieces — which is louder, faster, more layered, and which do you prefer and why?
- **Key activity:** Listen → appraise. Compare two contrasting clips (styles/cultures). For each: *notice* (pulse/tempo, dynamics, timbre, texture thin/thick), *feel* (mood), *judge* ("I prefer … because …"). Use at least three dimension words.
- **Check for understanding:** "Give an opinion with a reason." → child uses ≥3 dimension words and a *because*.
- **Differentiation:** support: appraise one clip with two words / stretch: compare both with texture included.
- **Materials:** two contrasting recordings (e.g. orchestral vs gamelan/jazz).
- **Joy:** having strong, well-argued musical opinions.

**Listening thread (Unit 4):** A **gamelan** (Indonesia) next to a **string quartet** — *notice → feel → judge*, naming timbre and texture (few/many layers) in each.

**End-of-unit 4 check:** Child plays high E, high D and low C, rehearses and performs a recorder piece in time with dynamics, and appraises two contrasting pieces with notice → feel → judge.

## Listening & appraising (the year-long thread)

The thread widens beyond Year 1–2's repertoire into world wind/percussion and improvising traditions: a recorder consort, Andean panpipes, a jazz solo, an Indian raga, gamelan, and a string quartet — alongside revisited orchestral landmarks (Holst). Every listen runs **notice → feel → judge with a reason**, and now reliably names several dimensions including **texture** (thin/thick). Full **music history by era** is built **→ Year 6**; this year keeps building the ear and the vocabulary.

## End-of-year outcomes (what a Year 3 child can do)

1. **Play the recorder** — produce a clean tongued sound with controlled breath, and play notes B, A, G, high E, high D and low C without squeaking.
2. **Perform tunes** — play simple songs (e.g. "Hot Cross Buns", "Au Clair de la Lune") in time and roughly in tune, from notation or memory.
3. **Read rhythmic notation** — read and play crotchets, paired quavers, minims and rests, grouped into bars of four.
4. **Improvise** — make up a 4-beat "answer" to a "question," and improvise a pentatonic tune that sounds good.
5. **Shape music** — apply deliberate dynamics (forte/piano, crescendo) and tempo to playing and improvising.
6. **Describe timbre & texture** — name instrument families by timbre and say whether a texture is thin or thick.
7. **Rehearse & perform** — practise a tricky passage, then perform a piece for an audience, starting and stopping together.
8. **Appraise widely** — listen to a diverse range (recorder, panpipes, jazz, raga, gamelan, strings) and appraise with notice → feel → judge and a *because* reason.
