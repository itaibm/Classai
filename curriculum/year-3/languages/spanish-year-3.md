# Spanish — Year 3 (ages 7–8, KS2) — Lesson Outlines

> Part of **the best international primary school** (see [`../../../SCHOOL-CHARTER.md`](../../../SCHOOL-CHARTER.md)).
> This file breaks **Year 3 Spanish** into **20–40 minute lesson outlines**, drawn from
> `knowledge-base/subjects/languages/spanish-ks2.md`, ready for a teacher (or the Classai tutor)
> to expand into full plans.

> **Year 3 = the first year of Spanish.** World-language teaching starts at KS2 (Year 3) per the
> knowledge base, so this is where every child meets their first Spanish word. Everything is by
> **ear first, spelling second**; instructions are in English, the language to practise is in
> Spanish. Pronunciation hints are plain-English approximations with the **STRESSED** syllable in
> capitals.

## Year overview

Year 3 builds the foundations of all four skills, weighted heavily toward **listening and
speaking** — children should *hear* and *say* far more Spanish than they read or write this year.

- **Listening:** tune the ear to Spanish sounds — silent *h*, *ll* = "y", rolled *rr*, *j* = throaty "h", *ñ*, the vowels a-e-i-o-u.
- **Speaking:** hold a tiny conversation (greet, name, how-are-you, goodbye); count; name colours, classroom things; say what you like with *me gusta*.
- **Reading:** recognise familiar written words after meeting them by ear (flashcards, labels).
- **Writing:** copy and label single words and short fixed phrases (with *¿ ¡* punctuation).
- **Phonics/pronunciation:** the Spanish alphabet sounds and the tricky letters.
- **Vocabulary:** greetings, numbers 0–31, colours, classroom objects, plus core "everyday" words.
- **Grammar (first taste):** noun gender (*el/un* vs *la/una*), *me gusta*, the *¿ ¡* marks.
- **Intercultural:** Spanish is spoken far beyond Spain; the flag's colours; everyday Spanish life.

How it builds: this is the launch year — there is no prior Spanish to build on, so it establishes
the sound system, the greeting routine, numbers and colours that **every later year recycles**.

## Unit 1 — ¡Hola! Greetings & first sounds

**Essential question:** How do I say hello, tell someone my name, and start sounding Spanish?

**Key vocabulary:** *hola, adiós, buenos días, buenas tardes, buenas noches, ¿cómo estás?, bien gracias, ¿cómo te llamas?, me llamo…, ¿y tú?, hasta luego, gracias, por favor, sí, no.*

### Lesson 1 — ¡Hola! Hello and goodbye
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can say *hola* and *adiós* and greet someone in Spanish."
- **Hook:** Footballers from Spain, Mexico and Argentina all greet teammates with one word — *¡Hola!*
- **Key activity:** Listen → repeat *hola* (OH-lah, silent *h*!), *adiós* (ah-dee-OS), *hasta luego* (AS-ta LWEH-go). Practise waving "in" with *hola*, waving "out" with *adiós*. Produce: greet three toys/the tutor coming and going.
- **Check for understanding:** "How do you say hello and goodbye?" → *Hola* / *Adiós* (or *Hasta luego*).
- **Differentiation:** support: just *hola*/*adiós* with a wave / stretch: add *hasta luego* and *buenos días*.
- **Materials:** puppet or soft toy, "in/out" door sign.
- **Joy:** greet a puppet that "only speaks Spanish" — it ignores you until you say *¡Hola!*

### Lesson 2 — ¿Cómo te llamas? My name is…
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can ask someone's name and give my own in Spanish."
- **Hook:** A video call to a Spanish friend always starts the same way — let's learn it.
- **Key activity:** Listen → repeat *¿Cómo te llamas?* (KOH-mo te YA-mas) and *Me llamo…* (me YA-mo). Drill *ll* = "y" (pair with *llama* the animal). Produce: pass a ball — whoever holds it asks/answers.
- **Check for understanding:** "I say *¿Cómo te llamas?* — what am I asking, and how do you answer?" → "What's your name?" → *Me llamo…* + name.
- **Differentiation:** support: answer *Me llamo…* only / stretch: ask back with *¿Y tú?*
- **Materials:** soft ball, name cards.
- **Joy:** "secret-agent name" round — invent a Spanish-sounding code name to introduce.

### Lesson 3 — ¿Cómo estás? Fine, thanks
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can ask and say how I am in Spanish."
- **Hook:** In Spanish you don't just say hi — you ask how someone *feels*. Watch.
- **Key activity:** Listen → repeat *¿Cómo estás?* (KOH-mo es-TAS), *Bien, gracias* (bee-EN GRA-thee-as), *muy bien*, *así así* (so-so). Thumbs up/down to show meaning. Produce: ask-and-answer around the room.
- **Check for understanding:** "Reply to *¿Cómo estás?*" → *Bien, gracias.*
- **Differentiation:** support: *bien* + thumbs up / stretch: choose between *muy bien / bien / así así* and add *¿Y tú?*
- **Materials:** feelings thumb cards.
- **Joy:** "mood weather" — act out *muy bien* (sunny) vs *así así* (cloudy) with big faces.

### Lesson 4 — The whole little conversation (¿ and ¡)
- **Duration:** 30 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can hold a five-line greeting conversation and spot the upside-down *¿* and *¡*."
- **Hook:** Spanish puts a clue at the *start* of a question and a shout — an upside-down mark. Find it!
- **Key activity:** Listen to the full dialogue (*¡Hola! ¿Cómo estás? … ¿Cómo te llamas? … ¡Adiós!*). Show the written script — children hunt the *¿* and *¡*. Practise → produce: perform the whole conversation in pairs.
- **Check for understanding:** "What does the upside-down *¿* tell you?" → a question is starting.
- **Differentiation:** support: use a sentence-strip prompt / stretch: perform with no prompt and add *muy bien*.
- **Materials:** dialogue script with large punctuation, two role cards.
- **Joy:** record the conversation as a "phone call" and play it back.

**End-of-unit check:** child performs the greeting conversation (greet → how are you → name → goodbye) and points to a *¿* and a *¡* in writing.

## Unit 2 — Los números: counting 0–31

**Essential question:** How do I count in Spanish and use numbers for my age?

**Key vocabulary:** *cero, uno, dos, tres, cuatro, cinco, seis, siete, ocho, nueve, diez; once, doce, trece, catorce, quince; dieciséis…diecinueve; veinte, veintiuno…veintinueve; treinta, treinta y uno; ¿cuántos años tienes?, tengo … años.*

### Lesson 5 — Cero a diez (0–10)
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can count from 0 to 10 in Spanish."
- **Hook:** How many fingers? Let's count them the Spanish way.
- **Key activity:** Listen → repeat *cero…diez*, watching *seis* (saze, one beat) vs *siete* (see-EH-te, two beats). Finger-count, clap-count. Produce: "number tennis" — tutor says a number, child says the next.
- **Check for understanding:** "Count 1 to 10 in Spanish." → uno…diez.
- **Differentiation:** support: count 1–5 first / stretch: count *backwards* from 10.
- **Materials:** number flashcards 0–10, fingers.
- **Joy:** clap-and-count race against a 10-second timer.

### Lesson 6 — Once a quince + ¿Cuántos años tienes?
- **Duration:** 30 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can count to 15 and say how old I am."
- **Hook:** In Spanish you don't say you *are* 7 — you *have* 7 years. Strange and true!
- **Key activity:** Listen → repeat the special words *once, doce, trece, catorce, quince*. Then *¿Cuántos años tienes?* → *Tengo siete años.* Practise → produce: each child says their real age.
- **Check for understanding:** "Say your age in Spanish." → *Tengo ___ años.*
- **Differentiation:** support: fill the blank *Tengo ___ años* / stretch: ask a friend and report their age.
- **Materials:** birthday-candle props, number cards 11–15.
- **Joy:** "guess my age" game — hold up fingers, others say the Spanish number.

### Lesson 7 — Dieciséis a treinta y uno (the joining trick)
- **Duration:** 30 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can count up to 31 and explain how 16–29 are joined."
- **Hook:** 24 is really "twenty-four" squished into one word. Let's build it.
- **Key activity:** Show 16–19 (*dieciséis…*) and 20–29 (*veinte, veintiuno…*) join as one word; 30 = *treinta*, then *y* comes back: *treinta y uno*. Build numbers on cards: *veinti-* + *cuatro*. Produce: count 16→31.
- **Check for understanding:** "Say 24, then 31." → *veinticuatro, treinta y uno.*
- **Differentiation:** support: count 20–25 only / stretch: say house/shirt numbers up to 31.
- **Materials:** split number cards (*veinti-* | *-cuatro*).
- **Joy:** "build-a-number" with magnetic word tiles — race to assemble 27.

**End-of-unit check:** child counts 1–20 aloud, builds 24 and 31 correctly, and answers *¿Cuántos años tienes?*

## Unit 3 — Los colores: colours

**Essential question:** How do I name colours and say which ones I like?

**Key vocabulary:** *rojo, azul, amarillo, verde, naranja, rosa, morado, negro, blanco, marrón, gris; me gusta el…, ¿te gusta el…?, sí/no me gusta.*

### Lesson 8 — Los colores básicos
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can name at least six colours in Spanish."
- **Hook:** What colour is the Spanish flag? Two colours hold the answer.
- **Key activity:** Listen → repeat *rojo* (RO-ho), *azul* (ah-THOOL), *amarillo* (ama-REE-yo, *ll*="y"), *verde, naranja, negro, blanco*. Colour-hunt: point to something each colour and name it.
- **Check for understanding:** "Name three colours in Spanish." → any three, e.g. *rojo, azul, verde.*
- **Differentiation:** support: match colour word to a paint chip / stretch: name all 11.
- **Materials:** colour flashcards, paint chips, room objects.
- **Joy:** "Colour I-spy" in Spanish around the room.

### Lesson 9 — Me gusta el… (saying which I like)
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can say which colour I like and ask a friend."
- **Hook:** What's your favourite colour? Today you can say it in Spanish.
- **Key activity:** Listen → repeat *Me gusta el rojo* (me GOOS-ta) as a fixed chunk; *¿Te gusta el verde?* → *Sí, me gusta / No, no me gusta.* Practise → produce: each child gives a favourite and asks *¿Y tú?*
- **Check for understanding:** "Say 'I like green.'" → *Me gusta el verde.*
- **Differentiation:** support: *Me gusta el ___* with a card / stretch: give two colours with *y* (*Me gusta el azul y el verde*).
- **Materials:** colour cards, thumbs up/down.
- **Joy:** colour "speed-dating" — go round asking *¿Te gusta el…?* fast.

### Lesson 10 — La bandera & flags (colours + culture)
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can describe flag colours in Spanish and name a Spanish-speaking country."
- **Hook:** Spain's flag is *rojo y amarillo*; Argentina's is *azul y blanco*. Same language, different flags!
- **Key activity:** Listen → name flag colours of Spain, Argentina, Mexico. Note Spanish is spoken in *many* countries, not just Spain. Produce: child describes a flag — *Es rojo y amarillo.*
- **Check for understanding:** "What colours are on the Spanish flag?" → *rojo y amarillo.*
- **Differentiation:** support: name one colour per flag / stretch: name the country in Spanish too (*España, Argentina, México*).
- **Materials:** flag pictures, world map.
- **Joy:** design your own flag and describe it in Spanish.

**End-of-unit check:** child names six colours, says *Me gusta el…* for a favourite, and describes the colours of one flag.

## Unit 4 — En la clase: classroom objects & gender

**Essential question:** What are these things called, and why do Spanish words come with *el* or *la*?

**Key vocabulary:** *un lápiz, un libro, un bolígrafo, un estuche, una mesa, una silla, una mochila, una regla, una goma, una pizarra; ¿qué es esto?, es un/una…; el/la, un/una.*

### Lesson 11 — ¿Qué es esto? Naming classroom things
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can name five classroom objects and ask *¿Qué es esto?*"
- **Hook:** Everything on your desk has a Spanish name — let's give them tags.
- **Key activity:** Listen → repeat *un lápiz* (oon LA-pith), *un libro, una mesa, una silla, una mochila.* Ask-answer *¿Qué es esto?* (keh es ES-to) → *Es un/una…* Produce: "show me…" game with real objects.
- **Check for understanding:** "Someone asks *¿Qué es esto?* about a book. Answer." → *Es un libro.*
- **Differentiation:** support: point to the named object / stretch: name all ten objects.
- **Materials:** real classroom objects, sticky labels.
- **Joy:** label your own desk with Spanish sticky notes.

### Lesson 12 — El, la, un, una: words have a gender
- **Duration:** 30 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can sort nouns into *el/un* (masculine) and *la/una* (feminine)."
- **Hook:** In Spanish, even a pencil is a "boy" or "girl" word. Which is which?
- **Key activity:** Explain *el/un* (masculine) vs *la/una* (feminine) — learn each noun *with* its word as a chunk. Usually *-o* = masculine, *-a* = feminine (with surprises later). Practise → produce: sort object cards into two hoops.
- **Check for understanding:** "Say 'a table' and 'a chair' with *un/una*." → *una mesa, una silla.*
- **Differentiation:** support: copy the article from a labelled card / stretch: predict *el/la* for a new word and check.
- **Materials:** two hoops/boxes labelled *el / la*, noun cards.
- **Joy:** "boy word / girl word" sorting relay against the clock.

### Lesson 13 — Objects + colours together
- **Duration:** 30 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can describe a classroom object with its colour (colour *after* the noun)."
- **Hook:** Not just a pencil — a *red* pencil. Where does the colour go in Spanish?
- **Key activity:** Show colour comes **after** the noun: *un lápiz rojo* (not "rojo lápiz"). Recycle colours + objects. Practise → produce: describe items in a pencil case — *un libro verde, una mochila azul.*
- **Check for understanding:** "Say 'a green book.'" → *un libro verde.*
- **Differentiation:** support: fill *un libro ___* with a colour card / stretch: describe three items in a row.
- **Materials:** assorted coloured objects, colour cards.
- **Joy:** "mystery bag" — pull an object, name it with its colour before the timer.

**End-of-unit check:** child names objects with the right *un/una*, asks/answers *¿Qué es esto?*, and describes one object with a colour after the noun.

## Unit 5 — Putting it together: my first Spanish

**Essential question:** Can I use everything — greetings, numbers, colours, objects — in one go?

**Key vocabulary:** review of Units 1–4; plus *también, mi, gracias, por favor.*

### Lesson 14 — Sound studio: tricky Spanish letters
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can say words with silent *h*, *ll*='y', rolled *rr*, *j*='h', and *ñ*."
- **Hook:** Five Spanish letters trick English speakers. Can you crack the code?
- **Key activity:** Listen → repeat: *hola* (silent *h*), *llama* (*ll*="y"), *perro* (rolled *rr*), *jamón* (*j*=throaty h), *España/niño* (*ñ*="ny"). Practise minimal pairs *pero/perro*. Produce: tongue-twister relay.
- **Check for understanding:** "Is the *h* in *hola* sounded?" → No — it's silent.
- **Differentiation:** support: focus on silent *h* and *ll* / stretch: roll the *rr* in *perro*.
- **Materials:** letter cards, picture prompts (*llama, perro*).
- **Joy:** "rolled-r" contest — who can purr *rr* longest?

### Lesson 15 — My profile card (review speaking)
- **Duration:** 30 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can introduce myself: name, age, and a favourite colour."
- **Hook:** Make a Spanish ID card and tell everyone about yourself.
- **Key activity:** Recombine: *¡Hola! Me llamo… Tengo ___ años. Me gusta el [colour].* Practise → produce: each child delivers their three-line profile; tutor asks *¿Cómo te llamas? ¿Cuántos años tienes?*
- **Check for understanding:** "Introduce yourself with name, age, favourite colour." → e.g. *Me llamo Alex. Tengo siete años. Me gusta el azul.*
- **Differentiation:** support: prompt strips for each line / stretch: add a fourth line (a classroom object you have: *Tengo una mochila roja*).
- **Materials:** blank ID cards, crayons.
- **Joy:** decorate and "laminate" your Spanish ID card to keep.

### Lesson 16 — Year-3 fiesta review
- **Duration:** 35 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can use greetings, numbers, colours and objects in a mini party game."
- **Hook:** A *fiesta* where the password to every station is in Spanish!
- **Key activity:** Carousel of quick stations — greet the puppet; count claps; colour I-spy; *¿Qué es esto?* table. Each station needs a correct Spanish phrase to pass. Produce: full participation in Spanish.
- **Check for understanding:** "Pass the greeting station." → performs a correct greeting exchange.
- **Differentiation:** support: a buddy at each station / stretch: be the "station leader" who asks the questions.
- **Materials:** station cards, puppet, objects, music.
- **Joy:** real *fiesta* feel — music between stations and a small "passport" stamped at each.

**End-of-unit check:** child completes all four stations using correct Spanish (greeting, a number, a colour, an object name).

## End-of-year can-do statements (Year 3)

By the end of Year 3, I can…
1. **Greet** someone, ask and say how I am, give my name and say goodbye in Spanish.
2. **Count** from 0 to 31 and say how old I am (*Tengo ___ años*).
3. **Name** at least eight colours and say which I like (*Me gusta el…*).
4. **Name** common classroom objects and ask/answer *¿Qué es esto? — Es un/una…*
5. **Use** *un/una* (and *el/la*) and know that Spanish nouns are masculine or feminine.
6. **Put** a colour after the noun (*un lápiz rojo*).
7. **Pronounce** the tricky Spanish letters: silent *h*, *ll*="y", rolled *rr*, *j*="h", *ñ*.
8. **Recognise** the *¿ ¡* marks and know Spanish is spoken in many countries, not just Spain.
