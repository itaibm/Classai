# Year 3 Science (ages 7–8, KS2) — Lesson Outlines

> Part of **the best international primary school** (see [`../../../SCHOOL-CHARTER.md`](../../../SCHOOL-CHARTER.md)).
> Source of truth: [`knowledge-base/subjects/science/year-3.md`](../../../knowledge-base/subjects/science/year-3.md).

## Year overview

Year 3 begins **Key Stage 2**, and science shifts from naming and describing to **explaining how things work as systems**. A child learns *what each plant part does* and how a flowering plant completes its life cycle; how the human body runs on a balanced diet, a skeleton and muscles; how rocks, fossils and soil are made; and the first **physics** of the year — light and shadows, reflection, forces and friction, and magnets. The teaching keeps the **explore → notice → name** inquiry spirit, but the **working-scientifically thread** now matures: this year the child **sets up simple fair tests** (changing one thing, keeping the rest the same), **makes systematic observations and measurements**, and **uses results to draw conclusions**. This builds on Year 2's first comparative tests and data-gathering, and gives the child the experimental habits the rest of KS2 depends on. Lessons run mostly 30–35 min; investigation lessons (light, friction) use the upper band so there is time to test, measure and conclude.

## Unit 1 — Plants and their life cycle

**Essential question:** What does each plant part do, and how does a plant make new plants?

**Key vocabulary:** function, roots, stem, leaves, flower, anchor, absorb, transport, make food, life cycle, germinate, pollination, pollen, nectar, seed dispersal, wind, animal.

### Lesson 1 — The jobs of a plant's parts
- **Duration:** 30 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can say the job of each plant part."
- **Hook:** "A plant has no mouth — so how does it eat?"
- **Key activity:** Explore — recap the parts, then assign jobs. Notice and name **functions**: **roots** anchor and absorb water/minerals; **stem** supports and carries water up; **leaves** make food using light; **flower** makes seeds. The big idea: plants **make** their own food in their leaves — they don't eat it. Match part → job.
- **Check for understanding:** "Where does a plant make its food?" → In its leaves, using light.
- **Differentiation:** support: match three part–job pairs; stretch: explain why a flopping unwatered plant means the roots couldn't supply water.
- **Materials:** a whole plant, part-and-job matching cards.
- **Joy:** the "plants are food factories, not eaters" reveal.

### Lesson 2 — Water travels up the stem (investigation)
- **Duration:** 35 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can show that water travels up the stem."
- **Hook:** "Water usually falls down — so how does it get all the way up to the leaves?"
- **Key activity:** Investigate — stand white celery (or a white flower) in water dyed with food colouring. Predict what will happen, then observe over a few hours/overnight. Notice — the colour rises **up** the stem into the leaves/petals. Name the conclusion: the stem carries water **up** from the roots. Fix "water goes down the stem."
- **Check for understanding:** "Which way does water travel in the stem — up or down?" → Up, from roots to leaves.
- **Differentiation:** support: observe and describe the colour rising; stretch: predict the result and explain the evidence.
- **Materials:** white celery or white carnation, water, food dye, clear cup.
- **Joy:** seeing the petals/leaves turn colour as the plant "drinks."

### Lesson 3 — Pollination
- **Duration:** 30 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can explain what pollination is and who does it."
- **Hook:** "No bees would mean no apples — why?"
- **Key activity:** Explore — how flowers make seeds. Notice — **pollen** must move from flower to flower; **bees and insects** (and wind) carry it while drinking nectar; this is **pollination**, and seeds form afterwards. Fix "bees make seeds" — bees carry pollen; the plant makes the seeds.
- **Check for understanding:** "What does pollination mean?" → Moving pollen from one flower to another.
- **Differentiation:** support: order the steps with pictures; stretch: explain why bees matter for the food we eat.
- **Materials:** flower diagram, bee/flower pictures, (optional) a real flower to find pollen.
- **Joy:** "becoming a bee" — using a cotton bud to move pollen between paper flowers.

### Lesson 4 — Seed dispersal and the life cycle
- **Duration:** 30 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can name ways seeds are dispersed and order the plant life cycle."
- **Hook:** "Why does a dandelion turn into a fluffy clock you can blow?"
- **Key activity:** Explore — sort seeds (or pictures) by how they travel: light & fluffy (**wind**), sticky burrs / inside fruit (**animal**), floating (**water**), popping pods. Name **seed dispersal** and why it matters (new plants don't crowd the parent). Order the full **life cycle**: germinate → grow → flower → pollination → seed → disperse. Fix "dispersal = germinating."
- **Check for understanding:** "Name one way seeds are dispersed." → Wind / animals / water / popping pods.
- **Differentiation:** support: sort two seed types; stretch: order the whole life cycle and explain each stage.
- **Materials:** real seeds (dandelion, sticky burrs, fruit pips), life-cycle cards.
- **Joy:** blowing a dandelion clock and naming it as real seed dispersal.

**End-of-unit 1 check:** Give the job of each plant part; explain that water travels up the stem; say what pollination is and who does it; name two seed-dispersal methods and order the plant life cycle.

## Unit 2 — Animals, including humans

**Essential question:** How does the human body get energy and move?

**Key vocabulary:** nutrition, balanced diet, carbohydrate, protein, fat, vitamins, energy, skeleton, support, protect, movement, muscle, pull, pair, biceps, triceps.

### Lesson 5 — Nutrition: animals can't make their own food
- **Duration:** 30 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can explain why animals must eat and name parts of a balanced diet."
- **Hook:** "A plant makes its own food — why can't you just stand in the sun and do the same?"
- **Key activity:** Explore — compare plant (makes food) and animal (must eat). Notice and name a **balanced diet**: carbohydrates and fats for **energy**, protein for **growth and repair**, fruit/veg for **vitamins**, plus water. Fix "humans make their own food." Sort foods onto a balanced plate.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why can't a human make its own food the way a plant does?" → We can't use light to make food; we must eat it.
- **Differentiation:** support: sort foods into two groups; stretch: build a full balanced plate and justify it.
- **Materials:** food picture cards, a balanced-plate diagram.
- **Joy:** designing a "match-day meal" for a footballer (carbs for energy).

### Lesson 6 — The skeleton: support, protect, move
- **Duration:** 30 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can name three jobs of the skeleton."
- **Hook:** "Without bones, would you be able to stand up at all?"
- **Key activity:** Explore — feel your own skull, ribs, spine. Notice and name the skeleton's three jobs: **support** (holds us up), **protect** (skull guards brain, ribs guard heart/lungs), **movement**. Compare animals with bones (you, a dog) and without (worm, jellyfish). Match bone → what it protects.
- **Check for understanding:** "Give two jobs of the skeleton." → Support, protection, movement (any two).
- **Differentiation:** support: match two bones to what they protect; stretch: explain how a jellyfish copes with no skeleton.
- **Materials:** skeleton diagram/model, bone-and-organ matching cards.
- **Joy:** feeling their own ribs and skull and naming the organ each one guards.

### Lesson 7 — Muscles work in pairs
- **Duration:** 35 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can explain why muscles work in pairs."
- **Hook:** "Bend your arm — can you feel a muscle bunch up? Now straighten it — where did the work go?"
- **Key activity:** Investigate — bend and straighten the arm, feeling the **biceps** (pulls forearm up) and **triceps** (pulls it back down). Notice — a muscle can only **pull**, never push, so muscles come in **pairs**. Name the rule. Fix "muscles push the bone."
- **Check for understanding:** "Why do muscles work in pairs?" → A muscle can only pull, so a partner is needed to pull the bone back.
- **Differentiation:** support: feel and name the working muscle; stretch: explain the biceps–triceps pair in their own words.
- **Materials:** arm-muscle diagram; (optional) a card-and-elastic muscle model.
- **Joy:** feeling their own biceps and triceps take turns as they flex.

**End-of-unit 2 check:** Explain why animals must eat (and name two parts of a balanced diet); give two jobs of the skeleton; explain why muscles work in pairs.

## Unit 3 — Rocks, fossils and soils

**Essential question:** How are rocks, fossils and soil made, and how do we group rocks?

**Key vocabulary:** rock, property, hard, soft, permeable, grains, crystals, fossil, buried, imprint, soil, organic matter, group.

### Lesson 8 — Grouping rocks by their properties (investigation)
- **Duration:** 30 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can group rocks using properties I can test."
- **Hook:** "On a pebble beach, no two stones are quite the same — how would a scientist sort them?"
- **Key activity:** Investigate — examine 4–5 rocks (e.g. chalk, granite, sandstone, slate). Test **hardness** (scratch test), whether water **soaks in** (drip water), and look for **grains or crystals**. Notice and group by property. Working scientifically: systematic observation and a simple test on each.
- **Check for understanding:** "Name one property you could use to group rocks." → Hardness (or: does water soak in, grains/crystals).
- **Differentiation:** support: group by hard vs soft only; stretch: group by two properties at once.
- **Materials:** assorted rocks, water, dropper, magnifier, coin for scratch test.
- **Joy:** the scratch-and-drip tests that reveal hidden differences.

### Lesson 9 — How fossils form
- **Duration:** 30 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can explain how a fossil forms."
- **Hook:** "How do we know about dinosaurs no human ever saw?"
- **Key activity:** Explore — look at fossil images (ammonite, leaf imprint). Order the steps: a living thing **dies → is buried → soft parts rot → hard parts slowly turn to rock** (or leave an imprint) over millions of years. Fix "a fossil is the actual bone" — usually it has turned to rock.
- **Check for understanding:** "How does a fossil form?" → A buried living thing's hard parts slowly turn to rock over a very long time.
- **Differentiation:** support: order three fossil-formation cards; stretch: explain what an ammonite fossil tells us about the rock (once under the sea).
- **Materials:** fossil pictures, fossil-formation sequence cards; (optional) make a press-mould "fossil" in clay.
- **Joy:** pressing a shell into clay to make their own "fossil" imprint.

### Lesson 10 — What is soil made of?
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can say what soil is made from."
- **Hook:** "Is soil just dirt — or is it secretly made of rock?"
- **Key activity:** Explore — look closely at soil (with an adult), find tiny rock bits, bits of rotted leaf, air and water. Notice and name: soil = **tiny broken-down rock + rotted plants and animals (organic matter)** + air and water. Fix "soil isn't made of rock." Link to why plants grow in it.
- **Check for understanding:** "What is soil made from?" → Tiny bits of broken rock plus rotted plants and animals.
- **Differentiation:** support: spot rock bits and leaf bits in a sample; stretch: explain why plants need soil.
- **Materials:** a soil sample on a tray, magnifier, water (to see it settle in layers in a jar).
- **Joy:** shaking soil in a water jar and watching the layers settle.

**End-of-unit 3 check:** Group rocks using one tested property; explain how a fossil forms; say what soil is made from.

## Unit 4 — Light

**Essential question:** How do we see, what makes a shadow, and how does light reflect?

**Key vocabulary:** light, light source, dark, see, opaque, block, shadow, reflection, reflect, mirror, shiny, dull, Sun safety.

### Lesson 11 — Light, dark and shadows (investigation)
- **Duration:** 35 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can explain why we can't see in the dark and what makes a shadow."
- **Hook:** "In a perfectly dark room, can your eyes still work? Why see nothing?"
- **Key activity:** Explore — name **light sources** (Sun, lamp, torch, flame, screen) and that **dark is just the absence of light**. Investigate shadows: hold an **opaque** hand between a torch and a wall; it **blocks** the light and makes a **shadow**. Fair test — move the hand closer to the torch and watch the shadow grow. Fix "dark is a thing that fills the room" and "a shadow is a reflection."
- **Check for understanding:** "You move your hand closer to the torch. Does the shadow get bigger or smaller?" → Bigger.
- **Differentiation:** support: make and name a shadow; stretch: explain why moving closer to the torch makes it bigger.
- **Materials:** torch, wall/screen, opaque objects, ruler to measure shadow size.
- **Joy:** making giant and tiny shadow puppets on the wall.

### Lesson 12 — Reflection and Sun safety
- **Duration:** 30 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can explain reflection and say why we never look at the Sun."
- **Hook:** "How does a cyclist's jacket 'light up' in car headlights?"
- **Key activity:** Investigate — in a dim room, shine a torch on a mirror, foil, paper and black card; rank how well each **reflects**. Notice — smooth shiny surfaces reflect best; dull surfaces scatter light. Name **reflection**. Crucial safety: **never look at the Sun, even with sunglasses**; use a hat and shade. Fix "only mirrors reflect" and "sunglasses make it safe to look at the Sun."
- **Check for understanding:** "Which reflects light best — a mirror or a piece of cloth?" → A mirror (smooth and shiny).
- **Differentiation:** support: rank two surfaces; stretch: explain why reflectors keep cyclists safe.
- **Materials:** torch, mirror, foil, paper, black card.
- **Joy:** bouncing a torch beam off a mirror to make a spot dance on the ceiling.

**End-of-unit 4 check:** Explain why we can't see in the dark; say what makes a shadow and how to make it bigger; explain reflection; state the Sun-safety rule.

## Unit 5 — Forces and magnets

**Essential question:** What is a force, why do things stop, and how do magnets work?

**Key vocabulary:** force, push, pull, contact force, friction, rough, smooth, non-contact force, magnet, magnetic, attract, repel, pole, north, south.

### Lesson 13 — Pushes, pulls and friction (investigation)
- **Duration:** 35 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can explain that a force is a push or pull and that friction slows things down."
- **Hook:** "Why does a rolling ball stop all by itself if nothing pushed it back?"
- **Key activity:** Explore — a **force** is a **push or pull** that can start, stop, speed up, slow or turn movement. Investigate **friction**: roll a toy car down the same ramp onto tile and carpet; measure how far it travels (a **fair test** — only the surface changes). Notice — **rough = more friction = stops sooner**. Fix "things stop because they run out of force" and "friction is always bad" (it lets us walk and grip).
- **Check for understanding:** "Why does a rolling ball stop on grass?" → Friction between the ball and grass slows it down.
- **Differentiation:** support: compare two surfaces and say which stops the car sooner; stretch: rank three surfaces and explain the fair test.
- **Materials:** ramp, toy car, tile/carpet/other surfaces, measuring tape.
- **Joy:** racing the car on different floors and measuring the winner.

### Lesson 14 — Magnets: magnetic or not, attract or repel (investigation)
- **Duration:** 30 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can sort materials as magnetic or not and show attract and repel."
- **Hook:** "Why does a magnet stick to the steel fridge but not the wooden table?"
- **Key activity:** Investigate — predict, then test household objects with a magnet (steel paperclip, copper coin, plastic spoon, wooden peg). Notice — only **iron, steel, nickel** are magnetic; most things, including copper and aluminium, are not. Then use two bar magnets: **opposite poles attract, like poles repel** — feel the invisible push (a **non-contact force**). Fix "all metals are magnetic" and "magnets always pull together."
- **Check for understanding:** "What happens when you bring two north poles together?" → They repel (push apart).
- **Differentiation:** support: sort magnetic vs not with three objects; stretch: explain why a magnet is a non-contact force.
- **Materials:** a magnet (or two bar magnets), assorted objects, sorting mats.
- **Joy:** feeling two magnets push apart before they even touch.

**End-of-unit 5 check:** Say what a force is; explain why a rolling ball stops and whether rough or smooth makes more friction; name a magnetic material; show and explain attract vs repel.

## End-of-year mastery checks

1. **Plant parts:** Give the job of the roots, stem, leaves and flower, and explain where a plant makes its food.
2. **Plant life cycle:** Explain what pollination is, name two seed-dispersal methods, and order the life cycle.
3. **Body systems:** Explain why animals must eat, give two jobs of the skeleton, and say why muscles work in pairs.
4. **Rocks, fossils, soil:** Group rocks by one tested property; explain how a fossil forms; say what soil is made of.
5. **Light & shadows:** Explain why we can't see in the dark and what makes a shadow bigger.
6. **Reflection & safety:** Explain reflection and state the Sun-safety rule.
7. **Forces & friction:** Explain why a rolling ball stops and whether rough or smooth surfaces make more friction.
8. **Magnets:** Name a magnetic material and show/explain attract and repel.
