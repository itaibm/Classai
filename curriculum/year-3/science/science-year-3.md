# Year 3 Science (ages 8–9) — Scope & Sequence

## Year overview

Year 3 science turns curious children into **careful investigators**. Every unit follows the working-scientifically cycle — *question → predict → plan a fair test → measure → record → conclude → evaluate* — and every unit contains at least one real investigation the children carry out themselves, measuring with thermometers, data loggers or phone sound meters where available, rulers, timers and scales.

**Strands this year.** *Biology:* grouping and classifying living things with keys; how environments change and affect living things (food chains, human impact, conservation); the human digestive system and teeth. *Chemistry:* solids, liquids and gases, changes of state and the temperatures at which they happen. *Earth science:* the water cycle and measuring weather (Year 4 studies Earth and space in depth). *Physics:* sound (vibration, pitch, volume, how sound travels) and electricity (circuits, switches, conductors and insulators, safety).

**What this year assumes (from Year 2, ages 7–8):** plant parts and life cycles; nutrition, skeletons and muscles; rocks, fossils and soils; light, shadows and reflection; forces and magnets; recording in tables and bar charts; simple fair tests with one variable changed. **What Year 4 (ages 9–10) relies on:** accurate use of classification keys (for life cycles and reproduction), confident use of thermometers and reading scales (for materials and changes), the particle idea that solids, liquids and gases behave differently (for dissolving, mixtures and reversible/irreversible changes), vibration and energy transfer (for forces and later light/sound), series circuits (for extending circuits in Year 5–6), and independent planning of a fair test.

**Scientists and ideas from around the world** appear in every unit: Carl Linnaeus (Sweden) and the naming of living things; Wangari Maathai (Kenya) and the Green Belt Movement; William Beaumont (USA) and early experiments on digestion; Anders Celsius (Sweden) and the temperature scale; Evelyn Glennie (Scotland), who performs as a percussionist while profoundly deaf and 'hears' vibrations; Alessandro Volta (Italy) and the first battery; Lewis Latimer (USA), who improved the carbon filament in light bulbs.

Lessons are **40 minutes**, two a week. Safety notes are given in *Materials*; a responsible adult supervises all heat, sharp tools and glass.

## Unit 1 — Being a scientist

**Essential question:** How do scientists find out whether an idea is true?

**Key vocabulary:** question, prediction, variable, fair test, change, measure, keep the same, observation, result, table, conclusion, evaluate, evidence

### Lesson 1 — Questions scientists ask
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can sort questions into those that can be investigated and those that can't, and turn a wondering into a testable question." (UK NC Y3–4 Working scientifically (ask relevant questions))
- **Hook:** "Why do some balls bounce higher than others? Is purple the best colour? Which can we actually test?"
- **Key activity:** Children generate 'I wonder' questions about everyday things; sort them into 'testable', 'researchable' and 'opinion'; teacher models rewriting a vague question into a testable one ('Does the height a ball is dropped from affect how high it bounces?').
- **Check for understanding:** "Why can 'Which ball bounces highest?' be tested but 'Which ball is best?' can't?" → bounce height can be measured; 'best' is an opinion with no measurement
- **Differentiation:** support: question stems ('Does ___ affect ___?') and sorting cards / stretch: rewrite three vague questions as testable ones and name what would be measured
- **Materials:** assorted balls, metre sticks, question cards
- **Joy:** "Wonder wall" where every testable question gets a star

### Lesson 2 — Investigation: the fair test
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can plan and carry out a fair test, changing one variable and keeping others the same." (UK NC Y3–4 WS (set up simple fair tests))
- **Hook:** "Does a ball bounce higher when dropped from higher up? Let's make it fair."
- **Key activity:** Groups plan: change (drop height), measure (bounce height), keep the same (ball, floor, how it's dropped). Carry out with three repeats per height; record in a table.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why did we use the same ball and the same floor every time?" → so only the drop height changed, and the result can be explained by it
- **Differentiation:** support: planning board with sticky notes for change/measure/keep the same / stretch: explain why repeating each drop three times makes results more reliable
- **Materials:** tennis balls, metre sticks taped to a wall, tables
- **Joy:** slow-motion phone video of the bounce to read heights accurately

### Lesson 3 — Results and conclusions
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can present results in a bar chart or line graph and write a conclusion using evidence." (UK NC Y3–4 WS (record and present findings; draw conclusions))
- **Hook:** "The numbers are in. What do they tell us?"
- **Key activity:** Children plot bounce results; spot the pattern; write a conclusion using 'The higher…, the higher…', and evaluate (anomalies, improvements).
- **Check for understanding:** "What is your conclusion, and which results are your evidence?" → e.g. the higher the drop, the higher the bounce, citing the numbers
- **Differentiation:** support: graph axes prepared / stretch: identify an anomalous result and suggest why it happened
- **Materials:** squared paper, results tables
- **Joy:** "Scientist's verdict" card displayed with graphs

**End-of-unit check:** Given a question (does the length of a paper helicopter's wings affect fall time?), name what to change, measure and keep the same, and explain why repeats matter.

## Unit 2 — Living things: grouping and classification keys

**Essential question:** How can we sort the millions of living things on Earth so we can name and understand them?

**Key vocabulary:** living thing, vertebrate, invertebrate, mammal, bird, fish, reptile, amphibian, insect, arachnid, mollusc, flowering plant, non-flowering plant, classify, characteristic, classification key, branching key, species

### Lesson 4 — Living or not?
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can explain the life processes that make something living." (UK NC Y4 Sc (living things))
- **Hook:** "Is fire alive? It grows, it moves, it 'eats' fuel, it needs air…"
- **Key activity:** Introduce life processes (movement, respiration, sensitivity, growth, reproduction, excretion, nutrition — MRS GREN). Sort cards: living, dead, never alive (e.g. a leaf on a tree, a fallen leaf, a stone, fire).
- **Check for understanding:** "Is fire living? Use the life processes to explain." → no — although it seems to grow and use air, it doesn't reproduce, feel, or have cells
- **Differentiation:** support: picture cards with three processes to check / stretch: explain the difference between 'dead' and 'never alive' with examples
- **Materials:** picture cards, real objects (seed, rock, leaf, shell)
- **Joy:** "Is it alive?" mystery box

### Lesson 5 — Grouping animals: vertebrates
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can classify vertebrates into five groups using their characteristics." (UK NC Y4 Sc (group living things))
- **Hook:** "A bat flies but isn't a bird. A whale swims but isn't a fish. Why?"
- **Key activity:** Characteristics table (backbone; body covering; breathing; young). Sort animals from every continent; tackle tricky ones (bat, whale, penguin, platypus).
- **Check for understanding:** "Why is a whale a mammal and not a fish?" → it breathes air with lungs, is warm-blooded, feeds its young milk and gives birth to live young
- **Differentiation:** support: picture cards with covering shown / stretch: explain why the platypus puzzled scientists
- **Materials:** animal cards, sorting hoops
- **Joy:** "Animal imposters" game

### Lesson 6 — Grouping animals: invertebrates
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can classify invertebrates into groups such as insects, arachnids, molluscs and worms." (UK NC Y4 Sc)
- **Hook:** "Is a spider an insect? Count its legs!"
- **Key activity:** Minibeast hunt with magnifiers; count legs and body parts; group into insects (6 legs, 3 body parts), arachnids (8 legs, 2 body parts), molluscs, worms, crustaceans. Return creatures carefully.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why is a spider not an insect?" → it has 8 legs and 2 body parts; insects have 6 legs and 3 body parts
- **Differentiation:** support: a picture card of each group's features / stretch: research a surprising invertebrate (octopus) and explain its group
- **Materials:** magnifiers, pooters, trays, identification charts
- **Joy:** minibeast hunt in the school grounds

### Lesson 7 — Grouping plants
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can classify plants into flowering and non-flowering and describe their features." (UK NC Y4 Sc)
- **Hook:** "Do all plants have flowers? Look at a fern and a moss."
- **Key activity:** Examine real specimens: grasses (flowering), ferns, mosses, conifers; sort; note spores vs seeds.
- **Check for understanding:** "Is grass a flowering plant? How do you know?" → yes — grasses have small, often green flowers that produce seeds
- **Differentiation:** support: labelled photos / stretch: explain how ferns reproduce without seeds
- **Materials:** plant specimens, hand lenses
- **Joy:** "Plant detective" specimen table

### Lesson 8 — Carl Linnaeus and scientific names
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can explain why scientists use a shared naming system for living things." (UK NC Y4 Sc; WS)
- **Hook:** "In Spanish it's 'perro', in Swahili 'mbwa', in Japanese 'inu' — how do scientists everywhere know they mean the same animal?"
- **Key activity:** Story of Linnaeus (Sweden, 1700s) and two-part Latin names (Canis familiaris; Panthera leo). Children match common and scientific names and invent names for a new creature.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why is a shared scientific name useful?" → scientists who speak different languages can be sure they mean the same species
- **Differentiation:** support: matching cards / stretch: explain what the two parts of the name mean (genus and species)
- **Materials:** name cards, world map
- **Joy:** invent a scientific name for a class pet

### Lesson 9 — Using a classification key
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can use a branching key to identify living things." (UK NC Y4 Sc (use classification keys))
- **Hook:** "You've found a creature in the garden. How can yes/no questions tell you what it is?"
- **Key activity:** Model using a branching key for minibeasts; children identify 8 creatures (photos or specimens) with yes/no questions.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why must a key's questions have only yes or no answers?" → each answer leads to one clear path, so everyone reaches the same identification
- **Differentiation:** support: key with pictures at each step / stretch: use a number-style (dichotomous) key with written questions
- **Materials:** keys, photo cards
- **Joy:** "Creature ID" challenge

### Lesson 10 — Making a classification key
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can design a branching key for a set of living things and test it with another group." (UK NC Y4 Sc (use and construct classification keys))
- **Hook:** "Could a younger child use your key to name every leaf in the playground — without your help?"
- **Key activity:** Groups first sort 6–8 leaves or animal cards physically in hoops, one yes/no question at a time, then draw the branching key on A3. Rules: questions must be about observable features (not 'Is it pretty?') and each must split the group. Another group tests the key with a mystery specimen and reports where it went wrong; the designers improve it.
- **Check for understanding:** "Which question did you ask first, and why does it split the group well?" → a question about an observable characteristic (e.g. 'Does it have more than 6 legs?') that splits the group roughly in half
- **Differentiation:** support: sort in hoops before drawing, with a question-stem card / stretch: build a key for 10 or more organisms that uses no more than four levels of questions
- **Materials:** leaves or animal picture cards, hoops, A3 paper
- **Joy:** swap-and-test challenge with a 'key-breaker' prize

### Lesson 11 — Investigation: local habitat survey
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can survey a local habitat, identify organisms with keys and record data." (UK NC Y4 Sc; WS)
- **Hook:** "What lives in a square metre of our school grounds?"
- **Key activity:** Quadrat survey: count and identify plants and invertebrates; record in a tally chart; compare two habitats (e.g. under a hedge vs open grass).
- **Check for understanding:** "Why might there be more woodlice under the hedge than in the open grass?" → it's damper, darker and has more decaying leaves — conditions woodlice need
- **Differentiation:** support: ID cards with pictures / stretch: explain how to make the comparison fair
- **Materials:** quadrats (or hoops), ID keys, clipboards
- **Joy:** outdoor survey expedition

### Lesson 12 — Fieldwork: identifying trees with a leaf key
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can use a published identification key in the field to name trees from their leaves." (UK NC Y4 Sc (use classification keys); WS (identify and classify))
- **Hook:** "Every tree in the school grounds has a name. Can a key help us learn all of them in one lesson?"
- **Key activity:** Teacher models using a real leaf key (simple vs compound leaves, edges, arrangement) on one tree. Pairs identify five trees or shrubs in the grounds or a local park, recording the key's path for each; back in class, compare answers and resolve disagreements by re-checking features. Adapt to local species anywhere in the world.
- **Check for understanding:** "Two groups gave different names for the same tree. How can the key's path show who is right?" → retrace each step — the group that answered a question incorrectly (e.g. called a compound leaf simple) took the wrong branch
- **Differentiation:** support: a photo key with only the first three questions, and an adult partner / stretch: identify a tree not on the key and write the extra question needed to add it
- **Materials:** leaf identification keys for local trees, clipboards, collecting bags, hand lenses
- **Joy:** "Tree passport": each identified tree earns a stamp and a leaf rubbing

### Lesson 13 — Unit review: classification
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can show mastery of grouping living things and using and making keys." (UK NC Y4 Sc)
- **Hook:** "A natural history museum has muddled its labels — can you put its collection right?"
- **Key activity:** Stations: classify 10 animals into vertebrate groups and invertebrate groups with reasons; use a key to identify four mystery organisms; spot and correct three mislabelled museum cards (e.g. a bat labelled 'bird', a spider labelled 'insect', a whale labelled 'fish'); finally write a key for four new specimens.
- **Check for understanding:** "The museum has labelled a bat as a bird. Explain the error using characteristics." → bats are mammals: they have fur, give birth to live young and feed them milk — wings alone don't make a bird
- **Differentiation:** support: picture cards with characteristics shown / stretch: design a key the museum could put beside the display
- **Materials:** specimen and photo cards, keys, label cards
- **Joy:** "Museum curator" badges for corrected labels

**End-of-unit check:** Classify ten animals into vertebrate and invertebrate groups with reasons; use a branching key to identify four organisms; design a key for six leaves.

## Unit 3 — Changing environments and food chains

**Essential question:** How do living things depend on each other, and what happens when their environment changes?

**Key vocabulary:** habitat, environment, food chain, producer, consumer, predator, prey, herbivore, carnivore, omnivore, energy, pollution, deforestation, conservation, nature reserve

### Lesson 14 — Food chains
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can construct food chains and explain the direction of the arrows." (UK NC Y4 Sc (construct and interpret food chains))
- **Hook:** "Grass → zebra → lion. Why does the arrow point towards the lion?"
- **Key activity:** Build chains from savannah, ocean and forest ecosystems with picture cards; arrows show energy flow; name producers and consumers.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why do all food chains begin with a green plant?" → plants make their own food using sunlight; every other living thing gets energy by eating
- **Differentiation:** support: picture cards with roles labelled / stretch: link chains into a food web
- **Materials:** picture cards, string
- **Joy:** "Web of life": a class food web with string

### Lesson 15 — Predators and prey
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can explain how predator and prey populations affect each other." (UK NC Y4 Sc (food chains))
- **Hook:** "If all the foxes disappeared, would the rabbits be happy for ever?"
- **Key activity:** Playground simulation: some children are 'foxes', more are 'rabbits', and bean bags are 'grass'; each round, rabbits must collect grass and foxes must tag rabbits to 'survive'; survivors 'reproduce' (bring in a friend). Record numbers each round on a class table and draw a simple line graph; discuss what happens when foxes are removed.
- **Check for understanding:** "What might happen to the grass if all the foxes disappeared? Explain the chain of effects." → rabbits would increase with no predators, eat more grass, and the grass would decrease — and then rabbits might go hungry
- **Differentiation:** support: a three-link chain with picture arrows to reason along / stretch: predict effects across a larger food web with two predators
- **Materials:** bean bags, bibs, cones, results table
- **Joy:** "Predator–prey tag" in the playground

### Lesson 16 — How environments change
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can describe natural and human changes to environments and their effects." (UK NC Y4 Sc (environments can change and pose dangers))
- **Hook:** "What happens to a forest after a fire — or a new road?"
- **Key activity:** Sort changes (seasons, floods, building, pollution, deforestation, new ponds); discuss positive and negative effects with examples from several continents.
- **Check for understanding:** "Give one human change that harms living things and one that helps them." → e.g. plastic pollution harms sea turtles; creating a nature reserve or pond helps
- **Differentiation:** support: picture sort / stretch: explain a change that is good for some species and bad for others
- **Materials:** photo cards
- **Joy:** "Before and after" photo detectives

### Lesson 17 — Wangari Maathai and the Green Belt Movement
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can explain how people can change environments for the better." (UK NC Y4 Sc; global citizenship)
- **Hook:** "One woman in Kenya started planting trees — and millions followed."
- **Key activity:** Story of Wangari Maathai (Nobel Peace Prize 2004); why trees matter for soil, water and wildlife.
- **Check for understanding:** "How did planting trees help people and wildlife in Kenya?" → trees prevent soil erosion, protect water and provide habitats and firewood
- **Differentiation:** support: picture story / stretch: research another conservation project
- **Materials:** biography picture book (e.g. *Wangari's Trees of Peace* by Jeanette Winter)
- **Joy:** plant a tree or seeds in the school grounds

### Lesson 18 — Investigation: pollution and plants
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can investigate how a pollutant affects plant growth." (UK NC Y4 Sc; WS)
- **Hook:** "Does salty water harm plants — like after roads are salted?"
- **Key activity:** Set up cress in water with different amounts of salt; predict; measure over a week.
- **Check for understanding:** "What do you predict will happen, and why?" → more salt → poorer growth, because salt stops roots taking up water
- **Differentiation:** support: planning board / stretch: plan how to measure growth fairly
- **Materials:** cress seeds, cotton wool, salt, rulers
- **Joy:** cress 'heads' in eggshells

### Lesson 19 — Investigation results: pollution and plants
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can measure, present and analyse results to draw a conclusion and evaluate the investigation." (UK NC Y4 Sc; WS (record findings; draw conclusions; suggest improvements))
- **Hook:** "After a week, the cress has something to tell us. Were our predictions right?"
- **Key activity:** Groups measure the height of 10 seedlings per pot and find the typical (middle) height; draw a bar chart of salt amount vs height; write a conclusion linking to the prediction; evaluate (what might have been unfair — light, watering?). Link to real-world road salt and seawater flooding farmland.
- **Check for understanding:** "What does your evidence show about salt and cress growth, and how confident are you?" → e.g. the more salt, the shorter the cress; confident if the pattern held across repeats, less so if light or watering varied
- **Differentiation:** support: axes and scale prepared; measure 5 seedlings / stretch: explain one anomaly and propose a better method
- **Materials:** rulers, graph paper, cress pots
- **Joy:** the 'results reveal' — pots unveiled from under a cloth

### Lesson 20 — Unit review: design a nature reserve
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can apply food chains and environmental change to design and justify a nature reserve." (UK NC Y4 Sc)
- **Hook:** "An island's wetland is threatened by a new port. Can you design a nature reserve to protect it?"
- **Key activity:** Groups receive a map and species cards; they build the food web, identify threats (pollution, habitat loss), and design a reserve with features (reed beds, no-fishing zones, nesting islands), justifying each with food-chain reasoning. Mastery questions end the lesson.
- **Check for understanding:** "Draw one food chain from the island and explain what would happen if its middle link disappeared." → an accurate chain with arrows and a reasoned effect up and down the chain
- **Differentiation:** support: picture cards with a pre-drawn partial food web / stretch: evaluate a trade-off: the port brings jobs — suggest a compromise that protects the key species
- **Materials:** island maps, species cards, coloured pens
- **Joy:** "Reserve pitch" to a panel of 'island council' members

**End-of-unit check:** Build a food chain with correct arrows; predict the effect of removing a species; explain one harmful and one helpful human change to an environment.

## Unit 4 — Animals including humans: digestion and teeth

**Essential question:** What happens to a meal on its journey through your body, and how do teeth get it started?

**Key vocabulary:** digestion, digestive system, mouth, saliva, oesophagus, stomach, small intestine, large intestine, rectum, anus, nutrients, absorb, incisor, canine, premolar, molar, enamel, plaque, decay, herbivore, carnivore, omnivore

### Lesson 21 — Types of teeth
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can identify incisors, canines, premolars and molars and explain the job of each." (UK NC Y4 Sc (types and functions of teeth))
- **Hook:** "Bite an apple, then chew it. Which teeth did each job?"
- **Key activity:** Children use mirrors to find their own teeth; handle a model jaw; match each type to its job (incisors cut, canines tear, premolars and molars grind) by biting/chewing an apple slice mindfully. Record a labelled tooth map.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why are molars flat and wide while incisors are thin and sharp?" → molars grind and crush food, which needs a wide surface; incisors cut and bite, which needs a sharp edge
- **Differentiation:** support: picture cards of each tooth type next to a tool with the same job (knife, fork, grinder) / stretch: explain why children have 20 milk teeth but adults usually have 32 permanent teeth
- **Materials:** hand mirrors, model jaw, apple slices (check allergies), tooth maps
- **Joy:** "Tool or tooth?" matching game with kitchen tools

### Lesson 22 — Animal teeth and diets
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can compare animal teeth and explain how they match what the animal eats." (UK NC Y4 Sc)
- **Hook:** "Could you tell what an animal eats just from its skull?"
- **Key activity:** Compare skull photos or replicas (sheep, dog/lion, human); link teeth shapes to herbivore, carnivore and omnivore diets. Mystery skulls: children predict the diet with reasons.
- **Check for understanding:** "A skull has large canines and sharp, pointed back teeth. What did the animal eat, and how do you know?" → it was a carnivore — canines grip and tear meat and pointed back teeth slice it
- **Differentiation:** support: skulls/photos labelled with tooth types / stretch: explain why a panda (a carnivore by family) has flat molars for bamboo
- **Materials:** skull replicas or photos, magnifiers
- **Joy:** "Mystery skull" detectives

### Lesson 23 — Investigation: what harms teeth?
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can plan and carry out a fair test on how drinks affect eggshell (as a model of tooth enamel)." (UK NC Y4 Sc; WS (fair test; predictions))
- **Hook:** "Eggshell is made of a similar mineral to tooth enamel. What will cola, orange juice, milk and water do to it?"
- **Key activity:** Groups plan: change (the drink), keep the same (egg size, amount of drink, time), observe (colour, softness, surface). Set up hard-boiled eggs in cups for 3–5 days; predict with reasons.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why must every egg be in the same amount of drink for the same time?" → so any difference is caused by the type of drink alone
- **Differentiation:** support: planning board with change/keep the same sticky notes / stretch: explain why eggshell is a model, not a perfect copy, of a real tooth
- **Materials:** hard-boiled eggs, clear cups, drinks, labels (adult prepares eggs)
- **Joy:** "Egg-periment" labelled lab bench

### Lesson 24 — Investigation results: teeth and drinks
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can observe and record results and draw a conclusion about caring for teeth." (UK NC Y4 Sc; WS)
- **Hook:** "The eggs are ready. Which drink did the most damage?"
- **Key activity:** Remove eggs; observe colour, softness and surface with magnifiers; record in a table; rank; conclusion. Then brush half of one stained egg with toothpaste and compare.
- **Check for understanding:** "What does your evidence suggest about sugary and acidic drinks, and what should we do about it?" → they stained or softened the shell most, so limit them and brush teeth twice a day
- **Differentiation:** support: results table with drawn boxes / stretch: explain why toothpaste helped and link to fluoride
- **Materials:** magnifiers, toothbrushes, toothpaste, gloves
- **Joy:** toothbrush 'rescue' of a stained egg

### Lesson 25 — The digestive journey
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can describe the main parts of the digestive system in order and what happens in each." (UK NC Y4 Sc (simple functions of the basic parts of the digestive system))
- **Hook:** "Your digestive system is about 9 metres long. Where does all that tube fit?"
- **Key activity:** Measure out 9 m of rope/wool labelled with parts (mouth, oesophagus, stomach, small intestine ~6–7 m, large intestine ~1.5 m). Label a body outline. Sequence cards with each part's job.
- **Check for understanding:** "What happens in the small intestine, and why is it so long?" → nutrients are absorbed into the blood; its great length (and folded lining) gives lots of surface to absorb them
- **Differentiation:** support: a body outline with parts pre-drawn to label / stretch: explain the role of saliva and stomach acid in breaking food down
- **Materials:** rope or wool, labels, body outlines
- **Joy:** unroll the 9 m digestive 'tube' across the playground

### Lesson 26 — Model digestion
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can use a model to explain digestion and evaluate what the model shows well and poorly." (UK NC Y4 Sc; WS (use models))
- **Hook:** "Let's digest a sandwich … in a bag."
- **Key activity:** Demonstration/model: bread and banana mashed in a zip bag with water and orange juice ('stomach'), squeezed through a stocking ('small intestine') over a bowl; remaining mush is 'waste'. Children match each step to the real organ and evaluate the model.
- **Check for understanding:** "What does the stocking represent, and what does the liquid dripping through it represent?" → the stocking is the small intestine; the liquid is the nutrients and water absorbed into the blood
- **Differentiation:** support: step cards with organ pictures / stretch: list two ways the model is unlike the real system (no muscles, no enzymes, not warm)
- **Materials:** zip bags, bread, banana, orange juice, water, old tights, bowls, aprons (adult-led)
- **Joy:** the gloriously messy model digestion

### Lesson 27 — William Beaumont and the stomach
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can explain how scientists found out how digestion works." (UK NC Y4 Sc; WS (how scientists work))
- **Hook:** "In the 1820s, a doctor could see into a living man's stomach. How?"
- **Key activity:** Tell the story of William Beaumont and Alexis St. Martin (a wound that healed with an opening into the stomach); Beaumont's observations of food on strings. Discuss ethics and evidence.
- **Check for understanding:** "What did Beaumont's experiments show about the stomach?" → stomach juices break food down chemically, not just by squeezing
- **Differentiation:** support: picture timeline / stretch: discuss whether the experiments were fair to St. Martin
- **Materials:** story cards, timeline
- **Joy:** "Doctor's notebook" role-play

### Lesson 28 — Healthy digestion
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can explain how fibre, water and a balanced diet help digestion." (UK NC Y4 Sc)
- **Hook:** "Why does your body need fibre if it can't digest it?"
- **Key activity:** Sort foods by fibre; discuss water and exercise; plan a day's meals from different cuisines that keep digestion healthy.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why is fibre important even though we don't digest it?" → it helps food move through the intestines and keeps them healthy
- **Differentiation:** support: food picture cards / stretch: explain why drinking water matters for the large intestine
- **Materials:** food cards, menus from several countries
- **Joy:** "World menu" plan

### Lesson 29 — Unit review: body tour
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can show mastery of teeth and digestion." (UK NC Y4 Sc)
- **Hook:** "Guide a tour through the body — you're a crumb of bread."
- **Key activity:** Children write/tell the journey of a crumb; label diagrams; answer mastery questions.

## Unit 5 — States of matter

**Essential question:** Why does ice melt, water boil and a puddle disappear — and what stays the same?

**Key vocabulary:** solid, liquid, gas, particle, state, melt, freeze, boil, evaporate, condense, melting point, boiling point, temperature, degrees Celsius (°C), thermometer, volume, shape, flow

### Lesson 30 — Solids, liquids and gases
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can sort materials into solids, liquids and gases by their properties." (UK NC Y4 Sc (compare and group materials by state))
- **Hook:** "Is sand a liquid? It pours!"
- **Key activity:** Explore stations: a wooden block, water, honey, air in a balloon, sand, a sponge. Test: does it keep its shape? can it be poured? does it fill its container? Sort with reasons; tackle tricky cases (sand, toothpaste, jelly).
- **Check for understanding:** "Sand can be poured. Why is it still a solid?" → each grain keeps its own shape and size; it pours because the grains are small and separate
- **Differentiation:** support: property checklist with pictures / stretch: explain why honey is a liquid even though it flows slowly
- **Materials:** stations of materials, trays, balloons
- **Joy:** "Is it a liquid?" mystery box challenge

### Lesson 31 — Particles: a model of matter
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can use a particle model to explain the properties of solids, liquids and gases." (UK NC Y4 Sc; WS (models))
- **Hook:** "What if you could shrink small enough to see what water is made of?"
- **Key activity:** Children act as particles in the hall: solid (packed, vibrating on the spot), liquid (close but sliding past each other), gas (spread out, moving fast in all directions). Draw particle diagrams.
- **Check for understanding:** "Use the particle model to explain why a gas fills its container but a solid doesn't." → gas particles move freely and spread out to fill any space; solid particles are held in place
- **Differentiation:** support: diagram templates with circles to arrange / stretch: explain why gases can be squashed but liquids hardly can
- **Materials:** hall space, particle diagram sheets
- **Joy:** "Human particles" dance

### Lesson 32 — Gases have mass
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can show that gases are real matter with mass and volume." (UK NC Y4 Sc)
- **Hook:** "Air weighs nothing — or does it?"
- **Key activity:** Demonstrations: a balanced stick with two balloons, one burst (the full one was heavier); fizzy drink bottle weighed before and after opening. Discuss.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why did the balance tip when one balloon was burst?" → the full balloon contained air, which has mass; bursting it released the air, so that side became lighter
- **Differentiation:** support: step-by-step picture cards / stretch: explain where the lost mass of the opened fizzy drink went (carbon dioxide gas escaped)
- **Materials:** balloons, metre stick balance, string, digital scale, fizzy drink
- **Joy:** "Weigh the air" demonstration

### Lesson 33 — Melting and freezing
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can explain melting and freezing as changes of state caused by heating and cooling." (UK NC Y4 Sc (changes of state when heated or cooled))
- **Hook:** "Chocolate, butter, ice and candle wax: which melts first in your warm hands?"
- **Key activity:** Place small samples in hands or on warm water plates; order by melting; freeze juice into lollies; introduce 'melting point'.
- **Check for understanding:** "Is melting ice the same substance as the ice? How do you know?" → yes — it's still water, just in a different state; it can be frozen back
- **Differentiation:** support: record in a picture table / stretch: explain why chocolate melts in your mouth but not in the wrapper
- **Materials:** ice cubes, chocolate, butter, wax (adult-handled), warm-water trays
- **Joy:** make juice ice lollies to eat later

### Lesson 34 — Measuring temperature
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can read a thermometer accurately in °C and know water's freezing and boiling points." (UK NC Y4 Sc; WS (measure temperature))
- **Hook:** "Anders Celsius set 0 and 100 as water's special temperatures. What happens at each?"
- **Key activity:** Practise reading thermometers (and digital probes) in cold water, room water, warm water; learn 0 °C (water freezes/ice melts) and 100 °C (water boils at sea level).
- **Check for understanding:** "Why is 0 °C important for water?" → it's the temperature at which water freezes and ice melts
- **Differentiation:** support: thermometer scale cards with intervals marked / stretch: read a scale with 2-degree intervals
- **Materials:** thermometers, digital probes, water at different temperatures (adult-prepared)
- **Joy:** temperature guessing game

### Lesson 35 — Investigation: the ice-cube challenge
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can plan and carry out a fair test to find which wrapping keeps an ice cube frozen longest." (UK NC Y4 Sc; WS (fair test; measure time))
- **Hook:** "The ice-cream van's freezer has broken. Which wrapping will keep the ice cream frozen until home?"
- **Key activity:** Groups choose wrappings (foil, bubble wrap, wool sock, newspaper, none); plan what to change, measure (time to fully melt, or mass of ice left after 30 minutes) and keep the same (cube size, starting temperature, location). Record every 10 minutes; present in a bar chart.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why did the ice wrapped in wool last longer than the unwrapped cube?" → wool is an insulator; it slows heat from the warm air reaching the ice, so it melts more slowly
- **Differentiation:** support: planning board with change/measure/keep the same, and only three wrappings / stretch: explain why a wool jumper keeps you warm but also keeps ice cold (it slows heat transfer both ways)
- **Materials:** ice cubes of equal size, wrappings, timers, scales, trays
- **Joy:** the "ice-cream rescue" results ceremony

### Lesson 36 — Evaporation
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can explain evaporation as liquid turning to gas and investigate what makes it faster." (UK NC Y4 Sc (evaporation; temperature))
- **Hook:** "Where does a puddle go on a sunny day? It doesn't soak into the concrete!"
- **Key activity:** Draw chalk around a puddle and re-draw every hour. Indoors, set up equal amounts of water in dishes: warm place vs cold place, wide dish vs narrow cup, with a fan vs without. Predict; measure water left after 2 days.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why does washing dry faster on a warm, windy day?" → heat gives water particles energy to escape as gas; wind carries the vapour away so more can evaporate
- **Differentiation:** support: picture prediction cards for each set-up / stretch: explain why a wide dish loses water faster than a narrow cup (larger surface)
- **Materials:** chalk, dishes, measuring cylinders, small fan
- **Joy:** "Disappearing puddle" chalk time-lapse

### Lesson 37 — Condensation
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can explain condensation as gas cooling into a liquid and give everyday examples." (UK NC Y4 Sc (condensation))
- **Hook:** "Why does a cold can from the fridge 'sweat' on a warm day? Is water leaking through the metal?"
- **Key activity:** Put ice and water in a dry metal can or glass; watch droplets form outside; use food colouring inside to prove it's not leaking. Breathe on a cold mirror. Link to steamy bathroom windows.
- **Check for understanding:** "The water inside was blue but the drops outside are clear. Where did they come from?" → from water vapour in the air, which cooled on the cold surface and condensed into liquid
- **Differentiation:** support: step-by-step picture sequence / stretch: explain why glasses steam up when you come indoors on a cold day
- **Materials:** metal cans or glasses, ice, food colouring, mirrors
- **Joy:** breath-art on cold mirrors

### Lesson 38 — Changes of state are reversible
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can explain that changes of state can be reversed and show them on a cycle diagram with temperatures." (UK NC Y4 Sc)
- **Hook:** "Can you turn water into ice into water into steam into water again — and still have the same water?"
- **Key activity:** Build a change-of-state cycle diagram (melting, freezing, evaporating/boiling, condensing) with the key temperatures for water (0 °C, 100 °C). Sort changes as reversible (melting chocolate, freezing juice) vs not (cooking an egg) — previewing Year 4.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why is melting chocolate a reversible change but baking a cake is not?" → melted chocolate can set again as chocolate; a baked cake can't be turned back into batter — new substances formed
- **Differentiation:** support: a cycle diagram with the arrows drawn and labels to place / stretch: explain why different substances melt at different temperatures (e.g. iron in a foundry)
- **Materials:** cycle diagram templates, picture cards
- **Joy:** make chocolate shapes: melt, pour into moulds, set

### Lesson 39 — Unit review: states of matter
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can show mastery of the states of matter and changes of state." (UK NC Y4 Sc)
- **Hook:** "You are a water molecule. Describe your adventures through every state."
- **Key activity:** Children write or draw a first-person 'water particle diary' through ice → water → vapour → water; then answer mastery questions (sorting, particle diagrams, thermometer reading, explaining condensation).
- **Check for understanding:** "Draw particles in a solid, a liquid and a gas and explain one property each drawing shows." → solid: packed in rows (keeps shape); liquid: close but jumbled (flows); gas: spread out (fills container)
- **Differentiation:** support: labelled diagrams to complete / stretch: explain why steam from a kettle becomes visible a short distance from the spout (condensing into tiny droplets)
- **Materials:** diagram sheets, thermometers
- **Joy:** "Water particle diary" comic

**End-of-unit check:** Sort materials into solids, liquids and gases with reasons; draw particle diagrams; read a thermometer; explain evaporation and condensation with an everyday example each; name water's freezing and boiling points.

## Unit 6 — Earth science: the water cycle and weather

**Essential question:** How does the same water travel endlessly between the sea, the sky and the land — and how can we measure the weather it brings?

**Key vocabulary:** water cycle, evaporation, condensation, precipitation, collection, run-off, groundwater, water vapour, cloud, rain gauge, thermometer, wind vane, anemometer, weather, climate, data

### Lesson 40 — The water cycle
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can describe the water cycle and explain the part played by evaporation and condensation." (UK NC Y4 Sc (water cycle))
- **Hook:** "The water in your glass might once have been drunk by a dinosaur. How is that possible?"
- **Key activity:** Build a 'water cycle in a bag': a little water in a sealed zip bag taped to a sunny window, with a sea and a cloud drawn on. Over the lesson and following days, observe evaporation, condensation and 'rain' running down. Draw and label the cycle (evaporation, condensation, precipitation, collection).
- **Check for understanding:** "In the bag, where did evaporation and condensation happen, and what caused each?" → evaporation from the water at the bottom, caused by the Sun's warmth; condensation at the top of the bag where it's cooler
- **Differentiation:** support: a labelled diagram with arrows to complete / stretch: explain why the total amount of water on Earth stays about the same
- **Materials:** zip bags, water, blue food colouring, markers, sunny window
- **Joy:** "Water cycle in a bag" on every window

### Lesson 41 — Following a raindrop
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can explain the journey water takes over land, including run-off, rivers and groundwater." (UK NC Y4 Sc; links to geography)
- **Hook:** "A raindrop lands on a mountain in the Andes. Where will it go next?"
- **Key activity:** Children build a landscape model from a crumpled plastic sheet over a tray (mountains, valleys); spray 'rain' and watch run-off form rivers and lakes; discuss soaking into ground (groundwater) on a sponge section. Write the journey of one raindrop.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why does rain run off quickly on a mountain but soak in on a field?" → steep, hard surfaces send water downhill fast; soil and plants absorb it slowly
- **Differentiation:** support: picture sequence of the raindrop's journey / stretch: explain how cutting down forests could increase flooding
- **Materials:** trays, plastic sheeting, spray bottles, sponges, food colouring
- **Joy:** build-a-landscape rainstorm

### Lesson 42 — Investigation: temperature and evaporation in the water cycle
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can investigate how temperature affects the rate of evaporation and relate it to weather around the world." (UK NC Y4 Sc; WS)
- **Hook:** "Why do puddles vanish faster in Cairo than in Oslo?"
- **Key activity:** Place equal amounts of water in identical dishes in three places with different temperatures (by a radiator, a shady corner, the fridge); measure temperature and water left each day for three days; plot a line graph.
- **Check for understanding:** "What pattern did you find, and how does it explain faster puddle-drying in hot countries?" → the warmer the place, the faster water evaporated — hot countries dry puddles faster
- **Differentiation:** support: results table prepared, with daily measurements supported / stretch: explain why the fridge dish still lost a little water
- **Materials:** identical dishes, measuring cylinders, thermometers
- **Joy:** daily 'water-level race' check

### Lesson 43 — Measuring weather
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can use instruments to measure temperature, rainfall and wind direction accurately." (UK NC Y4 Sc; NGSS 3-ESS2-1)
- **Hook:** "Weather forecasters rely on measurements. Can we build our own weather station?"

- **Check for understanding:** "What does your graph show about the link between cloud cover and temperature on our coldest days?" → a data-based answer (e.g. the clearest nights were coldest) with values from the graph

### Lesson 45 — Unit review: water on the move

## Unit 7 — Sound

**Essential question:** How is sound made, how does it travel to our ears, and why do some sounds seem high, low, loud or soft?

**Key vocabulary:** sound, vibration, vibrate, source, travel, medium, solid, liquid, gas, vacuum, ear, eardrum, pitch, high, low, volume, loud, quiet, amplitude, decibel, insulate, echo

### Lesson 46 — Sound is vibration
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can explain that sounds are made when something vibrates." (UK NC Y4 Sc (sounds are made by vibrations))
- **Hook:** "Put your fingers on your throat and hum. What can you feel?"
- **Key activity:** Stations: rice on a drum skin, a ruler twanged on a table, a tuning fork touched to water, a stretched elastic band, throat humming. Children observe and record the vibration at each and name the vibrating part.
- **Check for understanding:** "What was vibrating when the ruler made a sound, and how did you know?" → the overhanging end of the ruler — you could see it moving quickly up and down, and the sound stopped when it stopped
- **Differentiation:** support: picture cards showing where to look for each vibration / stretch: explain why a sound stops when you hold a cymbal or the ruler still

### Lesson 47 — How sound travels
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can explain that vibrations travel through a medium (solid, liquid or gas) to the ear." (UK NC Y4 Sc (vibrations travel through a medium to the ear))
- **Hook:** "Can you hear your friend tapping through the table better than through the air?"
- **Key activity:** Children tap gently on one end of a table with an ear pressed to the other end, then listen through the air; knock two stones together underwater in a bowl while listening with an ear against the bowl's side (supervised). Model particles passing vibrations along a line of children. Discuss why there's no sound in space.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why can't astronauts hear each other in space without radios?" → space is (almost) a vacuum — there are no particles to carry the vibrations
- **Differentiation:** support: a 'particle chain' role-play before recording / stretch: explain why sound travels faster through solids than through air, using the particle model
- **Materials:** tables, stones, bowl of water, slinky spring
- **Joy:** "Human sound wave": pass a push along a line of children

### Lesson 48 — The ear
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can describe how the ear detects vibrations and how to protect hearing." (UK NC Y4 Sc)
- **Hook:** "Your ear is a vibration detector. How does it work?"
- **Key activity:** Model ear: cling film stretched over a bowl ('eardrum') with salt grains; a sound nearby makes the salt jump. Label a simple ear diagram (outer ear, ear canal, eardrum, inner ear sends signals to the brain). Discuss safe listening volumes and hearing protection.
- **Check for understanding:** "What does the eardrum do, and what did the cling film model show?" → the eardrum vibrates when sound vibrations reach it; the salt jumped because the cling film vibrated
- **Differentiation:** support: a diagram with the four parts pre-drawn / stretch: explain why very loud sounds can damage hearing
- **Materials:** bowls, cling film, salt, a speaker or drum, ear diagrams
- **Joy:** "Jumping salt" ear model

### Lesson 49 — Evelyn Glennie: feeling sound
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can explain how a profoundly deaf musician senses sound through vibration." (UK NC Y4 Sc; WS (scientists' work and lives))
- **Hook:** "Evelyn Glennie is one of the world's great percussionists — and she is profoundly deaf. How does she play?"
- **Key activity:** Watch a short approved clip of Evelyn Glennie describing how she feels vibrations through her body; children play percussion barefoot on a wooden floor and describe what they feel through feet and hands.
- **Check for understanding:** "How can someone sense sound without hearing it through their ears?" → by feeling vibrations through the body — the same vibrations that reach the ear
- **Differentiation:** support: guided sensory prompts ('feel in your feet, hands, chest') / stretch: explain how this links to how sound travels through solids
- **Materials:** percussion instruments, wooden floor, video clip
- **Joy:** barefoot vibration concert

### Lesson 50 — Pitch
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can explain how the pitch of a sound depends on how fast something vibrates." (UK NC Y4 Sc (pitch and features of the object))
- **Hook:** "Why does a big drum sound low and a small drum high?"
- **Key activity:** Stations: elastic bands of different thickness/tightness over a box, bottles with different water levels (tap and blow), a ruler with more or less overhanging. Record the pattern: faster vibration = higher pitch; smaller/tighter/shorter → higher.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why does a shorter ruler end make a higher sound?" → the shorter end vibrates faster, and faster vibration produces a higher pitch
- **Differentiation:** support: a pattern sentence frame ('The ___ the ___, the higher the pitch') / stretch: explain why tapping and blowing across the same bottle give opposite pitch patterns (the glass+water vibrates vs the air column vibrates)
- **Materials:** elastic bands, boxes, glass bottles, water, rulers
- **Joy:** play a tune on a bottle xylophone

### Lesson 51 — Investigation: pitch and length
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can plan and carry out a fair test on how the length of a vibrating object affects pitch." (UK NC Y4 Sc; WS)
- **Hook:** "Can we design a panpipe that plays a scale?"
- **Key activity:** Groups test straws of different lengths (blowing across the top, with one end sealed by tape or a finger); keep straw width and blowing the same; order by pitch; use a free tuner app (teacher's device) to measure frequency if available.
- **Check for understanding:** "What pattern did you find between straw length and pitch, and what did you keep the same?" → shorter straws made higher pitches; the straw width and how hard we blew stayed the same

- **Joy:** class panpipe band

### Lesson 52 — Volume

- **Check for understanding:** "Why does hitting a drum harder make a louder sound?" → it makes the drum skin vibrate more strongly (bigger vibrations), which carries more energy to the ear

### Lesson 53 — Investigation: sound and distance

### Lesson 55 — Unit review: sound

### Lesson 58 — Will it light?

- **Check for understanding:** "This circuit has both wires on the cell's positive end. Will the bulb light? Why not?" → no — the loop isn't complete from one terminal of the cell, through the bulb, back to the other terminal
- **Differentiation:** support: build each circuit before predicting, with a working circuit to compare against / stretch: design a 'trick' circuit for a partner that looks complete but isn't, and explain the trick
- **Materials:** circuit cards, cells, bulbs, crocodile-clip wires
- **Joy:** "Circuit detectives": solve six case files

### Lesson 59 — Circuit symbols and diagrams
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can draw and read circuit diagrams using standard symbols." (UK NC Y4 Sc (Y6 content previewed: recognised symbols))
- **Hook:** "An electrician in Tokyo and one in Nairobi can read the same drawing. How?"

- **Check for understanding:** "Why do engineers draw circuit diagrams with symbols instead of pictures?" → symbols are quick to draw, clear, and understood by people everywhere regardless of language
- **Differentiation:** support: a symbol card on the desk and circuits already built to copy / stretch: draw a circuit with a switch, a buzzer and a bulb and build it from the drawing
- **Materials:** symbol cards, rulers, kits
- **Joy:** "Build from the blueprint" race

### Lesson 60 — Switches
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can explain how a switch opens and closes a circuit and make my own switch." (UK NC Y4 Sc (recognise that a switch opens and closes a circuit))
- **Hook:** "How does a light switch on the wall turn on a light on the ceiling without touching it?"
- **Key activity:** Use a commercial switch in a circuit; then make a switch from card, two split pins and a paper clip; explain 'open' (gap) and 'closed' (complete). Test in a circuit with a buzzer.
- **Check for understanding:** "What does a switch do inside a circuit? Use the words 'open' and 'closed'." → a closed switch completes the loop so electricity flows; an open switch makes a gap so it stops
- **Differentiation:** support: step-by-step photo guide for making the switch / stretch: design a pressure switch (two foil pads separated by sponge) that sounds a buzzer when stepped on
- **Materials:** card, split pins, paper clips, foil, sponge, kits
- **Joy:** build a 'burglar alarm' for a treasure box

### Lesson 61 — Investigation: conductors and insulators
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can test materials to find which conduct electricity and explain how this is used in wires and plugs." (UK NC Y4 Sc (common conductors and insulators; metals as good conductors))
- **Hook:** "Coin, pencil lead, key, plastic ruler, foil, rubber, wooden spoon — which could complete a circuit?"
- **Key activity:** Build a tester circuit with a gap; predict, then test 12 materials; record in a table; spot the pattern (metals conduct). Examine an old (cut, safe) cable: copper inside, plastic outside.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why is the pencil 'lead' a conductor when the pencil's wood isn't?" → the 'lead' is graphite, which conducts electricity; wood is an insulator
- **Differentiation:** support: a prediction table with pictures / stretch: explain why electrical wires are copper covered in plastic
- **Materials:** tester circuits, material samples, a safe cut section of cable
- **Joy:** "Conductor hunt" around the classroom

### Lesson 62 — Investigation: more cells, brighter bulbs?
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can investigate how the number of cells affects the brightness of a bulb." (UK NC Y4 Sc; WS (Y6 preview))
- **Hook:** "What happens if we add more cells — brighter, dimmer, or nothing?"
- **Key activity:** Plan a fair test: change the number of cells (1, 2, 3), keep the same bulb and wires; observe brightness against a brightness scale (or a light-meter app); record and conclude. Caution: bulbs rated for the voltage; teacher checks before connecting.
- **Check for understanding:** "What did you change, what did you keep the same, and what pattern did you see?" → changed the number of cells; kept bulb and wires the same; more cells made the bulb brighter
- **Differentiation:** support: a brightness scale card (1–5) / stretch: predict and test what happens with two bulbs and one cell
- **Materials:** cells, holders, bulbs, light-meter app
- **Joy:** "Brightness ladder" display

### Lesson 63 — Buzzers and motors
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can build circuits with buzzers and motors and explain what changes when components are reversed." (UK NC Y4 Sc)
- **Hook:** "Some components care which way round they go — others don't!"
- **Key activity:** Build circuits with a buzzer and a motor with a propeller; reverse the connections and observe (the motor spins the other way; many buzzers only work one way round).
- **Check for understanding:** "What happened when you reversed the motor's wires, and why might this be useful?" → the motor spun the other way — useful to make a toy go forwards and backwards
- **Differentiation:** support: picture guide / stretch: design a fan with a switch
- **Materials:** buzzers, motors, propellers, kits
- **Joy:** spinning propeller challenge

### Lesson 64 — Alessandro Volta and Lewis Latimer
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can explain how scientists and inventors developed the battery and the light bulb." (UK NC Y4 Sc; WS (scientists' contributions))
- **Hook:** "Who invented the battery — and who made light bulbs last long enough to light cities?"
- **Key activity:** Stories: Volta (Italy, around 1800) stacked metal discs and salty card to make the first battery; Lewis Latimer (USA), son of people who had escaped slavery, improved the carbon filament to make bulbs last longer. Children make a lemon or coin battery (supervised) to power an LED.
- **Check for understanding:** "How did Volta's pile make electricity, and how did our lemon battery copy it?" → two different metals with something salty or acidic between them produce a small current; our lemon had zinc and copper with lemon juice between
- **Differentiation:** support: picture timeline and step card / stretch: explain why many lemons or coins must be linked to light the LED
- **Materials:** lemons, zinc-coated nails, copper coins/strips, LEDs, wires
- **Joy:** lemon-battery LED lighting

### Lesson 65 — Design project: plan a steady-hand game
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can design an electrical steady-hand game with a circuit." (UK NC Y4 Sc; links to DT)
- **Hook:** "Can you steer a loop along a wire without making the buzzer sound?"
- **Key activity:** Groups design a steady-hand game: a bent wire, a wire loop, a buzzer and a cell in a circuit. Draw a labelled circuit diagram and list materials.
- **Check for understanding:** "Explain why the buzzer sounds when the loop touches the wire." → touching completes the circuit, so electricity flows and the buzzer sounds
- **Differentiation:** support: a template diagram / stretch: add a light that shows the game is 'on'
- **Materials:** design sheets, symbol cards
- **Joy:** design pitch

### Lesson 66 — Design project: make and test
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can build, test and improve my steady-hand game, fixing faults systematically." (UK NC Y4 Sc; WS; links to Design & Technology)
- **Hook:** "Engineers expect things to go wrong the first time. Your job is to find out why."
- **Key activity:** Groups build from their design: bend the course wire, fix it into a box lid, make a handle loop, connect the buzzer and cell. Test; if it fails, use a fault-finding checklist (loose clips? cell flat? bare metal touching? switch open?). Improve (e.g. tighter course, a start/finish rest insulated with tape).
- **Check for understanding:** "What went wrong when you first tested it, and how did you track down and fix the fault?" → names a specific fault (e.g. the tape covered the metal so the loop couldn't complete the circuit) and the check that found it
- **Differentiation:** support: a fault-finding flowchart and an adult 'circuit doctor' / stretch: add an LED that lights when the game is switched on, drawn in the circuit diagram
- **Materials:** stiff wire, buzzers, cells, tape, boxes, crocodile clips (adult cuts wire; safety glasses when bending)
- **Joy:** the Year 3 arcade: another class plays the games

### Lesson 67 — Unit review: electrician's apprentice
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can show mastery of circuits, switches, conductors, insulators and electrical safety." (UK NC Y4 Sc)
- **Hook:** "Every electrician starts as an apprentice. Today is your apprenticeship test."
- **Key activity:** Stations: build a circuit from a diagram; predict whether four drawn circuits light; test and classify unknown materials; fix a 'broken' circuit; spot hazards in a picture of a home.
- **Check for understanding:** "Draw a circuit with a switch that lights a bulb, and explain what happens when the switch opens." → a correct diagram with cell, bulb and switch; opening the switch makes a gap, so the loop is broken and the bulb goes out
- **Differentiation:** support: build first, then draw with a symbol card / stretch: design a circuit where two switches must both be closed to light the bulb, and explain where it could be useful (e.g. a safety machine)
- **Materials:** kits, hazard picture, unknown materials
- **Joy:** apprentice certificates with a lightning-bolt stamp

**End-of-unit check:** Build and draw a circuit with a switch; predict whether given circuits will light; classify conductors and insulators; state three electrical safety rules.

## Unit 9 — Year-end: my own investigation

**Essential question:** Can I plan, carry out and report a scientific investigation of my own?

**Key vocabulary:** question, hypothesis, prediction, variable, fair test, method, results, conclusion, evaluation, science fair

### Lesson 68 — Planning my own investigation
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can choose a testable question from this year's topics and plan a fair test independently." (UK NC Y3–4 WS (all))
- **Hook:** "Scientists choose their own questions. What do YOU want to find out?"
- **Key activity:** Children (in pairs) choose from a menu or invent a question linked to the year (e.g. Does the temperature of water affect how fast sugar dissolves? Does the length of an elastic band change its pitch? Which cloth keeps a drink warm longest?). Complete a full plan: question, prediction with reason, variables, equipment, method, risk assessment, results table. Teacher approves each plan.
- **Check for understanding:** "What will you change, what will you measure, and what will you keep the same?" → one variable changed, a measurement named with units, and at least two things kept the same
- **Differentiation:** support: a planning template with examples and a narrower menu of questions / stretch: plan repeats and explain how you'll deal with an odd result
- **Materials:** planning templates, equipment catalogue
- **Joy:** "Scientist's proposal" signed by the teacher as 'funding approved'

### Lesson 69 — Carrying out my investigation
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can carry out my planned investigation, measuring accurately and recording results." (UK NC Y3–4 WS (take accurate measurements; record))
- **Hook:** "Lab day: goggles on, clipboards ready."
- **Key activity:** Pairs carry out their investigations with repeats, recording in their own tables; the teacher circulates asking 'Is it fair? Are you measuring accurately?'. Photos of the set-up for the poster.
- **Check for understanding:** "Show me your results table. Which result are you least sure about, and why?" → identifies a result and a reason (e.g. the timer started late)
- **Differentiation:** support: adult support to read instruments; fewer repeats / stretch: add a second variable test after the first is complete
- **Materials:** equipment per plan, goggles, timers, thermometers, rulers
- **Joy:** "Lab day" with lab coats (old shirts)

### Lesson 70 — Science fair: presenting and evaluating
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can present my investigation with a graph, a conclusion and an evaluation, and question others' work." (UK NC Y3–4 WS (report and present findings; evaluate))
- **Hook:** "Welcome to the Year 3 Science Fair — visitors will ask hard questions!"
- **Key activity:** Pairs make a poster (question, prediction, method, results graph, conclusion, evaluation); present at a science fair to another class and families; visitors ask one 'how do you know?' question each.
- **Check for understanding:** "What did your investigation find, how confident are you, and what would you improve?" → states the conclusion with evidence, a confidence judgement and a specific improvement
- **Differentiation:** support: poster template with sections / stretch: compare your result with published science and explain any difference
- **Materials:** poster paper, printed photos, graphs
- **Joy:** Science Fair with 'Golden Goggles' awards

**End-of-unit check:** Plan, carry out and present an independent fair test with a results table, graph, conclusion and evaluation.

## Books & resources

- ***The Street Beneath My Feet* by Charlotte Guillain and Yuval Zommer** — a fold-out journey down through soil, rock and water to the Earth's core; brilliant for habitats, the water cycle and Earth science curiosity.
- ***Wangari's Trees of Peace* by Jeanette Winter** — a true story of Wangari Maathai and the Green Belt Movement; makes conservation personal and shows an African woman scientist-activist.
- ***A Drop of Water* by Walter Wick** — astonishing photographs of water freezing, evaporating and condensing; the best visual support for the states-of-matter and water-cycle units.
- ***The Way Things Work Now* by David Macaulay** — the classic illustrated explanation of machines and electricity, for curious readers and teacher reference.
- ***DK Eyewitness* titles (e.g. *Electricity*, *Human Body*)** — reliable, photo-rich non-fiction for research and reading in science lessons.
- ***Ada Twist, Scientist* by Andrea Beaty** — a picture book celebrating asking questions and testing ideas; a joyful launch for the 'Being a scientist' unit.
- ***Tiny Creatures: The World of Microbes* by Nicola Davies** — accessible, accurate and beautifully illustrated; extends classification beyond what we can see.
- **Explorify** (free, from the Wellcome Trust) — short thinking activities ("zoom in, zoom out", "odd one out") that sharpen observation and reasoning.
- **PSTT (Primary Science Teaching Trust)** resources and the **Association for Science Education** — evidence-informed investigation ideas and safety guidance.
- **Evelyn Glennie's 'Hearing Essay'** and interviews (on her official website) — her own account of sensing sound through vibration.

## End-of-year mastery checks

1. **Plans a fair test** — names the variable to change, what to measure (with units) and what to keep the same, and explains why repeats matter. *See it:* the independent investigation plan.
2. **Measures and records accurately** — reads thermometers, rulers and timers correctly and records in a clear table. *See it:* results tables from two investigations.
3. **Concludes and evaluates** — writes a conclusion supported by data and suggests an improvement. *See it:* science-fair poster.
4. **Classifies living things** — groups vertebrates and invertebrates by characteristics and uses and designs branching keys. *See it:* identifies four unknown organisms with a key.
5. **Explains food chains and environmental change** — builds food chains with correct arrows and predicts the effect of a change. *See it:* the nature-reserve design.
6. **Explains digestion and teeth** — names the parts of the digestive system and teeth types with their functions. *See it:* 'crumb's journey' labelled diagram.
7. **Explains states of matter** — sorts solids, liquids and gases, uses a particle model, and explains melting, freezing, evaporation and condensation with temperatures. *See it:* particle diagrams and the 'water particle diary'.
8. **Explains the water cycle and measures weather** — labels the cycle and interprets weather data. *See it:* the class weather-station graphs.
9. **Explains sound** — links vibration to sound, pitch to speed of vibration, volume to strength of vibration, and distance to fainter sounds. *See it:* the instrument showcase.
10. **Builds and explains circuits safely** — builds series circuits with switches, predicts whether they work, identifies conductors and insulators, and states safety rules. *See it:* the steady-hand game and apprentice test.

## Teacher guidance

**Every unit investigates.** Children should *do* the science: at least one full fair test per unit, planned and recorded by them. Resist doing demonstrations that children could do themselves.

**Common misconceptions to watch for.**
- *Whales and bats* — children classify by habitat or movement; insist on characteristics (breathing, body covering, young).
- *Spiders are insects* — count legs and body parts.
- *Food-chain arrows point to 'what eats'* — they show the direction energy flows (from the eaten to the eater).
- *Digestion starts in the stomach* — it starts in the mouth (chewing and saliva).
- *Gases are 'nothing'* — weigh the air (the balloon balance).
- *Sand and flour are liquids because they pour* — each grain keeps its shape.
- *Condensation is water leaking through the glass* — use coloured water inside to disprove.
- *Water 'disappears' when it evaporates* — it becomes an invisible gas (water vapour) in the air.
- *Steam is the white cloud above a kettle* — the white cloud is condensed droplets; true steam (water vapour) is invisible.
- *Sound travels through 'nothing'* — sound needs a medium; there is no sound in the vacuum of space.
- *Pitch and volume are the same thing* — pitch is how high or low; volume is how loud or soft.
- *Electricity is 'used up' in the bulb* — at this age, emphasise the complete loop; the idea of current flowing all the way round is developed in Years 5–6.
- *All metals are magnetic / all conductors are metal* — graphite (pencil 'lead') conducts; many metals (aluminium, copper) aren't magnetic.

**Safety.** Only low-voltage cells in class — never mains. Adults handle hot water, glass and cutting of bottles and wire. Check allergies before food investigations. Wash hands after handling soil and minibeasts, and return creatures to where they were found.

**Differentiation.** Support = more concrete: pre-drawn tables, picture planning boards, fewer variables, adult help reading instruments. Stretch = deeper reasoning: explain anomalies, design a second test, link to a real-world application, or evaluate a model's limits.

**Vertical connections.** Builds on Year 2's plants, skeletons and nutrition, rocks and soils, light and magnets. Year 4 builds directly on this year: life cycles and reproduction (using classification), properties and changes of materials (dissolving, reversible and irreversible changes, building on states of matter), Earth and space, and forces (gravity, air and water resistance, friction).
