# Year 3 Mathematics (ages 8–9) — Scope & Sequence

## Year overview

Year 3 is the year the number system opens out and the multiplication tables are **secured to 12 × 12**. It is taught in the Singapore / Maths-No-Problem mastery tradition: every new idea goes **concrete → pictorial → abstract** (base-10, place-value counters, Cuisenaire rods and fraction strips first; bar models and number lines next; symbols last), the whole class moves through the same small steps, and each unit ends with reasoning and problem-solving, not just procedures.

**What this year assumes (from Year 2, ages 7–8):** place value to 1,000; column addition and subtraction of 3-digit numbers; the 2, 3, 4, 5, 8 and 10 tables with related division; 2-digit × 1-digit multiplication using partitioning; unit and non-unit fractions, tenths as fractions, equivalence with diagrams and adding fractions with the same denominator within one whole; length, mass and capacity with simple conversions; perimeter; telling the time to the minute; right angles; bar charts and pictograms. The first unit begins with a diagnostic retrieval lesson, and the tables audit in Lesson 5 tells the teacher which facts need rebuilding.

**How the year sequences.** *Term 1* builds number sense — place value to 10,000 with rounding, negative numbers and Roman numerals; 4-digit column addition and subtraction with estimation and inverse checks; then the big tables unit that introduces the 6, 9, 7, 11 and 12 tables through patterns and derived facts; and length and perimeter in kilometres, metres and centimetres. *Term 2* turns tables into power: formal column multiplication and short division (with remainders interpreted in context), area by counting squares and by rows × columns, and a long fractions unit — equivalence, fractions greater than one, adding and subtracting with the same denominator, fractions of amounts. *Term 3* carries place value past the decimal point — tenths, hundredths, ÷10 and ÷100, rounding and comparing decimals, fraction–decimal equivalents — then applies decimals to money in several currencies, time (12- and 24-hour clocks, unit conversions), angles and shapes, symmetry, coordinates and translation, and line graphs. The year ends with a consolidation unit and the **12 × 12 secure check**.

**Times-tables fluency strand.** Tables are not a single unit. The new tables are *taught* in Term 1 (the 6 table in Lesson 30, then the 9, 7, 11 and 12 tables in Lessons 34–40, each derived from facts children already own: 6 = double 3, 9 = 10 − 1, 7 = 5 + 2, 11 = 10 + 1, 12 = 10 + 2). After that, 16 lessons marked "Fluency:" plus two retrieval checks keep every fact alive with spaced practice (Lessons 5, 30, 35, 40, 48, 56, 61, 66, 73, 79, 86, 95, 105, 115, 124, 135, 144, 149), and every lesson opens with a 5-minute warm-up. The year ends with the **12 × 12 secure check** in Lesson 168. "Secure" means: any fact to 12 × 12 and its division inverses answered correctly within about 4 seconds, with a derivation strategy the child can explain for any fact they momentarily forget.

**What Year 4 (ages 9–10) relies on.** Instant recall of all tables to 12 × 12 (for long multiplication, factors, multiples and primes); place value to 10,000 extended to 1,000,000; decimals to two places as the springboard for thousandths and percentages; confident equivalence and fractions greater than one (for adding fractions with related denominators and multiplying fractions by whole numbers); area by formula for rectangles (for compound shapes and volume); acute/obtuse angles (for measuring with a protractor); first-quadrant coordinates and translation (for reflection).

Lessons run **35–40 minutes**: 5-minute retrieval/fluency warm-up, explicit teaching with manipulatives, guided practice, independent practice, and a check. Examples use many currencies (dollars, euros, rupees, pesos, yen, pounds, shillings) and cities from every continent so the maths belongs to every child.

## Unit 1 — Place value to 10,000

**Essential question:** How does our number system let ten digits write any number, and how can "about" be more useful than "exactly"?

**Key vocabulary:** digit, place value, ones, tens, hundreds, thousands, ten thousands, partition, placeholder, compare, order, greater than, less than, round, nearest, estimate, negative, positive, zero, Roman numerals

### Lesson 1 — Retrieval: numbers to 1,000
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can show a 3-digit number with base-10 and explain what each digit is worth." (UK NC Y3 Npv review)
- **Hook:** "I'm thinking of a number with 7 hundreds, no tens and 4 ones — build it before I finish counting to five."
- **Key activity:** Diagnostic warm-up: children build 704, 740 and 470 with base-10, then compare and order them on a number line 0–1,000. I do one aloud, we do two, you do a set of five on mini-whiteboards; teacher notes who drops the zero placeholder.
- **Check for understanding:** "Tom says 704 and 740 are the same because they use the same digits. Show him with base-10 why he is wrong." → 704 is 7 hundreds + 4 ones; 740 is 7 hundreds + 4 tens, so 740 is 36 more
- **Differentiation:** support: keep a place-value mat with H | T | O columns and build every number before writing it / stretch: make the largest and smallest 3-digit numbers from 0, 5, 9 and explain why 0 cannot lead
- **Materials:** base-10 blocks, place-value mats, 0–1,000 number line, mini-whiteboards
- **Joy:** "Number detective": the teacher hides a number in a sealed envelope and gives place-value clues until the class cracks it

### Lesson 2 — Meet 1,000 and 10,000
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can explain that 10 hundreds make 1 thousand and 10 thousands make 10,000." (UK NC Y4 Npv; CCSS 4.NBT.1)
- **Hook:** "How many hundred-flats would we need to make a stack as tall as ten thousand?"
- **Key activity:** Build a thousand-cube from ten hundred-flats; count on in thousands with thousand-cubes to 10,000 and show 10,000 as ten thousand-cubes. Pictorially: bundles on a Th | H | T | O chart. Abstractly: skip-count 1,000, 2,000 … 10,000 forwards and backwards.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why is 10,000 written with four zeros?" → it has 1 ten-thousand and 0 thousands, 0 hundreds, 0 tens, 0 ones — every place must be filled
- **Differentiation:** support: physically exchange 10 flats for 1 cube before recording / stretch: how many hundreds are in 10,000? (100) Explain with the exchange chain
- **Materials:** base-10 thousand-cubes (or card copies), hundred-flats, place-value chart to ten thousands
- **Joy:** build a "10,000 tower" as a class from 10 thousand-cubes and photograph it

### Lesson 3 — Four-digit numbers: read, write, build
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can read, write and build any 4-digit number and say the value of each digit." (UK NC Y4 Npv; CCSS 4.NBT.2)
- **Hook:** "Mount Kilimanjaro is 5,895 metres tall — what does the 5 at the front mean, and the 5 at the end?"
- **Key activity:** Build numbers with place-value counters (1,000 / 100 / 10 / 1) on a Th | H | T | O grid; write in digits and words. We do 3,482 and 5,895; you do a card set including 6,019 and 4,300.
- **Check for understanding:** "In 5,895, the two 5s look the same. Explain why they are worth different amounts." → the first 5 is 5 thousands (5,000); the last is 5 ones — its column decides its value
- **Differentiation:** support: counters placed first, then digits written underneath each column / stretch: write the number that is 1,000 more and 100 less than 9,050 in one step
- **Materials:** place-value counters, Th | H | T | O grids, digit cards
- **Joy:** "Mountain cards": build the heights of mountains from six continents

### Lesson 4 — Zero as a placeholder
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can read and write 4-digit numbers containing zeros without losing a place." (UK NC Y4 Npv)
- **Hook:** "Someone wrote 'three thousand and forty-seven' as 347. What went wrong?"
- **Key activity:** Build 3,047, 3,407 and 3,470 with counters, leaving the empty column visible; record with a 0. Dictation of 8 numbers with zeros; children self-check on the grid.
- **Check for understanding:** "Write two thousand and six. Why do you need two zeros?" → 2,006 — the hundreds and tens places are empty, and zeros hold them so the 2 stays in the thousands
- **Differentiation:** support: grid with column headings; place a 'zero card' in every empty column / stretch: write in words a number with zeros in different places and swap with a partner for decoding
- **Materials:** place-value counters, grids, 'zero' cards
- **Joy:** "Broken calculator": the 0 key is sticky — children spot which displays are wrong

### Lesson 5 — Fluency: tables audit (2, 3, 4, 5, 8, 10)
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can recall the Year 2 tables and their division facts and find which facts I still need." (UK NC Y3 Nmd; CCSS 3.OA.7)
- **Hook:** "There are 60 facts in these six tables — but only 21 of them are really new if you know the tricks. Let's find out which ones you own."
- **Key activity:** Two-minute untimed recall grid of 2, 3, 4, 5, 8, 10 facts; children colour green (instant), amber (worked out), red (don't know). Teacher models turning an amber fact into a derived fact (8 × 7 = double 4 × 7). Children make a personal 'fact target' card.
- **Check for understanding:** "You forgot 4 × 8. Show two ways you could work it out from facts you know." → double 4 × 4 (16 → 32); or 8 × 4 = 4 × 8 by commutativity; or 5 × 8 − 8 = 32
- **Differentiation:** support: use an arrays card or counters to rebuild any red fact before recording it / stretch: explain why every fact in the 4 table is double the 2 table and every 8 fact double the 4
- **Materials:** multiplication grid (blank), coloured pencils, counters, fact target cards
- **Joy:** personal fact-target card that fills with stickers as facts turn green over the year

### Lesson 6 — Partition four-digit numbers in many ways
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can partition 4-digit numbers in standard and non-standard ways." (UK NC Y4 Npv; CCSS 4.NBT.2)
- **Hook:** "Can 4,256 be made with only hundreds and ones?"
- **Key activity:** Standard partition (4,000 + 200 + 50 + 6) then flexible partitions (42 hundreds + 56 ones; 3,000 + 1,200 + 56) with counters and part–whole models. You do: three partitions of 6,340 on a part–whole diagram.
- **Check for understanding:** "Is 5,000 + 13 hundreds + 4 the same number as 6,304? Explain." → yes: 13 hundreds = 1,300, and 5,000 + 1,300 + 4 = 6,304
- **Differentiation:** support: partition only in the standard way, using counters on the grid / stretch: find all the ways to make 2,000 using only thousands and hundreds counters
- **Materials:** place-value counters, part–whole mats, bar strips
- **Joy:** "Split the treasure": pirates must share out 4,256 gold coins in as many ways as possible

### Lesson 7 — Number lines to 10,000
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can place and estimate 4-digit numbers on number lines with different scales." (UK NC Y4 Npv)
- **Hook:** "A marathon is about 42,000 metres — where would 7,500 m be on a 0–10,000 m line?"
- **Key activity:** Mark 0–10,000 lines in thousands; place given numbers exactly, then estimate unlabelled arrows. Repeat on a 3,000–4,000 line marked in hundreds. Explain using halfway points.
- **Check for understanding:** "An arrow sits exactly halfway between 6,000 and 7,000. What number is it and how do you know?" → 6,500 — halfway between 6,000 and 7,000 is 500 more than 6,000
- **Differentiation:** support: use a line with all intervals labelled first / stretch: estimate an unmarked arrow on a 0–10,000 line to the nearest 100 and justify
- **Materials:** metre-stick / washing-line number line, number cards, pegs, printed lines
- **Joy:** "Washing-line walk": pegging number cards on a class line strung across the room

### Lesson 8 — Compare and order four-digit numbers
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can compare and order 4-digit numbers using place value and the symbols <, > and =." (UK NC Y4 Npv; CCSS 4.NBT.2)
- **Hook:** "Which is the taller building: 8,029 cm or 8,209 cm?"
- **Key activity:** Build both with counters; compare from the highest place until a place differs. Order river lengths, city populations in thousands and mountain heights. Record with < and >.
- **Check for understanding:** "Mia says 999 is bigger than 1,001 because 9 is bigger than 1. Which place should she compare first, and why?" → first count the places: 1,001 has thousands and 999 has none, so 1,001 is bigger
- **Differentiation:** support: line up numbers in a place-value grid before comparing / stretch: make 4-digit numbers from given digit cards so that a true statement ☐ < ☐ < ☐ has exactly one solution
- **Materials:** place-value counters, grids, digit cards, symbol cards
- **Joy:** "Higher or lower" card game with 4-digit numbers

### Lesson 9 — 1, 10, 100 and 1,000 more or less
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can find 1, 10, 100 or 1,000 more or less than a number, including across boundaries." (UK NC Y4 Npv)
- **Hook:** "The odometer says 4,999 km. What will it say after 1 more kilometre? After 100?"
- **Key activity:** Use counters to add or remove one counter in a place, then exchange when crossing a boundary (4,990 + 10 = 5,000). Pattern chains: 3,847 → +100 → +100 …
- **Check for understanding:** "What is 100 less than 6,050? Explain what happens to the hundreds." → 5,950 — there are 0 hundreds, so exchange 1 thousand for 10 hundreds, then take 1 away
- **Differentiation:** support: counters and physical exchange / stretch: create a chain of five jumps (mix of ±10, ±100, ±1,000) that returns to the start
- **Materials:** place-value counters, odometer picture strips
- **Joy:** "Odometer race": roll a die to decide the jump and race to 10,000

### Lesson 10 — Count in 6s, 7s, 9s, 25s and 1,000s
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can count forwards and backwards in 6s, 7s, 9s, 25s and 1,000s and spot patterns." (UK NC Y4 Npv / Nmd)
- **Hook:** "A quarter of a dollar is 25 cents, and four quarters make a dollar. Let's count in 25s all the way to 1,000."
- **Key activity:** Count in 25s on a 100 square and on a counting stick (25, 50, 75, 100 …); in 1,000s past 10,000; in 6s and 9s using the 3 table as a helper. Children fill missing numbers in sequences, including backwards through zero for 6s.
- **Check for understanding:** "Is 250 in the count of 25s? Is 245? Explain." → 250 yes (10 × 25); 245 no — counts of 25 from 0 always end in 00, 25, 50 or 75
- **Differentiation:** support: counting stick with multiples written on; count aloud in unison / stretch: describe a rule for every sequence and continue one that crosses 10,000
- **Materials:** counting stick, 100 squares, sequence cards
- **Joy:** "Clap and snap": the class counts in 7s, clapping on each multiple and snapping on each multiple of 14

### Lesson 11 — Rounding to the nearest 10
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can round any number to the nearest 10 using a number line." (UK NC Y4 Npv; CCSS 3.NBT.1)
- **Hook:** "A newspaper says 'about 70 people came'. Could 74 people have come? 76?"
- **Key activity:** Place a number on a 10-interval number line, find the two multiples of 10 it sits between, and choose the nearer. Discuss the halfway rule: 5 rounds up by convention. Move to 4-digit numbers (3,746 → 3,750).
- **Check for understanding:** "Why does 45 round to 50 but 44 round to 40?" → 44 is nearer 40; 45 is exactly halfway and the convention is to round halfway up
- **Differentiation:** support: a number line drawn for every question / stretch: list all whole numbers that round to 3,750 to the nearest 10
- **Materials:** number lines, counting stick, rounding 'hills' picture
- **Joy:** "Roll over the hill": a toy car on a paper hill rolls to the nearer ten

### Lesson 12 — Rounding to the nearest 100 and 1,000
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can round 4-digit numbers to the nearest 100 and 1,000 and say which digit decides." (UK NC Y4 Npv; CCSS 4.NBT.3)
- **Hook:** "The distance from Nairobi to Mombasa is 482 km. A sign says 'about 500 km'. Another says 'about 480 km'. Which is right?"
- **Key activity:** On number lines, round 2,749 to the nearest 100 (2,700) and nearest 1,000 (3,000). Identify the 'decider' digit — the one to the right of the rounding place. Practise mixed rounding tables.
- **Check for understanding:** "Round 4,950 to the nearest 100 and the nearest 1,000. Why are both answers different from what many children write?" → 5,000 to the nearest 100 (it crosses into the next thousand) and 5,000 to the nearest 1,000
- **Differentiation:** support: number line with only the two multiples marked / stretch: find a number that rounds to 3,000 to the nearest 1,000 but 2,500 to the nearest 100
- **Materials:** number lines, place-value grids, highlighters for the decider digit
- **Joy:** "Both signs are right": children write their own 'about' road signs for real distances between cities

### Lesson 13 — Estimation and 'about'
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can use rounding to estimate and decide whether an answer is reasonable." (UK NC Y4 Npv / Nas)
- **Hook:** "A shop says a bike costs 1,987 rupees and a helmet 1,012. Roughly how much for both?"
- **Key activity:** Round each number to the nearest 100 or 1,000 before calculating; compare estimate with an exact answer given by the teacher. Sort statements into 'sensible estimate' and 'not sensible'.
- **Check for understanding:** "Leo says 3,012 + 2,965 is about 50,000. Use rounding to show why he is wrong." → 3,000 + 3,000 = 6,000, so the answer is about 6,000, not 50,000
- **Differentiation:** support: round only to the nearest 1,000 with a number line / stretch: decide when rounding to 100 gives a better estimate than rounding to 1,000, with an example
- **Materials:** price cards in several currencies, number lines
- **Joy:** "Too high, too low?": estimate the number of beans in a jar using a sample of 100, then reveal

### Lesson 14 — Negative numbers
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can count through zero and order negative and positive numbers in context." (UK NC Y4 Npv)
- **Hook:** "In Ulaanbaatar, Mongolia, a winter morning can be −25 °C. What does the minus sign tell us?"
- **Key activity:** Vertical thermometer and horizontal number line from −20 to 20. Count backwards through zero; place temperatures; lift floors below ground (−1, −2). Order sets including negatives.
- **Check for understanding:** "Which is colder, −3 °C or −8 °C, and why is 8 'bigger' but colder?" → −8 °C: it is further below zero, so it is the smaller (colder) number
- **Differentiation:** support: use a large vertical thermometer and move a peg / stretch: find the difference between −4 °C and 6 °C using the number line (10 degrees)
- **Materials:** vertical thermometer model, −20 to 20 number line, pegs, lift floor cards
- **Joy:** "Lift operator": children call floors −3 to 10 and the class moves the lift

### Lesson 15 — Roman numerals to 100
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can read and write Roman numerals to 100 and explain how the system uses adding and subtracting." (UK NC Y4 Npv)
- **Hook:** "Why does the clock on the wall say IV for 4 but some clocks say IIII?"
- **Key activity:** Symbols I, V, X, L, C. Adding rule (VI = 6, XX = 20); subtractive pairs (IV, IX, XL, XC). Build numbers with lolly sticks; convert both ways. Note that Romans had no zero and that the system was used across Europe, North Africa and West Asia.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why can't 49 be written as IL?" → in the standard system only I before V or X, X before L or C, and C before D or M; 49 = XLIX (40 + 9)
- **Differentiation:** support: a symbol card on the desk with the 6 subtractive pairs / stretch: write 1 to 100 rules for when a symbol can repeat (at most three times) and test them
- **Materials:** lolly sticks, Roman-numeral cards, a real clock with Roman numerals
- **Joy:** "Secret Roman code": decode a treasure message where page numbers are in Roman numerals

### Lesson 16 — Unit review and problem-solving
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can solve place-value puzzles and explain my reasoning." (UK NC Y4 Npv)
- **Hook:** "Only four clues: my number has 4 digits, rounds to 6,000, has a 0 tens and digits that add to 12."
- **Key activity:** Mixed retrieval (partition, compare, round, negatives, Roman). Then a reasoning puzzle set: 'always, sometimes, never' statements (e.g. "a number with more digits is always bigger" — always, for whole numbers).
- **Check for understanding:** "Always, sometimes or never: rounding to the nearest 100 makes a number bigger. Explain with examples." → sometimes — 3,470 → 3,500 gets bigger; 3,420 → 3,400 gets smaller
- **Differentiation:** support: solve clue puzzles with counters and a grid to test each clue / stretch: write a clue puzzle with exactly one answer for a partner
- **Materials:** place-value counters, mini-whiteboards, clue cards
- **Joy:** "Mystery number" puzzle trail around the room

**End-of-unit check:** Build and partition 6,208 two ways; round 4,951 to the nearest 10, 100 and 1,000; put −6, 2, −1, 0 in order; write 49 in Roman numerals; explain why 1,001 > 999.

## Unit 2 — Addition and subtraction to 10,000

**Essential question:** How can I add and subtract big numbers accurately — and know my answer makes sense?

**Key vocabulary:** column method, exchange, regroup, add, subtract, sum, difference, inverse, estimate, check, bar model, part, whole, compare

### Lesson 17 — Retrieval: mental add and subtract
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can add and subtract 1s, 10s, 100s and 1,000s mentally without writing a column." (UK NC Y4 Nas)
- **Hook:** "5,400 + 300 — do we need a column method, or can your brain do it?"
- **Key activity:** Warm up with number bonds to 100 and 1,000 (65 + 35; 650 + 350). Then 'which place changes?' mental additions (4,256 + 30; 7,090 − 1,000). Children sort calculations into 'mental' or 'written'.
- **Check for understanding:** "Explain why 3,999 + 1 changes every digit but 3,999 + 1,000 changes only one." → adding 1 causes exchanges in every place; adding 1,000 adds to the thousands only (4,999)
- **Differentiation:** support: place-value counters to act out each change / stretch: make up three calculations that look hard but are easy mentally
- **Materials:** place-value counters, number bond cards
- **Joy:** "Mental or written?" team sorting race

### Lesson 18 — Column addition, no exchange
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can add two 4-digit numbers in columns and explain what each column means." (UK NC Y4 Nas; CCSS 4.NBT.4)
- **Hook:** "Two libraries in Lagos have 3,214 and 2,543 books. How many altogether?"
- **Key activity:** Represent both with place-value counters on a grid, combine column by column from the ones; record the column method beside the counters. Estimate first (3,000 + 3,000).
- **Check for understanding:** "Why do we line up the digits by the right-hand side?" → so ones add to ones, tens to tens etc.; lining up left would add thousands to ones in numbers of different lengths
- **Differentiation:** support: counters alongside every written step / stretch: 4-digit + 3-digit additions where lining up is the trap
- **Materials:** place-value counters, squared paper, grids
- **Joy:** "Library merger": combine real book counts from two class-created libraries

### Lesson 19 — Column addition with one exchange
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can add 4-digit numbers with one exchange and record the exchange correctly." (UK NC Y4 Nas; CCSS 4.NBT.4)
- **Hook:** "The ones make 14. Where do the extra 10 ones go?"
- **Key activity:** With counters, exchange ten 1s for one 10 and move it to the tens column; mirror in the written column with a small exchanged digit under the line. Then exchanges in tens and hundreds.
- **Check for understanding:** "In 2,457 + 1,326 someone wrote 3,7713. What happened?" → they wrote 13 in the ones place instead of exchanging; correct answer 3,783
- **Differentiation:** support: physical exchange with counters for each question / stretch: decide which pairs from a list will need an exchange without calculating
- **Materials:** place-value counters, squared paper
- **Joy:** "Exchange bank": one child is the banker who swaps ten counters for one

### Lesson 20 — Column addition with several exchanges
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can add 4-digit numbers with exchanges in more than one column." (UK NC Y4 Nas; CCSS 4.NBT.4)
- **Hook:** "What's the biggest total you can make by adding two 4-digit numbers? Will it still have 4 digits?"
- **Key activity:** Model 3,679 + 2,856 with counters, exchanging in ones, tens and hundreds; record. You do a graded set. Discuss 9,999 + 9,999 = 19,998 (five digits).
- **Check for understanding:** "Estimate 4,896 + 3,217 and then calculate. How does the estimate help you check?" → about 8,000 (5,000 + 3,000); exact 8,113 — close to the estimate, so reasonable
- **Differentiation:** support: additions with exchanges in only two columns, counters available / stretch: find two 4-digit numbers whose total is 10,000 with exchanges in every column
- **Materials:** place-value counters, squared paper
- **Joy:** "Digit dash": arrange digit cards to make the total closest to 10,000

### Lesson 21 — Column subtraction, no exchange
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can subtract 4-digit numbers in columns and explain each step." (UK NC Y4 Nas; CCSS 4.NBT.4)
- **Hook:** "A flight from Cairo to Cape Town is 7,238 km; a plane has flown 4,126 km. How far to go?"
- **Key activity:** Build the larger number only with counters, take away the smaller column by column; record the column method alongside. Link to the bar model (whole 7,238; one part 4,126).
- **Check for understanding:** "Why do we only build the first number when subtracting with counters?" → subtraction (take-away) starts with the whole and removes a part
- **Differentiation:** support: counters for every step / stretch: write a subtraction whose difference is 1,111 using only digits 2–9
- **Materials:** place-value counters, bar-model strips
- **Joy:** "Flight tracker": children update the 'distance to go' board for a pretend flight

### Lesson 22 — Column subtraction with exchange
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can subtract 4-digit numbers exchanging from the next column when needed." (UK NC Y4 Nas; CCSS 4.NBT.4)
- **Hook:** "Can you take 8 ones from 3 ones? What can we borrow from the neighbours?"
- **Key activity:** With counters, exchange 1 ten for 10 ones, then subtract. Record by crossing out and writing the new values. Extend to exchanges in two columns. Children say, "I can't take 8 from 3, so I exchange one ten for ten ones."
- **Check for understanding:** "Sam did 5,432 − 1,718 by taking the smaller digit from the bigger in each column and got 4,286. Explain the error." → he did 8 − 2 instead of exchanging; correct answer 3,714
- **Differentiation:** support: counters with every exchange physically made / stretch: subtractions with exchanges in three columns
- **Materials:** place-value counters, squared paper
- **Joy:** "Error doctor": children diagnose and fix the 'patient' calculations

### Lesson 23 — Subtraction across zeros
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can subtract from numbers with zeros such as 4,000 or 6,005." (UK NC Y4 Nas)
- **Hook:** "4,000 − 1 = ? Easy. So why is 4,000 − 1,357 so tricky on paper?"
- **Key activity:** Model the chain exchange with counters (1 thousand → 10 hundreds → 1 hundred to 10 tens → 1 ten to 10 ones). Compare with a mental strategy: 4,000 − 1,357 = 3,999 − 1,356 = 2,643 (subtract 1 from both).
- **Check for understanding:** "Show two ways to calculate 5,000 − 2,468 and explain which you prefer." → 2,532 — by chain exchange or by 4,999 − 2,467 + 1
- **Differentiation:** support: start with 1,000 − 368 using counters / stretch: explain why taking 1 from both numbers keeps the difference the same, using a bar model
- **Materials:** place-value counters, bar strips, number lines
- **Joy:** "Zero trap" challenge cards with a secret 'easy way' reveal

### Lesson 24 — Inverse operations and checking

### Lesson 32 — Multiplication as equal groups and arrays

### Lesson 33 — Multiply and divide by 1 and 0

### Lesson 34 — The 9 table

## Unit 6 — Area

**Essential question:** How much surface does a shape cover, and how is area different from perimeter?

### Lesson 81 — What is area?

### Lesson 84 — Area of rectilinear shapes

### Lesson 85 — Area and perimeter: not the same

### Lesson 87 — Square metres and estimating
- **Duration:** 40 min

### Lesson 88 — Unit review: zoo designer

## Unit 7 — Fractions

**Essential question:** How can the same amount have many fraction names, and how do fractions help us share, compare and measure?

**Key vocabulary:** fraction, numerator, denominator, unit fraction, non-unit fraction, equivalent, simplify, common factor, whole, improper fraction, mixed number, fraction of an amount, tenths, hundredths

### Lesson 89 — Retrieval: what makes a fraction
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can explain a fraction as equal parts of a whole and name the numerator and denominator." (UK NC Y3 Nf review; CCSS 3.NF.1)
- **Hook:** "This chocolate bar is snapped into 4 pieces — are they quarters? Look closely!"
- **Key activity:** Fold paper strips into halves, quarters and eighths; compare with unequal splits. Name numerator (how many parts we have) and denominator (how many equal parts in the whole). Children sort shape cards into 'equal parts' and 'not equal parts' and name the fractions.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why is a circle cut into 4 unequal pieces not split into quarters?" → quarters must be 4 equal parts of the whole
- **Differentiation:** support: fold and label paper strips / stretch: explain why ⅕ is smaller than ¼ using two identical strips
- **Materials:** paper strips, fraction strips, shape cards
- **Joy:** "Fair share judge": the class votes on each 'chocolate bar' split

### Lesson 90 — Fractions on a number line
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can place fractions, including those beyond 1, on a number line." (UK NC Y4 Nf; CCSS 3.NF.2)
- **Hook:** "Where does ¾ live between 0 and 1? And where does 5/4 live?"
- **Key activity:** Concrete: fraction strips laid along a metre stick. Pictorial: 0–1 and 0–2 lines divided into equal parts; count in quarters and eighths aloud. Abstract: label arrows and place fractions.
- **Check for understanding:** "Where is 5/4 on a 0–2 number line, and how do you know?" → one quarter past 1, because 4/4 = 1 and one more quarter is 5/4
- **Differentiation:** support: number lines already divided into the right parts / stretch: place ⅔, 5/6 and 7/6 on one 0–2 line and justify
- **Materials:** fraction strips, metre stick, number lines
- **Joy:** "Fraction frog": a frog hops along a floor number line in fraction jumps

### Lesson 91 — Equivalent fractions on a fraction wall
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can find equivalent fractions using a fraction wall." (UK NC Y4 Nf; CCSS 4.NF.1)
- **Hook:** "Would you rather have ½ of a pizza or 4/8 of the same pizza?"
- **Key activity:** Build a fraction wall from strips (1, ½, ⅓, ¼, ⅙, ⅛, 1/12); find all fractions lining up with ½, ⅓ and ¾. Record chains: ½ = 2/4 = 3/6 = 4/8 = 6/12.
- **Check for understanding:** "How does the fraction wall prove ½ = 4/8?" → the ½ bar lines up exactly with four ⅛ bars — same length, same amount
- **Differentiation:** support: physical strips to line up / stretch: find every fraction on the wall equal to ⅔ and explain why ⅝ isn't one
- **Materials:** fraction walls, coloured strips
- **Joy:** "Pizza party": players claim equivalent slices

### Lesson 92 — Equivalent fractions by multiplying
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can make equivalent fractions by multiplying numerator and denominator by the same number." (UK NC Y4 Nf; CCSS 4.NF.1)
- **Hook:** "There is a secret rule hiding in the fraction wall — let's find it."
- **Key activity:** Area models: draw ⅔, then cut each part into 3 → 6/9; into 4 → 8/12. Pattern: numerator and denominator are both multiplied by the same number. Record in a 'fraction family'.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why must you multiply both the numerator and denominator by the same number?" → splitting every part into n pieces makes n times as many parts in the whole and n times as many shaded — the amount stays the same
- **Differentiation:** support: fold paper to show the splitting before recording / stretch: generate three equivalents for 3/5 and explain why 3/5 = 5/7 is false
- **Materials:** paper for folding, squared paper, fraction strips
- **Joy:** "Fraction family trees" wall display

### Lesson 93 — Simplifying fractions
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can simplify fractions by dividing numerator and denominator by a common factor." (UK NC Y4 Nf)
- **Hook:** "8/12 — can we say the same amount with smaller numbers?"
- **Key activity:** Group fraction strips (put 12ths into groups of 4 → thirds). Divide numerator and denominator by a common factor; keep going until no common factor except 1. Link to factor pairs from Unit 3.
- **Check for understanding:** "Simplify 6/9. How do you know the amount hasn't changed?" → ⅔ — dividing both by 3 groups every 3 ninths into one third
- **Differentiation:** support: strips to regroup, starting with halves / stretch: simplify 24/36 in one step using the highest common factor (12)
- **Materials:** fraction strips, factor-pair lists
- **Joy:** "Fraction slim-down": simplify to reach the 'lightest' name

### Lesson 94 — Improper fractions and mixed numbers
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can convert between improper fractions and mixed numbers." (UK NC Y4 Nf)
- **Hook:** "Seven quarters of pizza were eaten at the party. How many whole pizzas is that?"
- **Key activity:** Concrete: fraction circles — make 7/4, group 4 quarters into a whole → 1¾. Pictorial: bar model of wholes. Abstract: 7/4 = 1¾; 11/3 = 3⅔; convert back (2⅖ = 12/5).
- **Check for understanding:** "Explain why 7/4 = 1¾ using a drawing." → 4 quarters make one whole, and 3 quarters are left over
- **Differentiation:** support: fraction circles for every example / stretch: convert 23/5 and 4⅞ both ways without drawings, explaining with division
- **Materials:** fraction circles, bar strips
- **Joy:** "Pizza shop": fill orders written as improper fractions

### Lesson 95 — Fluency: tables power equivalence
- **Duration:** 40 min

- **Hook:** "Every times table is a machine for making equivalent fractions."

- **Check for understanding:** "Use the grid to find the fraction equal to ¾ with denominator 12, and explain." → 9/12 — in the 3 and 4 rows, 9 sits above 12 in the same column

### Lesson 96 — Compare and order fractions
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can compare and order fractions with the same denominator or the same numerator." (UK NC Y4 Nf; CCSS 3.NF.3)
- **Hook:** "Would you rather have ⅓ or ⅕ of your favourite cake?"
- **Key activity:** Strips: same denominator (3/8 < 5/8 — more of the same size piece); same numerator (⅓ > ⅕ — fewer, bigger pieces). Order sets on a number line.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why is ⅓ bigger than ⅕ even though 5 is bigger than 3?" → the whole is cut into fewer pieces, so each third is bigger than each fifth
- **Differentiation:** support: use strips for every comparison / stretch: order ¾, ⅝ and ⅔ using equivalence (18/24, 15/24, 16/24)

### Lesson 97 — Add fractions with the same denominator
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can add fractions with the same denominator, including totals greater than 1." (UK NC Y4 Nf; CCSS 4.NF.3)
- **Hook:** "⅜ + ⅜ — is that 6/16?"
- **Key activity:** Strips: 3 eighths + 3 eighths = 6 eighths. The denominator names the size of the piece and doesn't change. Totals over 1: ⅝ + ⅝ = 10/8 = 1 2/8 = 1¼.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why is ⅜ + ⅜ not 6/16?" → we still have eighths — we are counting 6 of them; adding doesn't make pieces smaller
- **Differentiation:** support: strips for every sum / stretch: find three different pairs of eighths that total 1¼
- **Materials:** fraction strips, number lines
- **Joy:** "Recipe mixer": combine fractional ingredient cups

### Lesson 98 — Subtract fractions with the same denominator
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can subtract fractions with the same denominator, including from 1." (UK NC Y4 Nf; CCSS 4.NF.3)
- **Hook:** "⅞ of the flatbread is left and your brother eats ⅜. What's left?"
- **Key activity:** Take away on strips; count back on a number line. Subtract from 1 by renaming 1 as 8/8.
- **Check for understanding:** "Explain how to calculate 1 − ⅜." → 1 = 8/8, so 8/8 − 3/8 = 5/8

### Lesson 99 — Add and subtract mixed numbers
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can add and subtract mixed numbers with the same denominator." (UK NC Y4 Nf)
- **Hook:** "A recipe needs 2¾ cups of flour and 1½ … wait, ½ = 2/4. Can we add them?"

### Lesson 100 — Unit fractions of an amount
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can find a unit fraction of an amount by dividing." (UK NC Y4 Nf)

- **Check for understanding:** "Why do we divide by 5 to find ⅕ of something?" → ⅕ means one of 5 equal parts, and division makes equal parts
- **Differentiation:** support: counters shared physically / stretch: find ⅕ of 350 and ⅙ of 420 and explain using tables facts

- **Joy:** "Marble share" with real marbles

### Lesson 101 — Non-unit fractions of an amount

### Lesson 102 — Find the whole from a part

## Unit 8 — Decimals: tenths and hundredths

**Essential question:** How does place value continue to the right of the ones — and why do decimals and fractions describe the same amounts?

**Key vocabulary:** decimal, decimal point, tenth, hundredth, place value, divide by 10, divide by 100, equivalent, compare, order, round, whole number, half, quarter

### Lesson 109 — Tenths as decimals
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can write tenths as decimals and explain the decimal point." (UK NC Y4 Nf; CCSS 4.NF.6)
- **Hook:** "A sprinter's time shows 9.8 seconds. What does the .8 mean?"
- **Key activity:** Concrete: a base-10 rod as 1 whole cut into 10 cubes (each 0.1); place-value counters labelled 0.1. Pictorial: a place-value chart O . Tenths; a 0–1 number line in tenths. Abstract: 3/10 = 0.3; 1 7/10 = 1.7.
- **Check for understanding:** "Explain why 0.3 and 3/10 are the same amount." → both mean 3 of 10 equal parts of one whole; the decimal point marks the start of the tenths place
- **Differentiation:** support: number line in tenths with the fractions written under the decimals / stretch: write 23 tenths as a decimal (2.3) and explain the exchange
- **Materials:** base-10 rods and cubes, 0.1 counters, place-value charts, number lines

### Lesson 110 — Tenths on a number line
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can count in tenths past 1 and place decimals with one decimal place on a number line." (UK NC Y4 Nf)
- **Hook:** "The rain gauge shows 2.6 cm. Where is that between 2 and 3?"

### Lesson 111 — Hundredths as decimals
- **Duration:** 40 min

- **Hook:** "One cent is a hundredth of a dollar. How do we write 7 cents in dollars?"

## Unit 10 — Geometry: angles, shapes and symmetry

**Essential question:** How do angles, sides and lines of symmetry help us describe, sort and build shapes?

**Key vocabulary:** angle, right angle, acute, obtuse, straight line, turn, degree, parallel, perpendicular, polygon, regular, irregular, triangle, isosceles, equilateral, scalene, right-angled, quadrilateral, square, rectangle, rhombus, parallelogram, trapezium, kite, line of symmetry, symmetric

### Lesson 139 — Angles as turns
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can describe angles as amounts of turn and compare them with a right angle." (UK NC Y4 Gp; CCSS 4.MD.5)
- **Hook:** "A dancer spins a quarter turn, a half turn, a whole turn — what shapes do their arms make?"
- **Key activity:** Concrete: children turn their bodies (quarter, half, three-quarter, full turns); make angles with two geostrips and a split pin; compare with a right-angle tester made by folding paper twice. Pictorial: sketch angles and mark right angles with a square. Abstract: name quarter turn = 1 right angle, half turn = 2 right angles (a straight line).
- **Check for understanding:** "Is the angle bigger when the arms are longer? Explain with the geostrips." → no — the size of the angle is the amount of turn between the arms, not their length
- **Differentiation:** support: use the folded right-angle tester on every angle / stretch: find how many right angles make three-quarters of a turn and a full turn (3 and 4) and connect to compass points
- **Materials:** geostrips, split pins, paper for right-angle testers
- **Joy:** "Robot dance": children program a partner with turn instructions to music

### Lesson 140 — Acute and obtuse angles
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can identify, compare and order acute, obtuse and right angles." (UK NC Y4 Gp; CCSS 4.G.1)
- **Hook:** "Hunt the angles: the classroom is full of them — doors, books, scissors, clock hands."
- **Key activity:** Angle hunt with right-angle testers, recording acute (less than a right angle), right, obtuse (between a right angle and a straight line). Order angle cards from smallest to largest by overlaying tracing paper.
- **Check for understanding:** "Is the angle between the clock hands at 4 o'clock acute or obtuse? Explain." → obtuse — at 3 o'clock it's a right angle; at 4 the minute hand has turned further, so it's more than a right angle but less than a straight line
- **Differentiation:** support: compare every angle with the right-angle tester / stretch: draw a shape with exactly two acute and two obtuse angles and prove it with the tester
- **Materials:** right-angle testers, angle cards, tracing paper, a geared clock
- **Joy:** "Angle safari" photo hunt around school

- **Hook:** "Six weeks until the real 12 × 12 secure check — let's see where we are."

- **Check for understanding:** "Which three facts are you still slow at, and what is your rebuild strategy for each?" → child names facts and a strategy, e.g. 7 × 8 = 7 × 4 doubled = 56
- **Differentiation:** support: smaller check limited to the child's weakest tables / stretch: include related facts (0.7 × 8, 70 × 8)
- **Materials:** timer, fact cards, target cards
- **Joy:** progress stickers on the target card

### Lesson 150 — Unit review: geometry
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can show mastery of angles, shapes and symmetry." (UK NC Y4 Gp)
- **Hook:** "Shape gallery: curate an exhibition of shapes with labels explaining their properties."
- **Key activity:** Mixed mastery set, then each child makes a labelled 'exhibit' card for one shape.
- **Check for understanding:** "Order an acute, a right and an obtuse angle, and explain how you know." → acute < right < obtuse, compared with a right-angle tester
- **Differentiation:** support: sort and label with property cards / stretch: design a shape with given properties (one line of symmetry, two obtuse angles)
- **Materials:** shape cards, right-angle testers, mirrors
- **Joy:** "Shape gallery" exhibition

**End-of-unit check:** Order acute, right and obtuse angles; classify a set of triangles and quadrilaterals by properties; complete a symmetric figure across a vertical line; explain why every square is a rectangle but not every rectangle is a square.

## Unit 11 — Position and direction

**Essential question:** How can two numbers pinpoint any place on a grid, and how do shapes move without changing?

**Key vocabulary:** coordinate, x-axis, y-axis, origin, plot, grid, first quadrant, translation, left, right, up, down, vertex, polygon

### Lesson 151 — Describe position on a grid
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can describe position on a grid using coordinates in the first quadrant." (UK NC Y4 Gpd; CCSS 5.G.1)
- **Hook:** "Battleships! How can you tell a friend exactly where your ship is hidden?"
- **Key activity:** Floor grid with masking tape: children stand at points; teacher introduces (across, up) — 'along the corridor, then up the stairs'. Record points as (x, y).
- **Check for understanding:** "Why is (2, 5) a different point from (5, 2)?" → the first number is always across (x), the second up (y), so they go to different places
- **Differentiation:** support: floor grid with arrows labelled 'first across' and 'then up' / stretch: describe the position of the midpoint between (2, 3) and (6, 3) (4, 3)
- **Materials:** masking-tape floor grid, coordinate cards
- **Joy:** "Human battleships" on the floor grid

### Lesson 152 — Plot and read coordinates
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can plot points from coordinates and read the coordinates of marked points accurately." (UK NC Y4 Gpd; CCSS 5.G.1)
- **Hook:** "A pirate left a map with coordinates, not an X. Can you find the treasure before the rival crew?"
- **Key activity:** I do: plot (3, 5) aloud — 'along the corridor 3, up the stairs 5' — on a grid where the lines (not the spaces) are numbered. We do: read coordinates of five landmarks on the treasure map. You do: plot a list of points that, joined in order, reveal a hidden picture. Discuss the common swap error.
- **Check for understanding:** "A point is at (4, 0). Where exactly is it, and why does it sit on the x-axis?" → 4 across and 0 up — it hasn't moved up at all, so it lies on the x-axis
- **Differentiation:** support: grid with the axes coloured differently and a 'first across, then up' arrow card / stretch: plot points with a partner blindfolded who must say whether each spoken point is inside or outside a drawn shape
- **Materials:** coordinate grids, rulers, treasure-map sheets
- **Joy:** "Treasure island": the first team to decode the coordinates opens the treasure box

### Lesson 153 — Draw polygons from coordinates
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can plot vertices to draw polygons and use shape properties to find a missing vertex." (UK NC Y4 Gpd)
- **Hook:** "Three corners of a square are at (1, 1), (1, 4) and (4, 4). The fourth has gone missing!"
- **Key activity:** Plot the given vertices, join with a ruler, name the shape; reason about the missing vertex using properties (equal sides, parallel sides, right angles). Repeat for a rectangle and an isosceles triangle.
- **Check for understanding:** "Where is the fourth vertex of the square, and which property tells you?" → (4, 1) — opposite sides are equal and parallel, so it lines up below (4, 4) and across from (1, 1)
- **Differentiation:** support: use a geoboard to build the shape before plotting / stretch: given three vertices (1, 1), (4, 1), (2, 3), find all three possible fourth vertices that make a parallelogram
- **Materials:** grids, rulers, geoboards, elastic bands
- **Joy:** "Missing-corner mystery": each solved case earns a detective stamp

### Lesson 154 — Translation
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can translate shapes on a grid and describe a translation in words." (UK NC Y4 Gpd)
- **Hook:** "In chess, a rook slides without turning. What stays the same when a shape slides?"
- **Key activity:** Concrete: slide a card triangle along a floor grid and a paper grid, counting squares right/left then up/down. Pictorial: draw the image, moving each vertex the same way. Abstract: describe as '3 right, 2 up'.
- **Check for understanding:** "A triangle moves 4 right and 1 down. Does its size, shape or direction change? Explain." → no — every vertex moves 4 right and 1 down, so only the position changes
- **Differentiation:** support: physically slide a cut-out and trace its new position / stretch: describe the single translation that equals '3 right, 2 up' followed by '5 left, 1 up' (2 left, 3 up)
- **Materials:** squared paper, card cut-outs, floor grid
- **Joy:** "Slide puzzle": pairs race to translate pieces into a target outline

### Lesson 155 — Translation with coordinates
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can predict and describe coordinates after a translation." (UK NC Y4 Gpd)
- **Hook:** "If a point moves 3 right, what happens to its coordinates — can you predict before drawing?"
- **Key activity:** Translate shapes and record vertex coordinates before and after in a table; spot the rule (3 right → add 3 to x; 2 down → subtract 2 from y). Test predictions by plotting.
- **Check for understanding:** "Point (2, 5) moves 3 right and 2 down. What are its new coordinates, and how do you know without drawing?" → (5, 3) — right adds 3 to the x-coordinate, down subtracts 2 from the y-coordinate
- **Differentiation:** support: move one vertex at a time and record in the table / stretch: find the translation from (7, 2) to (1, 9), and the coordinates after two translations without drawing
- **Materials:** grids, before/after tables
- **Joy:** "Code-breaker": follow a chain of translations to land on a secret letter

### Lesson 156 — Position and direction: problem-solving
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can solve problems combining coordinates, shape and translation." (UK NC Y4 Gpd)
- **Hook:** "Design a video-game level: the explorer must reach the temple using only translations."
- **Key activity:** Pairs design a grid maze with obstacles; write a route as translations and coordinates; swap with another pair who follow and check.
- **Check for understanding:** "What single translation takes (1, 2) to (6, 7)?" → 5 right and 5 up
- **Differentiation:** support: mazes with no obstacles and one-step moves / stretch: route must avoid obstacles in the fewest translations; justify it's the fewest
- **Materials:** grids, maze templates, counters as explorers
- **Joy:** "Maze makers" swap-and-solve

### Lesson 157 — Unit review: Mars rover
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can show mastery of coordinates and translation." (UK NC Y4 Gpd)
- **Hook:** "Mission control: the rover must reach three rock samples on a grid map of Mars."
- **Key activity:** Mastery set: read and plot points, missing vertices, translations with coordinates; then program the rover's route in translations and check a partner's.
- **Check for understanding:** "Plot (3, 4), then move it 2 left and 3 up. What are the new coordinates?" → (1, 7)
- **Differentiation:** support: floor grid and a toy rover available / stretch: find the shortest total route visiting all three samples
- **Materials:** grids, toy rover or counter
- **Joy:** "Mars rover mission" with a countdown launch

**End-of-unit check:** Plot (2, 5) and (5, 2) and explain the difference; find the fourth vertex of a square from three; translate a triangle 3 right and 2 down and give its new coordinates without drawing.

## Unit 12 — Statistics

**Essential question:** How can tables and graphs help us ask good questions and tell true stories about data?

**Key vocabulary:** data, survey, tally, frequency, table, bar chart, line graph, time graph, axis, scale, interval, trend, compare, sum, difference, misleading

### Lesson 158 — Interpret bar charts with different scales
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can read and interpret bar charts with scales in 2s, 5s, 10s and 100s." (UK NC Y4 S; CCSS 3.MD.3)
- **Hook:** "Which sport is most popular in five countries? The chart knows — if we can read it."
- **Key activity:** I do: read a bar ending between gridlines by finding the value of each interval. We do: answer 'how many more / altogether' questions. You do: three charts with different scales, plus one with a scale that doesn't start at zero (discuss why it looks misleading).
- **Check for understanding:** "A bar ends halfway between 40 and 50 on a scale going up in 10s. What is its value, and how do you know?" → 45 — halfway between 40 and 50 is 5 more than 40
- **Differentiation:** support: charts with every gridline labelled / stretch: write a two-step question (sum then difference) for a partner and explain why one chart is misleading
- **Materials:** printed bar charts, rulers
- **Joy:** "Chart detective": spot the misleading chart in a 'newspaper'

### Lesson 159 — Collect and present data
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can collect data, organise it in a table and draw a bar chart with a sensible scale." (UK NC Y4 S)
- **Hook:** "Tasting day: mango, papaya, lychee, kiwi, guava — which world fruit wins?"
- **Key activity:** Class taste-test (check allergies first) with a tally; build a frequency table; choose a scale so the largest value fits; draw axes, label, title.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why did you choose that scale for your bar chart?" → so the largest value fits on the axis and every value can be read accurately
- **Differentiation:** support: axes drawn and scale given / stretch: draw a dual bar chart comparing two classes' results
- **Materials:** fruit samples, squared paper, rulers
- **Joy:** "World fruit tasting" survey

### Lesson 160 — Read time graphs
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can read and interpret line graphs showing change over time." (UK NC Y4 S)
- **Hook:** "How does the temperature change during one day in the desert?"
- **Key activity:** Read a time graph of temperature across a day in a desert city (e.g. Cairo) and a rainforest city (e.g. Manaus); read values, describe rises and falls; compare the two lines.
- **Check for understanding:** "Between which two times did the temperature rise fastest? How can you tell from the line?" → where the line is steepest going up — the biggest rise in the same time
- **Differentiation:** support: graphs with clear gridlines and values to find marked by arrows / stretch: explain why points on a time graph can be joined but bars on a bar chart can't (time is continuous; categories are separate)
- **Materials:** printed time graphs, rulers
- **Joy:** "Weather reporter": present the day's graph as a TV forecast

### Lesson 161 — Draw a line graph from an investigation
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can collect measurements over time and draw a line graph." (UK NC Y4 S)
- **Hook:** "How fast does a mug of hot water cool down? Let's be scientists."
- **Key activity:** Teacher pours warm (not boiling) water; groups read a thermometer every 5 minutes for 30 minutes; plot points on prepared axes, join with straight lines; describe the trend.
- **Check for understanding:** "What does your line tell you about how the water cooled?" → it cooled quickly at first, then more slowly
- **Differentiation:** support: axes and scale provided / stretch: predict the temperature after 60 minutes from the trend and explain the prediction
- **Materials:** thermometers, mugs, warm water (adult-poured), timers, graph paper
- **Joy:** "Cooling race": whose insulated cup stays warmest?

### Lesson 162 — Comparison, sum and difference problems
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can solve comparison, sum and difference problems using tables and graphs." (UK NC Y4 S)
- **Hook:** "A museum in Mexico City records visitors each month. When should it hire extra guides?"
- **Key activity:** Solve problems from tables and graphs (how many more, total over three months, the biggest increase); bar models for the calculations.
- **Check for understanding:** "How many more visitors came in July than in January? Show where each number came from." → read both values from the graph and subtract (e.g. 8,400 − 3,600 = 4,800)
- **Differentiation:** support: single-step questions with values already read / stretch: find the month-to-month change that was greatest and explain
- **Materials:** graphs, tables, bar strips
- **Joy:** "Museum manager" decision challenge

### Lesson 163 — Data investigation
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can plan and carry out a data investigation to answer a question, and draw a conclusion." (UK NC Y4 S)
- **Hook:** "Do children with longer legs jump further? Let's find out instead of guessing."
- **Key activity:** Groups choose a question, plan what to measure, collect data (e.g. leg length and standing jump in cm), record in a table, choose a graph, and write a conclusion with evidence and one limitation.
- **Check for understanding:** "Does your data answer your question? What evidence and what limitation can you name?" → group states a conclusion supported by their data and names a limitation (e.g. small sample)
- **Differentiation:** support: planning template with prompts / stretch: collect a second set to test whether the conclusion holds
- **Materials:** tape measures, metre sticks, squared paper
- **Joy:** "Science-fair" poster session

### Lesson 164 — Unit review: data news day
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can show mastery of reading, presenting and questioning data." (UK NC Y4 S)
- **Hook:** "Data news day: report the true story behind a graph — and expose a misleading one."
- **Key activity:** Mastery set: read scales, complete a table, draw a bar chart, read a time graph, solve difference problems; then critique a misleading graph.
- **Check for understanding:** "This graph's scale starts at 90. Why does it make a small difference look huge?" → the bars are cut off, so a difference of 5 looks like a huge gap
- **Differentiation:** support: graphs with all gridlines labelled / stretch: redraw the misleading graph honestly
- **Materials:** graphs, squared paper
- **Joy:** "Data news" broadcast

**End-of-unit check:** Read a bar ending between gridlines; draw a bar chart with a chosen scale; describe a trend on a time graph; answer a difference question from a table; explain why a graph's scale can mislead.

## Unit 13 — Year-end consolidation

**Essential question:** What do I now understand deeply, and how can I use it to solve problems I've never seen before?

**Key vocabulary:** reason, explain, prove, strategy, efficient, estimate, check, generalise, mastery

### Lesson 165 — Consolidation: number, place value and calculation
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can choose efficient mental and written methods and explain my choice." (UK NC Y4 Npv / Nas / Nmd review)
- **Hook:** "Choose your weapon: mental, jottings or column — which is fastest for each calculation?"
- **Key activity:** Sort 12 calculations (e.g. 4,000 − 1,999; 3,478 + 2,695; 25 × 8; 468 ÷ 4) by best method, solve, and justify. Retrieval of rounding, negatives and Roman numerals in a quick-fire warm-up.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why is 4,000 − 1,999 better done mentally than with columns?" → it's 4,000 − 2,000 + 1 = 2,001 — no exchanges needed
- **Differentiation:** support: fewer calculations, counters available / stretch: invent calculations that trick someone into using an inefficient method
- **Materials:** calculation cards, counters, mini-whiteboards
- **Joy:** "Method match-up" relay

### Lesson 166 — Consolidation: fractions and decimals
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can connect fractions, decimals and measures in mixed problems." (UK NC Y4 Nf review)
- **Hook:** "Fractions and decimals are cousins — can you introduce them to each other?"
- **Key activity:** Card-sort matching fractions, decimals and hundred-square pictures; then mixed problems (¾ of 2 m in cm; 0.45 + 0.55 m).
- **Check for understanding:** "Explain why ¾ of 2 m is 150 cm." → 2 m = 200 cm; ¼ of 200 is 50, so ¾ is 150 cm
- **Differentiation:** support: hundred squares and fraction strips for each problem / stretch: order ⅖, 0.45, 3/10 and 0.04 and justify
- **Materials:** matching cards, hundred squares, strips
- **Joy:** "Cousins" matching game

### Lesson 167 — Consolidation: measure, shape and data
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can apply area, perimeter, time, angles and data in a real problem." (UK NC Y4 Mm / Gp / S review)
- **Hook:** "Design a school garden: beds, paths, a timetable for watering and a chart of what grows."

- **Check for understanding:** "Your bed has area 24 m². Could its perimeter be 20 m? Could it be 50 m? Explain." → 20 m yes (4 × 6); 50 m yes (1 × 24 gives 50 m) — same area, different perimeters
- **Differentiation:** support: template with bed shapes drawn / stretch: keep total path perimeter under a budget

- **Joy:** "Garden designers" presentation

### Lesson 168 — The 12 × 12 secure check
- **Duration:** 40 min

- **Check for understanding:** "Choose any fact you nearly missed and show how you'd rebuild it if you forgot." → e.g. 7 × 12 = 7 × 10 + 7 × 2 = 84

- **Key activity:** Groups prepare a stall (e.g. 'reverse and subtract' magic, factor rectangles, area vs perimeter pens, coordinate treasure hunt) with an explanation card; families and younger classes visit.

## End-of-year mastery checks

1. **Tables secure** — answers any multiplication fact to 12 × 12 and its division facts within about 4 seconds, and can rebuild any forgotten fact from a known one. *See it:* the 12 × 12 secure check (Lesson 168) plus a spoken "how would you rebuild 7 × 8?"
2. **Place value to 10,000** — reads, writes, partitions (in more than one way), compares, orders and rounds 4-digit numbers to 10, 100 and 1,000. *See it:* build 6,208 two ways with counters and explain; round 4,950 to the nearest 100 and 1,000.
3. **Column addition and subtraction** — adds and subtracts 4-digit numbers with exchanges, including across zeros, and checks with estimation and the inverse. *See it:* 5,000 − 2,468 done two ways, with an estimate.
4. **Written multiplication and division** — multiplies 2- and 3-digit numbers by a 1-digit number in columns and divides with short division, interpreting remainders in context. *See it:* "How many 4-seat taxis for 30 people?" with the reasoning.
5. **Fractions** — finds and explains equivalent fractions, converts improper fractions and mixed numbers, adds and subtracts with the same denominator, and finds fractions of amounts and the whole from a part. *See it:* bar model for "⅗ of 35" and "¾ of a number is 24".
6. **Decimals** — reads and writes tenths and hundredths, divides by 10 and 100, compares, orders and rounds decimals, and knows ½, ¼, ¾ and tenths as decimals. *See it:* explain why 0.45 < 0.5 on a hundred square.
7. **Measure** — converts km/m/cm/mm, calculates perimeter of rectilinear shapes and area by counting and by multiplication, and explains why area and perimeter differ. *See it:* build two 12 cm² shapes with different perimeters.
8. **Time and money** — reads and converts 12- and 24-hour times, calculates durations, converts units of time and handles money in decimal notation. *See it:* read a real timetable to find a journey time.
9. **Geometry and position** — classifies angles, triangles and quadrilaterals by properties, completes symmetric figures, plots coordinates and describes translations. *See it:* "Is a square a rectangle?" argued with properties; translate a shape and predict coordinates.
10. **Data** — reads bar charts and time graphs with different scales, draws them from collected data and solves comparison problems. *See it:* the Unit 12 investigation poster.

## Teacher guidance

**Teaching every lesson.** Open with a 5-minute retrieval/fluency warm-up (tables plus one earlier idea), teach one small step explicitly with concrete materials in the child's hands, move to pictures (bar models, number lines, hundred squares) and only then to symbols. Use I do → we do → you do, and end with the check question answered in words, not just a number. If the check fails for more than a few children, re-teach the next day before moving on — mastery, not pace.

**Common misconceptions to watch for (and the fix).**
- *"Adding a zero" to multiply by 10* — breaks with decimals. Always say "the digits move one place to the left; zero holds the empty place."
- *Subtracting the smaller digit from the larger* in each column (5,432 − 1,718 → 4,286). Rebuild with counters and say the exchange aloud.
- *Rounding always goes up* — use a number line so the child sees which multiple is nearer; state the convention that exactly halfway rounds up.
- *Negative numbers: −8 > −3 because 8 > 3* — use a vertical thermometer: lower is smaller.
- *Remainders bigger than the divisor* or ignored in context — the four remainder decisions (round up, round down, use, share) are taught explicitly in Lesson 72.
- *Adding denominators* (⅖ + ⅖ = 4/10) — the denominator names the piece size; strips show the pieces don't shrink.
- *"Longer decimals are bigger"* (0.45 > 0.5) — compare tenths first; equalise with a trailing zero.
- *4 ÷ 100 = 0.4* — use a place-value slider and move two places.
- *Area and perimeter confused* — build shapes with tiles and fence them with string in the same lesson.
- *Angle size depends on arm length* — geostrips with long and short arms at the same turn.
- *Coordinates swapped* — "along the corridor, then up the stairs."
- *Time: 5:40 is 'nearly 5'* — 5:40 is a correct time, 40 minutes past 5 or 20 minutes to 6; the hour hand is closer to 6.

**Differentiation.** Support always means *more concrete* (materials, fewer steps, partly drawn bar models), never simply fewer questions of the same kind. Stretch means *deeper* (generalise, prove, work backwards, create a problem) rather than bigger numbers of the same difficulty. Children who finish early take the lesson's stretch task, then an NRICH problem on the same idea — not the next unit.

**Home and tutor notes.** A parent or the Classai tutor can deliver every lesson with the named household substitutes: dried beans for counters, egg boxes for the 6 and 12 tables, a tape measure for number lines, coins in the family's own currency. Tables practice is 5 minutes a day, little and often, framed as beating your own record.

**Vertical connections.** Year 2 laid the foundations (place value to 1,000, 3-digit columns, the 2/3/4/5/8/10 tables, tenths as fractions, perimeter, time to the minute, right angles). Year 4 builds straight on this year: numbers to 1,000,000, long multiplication (which needs instant tables), factors, primes and square numbers, adding fractions with related denominators, thousandths and percentages, area of compound shapes and volume, measuring angles in degrees and reflection. Any child not secure on tables or on tenths/hundredths by the summer should have a short catch-up plan before Year 4 begins.
