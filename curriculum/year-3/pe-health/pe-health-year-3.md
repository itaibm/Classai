# Year 3 Physical Education & Health (ages 8–9) — Scope & Sequence

## Year overview

Year 3 PE turns fundamental movement into **game, gymnastic, dance and athletic skill**, and builds the **health knowledge** that keeps a body and mind well. Children apply running, jumping, throwing, catching, balancing and striking in **invasion games** (basketball, netball, football, hockey — simplified), **net and wall games**, and **striking and fielding games** (cricket, rounders, baseball); link actions into **gymnastic sequences**; learn **dances from around the world**; run, jump and throw in **athletics**; navigate outdoors with maps in **orienteering**; and learn **water safety**. Two **health units** briefly retrieve the Year 2 basics (food, water, sleep, handwashing, body safety, calm breathing, calling for help) and go further: allergies and asthma, carrying loads and posture, road and cycling safety, food hygiene, growing bodies and body image, online secrets, the body's stress response, and first aid for burns and nosebleeds.

**Delivery.** PE is active and mostly **delivered by a teacher, coach or parent**, outdoors or in a hall — the Classai tutor can explain rules, show diagrams and run reflection, but the doing is physical and supervised. Every practical lesson follows: warm-up (raising heart rate and mobilising joints) → skill teaching with demonstration → practice in small groups → a small-sided game or performance → cool-down and reflection. Children should be **moving for most of every lesson**.

**Values.** Fair play, teamwork, respect for opponents and officials, and personal bests rather than beating others. Competition is taught as healthy: children learn to win and lose well.

**What this year assumes (from Years 1–2):** fundamental movement skills (running, hopping, skipping, jumping, throwing, catching, kicking, balancing), simple team games, basic gymnastic shapes and rolls, dancing to music, and first health ideas (handwashing, healthy food, sleep). **What Year 4 (ages 9–10) relies on:** consistent passing and receiving, understanding attack and defence, linked gymnastic sequences, basic athletic techniques, map reading for orienteering, confidence in water, and health knowledge — for more formal versions of sports, tactics and fitness training.

**Health guidance used.** The World Health Organization recommends children aged 5–17 get an average of **at least 60 minutes of moderate-to-vigorous physical activity a day**; children aged 6–12 generally need **9–12 hours of sleep** (American Academy of Sleep Medicine). Swimming lessons (where available) aim for children to swim at least 25 metres and perform safe self-rescue by the end of primary school; the water-safety lessons here teach the knowledge part on land.

Lessons are **40 minutes**, two a week. **Safety:** check the space for hazards, suitable footwear and clothing, water available, jewellery removed, medical needs (asthma inhalers, allergies) known, and adults trained in first aid present.

## Unit 1 — Fit for life: movement foundations and fitness

**Essential question:** What does my body need to move well, and how can I become fitter?

**Key vocabulary:** warm-up, cool-down, heart rate, pulse, muscles, stamina, strength, flexibility, speed, agility, balance, coordination, personal best

### Lesson 1 — Warming up and why
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can lead a warm-up and explain why it prepares the body for exercise." (UK NC KS2 PE (develop flexibility, strength, technique, control and balance))
- **Hook:** "Why do footballers jog and stretch before a match instead of just starting?"
- **Key activity:** Adult-led: pulse raiser (jogging, skipping, side-steps), mobility (arm circles, leg swings, hip circles), then short dynamic stretches. Children feel their pulse before and after; learn a 3-part warm-up (raise, mobilise, stretch) and in small groups lead one part.
- **Check for understanding:** "What happens to your heart rate during a warm-up, and why is that useful?" → it rises, pumping more blood and oxygen to the muscles so they're ready and less likely to be injured
- **Differentiation:** support: a picture card of the three warm-up parts, following a leader / stretch: design and lead a warm-up for a specific sport and explain your choices
- **Materials:** cones, music, stopwatch; safety: clear space, non-slip footwear
- **Joy:** "Warm-up DJ": groups choose music and lead their routine

### Lesson 2 — Stamina: running at a steady pace
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can run at a steady, sustainable pace for several minutes and explain pacing." (UK NC KS2 PE (running))
- **Hook:** "Is it better to sprint at the start of a long run or to keep a steady pace? Let's test it."
- **Key activity:** Adult-led: children run laps of a 100 m circuit for 4–6 minutes, counting laps; try 'too fast then walk' vs 'steady' and compare laps; learn to talk while running as a pace check.
- **Check for understanding:** "Which pace let you run further in the same time, and why?" → the steady pace — sprinting early made them tired and they had to slow or stop
- **Differentiation:** support: run–walk intervals with a buddy / stretch: run with an even lap time and record splits
- **Materials:** cones, stopwatch, lap tally cards; safety: water break, asthma inhalers at hand
- **Joy:** "Daily mile"-style class challenge

### Lesson 3 — Agility and speed
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can change direction quickly and safely with good footwork." (UK NC KS2 PE)
- **Hook:** "How does a rugby player dodge a tackle without falling over?"
- **Key activity:** Adult-led: agility ladder or chalk ladders; zig-zag cone runs; teach low centre of gravity, small steps and pushing off the outside foot; tag games.
- **Check for understanding:** "What do you do with your body to change direction quickly without slipping?" → bend knees, lower your centre of gravity, take small steps and push off the outside foot
- **Differentiation:** support: wider cone spacing and walking pace first / stretch: time a zig-zag run and try to beat your personal best with better technique
- **Materials:** cones, agility ladders or chalk; safety: dry, non-slip surface
- **Joy:** 'Dodge the dragon' tag game

### Lesson 4 — Strength and core
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can perform body-weight strength and core exercises with good form." (UK NC KS2 PE)
- **Hook:** "Can you hold a plank for as long as a panda can hold on to a branch?"
- **Key activity:** Adult-led circuit: plank, wall sit, bear crawl, star jumps, crab walk; 30 seconds each with rests; teach form (straight back, controlled movement).
- **Check for understanding:** "Why is good form more important than doing more repetitions?" → poor form can cause injury and doesn't train the right muscles
- **Differentiation:** support: easier versions (knee plank, shorter holds) / stretch: design a new station and explain which muscles it works
- **Materials:** mats, cones, timer; safety: mats for floor work
- **Joy:** 'Animal circuit' with animal-move stations

### Lesson 5 — Flexibility and balance
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can improve flexibility and balance with safe stretches and balance challenges." (UK NC KS2 PE)
- **Hook:** "Could you stand on one leg with your eyes closed for 10 seconds?"
- **Key activity:** Adult-led: static stretches in cool-down (held, not bounced); balance challenges (one leg, eyes closed, on a bench, partner mirroring); simple yoga poses (tree, warrior).
- **Check for understanding:** "Why should we hold stretches still instead of bouncing?" → bouncing can overstretch and injure muscles; holding lets them lengthen safely
- **Differentiation:** support: hold a wall for support / stretch: create a 3-pose balance sequence and hold each for 10 seconds
- **Materials:** mats; safety: stretch only to mild tension
- **Joy:** 'Statue garden' balance game

### Lesson 6 — Fitness personal best
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can measure my fitness in simple tests and set personal targets." (UK NC KS2 PE (compare performances to achieve personal bests))
- **Hook:** "Your only rival today is you."
- **Key activity:** Adult-led stations: shuttle run, standing long jump, plank hold, balance test, laps in 3 minutes; children record results privately and set a target to retest in Term 3.
- **Check for understanding:** "Which fitness area will you work on, and what will you do to improve it?" → names an area and a realistic plan (e.g. skip daily to improve stamina)
- **Differentiation:** support: recording sheet with pictures / stretch: explain which fitness component each test measures
- **Materials:** cones, tape measure, stopwatch, recording cards; safety: private results, no ranking
- **Joy:** 'Personal best passport' stamped at each station

**End-of-unit check:** Lead a three-part warm-up; explain what heart rate does during exercise; record personal-best fitness results and set one target.

## Unit 2 — Invasion games 1: basketball and netball skills

**Essential question:** How do teams keep the ball, move it forward and score — and how do we stop them?

**Key vocabulary:** invasion game, attack, defend, possession, pass, chest pass, bounce pass, shoulder pass, dribble, pivot, space, marking, interception, shoot, footwork, fair play

### Lesson 7 — Passing and receiving
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can make accurate chest and bounce passes and receive with 'ready hands'." (UK NC KS2 PE (use running, throwing and catching in combination))
- **Hook:** "Why do basketball players pass more than they dribble?"
- **Key activity:** Adult-led: teach chest pass (thumbs behind, step, push, follow through) and bounce pass (two-thirds of the way to the receiver); receiver shows 'W' hands as a target. Pairs pass at increasing distance; then 3v1 'piggy in the middle'.
- **Check for understanding:** "When is a bounce pass better than a chest pass?" → when a defender's arms are up between you — the bounce pass goes under them
- **Differentiation:** support: larger, softer balls and shorter distances / stretch: pass on the move to a moving partner
- **Materials:** size 5 basketballs or netballs, cones; safety: space between pairs
- **Joy:** 'Beat the clock' passing count

### Lesson 8 — Dribbling and footwork
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can dribble with control using fingertips and use a pivot." (UK NC KS2 PE)
- **Hook:** "Can you dribble without looking at the ball?"
- **Key activity:** Adult-led: dribble with fingertips, ball at waist height, eyes up; change hands; 'traffic lights' game; learn the netball landing and pivot (no stepping with the landing foot).
- **Check for understanding:** "Why should you look up while dribbling?" → to see teammates, space and defenders
- **Differentiation:** support: stationary dribbling first / stretch: dribble through cones with the weaker hand
- **Materials:** balls, cones
- **Joy:** 'Traffic lights' dribble game

### Lesson 9 — Finding space
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can move into space to receive a pass and explain why space matters." (UK NC KS2 PE (apply basic principles for attacking))
- **Hook:** "If you stand next to a defender, can your teammate pass to you?"
- **Key activity:** Adult-led: 'get free' games in a grid; teach dodging and signalling; 4v2 keep-ball.
- **Check for understanding:** "What makes a good space to move into?" → away from defenders, in the passer's line of sight and within passing range
- **Differentiation:** support: bigger grid, fewer defenders / stretch: create space for a teammate with a decoy run
- **Materials:** bibs, cones
- **Joy:** 4v2 keep-ball

### Lesson 10 — Defending: marking and interception
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can mark an opponent and intercept passes fairly." (UK NC KS2 PE (apply basic principles for defending))
- **Hook:** "How do you stop a team scoring without touching anyone?"
- **Key activity:** Adult-led: teach 'ball–you–player' positioning, arms up at 0.9 m in netball, reading the passer's eyes; 3v3 games focusing on interception.
- **Check for understanding:** "Where should you stand to mark an opponent, and why?" → between your player and the ball/goal, so you can see both and intercept
- **Differentiation:** support: defend in a smaller area / stretch: switch marking with a teammate
- **Materials:** bibs, balls; safety: non-contact rules
- **Joy:** 'Interception points' game

### Lesson 11 — Shooting
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can shoot at a target using the correct technique for netball or basketball." (UK NC KS2 PE)
- **Hook:** "Why do netball shooters bend their knees before shooting?"
- **Key activity:** Adult-led: teach the one-handed netball shot (ball above head, knees bend, extend up, follow through) and the basketball set shot (BEEF: balance, eyes, elbow, follow-through); shooting stations at lowered hoops at different distances.
- **Check for understanding:** "What does bending your knees add to a shot?" → power from the legs, so arms can focus on accuracy
- **Differentiation:** support: lower hoops and closer positions / stretch: shoot after receiving a pass on the move
- **Materials:** hoops (adjustable if possible), balls
- **Joy:** 'Around the world' shooting game

### Lesson 12 — Rules and fair play
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can play a small-sided game using simple rules and explain why rules matter." (UK NC KS2 PE (play competitive games, modified where appropriate))
- **Hook:** "What would a game be like with no rules at all?"
- **Key activity:** Adult-led: introduce key rules (no contact, footwork, stepping, restarting play), and let children referee short 3v3 games in turn.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why do we need rules like 'no contact'?" → to keep players safe and make the game fair
- **Differentiation:** support: play with fewer rules at first / stretch: referee a game fairly and explain one decision
- **Materials:** bibs, whistles, balls
- **Joy:** 'Junior referee' role

### Lesson 13 — Tactics: attacking as a team
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can use simple attacking tactics such as give-and-go and spreading out." (UK NC KS2 PE)
- **Hook:** "How can two players beat one defender?"
- **Key activity:** Adult-led: teach give-and-go (pass and move into space for the return), width (spread out), and quick passing; 3v2 and 4v3 practice.
- **Check for understanding:** "Explain how a give-and-go beats a defender." → you pass, move past the defender into space, and receive the ball back before they can react
- **Differentiation:** support: slower pace and walking practice / stretch: plan a set play and teach it to your team
- **Materials:** bibs, cones, balls
- **Joy:** 'Set play designers' tactic board

### Lesson 14 — Unit review: mini tournament
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can apply skills, rules and tactics in a small-sided tournament and reflect on teamwork." (UK NC KS2 PE (evaluate and recognise success))
- **Hook:** "Mini tournament: points for goals — and for fair play!"
- **Key activity:** Adult-led: 4v4 round-robin; fair-play points awarded; teams discuss tactics between games; reflection at the end.
- **Check for understanding:** "What tactic worked best for your team, and how did you show fair play?" → names a tactic (e.g. spreading out) and a fair-play action (e.g. shaking hands, owning a foul)
- **Differentiation:** support: smaller pitch, extra time / stretch: act as captain and adjust tactics between games
- **Materials:** bibs, balls, scoreboard
- **Joy:** tournament with a fair-play trophy

**End-of-unit check:** In a small-sided game, pass accurately, move into space, mark an opponent and explain one attacking tactic.

## Unit 3 — Gymnastics: balance, rolls and sequences

**Essential question:** How do gymnasts link balances, rolls and jumps into a smooth, controlled sequence?

**Key vocabulary:** balance, point of contact, patch, point, shape, straight, tuck, pike, straddle, star, roll, log roll, egg roll, forward roll, teddy-bear roll, jump, landing, travel, sequence, transition, mirror, canon, apparatus

### Lesson 15 — Shapes and balances
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can hold balances on different points and patches with tension and control." (UK NC KS2 PE (develop flexibility, strength, technique, control and balance))
- **Hook:** "Can you balance on three points? Two? One point and one patch?"
- **Key activity:** Adult-led: revise the five basic shapes (straight, tuck, pike, straddle, star); explore balances on 1–4 points of contact; teach 'tension' (squeeze muscles, fingers stretched) and holding for 3 seconds.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why is a balance on a large patch (like your back) easier than on one point?" → a larger base of support is more stable
- **Differentiation:** support: balances on 3–4 points with a partner spotting / stretch: hold a one-point balance with an extended free limb for 5 seconds
- **Materials:** mats, picture cards of shapes; safety: mats spaced, bare feet or gym shoes
- **Joy:** 'Statue gallery' where the class guesses the points of contact

### Lesson 16 — Rolling safely
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can perform log, egg and teddy-bear rolls with control, and a forward roll where ready." (UK NC KS2 PE (gymnastics))
- **Hook:** "Rolling is how gymnasts — and stunt performers — land safely."
- **Key activity:** Adult-led, on mats: log roll (straight), egg roll (tuck), teddy-bear roll (straddle, round), then forward roll down a gentle incline wedge for those ready: chin tucked, hands flat, push, roll on the back of the shoulders not the head.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why must you tuck your chin in a forward roll?" → it keeps weight off the head and neck and makes the back round so you roll smoothly
- **Differentiation:** support: log and egg rolls only; incline mat with adult support / stretch: finish a forward roll by standing without using hands
- **Materials:** mats, incline wedge; safety: adult spotting, no rolls on hard floors, no one forced to roll
- **Joy:** 'Roll the rainbow': rolls in different colours of mats

### Lesson 17 — Jumps and safe landings
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can perform shaped jumps and land safely with bent knees." (UK NC KS2 PE)
- **Hook:** "How do cats land from a height without getting hurt?"
- **Key activity:** Adult-led: teach 'soft landing' (toes-heels, knees bent over toes, arms forward, hold for 3); jumps: straight, tuck, star, half-turn; from low apparatus.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why do we bend our knees when we land?" → to absorb the impact and protect joints
- **Differentiation:** support: jump from the floor only / stretch: add a half-turn jump with a controlled landing
- **Materials:** mats, low benches/boxes; safety: landing mats, one at a time
- **Joy:** 'Cat landings' challenge

### Lesson 18 — Travelling and transitions
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can travel in different ways and link movements with smooth transitions." (UK NC KS2 PE)
- **Hook:** "How does a gymnast get from a balance into a roll without a wobble?"
- **Key activity:** Adult-led: travel (step patterns, bunny hops, crab, bear walk); practise transitions (balance → roll; roll → jump → balance) with smooth links.
- **Check for understanding:** "What makes a transition smooth?" → it flows without extra steps or pauses and keeps control
- **Differentiation:** support: link two movements only / stretch: link four movements with changes of level
- **Materials:** mats, benches
- **Joy:** 'Flow challenge' with music

### Lesson 19 — Partner work: mirror and canon
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can perform a short sequence with a partner in mirror and canon." (UK NC KS2 PE)
- **Hook:** "Can you move like your partner's reflection?"
- **Key activity:** Adult-led: mirroring shapes and balances; canon (one after the other); build a 4-part partner sequence.
- **Check for understanding:** "What is the difference between mirror and canon?" → mirror: moving at the same time as a reflection; canon: the same moves one after the other
- **Differentiation:** support: mirror only / stretch: include a counter-balance (leaning on each other safely)
- **Materials:** mats; safety: counter-balances taught with adult guidance
- **Joy:** partner performance

### Lesson 20 — Using apparatus
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can use benches, boxes and mats safely to perform balances, jumps and rolls." (UK NC KS2 PE)
- **Hook:** "The apparatus is set up — but first, how do we carry a bench safely?"
- **Key activity:** Adult-led: carrying apparatus (bend knees, four people for a bench), safety checks; stations for balances on benches, jumps off boxes, rolls along mats.
- **Check for understanding:** "How do we lift and carry a bench safely?" → with enough people, bending knees, backs straight, walking forwards and communicating
- **Differentiation:** support: lower apparatus and adult support / stretch: create a mini-sequence using two pieces of apparatus
- **Materials:** benches, boxes, mats, wedges; safety: adult checks every set-up
- **Joy:** apparatus 'adventure course'

### Lesson 21 — Unit review: sequence performance
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can perform a sequence of at least six movements with a clear start, finish and smooth transitions, and evaluate it." (UK NC KS2 PE (compare performances; evaluate))
- **Hook:** "Showtime: each gymnast performs, and the judges give feedback — kindly!"
- **Key activity:** Children rehearse and perform a sequence (start shape, balance, roll, jump, travel, finish shape); peers give feedback using criteria (control, tension, flow, variety); video playback for self-evaluation.
- **Check for understanding:** "Watch your video. What is one strength and one thing to improve?" → names a specific strength (e.g. held balances still) and improvement (e.g. smoother transition after the roll)
- **Differentiation:** support: a four-movement sequence with a picture plan / stretch: include a change of level, direction and speed
- **Materials:** mats, tablets for video
- **Joy:** gymnastics showcase with 'judges' scorecards'

**End-of-unit check:** Perform a six-movement sequence with a start and finish shape, a controlled balance, a roll, a jump and smooth transitions, and evaluate it using the video.

## Unit 4 — Health 1: food, safety and active living

**Essential question:** How do food and activity keep me healthy — and how can I keep myself and my friends safe every day?

**Key vocabulary:** nutrients, carbohydrate, protein, fat, fibre, vitamins, minerals, balanced diet, portion, allergy, asthma, inhaler, auto-injector, posture, spine, stopping distance, reflective, helmet, active minutes, average, habit

### Lesson 22 — A balanced plate
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can plan a balanced meal and explain what each food group does." (UK RSHE Health (healthy eating); links to Science)
- **Hook:** "If you ate only pasta for a week, what would your body be missing?"
- **Key activity:** Quick retrieval from Year 2 (which foods give fast or slow energy? why do water and sleep matter?); then use a balanced-plate model (fruit and vegetables, starchy foods, protein, dairy or alternatives, small amounts of oils); sort pictures of meals from around the world (jollof rice, dal and chapati, bibimbap, paella) into groups; plan a balanced lunchbox.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why do we need protein, and name two foods that contain it." → protein helps us grow and repair the body — e.g. beans, eggs, fish, lentils, meat, tofu
- **Differentiation:** support: picture cards with food groups colour-coded / stretch: adapt a meal to make it more balanced and explain the change
- **Materials:** food picture cards, plate templates, food packaging
- **Joy:** 'World lunchbox' design

### Lesson 23 — Allergies, asthma and helping a friend
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can explain what allergies and asthma are, recognise when a friend needs help, and know what to do." (UK RSHE Health (health and prevention; basic first aid); NHES Standard 5)
- **Hook:** "Some children carry an inhaler or an allergy pen everywhere. Why — and how could you help if they suddenly felt unwell?"
- **Key activity:** Adult-led, with the school nurse if possible: an allergy is the body's defence system over-reacting to something usually harmless (e.g. peanuts, eggs, milk, bee stings, pollen); signs of a serious reaction (swelling of the lips or face, itchy rash, difficulty breathing, feeling faint) mean *fetch an adult at once* — they may use the child's auto-injector pen and call the emergency number; asthma: the airways tighten and narrow, making breathing hard, and a reliever inhaler relaxes and opens them; why we don't share food and why warm-ups help children with asthma. Role-play: notice, get an adult, stay with your friend and keep them calm. Only the child themselves or a trained adult uses an inhaler or auto-injector.
- **Check for understanding:** "Your friend with a nut allergy eats a biscuit and says their mouth feels itchy and their lips are swelling. What should you do, and why quickly?" → fetch an adult straight away (they may use the auto-injector and call the emergency number) and stay with your friend; a serious allergic reaction can make breathing difficult within minutes
- **Differentiation:** support: picture cards for 'notice — get an adult — stay with them' / stretch: explain why a reliever inhaler makes breathing easier during an asthma attack
- **Materials:** picture scenario cards, trainer (practice) inhaler and auto-injector if the school nurse brings them; sensitivity: never single out children with allergies or asthma — they share their own story only if they want to
- **Joy:** 'Allergy-aware class' agreement signed by everyone

### Lesson 24 — Posture, backpacks and looking after my back
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can carry, lift and sit in ways that protect my back." (UK RSHE Health (health and prevention); SHAPE Standard 3)
- **Hook:** "Weigh your school bag. Is it heavier than it should be?"
- **Key activity:** Weigh school bags (never children) and compare with a guideline many health organisations use: a bag should be about 10% of body weight and no more than about 15%; work out the limits for sample body weights (e.g. 30 kg → 3 kg, up to 4.5 kg) — a maths link; wear both straps, pack the heaviest items nearest the back; safe lifting (bend the knees, keep the back straight, hold the load close); sitting and screen posture; spine-mobility moves (cat–cow, gentle twists) to finish.
- **Check for understanding:** "A 28 kg child carries a 6 kg bag on one shoulder. What two changes would you suggest, and why?" → lighten it to about 3–4 kg (roughly 10–15% of 28 kg) and use both straps, so the load is spread evenly and one side of the back and shoulders isn't strained
- **Differentiation:** support: find 10% using a tenths chart (divide by 10) / stretch: design a 'pack it right' guide for younger children with reasons
- **Materials:** bathroom scales, school bags, calculators, mats; sensitivity: calculations use sample body weights — children are not weighed in class
- **Joy:** 'Pack it right' relay

### Lesson 25 — Road and cycling safety
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can cross roads safely, fit a cycle helmet correctly and explain how to be seen." (UK RSHE Health (being safe); NHES Standard 7)
- **Hook:** "A car at 50 km/h needs about 25 metres to stop — let's mark that out on the playground. Could a driver stop if you stepped out?"
- **Key activity:** Outdoors on the playground: pace out the stopping distance; a safe crossing routine (find a safe place such as a crossing, stop at the kerb, look and listen in both directions — remembering which side traffic drives on where you live — and keep looking while you cross; never step out between parked cars); being seen (bright and reflective clothing by day, lights at night); helmet fit in pairs: level on the head about two fingers' width above the eyebrows, side straps meeting in a V just under each ear, chin strap snug with room for one finger; quick bike checks (brakes, tyres).
- **Check for understanding:** "Why can't a driver stop instantly when someone steps out, and what does that mean for how you cross?" → the driver needs time to notice and react, and the car keeps moving while it brakes, so it travels many metres; you wait until the road is clear or traffic has stopped, and keep looking as you cross
- **Differentiation:** support: crossing steps as picture cards / stretch: explain why stopping distances get longer on wet roads and at higher speeds
- **Materials:** tape measure, cones, cycle helmets in several sizes, reflective items, torch; safety: all practice on the playground, never on real roads
- **Joy:** 'Be bright, be seen' torch test in a darkened room

### Lesson 26 — Active habits
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can use an activity log to check whether I get 60 active minutes a day and plan how to add more." (UK RSHE Health (physical activity); maths link (averages))
- **Hook:** "The WHO says children should average at least 60 minutes a day of moving that makes you warm and a bit out of breath — how close are you?"
- **Key activity:** Retrieve Year 2 'how hard is my body working?' (talk test); the WHO guideline includes activities that make you breathe hard, and bone- and muscle-strengthening play (jumping, climbing) on at least three days a week; children keep a week's activity log (with families) and find their average; brainstorm active options at home; design a 'playground game' to increase activity.
- **Check for understanding:** "Maya's active minutes on five school days were 40, 75, 30, 90 and 65. What was her average, and what would you advise her?" → 300 ÷ 5 = 60 minutes, so she meets the guideline on average — but on two days (40 and 30) she was well short, so she could add active play on those days
- **Differentiation:** support: an activity menu and a calculator / stretch: design an active game for younger children that includes jumping or climbing (bone strength)
- **Materials:** activity logs, calculators, playground equipment
- **Joy:** play the invented games

### Lesson 27 — Unit review: healthy habits plan
- **Duration:** 40 min

## Unit 8 — Health 2: staying clean, safe, well and ready to help

**Essential question:** How can I keep my body clean, safe and well — and help others when something goes wrong?

**Key vocabulary:** hygiene, bacteria, cross-contamination, clean–separate–cook–chill, growth spurt, body image, edited image, body safety, private parts, consent, trusted adult, stress, adrenaline, calm, first aid, burn, nosebleed, recovery position, emergency number

### Lesson 48 — Food hygiene: stopping germs in the kitchen
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can explain how germs spread through food and use the four rules that keep food safe." (UK RSHE Health (health protection and prevention); links to Science)
- **Hook:** "In Year 2 the germ was glitter. In a kitchen the germs are invisible — so how do cooks stop them spreading?"
- **Key activity:** Quick retrieval: 20-second handwashing with soap, and coughing or sneezing into a tissue (bin it, wash hands) or your elbow. New: the four food-safety habits — clean (hands, surfaces, boards), separate (raw meat and fish on their own board, away from ready-to-eat food), cook (until steaming hot all the way through), chill (keep food in a fridge at about 5 °C or below); spot the risks in a kitchen picture; model how bacteria can double about every 20 minutes in warm conditions using counters (1 → 2 → 4 → 8 … → 64 after two hours).
- **Check for understanding:** "Why should raw chicken never be cut on the same board as salad, even though the chicken will be cooked?" → bacteria from the raw chicken can pass onto the salad, which isn't cooked, so nothing kills them before someone eats it
- **Differentiation:** support: sort picture cards into clean / separate / cook / chill / stretch: use the doubling model to explain why cooked food shouldn't sit in a warm room for hours
- **Materials:** kitchen-scene picture, coloured chopping boards (or pictures), counters, soap, sinks
- **Joy:** 'Kitchen detectives' spot-the-risk race

### Lesson 49 — Growing bodies and respecting differences
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can explain that bodies grow and change at different rates, spot edited images, and speak respectfully about all bodies." (UK RSHE Health (changing body; respectful relationships); NHES Standard 2)
- **Hook:** "Top athletes come in every shape — a gymnast about 1.4 m tall and a basketball player over 2 m. Which body is 'right' for sport?"
- **Key activity:** Using sample (not class) growth data: children grow at different rates, with growth spurts at different times; bodies differ in shape, size, skin and ability — and all deserve respect; how photos in adverts and online can be edited or filtered; kind language about bodies (what bodies can *do*); a brief preview that more changes come at puberty (taught in Year 4); quick retrieval of daily care (teeth twice a day with fluoride toothpaste — spit, don't rinse; sun protection).
- **Check for understanding:** "An advert shows a 'perfect' athlete's photo. Why shouldn't you compare your own body with it?" → the photo may be edited or filtered, and real bodies grow at different rates and come in different shapes; what matters is being healthy and what your body can do
- **Differentiation:** support: sort 'kind' and 'unkind' comments about bodies / stretch: rewrite an advert slogan so it celebrates what bodies can do
- **Materials:** sample growth chart, age-appropriate advert examples, sun-safety items; sensitivity: never measure or weigh children in front of others
- **Joy:** 'What my body can do' gallery

### Lesson 50 — Body safety: offline and online
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can apply the PANTS rule to situations at home, at school and online, and know how to get help." (UK RSHE (being safe; online relationships); NSPCC PANTS guidance)
- **Hook:** "Someone in a game chat says, 'Don't tell your parents about me — it's our secret.' Is that a fun surprise or a worrying secret?"
- **Key activity:** Retrieve the PANTS rule from Years 1–2 in calm, age-appropriate language (Privates are private; Always remember your body belongs to you; No means no; Talk about secrets that upset you; Speak up, someone can help); go further: the same rules apply online — no one should ask you for photos of your body or ask you to keep a chat secret; nothing is ever your fault, even if you broke a rule to get there; practise 'stop, block, tell' for online messages; update the five trusted adults and add one outside school or home (e.g. a helpline number for your country). Links to computing (online safety). Follow safeguarding procedures for any disclosure.
- **Check for understanding:** "Someone you've only met in an online game asks you to send a photo and keep it secret. What should you do, and which PANTS ideas help you?" → don't send it; stop chatting and block; tell a trusted adult — 'Talk about secrets that upset you' and 'Speak up, someone can help'; you won't be in trouble
- **Differentiation:** support: picture scenarios sorted into 'surprise' or 'tell' / stretch: explain why someone might ask you to keep a secret, and why you should keep telling until someone listens
- **Materials:** PANTS resources, scenario cards, helping-hand templates, a local helpline number
- **Joy:** 'Helping hand' of trusted adults, now with one new name

### Lesson 51 — Feelings, stress and the body's alarm
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can explain what my body does when I feel stressed and use slow breathing to calm it." (UK RSHE (mental wellbeing); links to Science (heart and lungs))
- **Hook:** "Your heart pounds on the start line before you've even run a step. Why?"
- **Key activity:** Retrieve Year 2 calming strategies; new: the body's 'alarm system' — when we feel threatened or under pressure, adrenaline makes the heart and breathing speed up, muscles tense and the tummy 'flutter': helpful for escaping danger, less helpful before a test or a performance; practise slow breathing with a longer out-breath (in for 4, out for 6) for two minutes and take your pulse before and after; add 5-4-3-2-1 senses and exercise as a mood-lifter; everyone makes a personal calm plan.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why does breathing out slowly help you calm down when your body's alarm is on?" → slow breathing with a long out-breath tells the body it is safe, so the heart and breathing slow down and muscles relax
- **Differentiation:** support: trace a 'breathing star' card while breathing / stretch: explain why the alarm response helped early humans and when it still helps today
- **Materials:** breathing cards, stopwatches, calm music
- **Joy:** 'calm corner' kit

### Lesson 52 — First aid: burns, nosebleeds and getting help
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can get help in an emergency and give first aid for burns, nosebleeds and minor cuts." (UK RSHE (basic first aid))
- **Hook:** "You spill a hot drink on your hand. Ice, butter or cool water — which one is right?"
- **Key activity:** Adult-led, with a trained first aider if possible. Retrieve Year 2: check for danger, shout for help, call the emergency number for your country (e.g. 112 across the EU, 999 in the UK, 911 in the US and Canada, 000 in Australia) and say where you are and what happened. - **Check for understanding:** "Why should someone with a nosebleed lean forward, not tip their head back?" → so the blood drains out of the nose instead of running down the throat, where it could make them cough, choke or be sick
- **Differentiation:** support: picture step cards for a burn and a nosebleed / stretch: explain why ice is not used on a burn (it can damage the skin further; cool running water is safer)
- **Materials:** first-aid kit, role-play phones, mats, bowl of water for practice
- **Joy:** 'Little lifesavers' certificates

- **Objective:** "By the end, I can show what I know about staying clean, safe and well." (UK RSHE)

- **Key activity:** Adult-led: rounders bat grip and side-on stance; cricket bat grip (hands together, top hand controlling); hit from a tee then from a gentle feed; aim into space.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why should your eyes stay on the ball?" → to judge its speed and position so you can time the hit
- **Differentiation:** support: hit from a tee / stretch: aim hits into gaps between fielders
- **Materials:** rounders bats, cricket bats (junior), tees, soft balls; safety: bat-swing zone
- **Joy:** 'Hit the gap' targets

### Lesson 58 — Rules of rounders
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can play a simplified game of rounders following the rules." (UK NC KS2 PE (competitive games))
- **Hook:** "How do you score a rounder?"
- **Key activity:** Adult-led: rules (4 posts, run anticlockwise, keep in contact at posts, ways to be out); play a short game.
- **Check for understanding:** "Name two ways a batter can be out in rounders." → caught, or a fielder touches the post you're running to before you reach it
- **Differentiation:** support: fewer rules / stretch: act as umpire
- **Materials:** posts, bats, balls
- **Joy:** class rounders game

### Lesson 59 — Cricket and baseball around the world
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can compare striking and fielding games and explain their popularity in different countries." (UK NC KS2 PE; cultural understanding)
- **Hook:** "Cricket is huge in India, Pakistan, Australia and the Caribbean; baseball in the USA, Japan, Cuba and the Dominican Republic. Why?"
- **Key activity:** Adult-led: play kwik cricket-style game with simplified rules; compare with baseball and rounders.
- **Check for understanding:** "How are cricket and rounders similar, and how are they different?" → both have batters, bowlers and fielders scoring runs; cricket uses wickets and two batters run between them
- **Differentiation:** support: picture comparison / stretch: explain one strategy fielders use to stop runs
- **Materials:** kwik cricket set
- **Joy:** mini-cricket match

### Lesson 60 — Unit review: striking and fielding festival
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can plan fielding positions, apply batting and fielding skills in games and evaluate our tactics." (UK NC KS2 PE (apply tactics; evaluate))
- **Hook:** "Tactics time: where should your fielders stand to stop the runs?"
- **Key activity:** Adult-led: teams draw fielding positions on a mini-pitch map before each innings (spread out, backstop, strong throwers deep), play short rounders and kwik cricket games, then review — did the placings work? Batters aim for gaps.
- **Check for understanding:** "Where did you place your fielders, and why? Did it work?" → spread to cover gaps with a backstop and strong throwers deep; judged by runs saved or batters out
- **Differentiation:** support: a pre-drawn fielding map with suggested positions / stretch: change field placings for a batter who always hits to one side and explain
- **Materials:** bats, balls, posts, stumps, fielding maps
- **Joy:** festival with a 'best fielding team' award

**End-of-unit check:** Throw, catch and field with good technique; bat into space; explain two ways to get out in rounders and one fielding tactic.

## Unit 10 — Athletics, outdoor adventure and water safety

**Essential question:** How can I run faster, jump further, throw further, find my way — and stay safe near water?

**Key vocabulary:** sprint, start, finish, relay, baton, changeover, jump, take-off, landing, throw, javelin (foam), shot (soft), personal best, orienteering, map, control point, orient, compass, water safety, float, self-rescue, lifeguard

### Lesson 61 — Sprinting and starts
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can sprint with good technique and react quickly at the start." (UK NC KS2 PE (athletics: running))
- **Hook:** "The fastest sprinters look relaxed. How?"
- **Key activity:** Adult-led: sprint technique (drive arms, high knees, run on the balls of the feet, relaxed face); standing starts on 'set, go'; reaction games; 30 m timed sprints.
- **Check for understanding:** "What do your arms do in a good sprint, and why?" → they drive forwards and back (not across the body) to balance and power the legs
- **Differentiation:** support: shorter distances and a partner model / stretch: time three sprints and find your best
- **Materials:** cones, stopwatches
- **Joy:** 'Reaction races' with different signals

### Lesson 62 — Relay running
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can pass a baton safely and quickly in a relay team." (UK NC KS2 PE)
- **Hook:** "How can a team be faster than its fastest runner?"
- **Key activity:** Adult-led: baton passing (upsweep, receiver's hand back, don't look), changeover zones, team order tactics.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why should the receiver start running before the baton arrives?" → so they're already moving at speed when they take it
- **Differentiation:** support: walking changeovers first / stretch: choose your team's running order and explain it
- **Materials:** batons, cones
- **Joy:** class relay championship

### Lesson 63 — Jumping for distance and height
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can perform a standing long jump and a running jump with a safe take-off and landing." (UK NC KS2 PE (jumping))
- **Hook:** "How far can you jump from standing — further than you are tall?"
- **Key activity:** Adult-led: standing long jump (swing arms, bend, drive, land on two feet); short run-up jump from a take-off zone into sand or mats; measure.
- **Check for understanding:** "How do your arms help you jump further?" → swinging them forward adds momentum
- **Differentiation:** support: standing jumps only / stretch: compare standing and running jumps and explain the difference
- **Materials:** tape measure, mats or sandpit
- **Joy:** 'Jump longer than your height' challenge

### Lesson 64 — Throwing for distance
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can throw a foam javelin and push a soft shot with correct technique." (UK NC KS2 PE (throwing))
- **Hook:** "Why do javelin throwers run up and throw at an angle?"
- **Key activity:** Adult-led: foam javelin (grip, pull-back, throw at about 45° upwards) and soft shot push (from the neck, push not throw); strict safety rules.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why do we push the shot instead of throwing it like a ball?" → it's heavy; pushing protects the shoulder and elbow and is the rule
- **Differentiation:** support: lighter implements / stretch: adjust the release angle to throw further
- **Materials:** foam javelins, soft shots, tape measure; safety: throwing lanes, wait for signal
- **Joy:** personal-best throw board

### Lesson 65 — Athletics personal best day
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can record personal bests across events and compare them with my Unit 1 fitness results." (UK NC KS2 PE (compare performances with previous ones to achieve personal bests))
- **Hook:** "Personal-best day: the only record that matters is your own."
- **Key activity:** Adult-led circuit: 30 m sprint, standing long jump, foam javelin, soft shot, 3-minute run laps and the Unit 1 plank and balance tests; children record results privately in their PB passport and compare with September.
- **Check for understanding:** "Where did you improve most since Unit 1, and what helped you improve?" → names an improved event with a reason (e.g. better arm drive in sprinting, regular skipping for stamina)
- **Differentiation:** support: an adult or buddy helps measure and record / stretch: set a specific summer target and plan how to train for it
- **Materials:** stopwatches, tape measures, foam javelins, soft shots, PB passports; safety: throwing lanes, water breaks
- **Joy:** personal-best certificates

### Lesson 66 — Orienteering: map scale and pacing
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can set a map, judge distance using the scale and my pace count, and navigate to control points." (UK NC KS2 PE (outdoor and adventurous activity); links to geography (scale))
- **Hook:** "The control is 50 metres away on the map. How many of your steps is that?"
- **Key activity:** Adult-led: quick retrieval from Year 2 — set the map so features line up with the real ones, thumb on your position. New: measure distance on the map with the scale (e.g. 1 cm = 10 m); find your pace count by walking a taped 10 m and counting double-steps (each time the same foot lands); use it on a star course where pairs run out to a control and back to the teacher each time, estimating the distance before they go.
- **Check for understanding:** "Your 10 m test took 8 double-steps. The control is 50 m away. About how many double-steps should you count?" → 50 m is five lots of 10 m, so about 5 × 8 = 40 double-steps
- **Differentiation:** support: an adult or older buddy and a map with photos of each control / stretch: plan and justify the quickest route to a far control
- **Materials:** school-grounds maps with a scale bar, tape measure, control markers with punches or codes; safety: boundaries agreed, adults posted
- **Joy:** star orienteering with secret code words at each control

### Lesson 67 — Orienteering: team challenge
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can navigate as a team to find control points in order, sharing roles." (UK NC KS2 PE (outdoor and adventurous activity: work in a team))
- **Hook:** "Race against the clock — but a team is only as fast as its slowest member!"
- **Key activity:** Adult-led: teams of three follow a score or line course; roles rotate (map reader, recorder, timekeeper); teams discuss route choices at each control. Debrief on teamwork and navigation mistakes.
- **Check for understanding:** "How did your team decide the route, and what did you do when you got lost?" → explains route choice (shortest or safest) and a recovery strategy (return to the last known point and re-orient the map)
- **Differentiation:** support: fewer controls on a simpler course / stretch: use a compass to check the map's orientation
- **Materials:** maps, control markers, compasses, stopwatches
- **Joy:** team orienteering race

### Lesson 68 — Orienteering: design a course
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can design and test an orienteering course for another group." (UK NC KS2 PE; links to geography (map skills))
- **Hook:** "Now you're the course planners — make it challenging but fair."
- **Key activity:** Pairs mark 5 control points on a blank school map, hide markers, write clue codes, and swap courses with another pair to test; give feedback on accuracy.
- **Check for understanding:** "How did you make sure your control points were marked accurately?" → placed each marker at a clear feature and checked it matched the map position
- **Differentiation:** support: choose from pre-marked possible controls / stretch: add a grid reference for each control
- **Materials:** blank maps, markers, pencils
- **Joy:** 'Course swap' challenge

### Lesson 69 — Water safety: rip currents, flags and cold water shock
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can spot a rip current, read beach flags and explain what cold water does to the body." (UK NC KS2 PE (swimming and water safety); UK RSHE (being safe))
- **Hook:** "Which part of this beach photo looks calmest? It may be the most dangerous."
- **Key activity:** Land-based lesson. Quick retrieval of the Year 2 water safety code (stop and think, stay together, float, call for help). New depth: how a rip current forms (water pushed in by waves flows back out to sea through a narrow channel) and how to spot one in photos (a darker, calmer-looking gap between breaking waves, foam or debris moving out to sea); what to do if caught (don't swim against it — rips can flow faster than strong swimmers; float, raise a hand and shout, then swim parallel to the shore to escape); beach flags differ by country, so learn the local system (e.g. swim between red-and-yellow flags where these are used); cold water shock — for about the first minute you gasp and breathe fast, so float and wait for it to pass before doing anything.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why is swimming straight back towards the beach the wrong choice in a rip current?" → the rip pulls outwards faster than you can swim, so you tire out; float and signal for help, then swim parallel to the shore to get out of the channel
- **Differentiation:** support: sort photo cards into 'rip' and 'no rip' / stretch: draw and label a diagram of how a rip forms and the escape route
- **Materials:** beach photos (with and without rips), flag cards, a tray of water to model a current (optional)
- **Joy:** 'Spot the rip' photo challenge

### Lesson 70 — Water safety: float, reach and throw
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can explain how to float to survive and help someone in trouble from the land using things around me." (UK NC KS2 PE (perform safe self-rescue in different water-based situations))
- **Hook:** "There's no lifebuoy at the lake. What around you could save a life?"
- **Key activity:** Land-based practice (and in the pool if lessons are available): retrieve 'float to live' (lean back, spread arms and legs, keep calm, catch your breath, then call for help). New: 'call, shout, reach or throw — don't go' with improvised aids — reach with a long stick, pole or jacket while lying down or kneeling back from the edge so you can't be pulled in; throw something that floats (a ball, an empty plastic bottle, a cool box); talk to the person ("kick your legs, grab it"); throwing-accuracy practice to hoops at 5 and 8 metres.
- **Check for understanding:** "At a lake with no lifebuoy, name two things you could use to help someone, and how you would keep yourself safe while doing it." → e.g. throw a football or empty bottle, or reach with a stick or jacket; call for help first, and lie down or stay back from the edge so you can't be pulled in — never go in
- **Differentiation:** support: picture sequence cards and role-play on land / stretch: plan the safest rescue for three different scenarios and justify each step
- **Materials:** floats, throw lines, empty plastic bottles, balls, sticks, hoops, role-play props
- **Joy:** 'Throw the float' target game on the field

**End-of-unit check:** Sprint, jump and throw with good technique and record personal bests; set a map, judge distance with the scale and a pace count and navigate to controls; spot a rip current and explain cold water shock; plan a safe rescue from land ('call, shout, reach or throw — don't go').

## End-of-year mastery checks

Assessed by watching children move and talking with them, not by written tests:
1. **Warms up and cools down** — leads a three-part warm-up and explains why. *See it:* a child-led warm-up.
2. **Invasion games** — passes, receives, moves into space and defends in small-sided games, and explains a tactic that transfers between games.
3. **Net and wall games** — rallies using a ready position, forehand and serve, and places the ball into space.
4. **Striking and fielding** — throws, catches and fields with good technique, bats into space, and knows the basic rules of rounders.
5. **Gymnastics** — performs a six-movement sequence with control, tension and smooth transitions.
6. **Dance** — performs a group dance with unison, canon and formation changes, and explains a dance's cultural origins.
7. **Athletics** — sprints, jumps and throws with correct technique and records personal bests.
8. **Outdoor adventure** — sets a map, judges distance with the scale and a pace count, and navigates to control points as part of a team.
9. **Water safety** — spots a rip current and explains how to escape it, explains cold water shock and 'float to live', and plans a safe rescue from land.
10. **Health** — plans a balanced meal and checks 60 active minutes a day from a log; knows what to do if a friend has an allergic reaction or asthma attack; carries a bag and crosses a road safely; uses clean–separate–cook–chill; applies the PANTS rule online and offline; explains the stress response and uses slow breathing; gives first aid for a burn and a nosebleed and can call for help in an emergency.

## Teacher guidance

**Maximum activity.** Keep talk short; children should be moving for most of every lesson. Use small-sided games (3v3, 4v4) so everyone touches the ball often.

**Safety first, always.** Check the space and equipment; ensure correct footwear and clothing, jewellery removed, water available and medical needs known (asthma, allergies, epilepsy). Use mats and spotting in gymnastics; never force a child to roll or balance on apparatus. Keep sticks below waist height. Throwing events need lanes and a clear 'throw/collect' signal. Water safety is taught on land unless qualified swimming teachers and lifeguards are present.

**Common misconceptions to watch for.**
- *Stretching cold muscles is a warm-up* — warm up first by raising heart rate; hold static stretches in the cool-down.
- *Sprinting from the start wins long runs* — pacing matters.
- *Everyone should chase the ball* — spacing and support win invasion games.
- *Defending means grabbing* — good defending is positioning and patience.
- *Healthy eating means never having treats* — balance and portion size matter.
- *Only 'sporty' people exercise* — everyone needs 60 active minutes a day; find activity you enjoy.
- *A strong swimmer is safe in any water* — cold water shock and currents affect everyone.
- *Keeping a worrying secret is loyal* — worrying secrets (including online ones) must be shared with a trusted adult.
- *Ice or butter helps a burn* — cool running water for 20 minutes.
- *Tip your head back for a nosebleed* — lean forward and pinch the soft part of the nose.
- *The calmest-looking water is the safest* — a rip current often looks like a calm gap between waves.

**Safeguarding.** Body-safety lessons may prompt disclosures. Know your school's safeguarding procedure, listen calmly, do not promise to keep secrets, and report immediately.

**Inclusion.** Adapt every activity for children with disabilities or medical conditions (e.g. seated volleyball, larger or bell balls, adjusted distances). Celebrate effort and personal progress, not only winning.

**Differentiation.** Support = simpler equipment (bigger, softer balls; lower nets), more space and time, cooperative before competitive. Stretch = add pressure (defenders, time), tactical decisions, leadership roles (captain, referee, coach).

**Vertical connections.** Builds on Years 1–2 fundamental movement and simple games. Health, orienteering and water-safety lessons open with a brief retrieval of the Year 2 versions (food and energy, water, sleep, handwashing, PANTS, calm breathing, emergency calls, setting the map, the water safety code) and then go further, so nothing is simply re-taught. Year 4 moves to more formal versions of sports with more tactics, longer gymnastic and dance sequences, fitness training principles, and health topics including the changing body.
