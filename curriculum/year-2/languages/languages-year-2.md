> Part of **the best international primary school** (see [`../../../SCHOOL-CHARTER.md`](../../../SCHOOL-CHARTER.md)).
> Built to [`../../PLANNING-BRIEF.md`](../../PLANNING-BRIEF.md). Drawn from

> the UK Key Stage 2 languages programme of study and the ACTFL Novice Low → Novice Mid benchmarks.

## Year overview

Year 2 is the **second year of Spanish**. Year 1 (ages 6–7) launched the language by ear: greetings
and feelings, numbers 0–20, colours and shapes, family, animals, the body, food, weather and the
days of the week, the Spanish sound rules, and a first *noticing* that words change for boys and
girls (*contento/contenta*, *este/esta*). Year 2 revisits those topics only to go deeper — every
repeated topic adds new structures (plurals, *mi/mis*, adjective agreement, *ayer*), reading and
writing. Year 2 keeps the emphasis on **listening and speaking** — children should hear and
say far more Spanish than they read or write — but now asks them to **string words into short
sentences** (*Tengo un perro negro. Mi cumpleaños es el cinco de mayo. Hoy hace sol.*), to **read**
familiar words and short sentences aloud with accurate Spanish sounds, and to **write** short
phrases from memory using a model.

**How the year sequences.** Unit 1 re-activates Year 1 language and secures the Spanish sound
system (vowels, *ll*, *ñ*, *j*, *rr*, silent *h*). Units 2–3 build **time and weather** — days,
months, dates and birthdays, then weather and seasons across the Spanish-speaking world (including
reversed seasons in the southern hemisphere). Unit 4 turns to **family** (*tengo*, *mi/mis*), Unit 5
to **animals and pets** with the first **adjective agreement** (*un gato negro / una gata negra*).
Unit 6 reads, retells and performs a **whole picture book** in Spanish (*La oruga muy hambrienta*),
recycling days, numbers and food. Unit 7 explores **festivals across Spain and Latin America**, and
Unit 8 is a year review ending in a **class performance and "Mi presentación"** — a 1-minute talk
about themselves.

**What Year 3 (ages 8–9) relies on from this year:** confident pronunciation; numbers to 50 used
in dates and ages; days, months and weather routines; *tengo / no tengo*, *me gusta / no me gusta*,
*es / hay*; nouns with *el/la/un/una* and adjectives placed after the noun and agreeing in gender.
Year 3 revisits family, animals, the calendar and weather with longer sentences built on *tener*
and *ser*, and adds the home and clothes.

**Swappable language.** Spanish is the default, chosen because it is spoken as a first language
in more than 20 countries across four continents. Another language (for example French or Hebrew —
see `knowledge-base/subjects/french` and `knowledge-base/subjects/hebrew`) can replace it with the
same unit structure, topics and lesson routine.

**Lesson routine (20–25 min):** a Spanish greeting and the date/weather routine (2 min) → song or
chant (3 min) → new language by ear with pictures and actions → games for repetition → speaking in
pairs → (from mid-unit) reading or writing with a model → a quick "¿Qué has aprendido?" check.
Instructions are in English where needed; the target language is always in Spanish. Pronunciation
hints below are plain-English approximations with the **STRESSED** syllable in capitals.

## Unit 1 — ¡Hola otra vez! Review and Spanish sounds

**Essential question:** How can I sound truly Spanish — and what do I remember from last year?

**Key vocabulary:** hola, adiós, buenos días, buenas tardes, ¿cómo estás?, estoy bien / mal / regular / cansado / contento, ¿cómo te llamas?, me llamo…, ¿cuántos años tienes?, tengo … años, los números 0–31, los colores, las vocales (a, e, i, o, u), el abecedario

### Lesson 1 — ¡Hola otra vez! Greetings and feelings
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can greet a partner, ask how they are and answer with a feeling." (KS2 Languages – speaking; ACTFL Novice Mid interpersonal)
- **Hook:** A puppet, "Paco," arrives back from holiday in Mexico and greets every child by name.
- **Key activity:** Revise *hola / buenos días / ¿cómo estás?* by ear; add feelings with faces and actions (*contento*, *cansado*, *regular*, *mal*); circle game passing the puppet: each child greets and answers.
- **Check for understanding:** "Paco says '¿Cómo estás?' — what is he asking, and how will you answer if you're tired?" → How are you? — *Estoy cansado/cansada.*
- **Differentiation:** support: feeling-face cards to point to while saying the word / stretch: add *¿y tú?* to keep the conversation going and use *muy* (*muy bien*).
- **Materials:** hand puppet, feeling-face flashcards, a map of the Spanish-speaking world
- **Joy:** "Feelings mirror" — partners copy each other's feeling face and name it.

### Lesson 2 — Las vocales: the five pure vowels
- **Duration:** 20 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can say the five Spanish vowel sounds clearly and read them in words." (KS2 Languages – phonics)
- **Hook:** The "vowel song" — *A, E, I, O, U, el burro sabe más que tú* (a traditional children's rhyme).
- **Key activity:** Model each short, pure vowel (a = "ah", e = "eh", i = "ee", o = "oh", u = "oo") with a hand action; read syllables *ma me mi mo mu*, *pa pe pi po pu*; read known words (*mesa*, *uno*, *hola*).
- **Check for understanding:** "Why does Spanish 'e' in 'mesa' not sound like English 'ee'?" → Spanish e is always a short "eh" sound.
- **Differentiation:** support: vowel action cards and echo-reading only / stretch: read unfamiliar two-syllable words (*luna*, *pipa*, *moto*) accurately.
- **Materials:** vowel action cards, syllable cards, a recording of the rhyme
- **Joy:** "Vowel orchestra" — five groups each sing one vowel when the conductor points.

### Lesson 3 — Tricky letters: ll, ñ, j, h, r and rr
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can pronounce ll, ñ, j, silent h and rr in Spanish words." (KS2 Languages – phonics)
- **Hook:** "Tongue-twister challenge": *Erre con erre, cigarro…* — a well-known Spanish trabalenguas (teacher reads it slowly).
- **Key activity:** Sound "studio": *ll* like "y" (*llama*), *ñ* like "ny" (*niño*), *j* like a breathy "h" (*jirafa*), *h* silent (*hola*), *rr* rolled (*perro*); sort picture-words by sound; practise rolling with "drrr" motor noises.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why do *rojo* and *perro* both have the long rolled sound, but *pero* does not?" → An r at the start of a word is always rolled, just like rr; a single r between two vowels is a quick tap. (New this year — Year 1 learned only the pero/perro pair.)
- **Differentiation:** support: three sounds only, lots of echoing / stretch: read five new words with these letters aloud without a model.
- **Materials:** sound-picture cards, mirrors (to watch mouths), recording device
- **Joy:** Motorbike "rr" races — whoever rolls longest wins.

### Lesson 4 — Números 21–31: the *veinti-* pattern
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can count and recognise numbers 0–31 quickly in Spanish, including the new numbers 21–31." (KS2 Languages – listening/speaking; Maths link)
- **Hook:** "Lotería" (Mexican bingo) boards with numbers.
- **Key activity:** Retrieve 0–20 from Year 1 with actions; recall the 16–19 "joining trick" (*dieciséis* = "ten and six"), then use it for the new numbers 21–29 (*veintiuno, veintidós, veintitrés…* — note the accents on *veintidós, veintitrés, veintiséis*) and 30–31 (*treinta, treinta y uno*); play number bingo and "¿Cuántos?" with objects.
- **Check for understanding:** "How is 'veintitrés' built?" → *veinte* (20) + *tres* (3) joined into one word.
- **Differentiation:** support: 0–20 with number line / stretch: say numbers backwards from 31 and find the pattern for 31 (*treinta y uno* — three words).
- **Materials:** lotería/bingo boards, counters, number cards, number line 0–31
- **Joy:** Lotería with a "¡Lotería!" shout for the winner.

### Lesson 5 — ¿Cuántos años tienes? Ages and introductions
- **Duration:** 20 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can introduce myself with my name and age and ask a partner." (KS2 Languages – speaking)
- **Hook:** A mystery voice message: "Hola, me llamo Lucía, tengo ocho años. ¿Y tú?"
- **Key activity:** Revise *me llamo / tengo … años*; practise with a partner; "speed-meeting" — find three classmates and record their names and ages in a grid.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why do we say 'tengo ocho años' (I have eight years) and not 'soy ocho años'?" → In Spanish, age uses *tener* (to have).
- **Differentiation:** support: sentence strips to read / stretch: add where they live (*vivo en…*).
- **Materials:** recorded message, speed-meeting grid, sentence strips
- **Joy:** Speed-meeting with a bell to change partners.

### Lesson 6 — Classroom objects (new) with colours
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can name eight classroom objects with el or la and say their colour." (KS2 Languages – vocabulary; grammar – gender)
- **Hook:** A "bolsa misteriosa" (mystery bag) of classroom objects.
- **Key activity:** New words (not taught in Year 1): *el lápiz, el libro, el cuaderno, el sacapuntas, la regla, la goma, la mochila, la silla*; pull out objects and name them with *el/la* and a retrieved colour (*el lápiz azul*, *la regla roja*); introduce *el/la* formally — every noun is masculine or feminine — building on Year 1's *este/esta*.
- **Check for understanding:** "You know *la regla* and *el libro*. Why do we say *la regla roja* but *el libro rojo*?" → *Regla* is a feminine (la) word, so the colour ends in -a; *libro* is masculine (el), so it ends in -o — and in both the colour comes after the object.
- **Differentiation:** support: object + colour cards to match / stretch: plurals (*dos lápices azules*).
- **Materials:** classroom objects, a cloth bag, colour cards
- **Joy:** Mystery-bag guessing by touch — "¿Es un lápiz?"

### Lesson 7 — Reading aloud with Spanish sounds
- **Duration:** 20 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can read familiar words and short phrases aloud with accurate Spanish sounds." (KS2 Languages – reading)
- **Hook:** Flashcards flipped from picture to word — "Can you read it the Spanish way?"
- **Key activity:** Read word cards from Unit 1; partner-read short phrases (*me llamo Ana*, *tengo siete años*); spot "false friends" in pronunciation (the silent h).
- **Check for understanding:** "How do you read 'hoja'?" → "OH-ha": silent h, j like a breathy h.
- **Differentiation:** support: picture-supported cards / stretch: read an unfamiliar sentence using the sound rules.
- **Materials:** word and phrase cards, a pocket chart
- **Joy:** "Flash-read" race against a sand timer.

### Lesson 8 — Unit check: ¡Hola otra vez!
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can hold a short conversation and read words with Spanish sounds." (Assessment)
- **Hook:** Paco the puppet interviews each pair.
- **Key activity:** Pairs perform a greeting–name–age–feeling conversation; teacher notes pronunciation; a listening task (circle the number/colour you hear); read five words aloud.
- **Check for understanding:** "Listen: 'veintiocho'. Which number is it?" → 28.
- **Differentiation:** support: conversation prompt cards / stretch: add a question of their own.
- **Materials:** puppet, listening sheet, word cards
- **Joy:** Paco awards a Spanish sticker ("¡Muy bien!").

**End-of-unit check:** Hold a four-line conversation (greeting, name, age, feeling) with a partner and read five Spanish words aloud using correct vowel and letter sounds.

## Unit 2 — El calendario: days, months and birthdays

**Essential question:** How do I talk about the days, the months and the most important date of all — my birthday?

**Key vocabulary:** lunes, martes, miércoles, jueves, viernes, sábado, domingo, hoy, mañana, ayer, la semana, enero, febrero, marzo, abril, mayo, junio, julio, agosto, septiembre, octubre, noviembre, diciembre, el mes, el cumpleaños, ¿cuándo es tu cumpleaños?, mi cumpleaños es el…, ¡feliz cumpleaños!, los números 32–50

### Lesson 9 — Los días de la semana
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can say the days of the week in order and say what day it is today." (KS2 Languages – vocabulary)
- **Hook:** A days-of-the-week song with actions.
- **Key activity:** Learn days by ear; note they are not capitalised in Spanish; start a daily routine: *Hoy es lunes*; order jumbled day cards.
- **Check for understanding:** "If *mañana es sábado*, what day is *hoy*, and what day was *ayer*?" → *Hoy es viernes; ayer fue jueves.* (Year 1 practised only *hoy* and *mañana*.)
- **Differentiation:** support: song lyrics with pictures / stretch: say *ayer* (yesterday) and *mañana* for any day.
- **Materials:** day cards, a class calendar, song recording
- **Joy:** "Human week" — seven children hold cards and shuffle; the class puts them in order.

### Lesson 10 — My week: activities
- **Duration:** 20 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can say what I do on a day using simple phrases (el lunes…)." (KS2 Languages – speaking)
- **Hook:** A picture timetable of Paco's week.
- **Key activity:** Learn 5 activity phrases (*natación* — swimming, *fútbol*, *música*, *arte*, *parque*); say "El lunes: natación"; pairs describe their week.
- **Check for understanding:** "How do you say 'On Saturday: football'?" → *El sábado: fútbol.*
- **Differentiation:** support: picture timetable to point to / stretch: add *me gusta* to say which day they like and why (*me gusta el sábado*).
- **Materials:** picture timetable, activity cards
- **Joy:** Design a "dream week" timetable.

### Lesson 11 — Los meses del año
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can say the months in order." (KS2 Languages – vocabulary)
- **Hook:** A months song or chant clapped in rhythm.
- **Key activity:** Learn months by ear with seasonal pictures; notice similarities with English (*julio*, *octubre*); order month cards; "Stand up if your birthday is in…".
- **Check for understanding:** "Which months look most like English? Which is hardest?" → e.g. *octubre* looks like October; *enero* (January) is hardest.
- **Differentiation:** support: month cards with pictures / stretch: say the month before and after any month.
- **Materials:** month cards, seasonal pictures
- **Joy:** "Month musical chairs."

### Lesson 12 — Números 32–50
- **Duration:** 20 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can count to 50 and read two-digit numbers in Spanish." (KS2 Languages; Maths link)
- **Hook:** "How many days until the school fiesta?" (a number between 32 and 50).
- **Key activity:** Teach *treinta y…* pattern (32–39), *cuarenta*, *cuarenta y…*, *cincuenta*; count in tens; play "¿Qué número?" with number cards.
- **Check for understanding:** "How do you say 45?" → *cuarenta y cinco* (three words, joined by *y*).
- **Differentiation:** support: tens only / stretch: count in 5s to 50.
- **Materials:** number cards, 100-square, dice
- **Joy:** "Number ball" — throw a soft ball and say the next number.

### Lesson 13 — La fecha: saying the date
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can say and read today's date in Spanish." (KS2 Languages – speaking/reading)
- **Hook:** The class calendar with a big question mark.
- **Key activity:** Model *Hoy es lunes, el cinco de mayo*; learn that the day comes before the month; read dates from cards; start a daily date routine led by a "calendar helper."
- **Check for understanding:** "How do you write 3 March in Spanish?" → *el tres de marzo* (lower-case month).
- **Differentiation:** support: date frame "Hoy es ___, el ___ de ___" / stretch: say a date in the past with *ayer fue…*.
- **Materials:** class calendar, date cards, frame
- **Joy:** Becoming the calendar helper for the week.

### Lesson 14 — ¿Cuándo es tu cumpleaños?
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can ask and answer when a birthday is." (KS2 Languages – speaking)
- **Hook:** A birthday cake picture with Spanish candles.
- **Key activity:** Practise *¿Cuándo es tu cumpleaños? — Mi cumpleaños es el … de …*; survey classmates; build a class birthday graph by month.
- **Check for understanding:** "Your partner says 'el veinte de octubre'. When is their birthday?" → 20 October.
- **Differentiation:** support: frame with month cards / stretch: ask *¿cuántos años tienes?* too and report back (*Ella tiene…*).
- **Materials:** survey sheets, birthday graph, cake picture
- **Joy:** Sing *Cumpleaños feliz* to everyone with a birthday this month.

### Lesson 15 — Reading and writing dates
- **Duration:** 20 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can write a date and a birthday sentence from a model." (KS2 Languages – writing)
- **Hook:** Birthday invitations in Spanish.
- **Key activity:** Read invitation cards (day, date, time in simple form); write own birthday sentence from a model with correct spelling and lower-case month.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why is 'Mayo' with a capital M wrong in the middle of a Spanish sentence?" → Months are not capitalised in Spanish.
- **Differentiation:** support: copy from a model with words to choose / stretch: write an invitation with day and date.
- **Materials:** invitation cards, writing frames
- **Joy:** Design a party invitation.

### Lesson 16 — Games and retrieval: el calendario
- **Duration:** 20 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can use days, months and dates quickly in games." (Retrieval)
- **Hook:** "Calendario relay."
- **Key activity:** Relay race: teams order days and months; "Simon dice" with dates; memory game matching numbers and words.
- **Check for understanding:** "What month comes after junio?" → *julio*.
- **Differentiation:** support: cards visible / stretch: dates spoken quickly by the teacher.
- **Materials:** relay cards, memory cards
- **Joy:** Relay race.

### Lesson 17 — Unit check: el calendario
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can show I understand and use days, months, numbers and dates." (Assessment)
- **Hook:** "Paco's diary" — listen to Paco's week.
- **Key activity:** Listening (tick days and dates heard), speaking (say today's date and your birthday), reading (match dates to numbers), writing (one birthday sentence).
- **Check for understanding:** "Listen: 'el catorce de febrero'. What date is it?" → 14 February.
- **Differentiation:** support: picture options / stretch: write two sentences.
- **Materials:** listening recording, check sheet
- **Joy:** Party-hat sticker.

**End-of-unit check:** Say today's full date and your birthday aloud, understand three spoken dates, and write your birthday sentence correctly.

## Unit 3 — El tiempo: weather and seasons

**Essential question:** What's the weather like today — and why is it summer in Argentina when it's winter in Spain?

**Key vocabulary:** ¿qué tiempo hace?, hace sol, hace calor, hace frío, hace viento, hace buen tiempo, hace mal tiempo, llueve, nieva, está nublado, hay tormenta, las estaciones, la primavera, el verano, el otoño, el invierno, en (enero)

### Lesson 18 — ¿Qué tiempo hace?
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can say what the weather is like using six weather phrases." (KS2 Languages – speaking)
- **Hook:** A weather-presenter's map and a toy microphone.
- **Key activity:** Retrieve Year 1's weather phrases with actions (fan yourself for *hace calor*, shiver for *hace frío*) in two minutes, then add new ones: *hace buen tiempo, hace mal tiempo, hay tormenta*; add to the daily routine; look out of the window: *Hoy…*.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why do we say 'hace' for sun, heat and cold but 'llueve' for rain?" → Different verbs: *hace* ("it makes") for some weather; *llueve* means "it rains."
- **Differentiation:** support: weather symbol cards to hold up / stretch: combine two (*hace sol pero hace frío*).
- **Materials:** weather symbol cards, a map, toy microphone
- **Joy:** "Weather actions" game — the teacher calls, the class acts.

### Lesson 19 — Weather around the Spanish-speaking world
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can describe the weather in different cities from a weather map." (KS2 Languages; Geography link)
- **Hook:** Real weather data for Madrid, Mexico City, Buenos Aires and Bogotá today.
- **Key activity:** Read a symbol map; pairs ask *¿Qué tiempo hace en Lima?* and answer; locate cities on the world map.
- **Check for understanding:** "What will you say if the map shows a cloud and rain in Bogotá?" → *En Bogotá está nublado y llueve.*
- **Differentiation:** support: two cities with symbols / stretch: add temperature (*veinte grados*).
- **Materials:** weather maps, world map, city cards
- **Joy:** TV weather forecast role play.

### Lesson 20 — Las estaciones
- **Duration:** 20 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can name the four seasons and link them to weather." (KS2 Languages – vocabulary)
- **Hook:** Vivaldi's *The Four Seasons* — "Which season does this music sound like?" (Music link)
- **Key activity:** Learn seasons; match to weather phrases; *En invierno hace frío*.
- **Check for understanding:** "Say one sentence about summer." → *En verano hace calor/hace sol.*
- **Differentiation:** support: picture-to-word matching / stretch: include a month (*En julio hace calor*).
- **Materials:** season pictures, music recording
- **Joy:** Seasons mime with music.

### Lesson 21 — Opposite seasons: north and south
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can explain in simple Spanish that seasons are opposite in the southern hemisphere." (KS2 Languages – intercultural; Geography/Science link)
- **Hook:** A photo of Christmas on a sunny beach in Argentina.
- **Key activity:** Compare *en diciembre en España hace frío; en Argentina hace calor*; use a globe and lamp to show tilt simply; pairs make "two-hemisphere" sentences.
- **Check for understanding:** "Is July winter or summer in Chile? How do you know?" → Winter (*invierno*), because Chile is in the southern hemisphere.
- **Differentiation:** support: sentence frame with two blanks / stretch: write a postcard sentence from Chile in July.
- **Materials:** globe, lamp, photos, world map
- **Joy:** "Summer Christmas" postcard.

### Lesson 22 — Weather song and poem
- **Duration:** 20 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can sing or recite a weather song with good pronunciation." (KS2 Languages – speaking/performance)
- **Hook:** A rain-stick and a weather song.
- **Key activity:** Learn a short weather song or chant; add actions and percussion; perform in groups.
- **Check for understanding:** "Which line talks about wind, and how did you show it?" → Identifies *hace viento* and the action.
- **Differentiation:** support: chorus only / stretch: invent a new verse with a different weather phrase.
- **Materials:** rain-stick, song lyrics, percussion
- **Joy:** Percussion "storm."

### Lesson 23 — Reading a weather report
- **Duration:** 20 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can read a short weather report and answer questions." (KS2 Languages – reading)
- **Hook:** A child-friendly weather report card.
- **Key activity:** Read a 4-sentence report; underline weather phrases; answer picture questions; spot the cognate *temperatura*.
- **Check for understanding:** "What will the weather be like in Sevilla on Saturday according to the report?" → e.g. *hace sol y hace calor*.
- **Differentiation:** support: report with symbols / stretch: longer report with days of the week.
- **Materials:** weather report cards
- **Joy:** "Weather detective" badges.

### Lesson 24 — Writing a weather forecast
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can write a 3-sentence weather forecast using a model." (KS2 Languages – writing)
- **Hook:** "You are the TV weather presenter."
- **Key activity:** Write a forecast for three cities or three days from a model; rehearse; perform in front of a map (filmed).
- **Check for understanding:** "Read your forecast: which words tell us the day?" → Points to *el lunes*, etc.
- **Differentiation:** support: fill-the-gap frame / stretch: add *pero* and temperature.
- **Materials:** writing frames, map, tablet for filming
- **Joy:** Filmed weather forecasts.

### Lesson 25 — Unit check: el tiempo
- **Duration:** 20 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can understand and describe weather and seasons." (Assessment)
- **Hook:** "Mystery forecast."
- **Key activity:** Listening (match forecasts to symbols), speaking (describe today and a season), reading (short report), writing (one sentence).
- **Check for understanding:** "What do you say when it's cloudy and cold?" → *Está nublado y hace frío.*
- **Differentiation:** support: picture choices / stretch: two-hemisphere sentence.
- **Materials:** recordings, check sheet
- **Joy:** Weather-sticker award.

**End-of-unit check:** Describe today's weather and a season in two sentences, understand a spoken forecast, and read a short weather report.

## Unit 4 — Mi familia

**Essential question:** How can I introduce the people who matter to me in Spanish?

**Key vocabulary:** la familia, la madre / mamá, el padre / papá, el hermano, la hermana, el abuelo, la abuela, el tío, la tía, el primo, la prima, el bebé, tengo, no tengo, se llama, mi, mis, ¿cuántos hermanos tienes?, hijo único / hija única

### Lesson 26 — ¿Quién es? Family words
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can name family members in Spanish." (KS2 Languages – vocabulary)
- **Hook:** Paco the puppet shows photos of his big Mexican family.
- **Key activity:** Retrieve Year 1's family words with a quick photo game, then add new ones (*el primo, la prima, el bebé, hijo único*); notice masculine/feminine pairs (*hermano/hermana*, *abuelo/abuela*, *tío/tía*); play "¿Quién es?" pointing at the photos.
- **Check for understanding:** "If 'abuelo' is grandfather, what do you think 'abuela' is? Why?" → Grandmother — the -a ending is often feminine.
- **Differentiation:** support: five core words with pictures / stretch: *primo/prima* and *tío/tía*.
- **Materials:** family photo cards, puppet
- **Joy:** "Family bingo."

### Lesson 27 — Tengo… / No tengo…
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can say who is in my family using tengo and no tengo." (KS2 Languages – speaking; grammar)
- **Hook:** "¿Cuántos hermanos tienes?" asked to the class.
- **Key activity:** Model *Tengo un hermano y dos hermanas. No tengo hermanos.*; note that *un* becomes nothing after *no* (*no tengo hermanos*); partner interviews; *hijo único / hija única*.
- **Check for understanding:** "A classmate says '*No tengo un hermano*'. What would a Spanish speaker usually say instead, and why?" → *No tengo hermanos* — after *no tengo* we usually drop *un/una* and use the plural.

**Key vocabulary:** el perro, el gato, el pez, el pájaro, el conejo, la tortuga, el caballo, el ratón, la serpiente, la mascota, ¿tienes una mascota?, grande, pequeño/a, negro/a, blanco/a, rojo/a, bonito/a, gordo/a, rápido/a, lento/a, verde, azul, marrón, el león, el elefante, el mono, la jirafa, el cocodrilo, el loro, la llama, el jaguar

## End-of-year mastery checks

1. **Pronunciation** — reads familiar and new words aloud with accurate Spanish vowels and the letters ll, ñ, j, h, rr. *Seen:* reading ten words aloud.
2. **Conversation** — greets, asks and answers name, age, feelings and birthday in a short exchange. *Seen:* partner conversation.
3. **Numbers to 50** — understands and says numbers 0–50, including in dates and ages. *Seen:* number bingo and date routine.
4. **Calendar** — says the days, months and today's date; asks and answers *¿Cuándo es tu cumpleaños?* *Seen:* calendar-helper routine.
5. **Weather** — describes the weather and seasons, including that seasons are opposite in the southern hemisphere. *Seen:* weather forecast.
6. **Family** — describes family members with *tengo / no tengo*, *mi / mis* and *se llama*. *Seen:* family presentation.
7. **Adjectives** — places adjectives after nouns and makes -o/-a agreement correctly with familiar nouns. *Seen:* animal riddle.
8. **Reading** — understands a short unseen text about a child (5–6 sentences) and answers true/false questions. *Seen:* Mateo's letter.
9. **Writing** — writes 3–5 short sentences about themselves from memory with mostly correct spelling and ¿ ¡ punctuation. *Seen:* writing check.
10. **Intercultural understanding** — names at least three Spanish-speaking countries on a map and describes one festival. *Seen:* festival postcard.

## Teacher guidance

**Common errors (and the fix)**
- *English vowel sounds* ("may-sa" for *mesa*): model short, pure vowels and use the vowel actions daily.
- *Pronouncing the h* (*hola* as "ho-la" with an h): h is always silent in Spanish.
- *Adjective before the noun* (*un negro gato*): colours and most describing words come after the noun.
- *No agreement* (*la casa blanco*): -o adjectives change to -a for feminine nouns; colour-code masculine and feminine.
- *Capitalising days and months*: they are lower case in Spanish.
- *Forgetting ¿ and ¡*: Spanish marks questions and exclamations at the start too.
- *"Soy ocho años"*: age uses *tener* — *tengo ocho años*.
- *Exceptions to watch:* *la mano* (feminine despite -o), *el día* (masculine despite -a), *el agua* (feminine noun that takes *el*) — mention only when they come up.

**Variation across the Spanish-speaking world.** Accept regional vocabulary and pronunciation (e.g. *el ordenador* in Spain vs *la computadora* in Latin America; *vosotros* is used in Spain but not in Latin America — not taught this year). Present Spanish as the language of many countries, not only Spain.

**Little and often.** Two 20–25 minute lessons plus a 2-minute daily routine (greeting, date, weather) outperform one long lesson. Use Spanish for classroom routines (*silencio, por favor; ¡muy bien!; ¡vamos!*).

**Assessment is by listening and watching.** Keep a simple tracker for the four skills; celebrate risk-taking — a child who tries a sentence with an error is making more progress than one who stays silent.

**Vertical alignment.** Year 1 built greetings, numbers 0–20, colours, family, animals, body, food, weather and days by ear, with the Spanish sound rules. This year adds numbers to 50, classroom objects, months and dates, plurals, *mi/mis*, adjective agreement, reading and writing. Year 3 (ages 8–9) revisits family, animals, the calendar and weather with *tener* and *ser* in longer sentences and adds the home and clothes — so secure pronunciation, dates, *tengo*, *me gusta* and adjective agreement before the summer.
