## Lesson 19 — Questions & exclamations (the special marks)
**English · Year 2 · Unit 4 (Sentence writing — VGP)** | **Duration:** 20 min | **Objective:** "By the end, I can write a question with a ? and a true exclamation starting *What/How*."

**You will need:** statement cards (*The sky is blue. The cake is big.*), a question-word bank card (*who, what, where, when, why, how*), mini-whiteboard, a "? hook" and "! " card.
**Prior knowledge to retrieve:** sentence types and end marks (Lesson 18); capital letters and full stops.
**Key vocabulary:** **question**, **question mark (?)**, **exclamation**, **exclamation mark (!)**, **What…!/How…!**
**What to emphasize (the heart of the lesson):** a question makes your **voice go up** and needs a **?**; a *true* exclamation **sentence** starts with **What** or **How** and ends with **!**. Pre-empt putting a full stop on a question.

---

### ⏱ 0–3 min · HOOK
> **Say:** "Listen to my voice: 'The sky is blue.' Flat. Now: 'Is the sky blue?' — hear it go UP at the end? An *asking* sentence needs a special hook on the end — a question mark. Let's master it."
**Show:** the "? hook" card.  **Do:** say a statement then a question, exaggerating the rising voice.
**Ask (retrieve prior knowledge):** "What mark ends a normal telling sentence?" → *listen for:* "a full stop."

### ⏱ 3–8 min · EXPLAIN (I do)
> **Say:** "To make a **question**, I often start with a question word — *who, what, where, when, why, how* — or flip the order: 'The sky is blue.' → 'Is the sky blue?' and I end with a **?**. A real **exclamation sentence** starts with **What** or **How**: 'The cake is big.' → '**What** a big cake**!**' It shows a big feeling."
**Show / Do:** transform two statements into questions; transform one into a *What…!* exclamation on the board.
**Emphasize:** "Asking → **?** ; *What/How* + big feeling → **!**"

### ⏱ 8–12 min · EXAMPLE (we do)
> **Say:** "Here's a question I wrote wrong — it ends in a full stop. Fix it with me."
**Do:** child changes 'Is it raining.' to 'Is it raining?'; together turn 'The dog is fast.' into 'What a fast dog!'
**Ask:** "Turn 'The cake is big.' into an exclamation." → *listen for:* "What a big cake!"

### ⏱ 12–16 min · CHECK
**Ask:** "Turn 'The cake is big.' into an exclamation, then write a question about the cake."
- ✅ **Expected:** *What a big cake!* and e.g. *Is the cake big?* — probe: *"How does your voice sound for the question?"*
- ❌ **If exclamation doesn't start What/How:** confusing loud statement with exclamation sentence → **fix:** "An exclamation *sentence* begins **What** or **How** — start it again with 'What…'."
- ❌ **If question ends in a full stop:** → **fix:** "Read it aloud — your voice went up, so it needs the **?** hook."

### ⏱ 16–19 min · PRACTICE (you do)
**Do:** Child writes **two** questions (using the word bank) and **one** *What/How* exclamation. Once secure, interleave: spot which of three sentences is a question vs exclamation vs statement.
**Watch for:** missing ? ; exclamations that are really just statements with a !.
> **If the child is stuck (missed twice):** 1) don't reveal; 2) name *why* — end mark or exclamation form; 3) say it aloud together and listen for the rising voice; 4) one hint: "Start with a question word"; 5) re-ask a simpler flip; 6) "Mistakes show us what to practise — smaller step."

### ⏱ 19–20 min · RECAP
> **Say:** "How does a question sound, and how do exclamation sentences start?" → *listen for:* "voice up, ? ; start with What or How, !"
**Process praise:** "You heard your voice rise and added the ? every time — great listening-to-your-writing."
**Revisit later:** sentence types feed the writing in Unit 5; quick recall in Lesson 25.

---

**Differentiation**
- **Support (gentle):** question-word bank (*who, what, where, when, why, how*) on the desk.
- **Stretch (challenge):** explain why a command with *!* (e.g. "Stop!") is not an exclamation *sentence*.

**Extension (if time allows):** write a tiny interview — three questions for a favourite character.
**Assessment evidence:** child writes correctly punctuated questions and a *What/How* exclamation.

## Lesson 20 — Joining clauses: and / but / or, and because / when / if / that
**English · Year 2 · Unit 4 (Sentence writing — VGP)** | **Duration:** 25 min | **Objective:** "By the end, I can join two ideas with the right conjunction, including *because* for a reason."

**You will need:** sentence-pair cards, a conjunction bank card (*and, but, or* / *because, when, if, that* with meanings), mini-whiteboard.
**Prior knowledge to retrieve:** what a sentence is; end marks.
**Key vocabulary:** **conjunction** (joining word), **and/but/or** (join equal ideas), **because** (gives a reason), **when/if/that**.
**What to emphasize (the heart of the lesson):** the joining word must **match the meaning** — especially **because** gives a **reason**. Pre-empt joining everything with "and".

---

### ⏱ 0–3 min · HOOK
> **Say:** "'I was happy.' …Why, though? Add the magic word **because** and tell us the reason: 'I was happy **because** it was my birthday!' Today we join ideas with exactly the right word."
**Show:** the half-sentence 'I was happy ___'.  **Do:** invite a 'because' ending.
**Ask (retrieve prior knowledge):** "Give me two tiny sentences about a dog." → *listen for:* two simple clauses to join.

### ⏱ 3–9 min · EXPLAIN (I do)
> **Say:** "**Conjunctions** join ideas. Some join *equal* ideas: **and** (adds), **but** (shows a difference), **or** (a choice) — 'I like cats **and** dogs', 'It's small **but** strong'. Others add an extra part: **because** (a reason), **when** (a time), **if** (a maybe), **that**. Watch: 'I wore my coat **because** it was cold' — *because* tells us *why*."
**Show / Do:** join pairs on the board choosing the word that fits the meaning; underline *because* + the reason.
**Emphasize:** "Pick the joining word that **matches the meaning** — *because* = a reason."

### ⏱ 9–14 min · EXAMPLE (we do)
> **Say:** "Join these two with me: 'It was raining' + 'we stayed in'. Which word shows *why* we stayed in?"
**Do:** child chooses 'because' / 'so'; then joins 'You can have an apple' + 'a pear' with 'or'.
**Ask:** "Which word shows a reason: *and, but, because*?" → *listen for:* "because."

### ⏱ 14–18 min · CHECK
**Ask:** "Finish with the best word: 'I was tired ___ I went to bed.' (and / but / because)"
- ✅ **Expected:** because — probe: *"What does 'because' tell the reader?"* (the reason).
- ❌ **If "and":** defaulting to *and* → **fix (KB):** "Does 'and' tell us *why*? We need the reason word — **because**."
- ❌ **If "but":** picked a contrast for a reason → **fix:** "'But' shows a difference. Going to bed *because* you're tired is a reason."

### ⏱ 18–23 min · PRACTICE (you do)
**Do:** Child completes **five** sentences each with the best conjunction (mix of *and/but/or/because*). Once secure, interleave: write one sentence using *when* or *if* correctly.
**Watch for:** over-using *and*; *because* with no real reason after it.
> **If the child is stuck (missed twice):** 1) don't reveal; 2) name *why* — meaning mismatch; 3) read the two ideas and ask "add? difference? choice? reason?"; 4) one hint: point to the conjunction bank meanings; 5) re-ask an *and/because* pair; 6) "smaller step."

### ⏱ 23–25 min · RECAP
> **Say:** "Tell me a sentence with *because* and a real reason." → *listen for:* a clause + because + a genuine reason.
**Process praise:** "You stopped reaching for 'and' and chose the word that fit the meaning — that's precise writing."
**Revisit later:** *because* reasons power the inference work in Unit 5.

---

**Differentiation**
- **Support (gentle):** conjunction bank with child-friendly meanings beside each word.
- **Stretch (challenge):** write a sentence using *when* or *if* correctly, then one with *that*.

**Extension (if time allows):** finish three silly "I was late because…" sentences with your own reasons.
**Assessment evidence:** child joins clauses with the meaning-correct conjunction and gives a real reason after *because*.

> **End-of-Unit 4 check:** Child sorts a word into noun/adjective/verb, expands a noun into a phrase with a comma, sorts three sentences into types with end marks, and joins a pair with *because* giving a real reason. Mastery = word class correct, comma used, end marks right, reasoned *because*.

## Unit 5 — Comprehension & composition

**Essential question:** How do I read between the lines, and how do I write a clear set of instructions?
**Key vocabulary:** retrieval, inference, clue, evidence, *because the text says…*, instructions, command, order words (*first, next, then, finally*), "you will need".

## Lesson 21 — Retrieval vs inference (be a reading detective)
**English · Year 2 · Unit 5 (Comprehension & composition)** | **Duration:** 20 min | **Objective:** "By the end, I can tell a retrieval question from an inference question."

**You will need:** a short passage (Mia opens the fridge; her tummy rumbles), two question cards, a highlighter, a detective prop (magnifier).
**Prior knowledge to retrieve:** reading a sentence for meaning; answering "what happened?"
**Key vocabulary:** **retrieval** ("the answer is written there"), **inference** ("you work it out from clues"), **clue**, **evidence**.
**What to emphasize (the heart of the lesson):** some answers are **right there** (retrieval); others you **work out from clues** (inference). Naming which kind tells you *how* to find the answer.

---

### ⏱ 0–3 min · HOOK
> **Say:** "Detective time! Some answers are written right there on the page — easy to grab. Others are *hidden* — you crack them from the clues, like a real detective. Let's learn to tell them apart."
**Show:** the magnifier; the short passage.  **Do:** read the passage aloud once, warmly.
**Ask (retrieve prior knowledge):** "In the story, what did Mia open?" → *listen for:* "the fridge" (notice: it's written there).

### ⏱ 3–8 min · EXPLAIN (I do)
> **Say:** "Two kinds of question. **Retrieval**: the answer is written in the words — 'What did Mia open?' The text *says* 'the fridge'. **Inference**: you work it out — 'How does Mia feel?' It never says 'hungry', but her *tummy rumbled* — that's a **clue**. Retrieval = find it; inference = work it out from clues."
**Show / Do:** point to where the retrieval answer 'sits'; underline the clue for the inference.
**Emphasize:** "Retrieval = **find it**. Inference = **work it out** from a **clue**."

### ⏱ 8–12 min · EXAMPLE (we do)
> **Say:** "Sort these two with me: 'What did Mia open?' and 'Why did she open the fridge?' Which is find-it, which is work-it-out?"
**Do:** child labels each card retrieval or inference and points to the clue for the inference.
**Ask:** "Is 'What colour was the coat?' retrieval or inference?" → *listen for:* "retrieval."

### ⏱ 12–16 min · CHECK
**Ask:** "Is 'What colour was the coat?' retrieval or inference — and how do you know?"
- ✅ **Expected:** retrieval — the answer is written in the text — probe: *"Where would you point to find it?"*
- ❌ **If "inference":** thinks all reading is working-out → **fix:** "Can you put your finger on the exact words that say the colour? If yes, it's retrieval."
- ❌ **If can't tell:** → **fix:** "Try to find the answer written down. Found it word-for-word? Retrieval. Had to think? Inference."

### ⏱ 16–19 min · PRACTICE (you do)
**Do:** Child reads a fresh 3-sentence passage and labels **two** questions as retrieval or inference, pointing to the clue/words. Once secure, interleave: child writes one retrieval question of their own.
**Watch for:** labelling everything 'inference'; not locating the retrieval words.
> **If the child is stuck (missed twice):** 1) don't reveal; 2) name *why* — not testing 'is it written?'; 3) highlight the answer words together; 4) one hint: "Point to the exact words"; 5) re-ask an obvious retrieval; 6) "smaller step."

### ⏱ 19–20 min · RECAP
> **Say:** "What's the difference between a retrieval and an inference question?" → *listen for:* "retrieval is written there; inference you work out from clues."
**Process praise:** "You pointed to the exact words for retrieval and hunted the clue for inference — proper detective reading."
**Revisit later:** inference with evidence next lesson builds straight on this.

---

**Differentiation**
- **Support (gentle):** highlight where the retrieval answer "sits" on the page before sorting.
- **Stretch (challenge):** write one of each question type about the passage.

**Extension (if time allows):** be the question-setter — write a retrieval and an inference question for the tutor.
**Assessment evidence:** child correctly labels retrieval vs inference questions and locates evidence.

## Lesson 22 — Inference with a clue (and proving it)
**English · Year 2 · Unit 5 (Comprehension & composition)** | **Duration:** 25 min | **Objective:** "By the end, I can make an inference and back it up with a clue from the text."

**You will need:** short passages with clue words (Tom's hands shaking, can't speak), an "I think… because the text says…" stem card, highlighter.
**Prior knowledge to retrieve:** retrieval vs inference (Lesson 21); clues.
**Key vocabulary:** **infer**, **clue**, **evidence**, **prove**, *I think… because the text says…*
**What to emphasize (the heart of the lesson):** an inference must be **backed by a clue** from the text — say *what* you think **and** *why*. Pre-empt guessing with no evidence.

---

### ⏱ 0–3 min · HOOK
> **Say:** "The text says: 'Tom's hands were shaking and he couldn't speak.' It never uses the word *scared* — but we just *know* he is. How? The clues tell us. Today we prove our thinking with evidence."
**Show:** the Tom sentence on the board.  **Do:** read it with feeling; pause.
**Ask (retrieve prior knowledge):** "Is 'How does Tom feel?' a find-it or a work-it-out question?" → *listen for:* "work it out / inference."

### ⏱ 3–9 min · EXPLAIN (I do)
> **Say:** "Here's how I prove an inference. I think Tom is **scared**. Why? **Because the text says** his hands were shaking and he couldn't speak. See my two parts — what I think, AND the clue. I always use the stem: 'I think… **because the text says**…'."
**Show / Do:** model the full sentence; underline the clue words in the passage.
**Emphasize:** "Say *what* you think **and** the **clue** — 'because the text says…'."

### ⏱ 9–15 min · EXAMPLE (we do)
> **Say:** "New passage: 'Mai skipped to school with a huge grin.' How does Mai feel — and what's our clue?"
**Do:** child infers 'happy' and finds the clue ('skipped', 'huge grin'); tutor scribes the stem sentence.
**Ask:** "How do we know Tom is scared?" → *listen for:* "his hands shake and he can't speak."

### ⏱ 15–19 min · CHECK
**Ask:** "Using the passage, answer 'How does the character feel?' with the stem 'I think… because the text says…'."
- ✅ **Expected:** a feeling + a real clue from the text — probe: *"Which words are your clue?"*
- ❌ **If gives a feeling but no clue:** guessing → **fix:** "What in the *text* made you think that? Point to the words and add 'because the text says…'."
- ❌ **If retells the whole passage:** → **fix:** "Just the one clue that shows the feeling — underline it."

### ⏱ 19–23 min · PRACTICE (you do)
**Do:** Child answers **one** inference question in full using the stem and a underlined clue. Once secure, interleave: infer a *second* feeling with its own clue.
**Watch for:** answers with no evidence; clue that doesn't match the feeling.
> **If the child is stuck (missed twice):** 1) don't reveal; 2) name *why* — missing/ mismatched clue; 3) highlight clue words together; 4) one hint: "Finish 'because the text says…'"; 5) re-ask with an easy clue; 6) "smaller step."

### ⏱ 23–25 min · RECAP
> **Say:** "What two parts does a good inference answer have?" → *listen for:* "what I think AND the clue (because the text says)."
**Process praise:** "You proved your idea with a clue from the text — that's exactly how strong readers think."
**Revisit later:** evidence-backed answers return in the Lesson 25 review.

---

**Differentiation**
- **Support (gentle):** clue pre-underlined; sentence stem on the desk.
- **Stretch (challenge):** infer two feelings, each with its own clue; infer a character's *next* action.

**Extension (if time allows):** find a clue in a favourite storybook at home and prove a feeling.
**Assessment evidence:** child makes an inference and supports it with a quoted/located clue using the stem.

## Lesson 23 — How instructions work (commands & order words)
**English · Year 2 · Unit 5 (Comprehension & composition)** | **Duration:** 20 min | **Objective:** "By the end, I can identify the features of instructions and order steps correctly."

**You will need:** jumbled jam-sandwich step cards, an order-word bank (*first, next, then, finally*), a real (or pretend) instruction sheet.
**Prior knowledge to retrieve:** commands (bossy-verb sentences) from sentence types.
**Key vocabulary:** **instructions**, **command**, **bossy verb**, **order words** (*first, next, then, finally*), **"you will need"**, **title**.
**What to emphasize (the heart of the lesson):** instructions have **features** — a title, a "you will need" list, **numbered steps** that start with a **bossy verb**, in the right **order**. Pre-empt steps written as statements or out of order.

---

### ⏱ 0–3 min · HOOK
> **Say:** "Could someone in your family make a jam sandwich using ONLY your writing, with you out of the room? Brilliant instructions can do that. Let's find out what makes them work."
**Show:** the jumbled step cards in a muddle.  **Do:** pretend to follow them wrongly (jam before bread!).
**Ask (retrieve prior knowledge):** "A 'bossy' sentence that tells you to do something — what's it called?" → *listen for:* "a command."

### ⏱ 3–8 min · EXPLAIN (I do)
> **Say:** "Good instructions have features: a **title** ('How to make a jam sandwich'), a **'you will need'** list, then **numbered steps**. Each step starts with a **bossy verb** — *Get, Spread, Put* — and we use **order words**: *first, next, then, finally*. The **order** matters: you can't spread jam before you get the bread!"
**Show / Do:** point to each feature on the example sheet; read steps in order with the order words.
**Emphasize:** "Steps are **commands** (bossy verb) in the **right order**."

### ⏱ 8–12 min · EXAMPLE (we do)
> **Say:** "These four sandwich steps are jumbled — let's order them together. Which comes *first*?"
**Do:** child sequences the four steps and reads them with *first/next/then/finally*.
**Ask:** "What kind of sentence starts each step?" → *listen for:* "a command / bossy verb."

### ⏱ 12–16 min · CHECK
**Ask:** "What kind of sentence starts each instruction step, and give me one bossy verb."
- ✅ **Expected:** a command; e.g. *Spread / Get / Put* — probe: *"Why start with the verb?"* (tells you exactly what to do).
- ❌ **If "a telling sentence/statement":** → **fix:** "Instructions *boss* you: they start with the doing word — *Spread the jam*, not *The jam is spread*."
- ❌ **If orders steps wrongly:** → **fix:** "Act it out — could you really do step 2 before step 1?"

### ⏱ 16–19 min · PRACTICE (you do)
**Do:** Child orders a fresh jumbled set of steps and **underlines each bossy verb**. Once secure, interleave: spot a missing step and add it.
**Watch for:** statements instead of commands; illogical order.
> **If the child is stuck (missed twice):** 1) don't reveal; 2) name *why* — order or command form; 3) act the steps out physically; 4) one hint: "Start the step with a doing word"; 5) re-order just two steps; 6) "smaller step."

### ⏱ 19–20 min · RECAP
> **Say:** "Name two features of good instructions." → *listen for:* "title / you will need / numbered command steps / order words."
**Process praise:** "You ordered the steps so a reader could actually follow them — clear, helpful writing."
**Revisit later:** writing your own instructions next lesson uses exactly these features.

---

**Differentiation**
- **Support (gentle):** pictures alongside each step to support ordering.
- **Stretch (challenge):** spot a missing step and add it; improve a vague step.

**Extension (if time allows):** find instructions at home (a recipe/game) and tick off the features.
**Assessment evidence:** child orders steps correctly and identifies command sentences with bossy verbs.

## Lesson 24 — Write your own instructions
**English · Year 2 · Unit 5 (Comprehension & composition)** | **Duration:** 30 min | **Objective:** "By the end, I can write a clear set of instructions with commands and order words."

**You will need:** an instruction writing frame (title / you will need / numbered steps), order-word bank, the child's choice of a simple how-to (a game, craft or snack), pencil.
**Prior knowledge to retrieve:** instruction features and commands (Lesson 23).
**Key vocabulary:** **title**, **"you will need"**, **command**, **bossy verb**, **order words**, **step**.
**What to emphasize (the heart of the lesson):** plan first, then write **numbered command steps in order** with order words — and **say each step aloud before writing it** (oral rehearsal). This is a composition lesson — protect writing time.

---

### ⏱ 0–3 min · HOOK
> **Say:** "Today YOU'RE the instruction author! Pick something you know how to do — play a game, make a snack, do a craft — and write it so clearly that someone in your family could do it without you. What will you teach?"
**Show:** the writing frame.  **Do:** help the child pick a simple, doable topic.
**Ask (retrieve prior knowledge):** "What three features must our instructions have?" → *listen for:* "title, you will need, numbered command steps."

### ⏱ 3–9 min · EXPLAIN (I do — model planning)
> **Say:** "Watch me plan one. **Title**: 'How to make toast'. **You will need**: bread, a toaster. **Steps**: *First*, **put** the bread in. *Next*, **press** the button. *Then*, **wait**. *Finally*, **spread** the butter. See — each step a bossy verb, in order, with an order word."
**Show / Do:** fill the frame aloud, underlining each bossy verb.
**Emphasize:** "Plan it, then each step: **order word + bossy verb**, in the right order."

### ⏱ 9–13 min · EXAMPLE (we do)
> **Say:** "Let's plan the start of YOURS together. Title? 'You will need'? Now say step one aloud as a command."
**Do:** child sets title and 'you will need', rehearses step one orally before writing.
**Ask:** "Why start each step with a bossy verb?" → *listen for:* "so the reader knows exactly what to do."

### ⏱ 13–26 min · PRACTICE (you do — write)
**Do:** Child writes a **title**, a **"you will need"** list, and **3–4 numbered command steps** with order words, rehearsing each step aloud before writing it. Once drafted, they read it back to check order and bossy verbs. Stretch: follow their own instructions to find gaps.
**Watch for:** statements creeping in; missing order words; steps out of sequence.
> **If the child is stuck (missed twice):** 1) don't reveal; 2) name *why* — planning or command form; 3) say the next step aloud together, then write it; 4) one hint: "Start with the doing word"; 5) shrink to a 3-step version; 6) "smaller step."

### ⏱ 26–30 min · RECAP
> **Say:** "Read me your instructions — could I really follow them?" → *listen for:* clear, ordered command steps.
**Process praise:** "You rehearsed each step aloud before writing — that's why your instructions are so easy to follow."
**Revisit later:** the whole-year review next lesson pulls this together.

---

**Differentiation**
- **Support (gentle):** writing frame with sentence starters (*First, … Next, …*); a familiar 3-step topic.
- **Stretch (challenge):** test the instructions by following them exactly and fixing any gap.

**Extension (if time allows):** add a labelled picture for one step, or a "top tip" line.
**Assessment evidence:** child writes ordered, numbered command steps with order words, a title and a "you will need" list.

## Lesson 25 — End-of-year review: read it, prove it, write it (retrieval)
**English · Year 2 · Unit 5 (Comprehension & composition)** | **Duration:** 25 min | **Objective:** "By the end, I can read fluently, infer with evidence, and write a correctly punctuated sentence."

**You will need:** mixed task cards, a short passage (two reading levels), two longer words to beat-read (*stegosaurus, playground*), an "I think… because…" stem card, mini-whiteboard, a "personal best" tracker.
**Prior knowledge to retrieve:** the whole year — fluent reading, inference with a clue, capitals/end marks, *because* reasons.
**Key vocabulary:** (year revisited) **syllable-chop**, **fluency**, **inference**, **clue**, **capital**, **end mark**, **because**.
**What to emphasize (the heart of the lesson):** a confidence-building **mixed retrieval** — read fluently, **prove an inference with a clue**, and **write one fully-punctuated sentence**. Celebrate growth; low-stakes.

---

### ⏱ 0–3 min · HOOK
> **Say:** "Show-off day! Everything you've learned this year — reading, detective-thinking, writing — all in one mini challenge. Let's beat your personal best!"
**Show:** the task cards fanned out.  **Do:** set the friendly 'personal best' tone.
**Ask (retrieve prior knowledge):** "How do we read a really long word?" → *listen for:* "chop it into syllables / beats."

### ⏱ 3–6 min · EXPLAIN (I do — model the routine)
> **Say:** "Three stations today. One: beat-read a long word and read a sentence smoothly. Two: answer an inference 'I think… because the text says…'. Three: write one sentence with a **capital**, an **end mark**, and a **because** reason. Watch one of each from me."
**Show / Do:** beat-read *steg-o-saur-us*; model an inference stem; write 'I was happy because it was sunny.'
**Emphasize:** "Read smoothly, **prove** with a clue, **punctuate** every sentence."

### ⏱ 6–9 min · EXAMPLE (we do)
> **Say:** "Beat-read *playground* with me, then read this sentence with expression."
**Do:** child syllable-chops *play-ground* and reads a sentence fluently.
**Ask:** "Give me a sentence stem that proves an inference." → *listen for:* "I think… because the text says…"

### ⏱ 9–13 min · CHECK
**Ask:** "Read this sentence smoothly, then tell me: what makes a written sentence complete?"
- ✅ **Expected:** fluent read; "a capital at the start and an end mark (. ? !)" — probe: *"What else could you add to say *why*?"* (a because reason).
- ❌ **If reads word-by-word:** → **fix:** "Chop the long word into beats, then read the line again — let it flow."
- ❌ **If forgets capital/end mark:** → **fix:** "Every sentence wears a capital crown at the front and an end mark at the back."

### ⏱ 13–22 min · PRACTICE (you do)
**Do:** Child completes the three tasks solo: (1) beat-read two long words and read a sentence fluently; (2) answer one inference question with a clue using the stem; (3) write one sentence with a capital, end mark, and a *because* reason. Once secure, interleave: add an **expanded noun phrase** to the written sentence.
**Watch for:** evidence-free inferences; missing punctuation; word-by-word reading.
> **If the child is stuck (missed twice):** 1) don't reveal; 2) name *why* — which skill slipped; 3) give that skill's scaffold (syllable chop / stem / capital reminder); 4) one hint; 5) re-ask an easier version; 6) "smaller step."

### ⏱ 22–25 min · RECAP
> **Say:** "Tell me one thing you can do in English now that you couldn't at the start of the year." → *listen for:* a genuine growth statement.
**Process praise:** "You read smoothly, proved your thinking with a clue, AND punctuated your sentence — a brilliant Year 2 reader and writer."
**Revisit later:** carry any shaky strand into Year 3 as an opening warm-up.

---

**Differentiation**
- **Support (gentle):** choose the easier of two passages; sentence stems and a capital/end-mark reminder on the desk.
- **Stretch (challenge):** add an expanded noun phrase to the written sentence; infer with two clues.

**Extension (if time allows):** write a two-sentence book recommendation using a *because* reason.
**Assessment evidence:** child reads fluently, makes an evidenced inference, and writes a correctly punctuated sentence with a reason — evidence across the year.

> **End-of-Unit 5 check:** Child labels a retrieval vs an inference question, makes an inference with a clue ("…because the text says…"), and writes a 3–4 step set of instructions with commands and order words. Mastery = inference evidenced; instructions ordered with bossy verbs and a "you will need".
