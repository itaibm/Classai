# Lesson 22 — Inference with a clue (and proving it)
**English · Year 2 · Unit 5 (Comprehension & composition)** · 25 min · **Delivery: 🤖 fully AI**
**Objective:** "By the end, I can make an inference and back it up with a clue from the text."

## Flow (all AI, detective casebook frame)

- **Hook (0–3):** the Tom mystery sentence on screen — "his hands were shaking and he couldn't speak." The word *scared* is never written… so how do we KNOW? Retrieval from L21: find-it or work-it-out?
- **Explain (3–9):** the two-part proof, modelled slowly: **what I think** (Tom is scared) + **the clue** (because the text says his hands were shaking). The magic stem "**I think… because the text says…**" chanted twice.
- **Example (9–15):** shared case — Mai ("skipped… huge grin") — child names the feeling AND points to the clue words; fillBlank cements *because* as the glue.
- **Check (15–19):** solo proof on Leo ("dragged his feet… kite in bits"), shortText with sample. Wrong-answer playbook: feeling-with-no-clue (guess), whole-passage retell, and a clue/feeling mismatch.
- **Practice (19–23):** casebook drills from the 9-item bank — no proof, no badge.
- **Recap (23–25):** two parts stated back; speak block — say the stem like you're teaching a friend.

**Misconceptions targeted:** inference without pointing to a clue; answers from opinion/memory ("I'd be excited, so he is"); retelling instead of one clue; clue that contradicts the idea; inventing details beyond the evidence.

**Practice bank:** 9 items — L1 read the feeling, spot Tom's clue (2) · L2 the *because* stem, Nina (quiet for the sleeping baby), weather-from-wellies, clue↔feeling matchPairs (4) · L3 Aisha (prove the feeling, not the score), predict Jack's next move, Poppy's TWO feelings with two clues (3). Every item: wrong answers diagnosed, hints, scripted reteach.

**Adaptivity:** start level 2 · 2 right → up · 2 misses → reteach with highlighted clues + level 1 · mastery = 3+ full-stem proofs incl. one non-feeling inference.
