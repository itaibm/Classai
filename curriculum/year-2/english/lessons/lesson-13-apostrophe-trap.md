# Lesson 13 — The apostrophe trap: NOT for plurals
**English · Year 2 · Unit 3 (Apostrophes & homophones)** · 20 min · **Delivery: 🤖 fully AI**
**Objective:** "By the end, I can tell a plural (no apostrophe) from possession/contraction (apostrophe)."
*(Human view of [`lesson-13-apostrophe-trap.json`](./lesson-13-apostrophe-trap.json) — the JSON is the source of truth. No preparation needed from you.)*

## Flow (all AI, "sign inspector" frame)

- **Hook (0–3):** the shop sign *apple's 50p* on the whiteboard — "do the apples OWN anything? No — that apostrophe is a MISTAKE." Retrieval of both jobs (squash, belonging). Badge on.
- **Explain (3–8):** the big rule — apostrophes are NEVER for plain plurals — plus the one diagnostic question ("belonging? missing letters? neither → no apostrophe") and the *its/it's* "say *it is*" test, all on a four-row contrast table.
- **Example (8–12):** shared fixes of *two car's* and *fresh egg's*, then a **categorize** sort: contraction / possession / plural-no-apostrophe (6 items).
- **Check (12–14):** the End-of-Unit item as a **trueFalse**: "two dogs is written *dog's*" → False; both wrong paths scripted (greengrocer True; over-corrected *dogs'*).
- **Practice (14–18):** adaptive 9-item bank — plural rule + *banana's for sale* fix (level 1), categorize sort, *its/it's* both directions, protect-the-real-apostrophe (level 2), and stretch: spot the one correct sentence among fakes, write-your-own correct shop sign, the double *its/it's* gap (level 3).
- **Recap (18–20):** child states the inspector's rule; process praise; teaser for the there/their/they're homophones.

**Adaptivity:** start level 2 · 2 right → up · 2 misses on a skill → spoken yes/no-gate reteach + level 1 · mastery = 4+ correct incl. a sort, a fixed sign and an *its/it's*.

**Video:** none — the wrong-sign whiteboard and sorting carry the visuals.

**Differentiation:** support — single "is something belonging?" gate, plurals vs possessives only · stretch — *its/it's* explained + a real greengrocer's apostrophe caught in the wild.
**Evidence:** 4+ of 6 apostrophe-or-not decisions right; states "plurals never take an apostrophe".
