## Year overview

Year 2 is the year a child stops *learning to read* and starts *reading to learn* — and the year
their writing becomes joined, paragraphed and shaped for a reader. It **finishes systematic
synthetic phonics** (Phase 6: suffix and spelling rules, prefixes, and the less common spellings
of sounds), builds **reading fluency** (accuracy, pace and expression) to around 90+ words per
minute on age-appropriate text by the summer, and moves the centre of gravity to
**comprehension** — retrieval, inference with evidence, prediction, vocabulary, summarising — all
taught through a **named class-text spine** of whole books. **Joined handwriting** starts in
Term 1 and is practised in short daily sessions. Writing genres are taught *through* the class
texts (a diary after the owl, a letter after Anna Hibiscus, a retold tale after the world's
Cinderellas, a news report after *The Firework-Maker's Daughter*, an explanation after the journey to the Earth's
core, a new chapter after *Charlotte's Web*, a biography after *Look Up!*), and grammar is taught *in service of that writing*, never as
isolated drills. Poetry is read, learned by heart and performed every term.

**What Year 2 assumes from Year 1 (ages 6–7):** secure Phase 5 phonics (alternative spellings
and pronunciations) and early Phase 6 suffixes (-s/-es, -ing, -ed, -er, -est with the simple rules);
reading most common exception words; reading short chapter books aloud with growing fluency;
writing several sentences in sequence with capital letters, full stops, question and exclamation
marks, and the conjunctions *and, but, or, because, when, if, that*; forming lower-case letters
correctly with ascenders and descenders.

**What Year 3 (ages 8–9) relies on from this year:** phonics complete, so every child can decode
unfamiliar multi-syllable words; fluent reading of longer novels; joined, legible handwriting;
writing organised in paragraphs; direct speech punctuated with inverted commas; the prefixes and
suffixes that Year 3 extends (-tion families, -ous); and comprehension answers that always point
to evidence in the text.

**Daily shape.** Each English lesson (20–30 min) is one of: *phonics & spelling*, *reading &
comprehension*, *grammar for writing*, *writing* (plan → draft → revise → publish), *handwriting*,
*poetry*, or *speaking & listening*. Outside these lessons every child also gets **daily reading
volume**: 10–15 minutes of independent or paired reading from a well-stocked book corner, a daily
read-aloud from the class text, and a 5-minute handwriting or spelling practice.

## Unit 1 — Phonics bridge, fluency and joined handwriting

**Essential question:** How do good readers read long words they have never seen — and how can joined writing make my ideas flow?

**Key vocabulary:** phoneme, grapheme, digraph, trigraph, syllable, root word, suffix, prefix, vowel, consonant, fluency, expression, pace, join, diagonal join, horizontal join, ascender, descender

### Lesson 1 — Where am I? Phonics and reading check-in
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can show my teacher how I read and spell words with every sound I know." (UK NC Y3/4 Word reading; CCSS RF.2.3)
- **Hook:** "Secret word envelopes" — each envelope holds a word to read; some are real, some are made-up alien words.
- **Key activity:** Teacher runs a quick one-to-one phonics screen (a grapheme chart of Phase 3–5 sounds, 20 real and pseudo-words, 10 words to spell) while the class does paired reading. Results group children for Unit 1 keep-up sessions.
- **Check for understanding:** "Read 'splendid' and 'shrimp' and tell me how you broke them up." → By sounds/syllables: splen-did; shr-i-mp — blending adjacent consonants.
- **Differentiation:** support: stop the screen at the first phase with errors and start keep-up teaching there / stretch: read four-syllable words and explain which grapheme is tricky.
- **Materials:** phonics grapheme chart, real/pseudo-word cards, spelling sheet, class record grid, decodable readers at several levels
- **Joy:** Alien words are read to a toy alien who "nods" when they're decoded correctly.

### Lesson 2 — Syllable busting: reading long words
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can read a long word by splitting it into syllables and blending each part." (UK NC Y3/4 Word reading; CCSS RF.2.3c, RF.3.3c)
- **Hook:** Dinosaur names on cards: "Pachycephalosaurus — can we read it?"
- **Key activity:** I do: clap syllables, draw a scoop under each, read each chunk, then the whole word. We do: fantastic, remember, hippopotamus, invitation. You do: read a list of 12 multi-syllable words and a decodable passage containing them.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why is 'mag-net' easier to read than 'magnet' all at once?" → Each syllable has one vowel sound and is short enough to blend.
- **Differentiation:** support: two-syllable compound and closed-syllable words (sunset, rabbit) with syllable cards to split / stretch: place names from around the world (Kilimanjaro, Mississippi) and explain the split.
- **Materials:** syllable cards, dinosaur and place-name cards, scissors, decodable passage
- **Joy:** "Dinosaur roll-call" — children announce dinosaurs like zookeepers.

### Lesson 3 — Suffixes review: -ing, -ed, -er, -est and the three rules
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can add -ing, -ed, -er and -est using the 'just add', 'drop the e' and 'double the consonant' rules." (UK NC Y1–2 spelling review; CCSS L.2.2d)
- **Hook:** "The robot wrote 'hopeing' and 'runing'. What rules did it forget?"
- **Key activity:** Root-word sort into three hoops: just add (jump → jumping), drop the e (hope → hoping), double after a short vowel with one final consonant (run → running). We do → you do with word-building cards.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why does 'hop' become 'hopping' but 'hope' become 'hoping'?" → hop has a short vowel and one consonant, so we double; hope ends in e, so we drop the e.
- **Differentiation:** support: just-add and drop-the-e only, with root and suffix cards to physically join / stretch: find an exception (e.g. 'fix' → 'fixing': x is never doubled) and explain.
- **Materials:** root-word cards, suffix cards, sorting hoops, mini-whiteboards
- **Joy:** "Fix the robot" — each corrected word earns a robot part on the board.

### Lesson 4 — Suffixes review: y → i and -ful, -less, -ness, -ment, -ly
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can add suffixes to words ending in y and use -ful, -less, -ness, -ment and -ly to change meaning." (UK NC Y2 spelling review; CCSS L.2.4b)
- **Hook:** "Is it happyness or happiness?"
- **Key activity:** Teach the rule exactly: a y after a consonant changes to i before most suffixes (happy → happier, happiness), but stays before -ing (cry → crying) and after a vowel (play → played, enjoyment). Build new words and discuss meaning changes (careful/careless).
- **Check for understanding:** "Why is 'played' spelt with a y but 'cried' with an i?" → In play, y comes after a vowel so it stays; in cry, y comes after a consonant so it changes to i.
- **Differentiation:** support: -ful, -less, -ness with no spelling change first / stretch: words that take several suffixes (care → careful → carefully → carelessness).
- **Materials:** word-building cards, suffix cards, class rule poster
- **Joy:** "Word-builder towers" — stack suffix blocks to build the longest real word.

### Lesson 5 — Joined handwriting 1: posture, grip and diagonal joins
- **Duration:** 20 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can join letters with a diagonal join to letters without ascenders (e.g. in, ai, ar, un)." (UK NC Y3/4 Handwriting)
- **Hook:** "Joined writing is like a dance: the pen doesn't leave the floor."
- **Key activity:** Warm-up: finger and shoulder exercises, posture check (feet flat, paper tilted, tripod grip). Teacher models diagonal joins on the board, children air-write, then trace, then write in lined books: in, an, ai, un, ar, words (rain, run, train).
- **Check for understanding:** "Where does the join start and finish on 'in'?" → It starts at the bottom of the i and rises diagonally to the start of the n.
- **Differentiation:** support: tracing sheets with dotted joins and a pencil grip / stretch: write a short phrase with every join the same slant and size.
- **Materials:** handwriting books with guidelines, pencils, pencil grips, sloped writing boards, a handwriting scheme (e.g. Penpals for Handwriting or Nelson Handwriting)
- **Joy:** "Dance of the pen" — join letters in time to slow music.

### Lesson 6 — Prefixes that change meaning: un-, dis-, mis-
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can add un-, dis- and mis- to root words and explain how the meaning changes." (UK NC Y3/4 Spelling – prefixes; CCSS L.2.4b)
- **Hook:** "The magician said the rabbit would 'appear' — then it 'disappeared'!"
- **Key activity:** Teach that a prefix goes at the front and never changes the root's spelling (dis + appear = disappear; mis + spell = misspell — two s's). Build and define words; sort by prefix; use in oral sentences.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why does 'misspell' have two s's?" → mis- ends in s and spell starts with s; we keep both because a prefix doesn't change the root.
- **Differentiation:** support: un- only with picture cards (unlock, untie, unhappy) / stretch: find words where dis-, un- or mis- is not a prefix (e.g. 'uncle', 'dish') and explain.
- **Materials:** prefix and root cards, a toy magician's hat, dictionaries
- **Joy:** "Magic hat" — pull out a root word, add a prefix and act out the new meaning.

### Lesson 7 — Fluency 1: reading with phrasing
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can read a passage in phrases, not word by word." (UK NC Y3/4 Reading – fluency; CCSS RF.2.4b)
- **Hook:** Teacher reads a paragraph robot-style, then fluently: "Which one would you want at bedtime?"
- **Key activity:** Scoop phrases with a pencil under a decodable passage; teacher models, echo read, choral read, then partner repeated reading (three reads, timing the last). Record words correct per minute (WCPM) privately in a reading log.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why do we pause at a comma and stop at a full stop?" → Punctuation shows where the meaning groups break, so the reader can make sense.
- **Differentiation:** support: shorter passage at their decodable level with phrases pre-marked / stretch: mark phrases themselves in a harder passage and read it to the class.
- **Materials:** decodable passages (e.g. from Little Wandle Letters and Sounds Revised or Read Write Inc. Book Bag Books), pencils, timers, reading logs
- **Joy:** "Beat your own time" — children graph their own reading speed like athletes.

### Lesson 8 — Reading uncommon spellings: /ʌ/ ou, /ɪ/ y and /eɪ/ ei, eigh, ey
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can read and spell words like young, myth and eight." (UK NC Y3/4 Spelling – Appendix 1; CCSS RF.3.3)
- **Hook:** "Spot the sound-spies: which letters are pretending to be another sound?"
- **Key activity:** Introduce three spelling families with word lists: ou as /ʌ/ (young, touch, double, trouble, country), y as /ɪ/ (myth, gym, Egypt, pyramid, mystery), ei/eigh/ey as /eɪ/ (vein, eight, weigh, neighbour, they, obey). Read, sort, then spell from dictation.
- **Check for understanding:** "Which spelling of /eɪ/ does 'eight' use, and how will you remember it?" → eigh; e.g. link it to weigh and neighbour.
- **Differentiation:** support: focus on one family with picture cues / stretch: find more words in a dictionary for each family.
- **Materials:** word cards, sorting mats, dictionaries, picture cards (pyramid, gym)
- **Joy:** "Sound spies" — a mission sheet to find the spies in a secret message.

### Lesson 9 — Joined handwriting 2: diagonal joins to ascenders
- **Duration:** 20 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can join letters diagonally to tall letters (e.g. ab, ul, it, ch)." (UK NC Y3/4 Handwriting)
- **Hook:** "A ski-jump: the join climbs to the top of the tall letter."
- **Key activity:** Model the diagonal join that rises to the ascender height (al, ab, ul, it, ch, th); practise pairs, then words (little, table, child) and a short sentence.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why does the join to 'l' go higher than the join to 'n'?" → l is an ascender, so the join must reach its top to start it.
- **Differentiation:** support: large-lined paper and tracing / stretch: write a joined sentence using at least five ascender joins.
- **Materials:** handwriting books, lined paper with ascender guidelines, pencils
- **Joy:** Draw a ski-slope doodle along the margin as a warm-up.

### Lesson 10 — Common exception words from the Year 3–4 list (1)
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can spell ten tricky words by finding the tricky part and using a memory trick." (UK NC Y3/4 Word list; CCSS L.2.2d)
- **Hook:** "Some words break the rules. Let's catch their tricky part."
- **Key activity:** Words: because (review), different, important, remember, believe, February, heard, island, often, surprise. For each, say it, find the tricky part (the silent s in island), make a mnemonic, write it three times joined, cover and check.
- **Check for understanding:** "What is the tricky part of 'island' and how will you remember it?" → The silent s; 'is land' — an island is land.
- **Differentiation:** support: five words with picture mnemonics / stretch: invent mnemonics for three more list words (separate, business, library).
- **Materials:** word cards, look-say-cover-write-check sheets, coloured pens for the tricky part
- **Joy:** Silly mnemonic contest — the class votes for the funniest memory trick.

### Lesson 11 — Fluency 2: reading with expression
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can use my voice to show feelings and questions when I read aloud." (UK NC Y3/4 Reading – performance; CCSS RF.2.4b)
- **Hook:** "Read 'Oh no, not again' as if you are bored, scared and excited."
- **Key activity:** Model expression cues: punctuation (?, !), bold words, speech. Children practise a short readers'-theatre script from a decodable or class text; partners give feedback using "I liked how you…".
- **Check for understanding:** "How should your voice change for a question mark?" → Rise at the end, as if asking.
- **Differentiation:** support: shorter role with repeated lines / stretch: narrator part with changes of mood.
- **Materials:** readers'-theatre scripts, emotion cards
- **Joy:** Perform the script for another class.

### Lesson 12 — Decodable reading and comprehension: Flotsam (wordless)
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can tell what is happening in a picture story and explain the clues I used." (UK NC Y3/4 Reading – inference; CCSS RL.2.7)
- **Hook:** A mysterious underwater camera appears on a beach (David Wiesner's *Flotsam*).
- **Key activity:** Share the wordless book; pause at key spreads; children narrate orally with sentence stems ("I think… because I can see…"); record key inferences on sticky notes.
- **Check for understanding:** "How do we know the camera has been in the sea for a long time?" → The photos show people from long ago / it is covered in barnacles (clue from the pictures).
- **Differentiation:** support: picture cards with vocabulary / stretch: write captions for two spreads using precise nouns.
- **Materials:** *Flotsam* by David Wiesner, sticky notes, magnifying glasses
- **Joy:** Magnifying-glass hunt for hidden details in the pictures.

### Lesson 13 — Spelling check and keep-up planning
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can show which suffix and prefix rules I have mastered." (UK NC Y3/4 Spelling – assessment)
- **Hook:** "Spelling bee practice round" — teams, no elimination.
- **Key activity:** Dictation of 20 words and two sentences covering Unit 1 rules; children self-check with a key and circle one rule to practise.
- **Check for understanding:** "Explain the rule you used for 'carrying'." → carry ends in y after a consonant, but before -ing the y stays.
- **Differentiation:** support: 10 words and one sentence / stretch: explain two rule exceptions.
- **Materials:** dictation sheet, answer keys, coloured pens
- **Joy:** Team spelling bee with bell.

### Lesson 14 — Unit review: reading like a detective
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can read a new passage fluently and answer questions using the text." (Retrieval — Unit 1)
- **Hook:** "A mysterious letter arrived for the class."
- **Key activity:** Read a new decodable letter aloud with a partner (fluency check), then answer retrieval and inference questions, underlining evidence.
- **Check for understanding:** "Point to the words that tell you how the writer feels." → Underlines the evidence, e.g. "my hands were shaking."
- **Differentiation:** support: shorter passage / stretch: write a reply using two new prefixes.
- **Materials:** printed letter passage, highlighters
- **Joy:** Write a reply to the mysterious writer.

**End-of-unit check:** Read a 150-word decodable passage with phrasing (teacher records accuracy and WCPM), spell 10 words using the suffix rules, and write one joined sentence using diagonal joins.

## Unit 2 — The Owl Who Was Afraid of the Dark: feelings, inference and diary writing

**Essential question:** How can the words an author chooses show us how a character feels — and how do characters change?

**Key vocabulary:** character, feelings, inference, evidence, chapter, dialogue, prediction, diary, first person, past tense, time connective, adjective, noun phrase, conjunction, nocturnal, brave, afraid, fascinating

> Class text: *The Owl Who Was Afraid of the Dark* by Jill Tomlinson. Plop, a baby barn owl, is afraid of the dark; his mother sends him to ask others about it, and in each chapter someone shows him a different side of the night.

### Lesson 15 — Meet Plop: first impressions
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can describe a character using evidence from the text." (UK NC Y3/4 Reading – comprehension; CCSS RL.2.3)
- **Hook:** A soft owl toy in a box and an owl hoot — "Who is hiding?"
- **Key activity:** Read chapter 1 aloud; children collect words about Plop (e.g. fat and fluffy) on a character outline; discuss: "Is it odd that an owl is afraid of the dark? Why?"
- **Check for understanding:** "Why is Plop's fear surprising for a barn owl?" → Owls are nocturnal — they hunt and fly at night.
- **Differentiation:** support: picture cards and word bank / stretch: explain how Plop's words show his feelings.
- **Materials:** *The Owl Who Was Afraid of the Dark*, owl toy, character outline sheet, facts about barn owls
- **Joy:** Hoot like an owl at the end of the chapter.

### Lesson 16 — Barn owls: fact and fiction
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can find facts in a non-fiction text and compare them with a story." (UK NC Y3/4 Reading – non-fiction; CCSS RI.2.1)
- **Hook:** "Can owls really turn their heads all the way round?"
- **Key activity:** Read a short non-fiction page about barn owls; sort statements into "fact" or "story only" (owls talking).
- **Check for understanding:** "Is 'owls can see well at night' a fact or story-only? How do you know?" → Fact; it's in the non-fiction text.
- **Differentiation:** support: picture-supported fact cards / stretch: find one fact that the story uses.
- **Materials:** barn-owl fact sheet, sorting cards
- **Joy:** Owl-pellet photos (or real sterilised pellets) examined with tweezers.

### Lesson 17 — Prediction: what will Plop learn?
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can predict what will happen using clues from the text." (UK NC Y3/4 Reading – prediction; CCSS RL.2.1)
- **Hook:** Mrs Barn Owl says "Go and ask…" — "Who will Plop meet?"
- **Key activity:** Read the next chapter; stop at the key moment; children write predictions with "because"; check afterwards.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why was your prediction sensible even if it was wrong?" → It used clues from the text.
- **Differentiation:** support: choose between two predictions / stretch: predict how Plop will change by the end.
- **Materials:** class text, prediction cards
- **Joy:** "Prediction envelope" — sealed and opened at the end of the book.

### Lesson 18 — Spelling: prefixes re- and pre-
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can add re- and pre- and explain their meanings (again, before)." (UK NC Y3/4 Spelling – prefixes; CCSS L.2.4b)
- **Hook:** "What does 'rewind' mean?"
- **Key activity:** Build words (redo, rebuild, return, preview, prepare, prehistoric); explain meaning; use in sentences.
- **Check for understanding:** "What does 'prehistoric' tell you about the time?" → Before history was written down.
- **Differentiation:** support: re- only / stretch: sort words where 're' is and isn't a prefix (reply vs read).
- **Materials:** prefix cards, root cards, dictionaries
- **Joy:** "Rewind" game — act out an action, then "re-do" it.

### Lesson 19 — Inference: how does Plop feel?
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can infer a character's feelings and prove it with evidence." (UK NC Y3/4 Reading – inference; CCSS RL.2.3)
- **Hook:** Emotion faces — "Which face is Plop now?"
- **Key activity:** Re-read a chapter; find words and actions that show feelings; record on "feelings graph" across the chapter.
- **Check for understanding:** "How do you know Plop is less afraid now? Quote the text." → Points to a line showing his changing feelings.
- **Differentiation:** support: emotion cards / stretch: explain how feelings change within one chapter.
- **Materials:** class text, emotion cards, feelings graph
- **Joy:** "Freeze frame" — act a moment and hold it.

### Lesson 20 — Handwriting: horizontal joins (ou, vi, wi, ow)
- **Duration:** 20 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can join from o, v, w and r with a horizontal join." (UK NC Y3/4 Handwriting)
- **Hook:** "A washing line: horizontal joins hang from the top."
- **Key activity:** Model horizontal joins; practise pairs (ou, ow, vi, wi, ra); words (owl, out, wind, river).
- **Check for understanding:** "Why does the join from o start at the top?" → o finishes at the top, so the join goes across from there.
- **Differentiation:** support: tracing / stretch: sentence with owl words.
- **Materials:** handwriting books, lined paper
- **Joy:** Write "owl" words with a silver pen on dark paper.

### Lesson 21 — Vocabulary: words about the dark
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can use precise words to describe the night." (UK NC Y3/4 Vocabulary; CCSS L.2.5b)
- **Hook:** Turn off the lights; listen — what can you hear?
- **Key activity:** Collect words (shadowy, velvet, glittering, silent); shades of meaning ladder (dim → dark → pitch-black).
- **Check for understanding:** "Why is 'pitch-black' stronger than 'dark'?" → It means completely dark, with no light at all.
- **Differentiation:** support: picture word bank / stretch: create similes for the night.
- **Materials:** word ladders, torch
- **Joy:** Night-time listening walk in a darkened room with a torch.

### Lesson 22 — Grammar for writing: expanded noun phrases
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can expand nouns with adjectives and prepositional phrases (the fluffy owl in the tall tree)." (UK NC Y2–3 VGP; CCSS L.2.1f)
- **Hook:** "An owl. Boring! Make it more exciting."
- **Key activity:** Build noun phrases with word cards; add adjectives, then a prepositional phrase; use them in sentences about the night.
- **Check for understanding:** "What does 'in the tall tree' add to 'the owl'?" → It tells us where the owl is, painting a clearer picture.
- **Differentiation:** support: one adjective / stretch: two adjectives with a comma and a prepositional phrase.
- **Materials:** noun, adjective, preposition cards
- **Joy:** "Noun phrase slot machine" — spin to create silly phrases.

### Lesson 23 — Reading: the chapter pattern
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can explain how the book's chapters follow a pattern." (UK NC Y3/4 Reading – structure; CCSS RL.2.5)
- **Hook:** Chapter titles on cards — "What do you notice?"
- **Key activity:** Map each chapter: who Plop meets, what they say about the dark, how he feels after. Complete a pattern table.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why might the author repeat this pattern?" → It shows Plop learning a little more each time, so we see him change.
- **Differentiation:** support: partially completed table / stretch: invent a new chapter following the pattern.
- **Materials:** class text, pattern table
- **Joy:** Invent a new person Plop could meet.

### Lesson 24 — Spelling: common exception words (2)
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can spell ten more tricky words from the Year 3–4 list by knowing their tricky parts." (UK NC Y3/4 Word list; CCSS L.2.2d)
- **Hook:** "Tricky-word treasure hunt" — ten words hidden around the room, each with its tricky part in red.
- **Key activity:** Words: answer, appear, arrive, breath, build, busy, caught, early, earth, enough. For each: say it in "spelling voice" (an-SWER), find the tricky part, make a mnemonic, write it joined three times, then cover–write–check.
- **Check for understanding:** "What is tricky about 'build' and 'busy'?" → In build, 'ui' spells a short /i/; in busy, 'u' spells /i/ — neither follows the usual sound.
- **Differentiation:** support: five words with picture mnemonics and letter tiles / stretch: add breath/breathe and explain how the e changes the sound and word class (noun vs verb).
- **Materials:** word cards hidden around the room, look–say–cover–write–check sheets, red pens, letter tiles
- **Joy:** The treasure hunt — each word found earns a letter of a secret message.

### Lesson 25 — Speaking and listening: hot-seating Plop

### Lesson 26 — Grammar for writing: conjunctions for time (when, before, after, while)
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can join ideas with time conjunctions and punctuate the sentence when the conjunction comes first." (UK NC Y3 VGP; CCSS L.3.1h)
- **Hook:** Two sentence halves on cards: "Plop peered over the edge." / "The sun went down." — "How can we join them?"
- **Key activity:** Join clauses with when, before, after, while; physically move the conjunction clause to the front and add a comma ("When the sun went down, Plop peered over the edge."). Children write four sentences about Plop's night.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why do we add a comma in 'After he met the boy, Plop felt braver' but not in 'Plop felt braver after he met the boy'?" → When the subordinate clause comes first, a comma marks where it ends and the main clause begins.
- **Differentiation:** support: when and after only, with cut-up sentence strips / stretch: use while and until and explain how each changes the timing.
- **Materials:** sentence-strip cards, scissors, mini-whiteboards

### Lesson 28 — Diary writing: what makes a good diary?

### Lesson 61 — Spelling: the suffix -ly and its rules
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can add -ly to make adverbs, including the special rules." (UK NC Y3/4 Spelling – suffixes; CCSS L.2.1e)

## Unit 6 — The Firework-Maker's Daughter: vivid language and news reports

**Essential question:** How does a storyteller make us see, hear and feel a world of fire and danger — and how would a reporter tell the same events as news?

**Key vocabulary:** firework, volcano, quest, courage, simile, metaphor, onomatopoeia, prediction, dilemma, point of view, headline, lead paragraph, quotation, caption, third person, past tense, present perfect, fact, opinion

> Class text: *The Firework-Maker's Daughter* by Philip Pullman — Lila, daughter of Lalchand the firework-maker, sets out alone for the volcano Mount Merapi to face Razvani the Fire-Fiend and win the Royal Sulphur every true firework-maker needs, while her friend Chulak and Hamlet, the King's talking white elephant, race after her.

### Lesson 78 — The opening: what does Lila want?

- **Differentiation:** support: question-word cards (What? Why? Who?) and a picture of the workshop / stretch: write two sentences predicting Lila's biggest problem and the clue that suggests it.

- **Key activity:** Copy poems in best joined handwriting; illustrate; create contents page in alphabetical order by title.
- **Check for understanding:** "How is a contents page organised, and why?" → By page number or alphabetically so readers can find poems.
- **Differentiation:** support: typed copy option / stretch: write an introduction to the anthology.
- **Materials:** paper, pens, binding
- **Joy:** Anthology copies for the school library.

### Lesson 158 — Poetry performance and check

- **Objective:** "By the end, I can perform a learned poem and my own poem." (Assessment – spoken language)
- **Hook:** "Poetry picnic" outdoors.
- **Key activity:** Performances; peer and teacher feedback on expression, fluency and memory.
- **Check for understanding:** "How has your performing improved since the Term 1 Poetry Café?" → Names a specific improvement.

## Unit 12 — Year review: reading, spelling and writing showcase

**Essential question:** How have I grown as a reader, writer, speaker and speller this year — and what is my next step?

**Key vocabulary:** review, fluency, comprehension, evidence, root word, word family, genre, audience, purpose, edit, portfolio, goal

### Lesson 159 — Spelling review: prefixes and word families
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can spell words with this year's prefixes and build word families from a root." (Retrieval; UK NC Y3/4 Spelling)
- **Hook:** "Word-family trees" — the roots *act*, *port* and *view* written on paper tree trunks.
- **Key activity:** Build families on the trees (act → action, react, actor, inactive; port → report, import, portable; view → review, preview, viewer). Revisit un-, dis-, mis-, re-, pre-, sub-, super-, inter-, anti-, auto- with a 10-word dictation.
- **Check for understanding:** "How does knowing 'act' help you spell 'reaction'?" → The root stays the same; you add the prefix re- and the suffix -ion.
- **Differentiation:** support: one family with word cards to arrange / stretch: families where the root's spelling changes (child → children; happy → happiness) and explain the rule.
- **Materials:** paper tree trunks, leaf-shaped cards, dictionaries, dictation sheet
- **Joy:** The class "word-family forest" grows across the wall.

### Lesson 160 — Spelling review: suffixes and the Year 3–4 word list
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can apply the suffix rules and spell the list words I have learned." (Retrieval; UK NC Y3/4 Spelling)
- **Hook:** "Spelling bee grand final" — teams, a bell, no elimination.
- **Key activity:** Team bee using suffix words (-ly, -ous, -tion, -sion, -cian, -ture, -sure) and list words; after each word the team must name the rule or tricky part. Each child writes a personal list of 10 words to practise over the summer.
- **Check for understanding:** "Explain the spelling of 'hopefully'." → hope + ful + ly: 'hope' keeps its e because -ful starts with a consonant; -ly is just added.
- **Differentiation:** support: team roles (one child spells, one checks the rule chart) / stretch: bonus words such as 'occasionally' and 'separate'.
- **Materials:** word lists, rule chart, bell, personal spelling cards
- **Joy:** The bee — with a golden-pencil trophy for the most improved team.

### Lesson 161 — Reading fluency assessment
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can show how my reading fluency has grown since September." (Assessment; CCSS RF.2.4)
- **Hook:** Each child's September reading-speed graph and the book they read aloud then.
- **Key activity:** One-to-one fluency read of an unseen age-appropriate passage (words correct per minute, accuracy, expression) while the class does paired reading of class-text favourites. Children add the final point to their graph.
- **Check for understanding:** "What has helped your reading most this year, and what will you keep doing?" → A specific habit, e.g. repeated reading or reading to a sibling.
- **Differentiation:** support: warm up on a familiar passage first / stretch: an unseen passage from a Year 3 class novel.
- **Materials:** fluency passages at two levels, stopwatch, reading-speed graphs
- **Joy:** Seeing the graph climb — the class celebrates everyone's growth, not their rank.

### Lesson 162 — Reading comprehension: an unseen story
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can answer retrieval, inference and vocabulary questions on an unseen story, giving evidence." (Assessment; CCSS RL.2.1, RL.2.3)
- **Hook:** "A story from a place we haven't visited yet" — a short tale from South America.
- **Key activity:** Read independently; answer 8 questions (retrieval, inference, vocabulary in context, prediction, a question about the author's choices); underline evidence for every inference.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why did the character act that way? Give evidence." → An inference supported by a quotation.
- **Differentiation:** support: the story read aloud first, fewer questions / stretch: an extra question comparing it with a class text.
- **Materials:** unseen story, question booklet, highlighters
- **Joy:** Children choose the next class read-aloud from three blurbs.

### Lesson 163 — Reading comprehension: unseen non-fiction
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can find, summarise and explain information in an unseen non-fiction text." (Assessment; CCSS RI.2.1, RI.2.5)
- **Hook:** "Mystery animal fact file" — the animal's name is hidden.
- **Key activity:** Read a fact file with headings, captions and a diagram; answer questions on features, facts, a summary sentence and one "why" question; identify the animal at the end.
- **Check for understanding:** "Summarise the text in one sentence." → Captures the main idea (e.g. how the animal survives in its habitat), not a single detail.
- **Differentiation:** support: questions read aloud, a word bank / stretch: say which feature was most useful and why.
- **Materials:** fact file, question sheet
- **Joy:** The mystery animal reveal (an axolotl, a pangolin…).

### Lesson 164 — Grammar for writing review: the sentence doctor
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can fix and use this year's grammar and punctuation in my own writing." (Retrieval; UK NC Y3 VGP)
- **Hook:** "The sentence doctor's clinic is open" — poorly sentences on stretchers.
- **Key activity:** Diagnose and cure sentences: missing inverted commas, a/an errors, wrong tense (present perfect vs past), missing commas after fronted clauses, unclear pronouns, a paragraph that needs splitting. Then write a "healthy" example of each.
- **Check for understanding:** "Fix: I like it said Sam." → "I like it," said Sam. (inverted commas around the words spoken; comma inside before the closing mark)
- **Differentiation:** support: one error per sentence with a hint card / stretch: explain each cure using the grammar term.
- **Materials:** sentence cards, toy doctor's kits, mini-whiteboards
- **Joy:** Doctor's coats and stethoscopes for the clinic.

### Lesson 165 — Independent writing: choose and plan
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can choose a genre, audience and purpose and plan my own piece." (UK NC Y3/4 Writing – plan; CCSS W.2.3/W.2.2)

### Lesson 166 — Independent writing: draft

## End-of-year mastery checks

1. **Decoding is complete** — reads unfamiliar multi-syllable words accurately using syllables, prefixes and suffixes. *Seen:* reads a list of 20 unseen words (e.g. 'misunderstanding', 'mysterious', 'machinery') with 90%+ accuracy.
2. **Fluency** — reads an age-appropriate unseen passage aloud at around 90 words correct per minute or more, with phrasing and expression that show understanding. *Seen:* the one-to-one fluency read (L161).
3. **Comprehension with evidence** — answers retrieval, inference and vocabulary questions on unseen fiction and non-fiction, supporting every inference with a quotation. *Seen:* L162–163 booklets.
4. **Spelling rules** — applies the suffix rules (just add, drop e, double, y → i, -le → -ly, -ic → -ically), the prefixes taught, the -tion/-sion/-ssion/-cian, -ous, -ture/-sure families, and spells most Year 3–4 list words taught. *Seen:* dictation of 20 words and use in independent writing.
5. **Joined handwriting** — writes legibly in a joined style with consistent size, spacing and slant. *Seen:* the published independent piece.
6. **Paragraphs** — organises writing in paragraphs with topic sentences, and uses headings/subheadings in non-fiction. *Seen:* the biography and explanation pieces.
7. **Punctuation and grammar for writing** — uses inverted commas for direct speech with reporting clauses; commas after fronted clauses; a/an correctly; past and present perfect appropriately. *Seen:* dialogue in the Cinderella and *Charlotte's Web* chapters.
8. **Genre writing** — plans, drafts, revises and publishes a narrative, a letter, a news report, an explanation and a biography with the right features for audience and purpose. *Seen:* the writing portfolio.
9. **Speaking and listening** — recites a poem of 12+ lines from memory with expression, presents information clearly and takes part in debates, building on others' points. *Seen:* poetry slam, living museum, *Charlotte's Web* debate.
10. **Reading for pleasure** — can name favourite authors and books, recommend a book with reasons and chooses to read independently. *Seen:* book-talk recommendations and reading log.

## Teacher guidance

**Phonics must be finished, not assumed.** The Lesson 1 screen identifies any child who has not secured Phase 5; they need daily 10-minute keep-up sessions from a validated programme, with *decodable* books at their level, until they are secure — alongside (never instead of) the class text, which they access through read-aloud. Do not give a child still learning to decode a book full of graphemes they haven't been taught for independent reading.

**Common misconceptions (and the fix)**
- *Doubling or dropping letters wrongly* ('hopeing', 'runing', 'happyness'): teach the rules exactly — double only after a single short vowel + single consonant (and never double x, w or y); in longer words, double only when the last syllable is stressed (begin → beginning, but visit → visited, garden → gardening); y → i only after a consonant, and y stays before -ing.
- *Prefixes changing the root* ('mispell', 'dissappear'): a prefix never changes the root — just add it (mis + spell = misspell; dis + appear = disappear).
- *Apostrophes for plurals* ('apple's for sale'): revisit that apostrophes show omission or possession only.
- *Inverted commas around the whole sentence* or punctuation outside them: only the exact spoken words go inside, with their punctuation.
- *'a' vs 'an' by letter not sound* ('a hour', 'an university'): listen to the first sound.
- *Inference without evidence* ("She's sad because I think so"): always ask "Which words told you?"
- *Retelling instead of summarising*: a summary keeps the main idea and drops details — practise with strict word limits.

**Reading volume matters as much as the lessons.** Protect daily independent reading and daily read-aloud (15 minutes each). A child who reads 20 minutes a day meets well over a million words a year.

**International and inclusive.** The spine deliberately includes authors and settings from Nigeria/West Africa, China, Zimbabwe, Egypt, North America, a South-East-Asian-inspired kingdom, the Caribbean diaspora and the UK. Swap local-context examples (letters to a pen-pal class, a local hero for the biography) to fit your own country. Where a book references a festival or custom unfamiliar to the class (e.g. Christmas in *Talking Turkeys*), treat it as a window into another family's life.

**Vertical alignment.** Year 1 completed Phase 5 and the core Phase 6 suffixes, sentence punctuation and early conjunctions; Year 2 finishes phonics, joins handwriting, and introduces paragraphs, direct speech and genre writing through whole books. Year 3 (ages 8–9) extends to longer novels, fronted adverbials in all writing, the possessive apostrophe with plurals, more complex suffixes, and writing for effect — so the non-negotiables to leave Year 2 with are fluent decoding, joined handwriting, paragraphs, and evidence-based answers.

**Grammar serves writing.** Every grammar lesson ends with children using the feature in their current piece. Do not run isolated grammar drills or test-prep exercises; judge grammar by what appears in children's own writing.
