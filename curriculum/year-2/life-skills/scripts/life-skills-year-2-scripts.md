# Year 2 Life Skills — Tutor Scripts (Lessons 1–20)

Complete tutor scripts for [Year 2 Life Skills](../life-skills-year-2.md), built to the [scripting guide](../../../SCRIPTING-GUIDE.md) and grounded in `knowledge-base/subjects/computing-life-skills/life-skills.md`. The tone is **warm and never shaming** — big feelings, mistakes and falling-out are normal. Lessons are talk + story + role-play. Every beat ⏱ sums to the duration, with verbatim Say / Ask, the response to listen for, the "if the child is stuck" support (here: keep it emotionally safe, simplify, re-ask warmly), differentiation and assessment evidence. The "check" is usually a reflective question, not a single right answer.

---

## Lesson 1 — My feelings have a warning system
**Life Skills · Year 2 · Unit 1 (Emotional regulation)** | **Duration:** 25 min | **Objective:** "By the end, I can name a body signal that tells me a big feeling is coming."
**You will need:** body-outline ("gingerbread") sheets, crayons, a toy kettle or picture.
**Prior knowledge to retrieve:** Year 1 — naming feelings; one calming breath.
**Key vocabulary:** **body signal**, **warning**, **trigger**, **calm**, **frustrated/nervous**.
**What to emphasize:** big feelings give **body warning signals** (hot cheeks, fast heart, tight tummy) — spotting them early helps us calm down. Warm, normalising.
---
### ⏱ 0–4 min · HOOK
> **Say:** "Before a kettle boils, it gets hot and noisy first — a warning. Your body does the same before a BIG feeling boils over. What does *your* body do?"
**Show:** the toy kettle.  **Do:** mime a kettle heating up and whistling.
**Ask (retrieve prior knowledge):** "Name a big feeling we learned last year." → *listen for:* "angry / sad / worried / excited."
### ⏱ 4–10 min · EXPLAIN (I do)
> **Say:** "Big feelings send **body signals** — like warning lights. When I'm cross, my **cheeks go hot**, my **heart beats fast**, my **fists go tight**. When I'm nervous, my **tummy goes wobbly**. These aren't bad — they're helpful! They warn me so I can calm down *before* I boil over."
**Do:** point to each body part on the outline as you name the signal.
**Emphasize:** "Body signals are **warnings** — spotting them early helps me stay calm."
### ⏱ 10–15 min · EXAMPLE (we do)
> **Say:** "Let's mark on this body where YOU feel anger — hot face? tight fists? Show me."
**Do:** child marks one or two body signals on their outline.
**Ask:** "Name one body signal that warns you a big feeling is coming." → *listen for:* "hot face / fast heart / tight tummy / clenched fists."
### ⏱ 15–20 min · CHECK (reflective)
**Ask:** "What's one body signal that tells you a big feeling is on its way?"
- ✅ **Expected:** a real body signal. — probe: *"Which feeling gives you that signal?"*
- ❌ **If names the feeling, not the body:** → **fix:** "That's the feeling — where do you feel it in your *body*? Tummy? Chest? Hands?"
- ❌ **If "I don't get any":** → **fix:** "Think of the last time you were cross — what did your hands or face do?"
### ⏱ 20–25 min · PRACTICE (you do)
**Do:** Child finishes colouring their "feelings detector" body map and shares one signal they've really noticed. Once secure, interleave: link a signal to a feeling ("tight tummy = nervous").
**Watch for:** confusing feeling words with body signals.
> **If the child is stuck:** 1) keep it warm; 2) name *why* — body vs feeling; 3) point to a body part together; 4) prompt: "where does cross live in your body?"; 5) one signal; 6) "Everyone's signals are different — yours are clever warnings."
### ⏱ 25 min · RECAP
> **Say:** "Why are body signals helpful?" → *listen for:* "they warn me so I can calm down early."
**Process praise:** "You noticed your own warning signals — that's the first step to being the boss of big feelings."
**Revisit later:** tomorrow we build a toolbox to calm those feelings.
---
**Differentiation** — support: point to a body part on the outline. / stretch: link a signal to a feeling.
**Extension / if the child finishes early:** add a 'warning light' colour where the feeling is strongest.
**Assessment evidence:** child names a body signal that warns of a big feeling.

## Lesson 2 — Building my calm-down toolbox
**Life Skills · Year 2 · Unit 1 (Emotional regulation)** | **Duration:** 30 min | **Objective:** "By the end, I can choose from three calming tools and pick a favourite."
**You will need:** "tool" cards, a real box, toolbox templates, crayons.
**Prior knowledge to retrieve:** body warning signals (L1); Year 1 breath.
**Key vocabulary:** **calm-down tool**, **belly breathing**, **finger breathing**, **squeeze and release**, **count back**.
**What to emphasize:** we have **several tools** to calm big feelings — try them and find your favourites. Warm, playful.
---
### ⏱ 0–3 min · HOOK
> **Say:** "I've got a real toolbox here — but these tools don't fix wobbly chairs. They fix big feelings! Let's open it."
**Do:** open the box of calm-down cards.
**Ask (retrieve prior knowledge):** "What warns you a big feeling is coming?" → *listen for:* a body signal.
### ⏱ 3–10 min · EXPLAIN (I do)
> **Say:** "Here are four tools. **Belly breathing** — hand on tummy, slow breath in and out. **Five-finger breathing** — trace your hand, breathe up and down each finger. **Squeeze and release** — scrunch your muscles tight, then let go. **Count back from ten**. Watch me do each one. Different tools suit different days."
**Do:** demonstrate all four tools.
**Emphasize:** "Lots of tools — find the ones that work for *you*."
### ⏱ 10–17 min · EXAMPLE (we do)
> **Say:** "Try finger breathing with me — trace and breathe… now squeeze and release."
**Do:** child practises two tools.
**Ask:** "Name two calming tools you could use when a feeling gets big." → *listen for:* any two (belly/finger breathing, squeeze, count back, take a break).
### ⏱ 17–22 min · CHECK (reflective)
**Ask:** "Show me one calming tool and tell me when you'd use it."
- ✅ **Expected:** demonstrates a tool + a situation. — probe: *"Which is your favourite so far?"*
- ❌ **If can't recall a tool:** → **fix:** "Let's do finger breathing together — trace and breathe."
- ❌ **If picks none / unsure:** → **fix:** "Try two and feel which one calms you most — there's no wrong choice."
### ⏱ 22–30 min · PRACTICE (you do)
**Do:** Child tries each tool, then builds a personal toolbox card of their top three and decorates it. Once secure, interleave: explain which tool fits which feeling.
**Watch for:** rushing the breaths; not actually trying them.
> **If the child is stuck:** 1) keep it gentle; 2) name *why* — needs to feel it; 3) do one tool slowly together; 4) prompt: "did your body slow down?"; 5) pick one favourite; 6) "Your toolbox, your choice."
### ⏱ 30 min · RECAP
> **Say:** "Tell me your top calming tool." → *listen for:* a named tool.
**Process praise:** "You built your own calm-down toolbox — now you've always got it with you."
**Revisit later:** next, the pause button — using a tool *before* you react.
---
**Differentiation** — support: practise just one tool well. / stretch: match a tool to a feeling.
**Extension / if the child finishes early:** teach a tool to the tutor.
**Assessment evidence:** child demonstrates and chooses calming tools.

## Lesson 3 — The pause button
**Life Skills · Year 2 · Unit 1 (Emotional regulation)** | **Duration:** 25 min | **Objective:** "By the end, I can pause before I act on a big feeling."
**You will need:** music, freeze-dance prompts, scenario cards (pencil taken; lost a game).
**Prior knowledge to retrieve:** body signals (L1); calming tools (L2).
**Key vocabulary:** **pause**, **freeze**, **feel it → freeze → name it → tool → choose**.
**What to emphasize:** **pause before acting** so the feeling doesn't decide for you — *you* choose calmly.
---
### ⏱ 0–3 min · HOOK
> **Say:** "Freeze-dance time! When the music stops, freeze. Did you know your body has a **freeze button** for big feelings too?"
**Do:** play a quick freeze-dance round.
**Ask (retrieve prior knowledge):** "What's one calming tool?" → *listen for:* a named tool.
### ⏱ 3–9 min · EXPLAIN (I do)
> **Say:** "Here's the order: **feel it → freeze/pause → name it → use a tool → choose what to do**. The pause is the magic bit — it stops the feeling from grabbing the steering wheel. Watch: someone takes my pencil — I feel cross (hot cheeks!) → I freeze → 'I'm cross' → breathe → *then* I choose to ask for it back calmly."
**Do:** model the five-step pause on a scenario.
**Emphasize:** "**Pause first** — then *you* choose, not the feeling."
### ⏱ 9–14 min · EXAMPLE (we do)
> **Say:** "Let's try one — you lose a game. Feel it, freeze, name it, breathe… now what could you choose?"
**Do:** child walks through the pause steps on a scenario.
**Ask:** "Why is it clever to pause before acting on a big feeling?" → *listen for:* "the feeling doesn't decide — you choose calmly."
### ⏱ 14–19 min · CHECK (reflective)
**Ask:** "Why do we pause before acting when a feeling is big?"
- ✅ **Expected:** so you choose calmly instead of the feeling deciding. — probe: *"What might happen *without* the pause?"*
- ❌ **If "to stop feeling it":** → **fix:** "Feelings are okay — the pause helps you *choose* what to do with them."
- ❌ **If skips straight to reacting:** → **fix:** "Freeze first — then name it and breathe before you act."
### ⏱ 19–25 min · PRACTICE (you do)
**Do:** Child role-plays scenarios, pausing before reacting. Once secure, interleave: act two endings — with and without the pause — and compare.
**Watch for:** reacting without pausing.
> **If the child is stuck:** 1) keep it safe; 2) name *why* — the pause; 3) do "freeze and breathe" together; 4) prompt: "freeze — what does your body say?"; 5) one scenario; 6) "The pause is your superpower."
### ⏱ 25 min · RECAP
> **Say:** "Say the pause steps." → *listen for:* "feel it, freeze, name it, tool, choose."
**Process praise:** "You hit the pause button and *chose* — that's being the boss of your feelings."
**Revisit later:** Unit 1 check; these tools help in friendships next.
---
**Differentiation** — support: practise just "freeze and breathe." / stretch: act both endings (with/without pause).
**Extension / if the child finishes early:** make a "pause button" card for their desk.
**Assessment evidence:** child explains and demonstrates pausing before acting.

> **End-of-unit check (Unit 1):** Child names a body warning signal, demonstrates two calming tools, and explains/shows the pause-before-acting step.

## Lesson 4 — Reading faces and bodies
**Life Skills · Year 2 · Unit 2 (Empathy & kind friendship)** | **Duration:** 25 min | **Objective:** "By the end, I can work out how someone feels from their face and body."
**You will need:** feeling photo cards, role-play prompts.
**Prior knowledge to retrieve:** naming feelings.
**Key vocabulary:** **empathy**, **notice**, **clue**, **face**, **body language**.
**What to emphasize:** you can read feelings from **face and body clues**, even with no words.
---
### ⏱ 0–3 min · HOOK
> **Say:** *(act sad silently — slumped, looking down)* "I haven't said a single word. But… how do you think I'm feeling? How can you tell?"
**Do:** hold the silent sad pose.
**Ask (retrieve prior knowledge):** "Name a feeling." → *listen for:* any feeling word.
### ⏱ 3–9 min · EXPLAIN (I do)
> **Say:** "We can be **feelings detectives** — reading clues on someone's **face and body**. Slumped shoulders, no smile, looking down, hiding, turning away — clues for sad or worried. Big smile, bouncy, open arms — happy. You don't need words to notice."
**Do:** show 2–3 photo cards and read the clues aloud.
**Emphasize:** "Read the **face and body clues** to know how someone feels."
### ⏱ 9–14 min · EXAMPLE (we do)
> **Say:** "Detective time — look at this photo. What's the feeling, and which clue tells you?"
**Do:** child reads a card's feeling and names a clue.
**Ask:** "How can you tell someone is upset without them saying so?" → *listen for:* "their face/body — frowning, looking down, slumped, quiet."
### ⏱ 14–19 min · CHECK (reflective)
**Ask:** "Name two clues that show someone is sad — no words allowed."
- ✅ **Expected:** two body/face clues (slumped, no smile, looking down...). — probe: *"What might have happened to them?"*
- ❌ **If guesses randomly:** → **fix:** "Look at the *clues* — what are their shoulders/mouth/eyes doing?"
- ❌ **If only 'they'd tell me':** → **fix:** "Sometimes people don't say — so we read their face and body."
### ⏱ 19–25 min · PRACTICE (you do)
**Do:** Child plays "feelings detective" with photo cards/role-plays, naming feeling + clue. Once secure, interleave: guess the feeling *and* a possible reason.
**Watch for:** guessing without using clues.
> **If the child is stuck:** 1) keep it warm; 2) name *why* — using clues; 3) point to the face/shoulders together; 4) prompt: "happy people do what with their mouth?"; 5) match one; 6) "You're reading clues like a real detective."
### ⏱ 25 min · RECAP
> **Say:** "Where do we look to know how someone feels?" → *listen for:* "their face and body."
**Process praise:** "You read the clues so carefully — that noticing is the start of empathy."
**Revisit later:** next, imagining how it feels to *be* that person.
---
**Differentiation** — support: match a face card to a feeling word. / stretch: guess the feeling *and* a reason.
**Extension / if the child finishes early:** mime a feeling for the tutor to read.
**Assessment evidence:** child reads feelings from face and body clues.

## Lesson 5 — Standing in someone's shoes
**Life Skills · Year 2 · Unit 2 (Empathy & kind friendship)** | **Duration:** 25 min | **Objective:** "By the end, I can imagine how a situation feels for someone else."
**You will need:** a "left out" story, a pair of paper "empathy shoes."
**Prior knowledge to retrieve:** reading feelings (L4).
**Key vocabulary:** **empathy**, **point of view**, **imagine**, **left out**.
**What to emphasize:** **empathy** is imagining and **caring** how someone else feels — standing in their shoes.
---
### ⏱ 0–3 min · HOOK
> **Say:** "Imagine a brand-new child at the park, standing all alone, while everyone else has friends to play with. How might their tummy feel?"
**Show:** the paper empathy shoes.
**Ask (retrieve prior knowledge):** "How do we tell how someone feels?" → *listen for:* "face and body clues."
### ⏱ 3–9 min · EXPLAIN (I do)
> **Say:** "**Empathy** means imagining how someone else feels — and *caring* about it. I 'stand in their shoes.' Listen to this story of someone left out. I'll pause and ask: how does she feel? What does she *wish* someone would do? That's me using empathy."
**Do:** read part of the story; model an empathy thought aloud.
**Emphasize:** "**Empathy = imagine + care** how someone else feels."
### ⏱ 9–14 min · EXAMPLE (we do)
> **Say:** "Step into the empathy shoes with me. How does the left-out child feel? What would help?"
**Do:** child steps into the shoes and names a feeling + a kind wish.
**Ask:** "What is empathy?" → *listen for:* "imagining and caring how someone else feels."
### ⏱ 14–19 min · CHECK (reflective)
**Ask:** "What does empathy mean, and how might the left-out child feel?"
- ✅ **Expected:** imagining/caring how others feel; e.g. "sad, lonely, wishing to be included." — probe: *"What would *you* wish for in their shoes?"*
- ❌ **If only describes own feelings:** → **fix:** "Step into *their* shoes — how would *they* feel?"
- ❌ **If "I don't know":** → **fix:** "If that were you alone at the park, how would you feel?"
### ⏱ 19–25 min · PRACTICE (you do)
**Do:** Child steps into the shoes for the story and says a kind thing they'd do. Once secure, interleave: describe the other person's feelings in their own words.
**Watch for:** staying in own perspective.
> **If the child is stuck:** 1) keep it warm; 2) name *why* — perspective; 3) "pretend it's you" together; 4) prompt: "how would your tummy feel?"; 5) one feeling; 6) "Lovely — you really imagined how they feel."
### ⏱ 25 min · RECAP
> **Say:** "What does it mean to stand in someone's shoes?" → *listen for:* "imagine how they feel."
**Process praise:** "You truly imagined someone else's feelings — that's empathy in action."
**Revisit later:** next, turning empathy into a kind act.
---
**Differentiation** — support: choose a kind action from picture cards. / stretch: describe the other's feelings in own words.
**Extension / if the child finishes early:** write a kind sentence the left-out child would love to hear.
**Assessment evidence:** child imagines and describes another's feelings (empathy).

## Lesson 6 — Making someone's day better
**Life Skills · Year 2 · Unit 2 (Empathy & kind friendship)** | **Duration:** 25 min | **Objective:** "By the end, I can choose a kind act that fits how someone is feeling."
**You will need:** feeling-to-action match cards, a kindness mission slip.
**Prior knowledge to retrieve:** reading feelings (L4); empathy (L5).
**Key vocabulary:** **kind act**, **fits the feeling**, **comfort**, **include**, **give space**.
**What to emphasize:** the **kind act should fit the feeling** (sad → sit with them; left out → invite them; cross → give space).
---
### ⏱ 0–3 min · HOOK
> **Say:** "A friend drops their whole lunch and looks like they might cry. Quick — what would help *right now*?"
**Do:** mime the dropped-lunch moment.
**Ask (retrieve prior knowledge):** "What's empathy?" → *listen for:* "imagining/caring how someone feels."
### ⏱ 3–9 min · EXPLAIN (I do)
> **Say:** "A kind act works best when it **fits the feeling**. Sad → sit with them, say something kind. Left out → invite them to play. Cross → sometimes give them a little space first. Watch me match: this friend is lonely → I invite them in. The right kindness depends on what they feel."
**Do:** match 2–3 feeling cards to helpful acts.
**Emphasize:** "Choose the kind act that **fits how they feel**."
### ⏱ 9–14 min · EXAMPLE (we do)
> **Say:** "Match with me — someone's left out, what kind act fits? Someone's cross?"
**Do:** child matches feelings to kind acts.
**Ask:** "Your friend looks lonely at the park — name one kind thing that would help." → *listen for:* "invite them to play / sit with them / ask if they're okay."
### ⏱ 14–19 min · CHECK (reflective)
**Ask:** "A friend is lonely — what's a kind act that fits, and why does it fit?"
- ✅ **Expected:** a fitting act + reason (e.g. invite them in, so they're not alone). — probe: *"Would that act fit a *cross* friend too?"*
- ❌ **If a mismatched act:** → **fix:** "Would that help a *lonely* friend? What would include them?"
- ❌ **If "be kind" with no specifics:** → **fix:** "Kind how, exactly? What would you *do*?"
### ⏱ 19–25 min · PRACTICE (you do)
**Do:** Child plans and carries out one "empathy mission" for a real person in their life (a sibling, parent, friend or neighbour). Once secure, interleave: explain why that act fits that feeling.
**Watch for:** generic 'be nice'; mismatched acts.
> **If the child is stuck:** 1) keep it warm; 2) name *why* — fit; 3) pick from two acts together; 4) prompt: "what does a lonely person need?"; 5) one act; 6) "That kindness really fits — well chosen."
### ⏱ 25 min · RECAP
> **Say:** "Why match the kind act to the feeling?" → *listen for:* "so it actually helps how they feel."
**Process praise:** "You chose a kindness that truly fit — and you made someone's day better."
**Revisit later:** Unit 2 check; next unit — keeping going when things are hard.
---
**Differentiation** — support: pick from two kind actions. / stretch: explain why the act fits the feeling.
**Extension / if the child finishes early:** plan a secret kindness for someone at home.
**Assessment evidence:** child chooses a kind act matched to a feeling.

> **End-of-unit check (Unit 2):** Child reads feelings from face and body, explains empathy, and chooses a kind act matched to how someone feels.

## Lesson 7 — The dip: hard means I'm learning
**Life Skills · Year 2 · Unit 3 (Growth mindset)** | **Duration:** 25 min | **Objective:** "By the end, I can say that the tricky 'stuck' feeling means my brain is growing."
**You will need:** a "learning hill" poster (with a dip), tokens.
**Prior knowledge to retrieve:** Year 1 "yet"; trying again.
**Key vocabulary:** **challenge**, **the dip**, **stuck**, **brain growing**, **proud**.
**What to emphasize:** feeling **stuck means your brain is working hard and growing** — the dip is the *middle*, not the end. Never shaming.
---
### ⏱ 0–3 min · HOOK
> **Say:** "Here's a learning hill. You start at the bottom, climb up… but there's a tricky **dip** in the middle where it feels hard. Everyone hits the dip! It's not the end — it's the middle."
**Show:** the learning-hill poster.
**Ask (retrieve prior knowledge):** "What little word did we add last year to 'I can't do it'?" → *listen for:* "yet."
### ⏱ 3–9 min · EXPLAIN (I do)
> **Say:** "When something feels too hard and you feel **stuck**, that's not failing — it's your **brain growing**, like a muscle getting stronger. I'll tell you about a time something felt impossible for me… then suddenly clicked. The 'stuck' feeling means: keep going, you're in the dip."
**Do:** place a token in the dip; tell a quick personal 'it clicked' story.
**Emphasize:** "**Stuck = brain growing.** The dip is the middle, not the end."
### ⏱ 9–14 min · EXAMPLE (we do)
> **Say:** "Where are you on the hill for something tricky right now — bottom, dip, or top? Place your token."
**Do:** child places a token for a current challenge.
**Ask:** "What does the 'stuck' feeling really mean?" → *listen for:* "my brain is working hard and growing."
### ⏱ 14–19 min · CHECK (reflective)
**Ask:** "When something feels too hard and you're stuck — what's really happening?"
- ✅ **Expected:** brain growing / learning, not failing. — probe: *"So what should you do in the dip?"* (keep going).
- ❌ **If "I'm bad at it":** → **fix (warmly):** "Not bad — *in the dip*. That feeling means you're learning. Add 'yet'."
- ❌ **If "give up":** → **fix:** "The dip is the middle — the top is just ahead. What could you try?"
### ⏱ 19–25 min · PRACTICE (you do)
**Do:** Child moves their token up the hill as they picture succeeding, and names the dip feeling as 'learning'. Once secure, interleave: tell a story of getting through their own dip.
**Watch for:** self-critical talk.
> **If the child is stuck:** 1) keep it kind; 2) name *why* — reframing; 3) recall a past success together; 4) prompt: "remember when X felt hard then clicked?"; 5) one example; 6) "Stuck means growing — you're doing it."
### ⏱ 25 min · RECAP
> **Say:** "Finish: 'When I feel stuck, my brain is…'." → *listen for:* "growing / learning."
**Process praise:** "You turned 'I'm stuck' into 'I'm learning' — that's a powerful, kind way to think."
**Revisit later:** tomorrow — what to actually *do* in the dip.
---
**Differentiation** — support: place a token on the hill. / stretch: tell a story of their own dip.
**Extension / if the child finishes early:** draw themselves at the top of the hill, succeeding.
**Assessment evidence:** child reframes 'stuck' as the brain growing.

## Lesson 8 — Try another way
**Life Skills · Year 2 · Unit 3 (Growth mindset)** | **Duration:** 25 min | **Objective:** "By the end, I can name two different strategies to try when one isn't working."
**You will need:** a box with several keys (one works), a strategy menu card.
**Prior knowledge to retrieve:** the dip = learning (L7).
**Key vocabulary:** **strategy**, **try another way**, **break it up**, **ask for help**, **take a break**.
**What to emphasize:** when one way doesn't work, **try a different strategy** — there are several. Never just give up.
---
### ⏱ 0–3 min · HOOK
> **Say:** "Here's a locked box and three keys — but only one opens it. What do you do when the first key doesn't fit? Throw the box away? No — you try another!"
**Do:** try a key that doesn't work.
**Ask (retrieve prior knowledge):** "What does the stuck feeling mean?" → *listen for:* "brain growing."
### ⏱ 3–9 min · EXPLAIN (I do)
> **Say:** "When my first try fails, I have a menu of **strategies**: try again; **try a different way**; **break it into steps**; **ask for help**; **take a break and come back**. Watch — first key fails, I don't quit, I try the next strategy."
**Do:** try keys, narrating the 'try another way' strategy.
**Emphasize:** "First way failed? **Try a different strategy** — never just give up."
### ⏱ 9–14 min · EXAMPLE (we do)
> **Say:** "Pick a tricky task with me. If the first try fails, name two things from the menu you could try."
**Do:** child names two strategies for a real task.
**Ask:** "Your first try didn't work — name two things you could try next." → *listen for:* any two (try again, different way, break it up, ask for help, take a break).
### ⏱ 14–19 min · CHECK (reflective)
**Ask:** "Name two strategies to try when one way isn't working."
- ✅ **Expected:** two valid strategies. — probe: *"Which would you try first for *your* tricky thing?"*
- ❌ **If "give up / I can't":** → **fix:** "Look at the menu — pick two. There's always another key to try."
- ❌ **If only "try harder":** → **fix:** "Trying harder is one way — what's a *different* way? Break it up? Ask for help?"
### ⏱ 19–25 min · PRACTICE (you do)
**Do:** Child picks a tricky task and names two strategies; tries the keys till the box opens. Once secure, interleave: pick the *best* strategy for a specific problem.
**Watch for:** giving up; only one strategy.
> **If the child is stuck:** 1) keep it warm; 2) name *why* — needs options; 3) read the menu together; 4) prompt: "what's another way?"; 5) one strategy; 6) "There's always another key."
### ⏱ 25 min · RECAP
> **Say:** "What do you do when the first way doesn't work?" → *listen for:* "try a different strategy."
**Process praise:** "You kept trying different keys until it clicked — that's exactly what good learners do."
**Revisit later:** next — asking for help is one of the bravest strategies.
---
**Differentiation** — support: choose from a strategy menu. / stretch: pick the best strategy for a problem.
**Extension / if the child finishes early:** add a new strategy to the menu.
**Assessment evidence:** child names two strategies to try when stuck.

## Lesson 9 — Asking for help is brave
**Life Skills · Year 2 · Unit 3 (Growth mindset)** | **Duration:** 20 min | **Objective:** "By the end, I can ask for help in a clear, brave way."
**You will need:** help-asking sentence strips.
**Prior knowledge to retrieve:** strategies incl. 'ask for help' (L8).
**Key vocabulary:** **ask for help**, **brave**, **clear**, **I've tried…**.
**What to emphasize:** asking for help is **brave and clever**, not giving up — and a **clear** ask gets better help.
---
### ⏱ 0–3 min · HOOK
> **Say:** "Quick question: is asking for help *giving up*… or a *clever move*? It's clever AND brave! Let's learn to ask really well."
**Do:** mime struggling, then bravely asking for help.
**Ask (retrieve prior knowledge):** "Name a strategy when you're stuck." → *listen for:* one strategy.
### ⏱ 3–8 min · EXPLAIN (I do)
> **Say:** "A clear ask helps people help you. The brave sentence is: **'I'm stuck on ___. I've tried ___. Can you help me with ___?'** That tells them exactly what you need. Watch me ask a grown-up this way."
**Do:** model the three-part ask.
**Emphasize:** "**Asking is brave** — and a *clear* ask gets you the right help."
### ⏱ 8–12 min · EXAMPLE (we do)
> **Say:** "Fill in the sentence with me for a tricky thing — 'I'm stuck on…, I've tried…, can you help with…?'"
**Do:** child completes the help-asking sentence.
**Ask:** "Is asking for help a sign you've failed?" → *listen for:* "no — it's brave and smart."
### ⏱ 12–16 min · CHECK (reflective)

> **If the child is stuck:** 1) keep it warm; 2) name *why* — want + reason; 3) choose from picture cards together; 4) prompt: "what would make waiting worth it?"; 5) one goal; 6) "Great goal — worth saving for!"
### ⏱ 25 min · RECAP
> **Say:** "Why save for one bigger want?" → *listen for:* "it's worth waiting for / can't buy it with small spends."
**Process praise:** "You chose a goal and a reason — that's planning like a real saver."
**Revisit later:** next — splitting money into spend and save.
---
**Differentiation** — support: choose a goal from picture cards. / stretch: name a small thing to skip.
**Extension / if the child finishes early:** work out roughly how many coins the goal needs.
**Assessment evidence:** child chooses a saving goal and justifies the wait.

## Lesson 18 — Spend a little, save a little
**Life Skills · Year 2 · Unit 6 (Money sense: saving up)** | **Duration:** 30 min | **Objective:** "By the end, I can split a small amount of money into 'spend now' and 'save'."
**You will need:** SPEND and SAVE pots, play coins, small "shop" items, goal cards.
**Prior knowledge to retrieve:** saving (L16); a goal (L17).
**Key vocabulary:** **spend**, **save**, **split/share**, **pot**, **goal**.
**What to emphasize:** you can **spend a little now AND save a little** toward a goal — money can be **shared between** spending and saving.
---
### ⏱ 0–3 min · HOOK
> **Say:** "Two pots today: **SPEND** and **SAVE**. If I gave you three coins, how would you share them between the pots?"
**Show:** the two labelled pots and coins.
**Ask (retrieve prior knowledge):** "What's your saving goal?" → *listen for:* their goal from L17.
### ⏱ 3–9 min · EXPLAIN (I do)
> **Say:** "You don't have to spend *all* or save *all* — you can do **both**. Watch: I get three coins. I put one in **SPEND** (a small treat now) and two in **SAVE** (toward my goal). Some now, some for later. The SAVE coins build toward the goal card."
**Do:** split three coins between the pots; show SPEND buying a small item, SAVE growing.
**Emphasize:** "You can **spend a little AND save a little** — share the coins."
### ⏱ 9–15 min · EXAMPLE (we do)
> **Say:** "Split three coins with me — how many to spend, how many to save?"
**Do:** child splits coins between the pots and uses SPEND in the shop.
**Ask:** "If you get three coins and want to save some, how could you split them?" → *listen for:* a sensible split (e.g. one spend, two save).
### ⏱ 15–20 min · CHECK (reflective)
**Ask:** "Show me a way to split three coins so you spend *and* save."
- ✅ **Expected:** any sensible split (e.g. 1 spend / 2 save). — probe: *"Which pot gets you to your goal?"*
- ❌ **If spends all:** → **fix:** "Could you save even *one* coin toward your goal?"
- ❌ **If thinks it's all-or-nothing:** → **fix:** "You can do both — some in each pot."
### ⏱ 20–30 min · PRACTICE (you do)
**Do:** Child splits coins between SPEND and SAVE, plays the shop with SPEND coins, and grows SAVE toward the goal card; reflects on the choice. Once secure, interleave: work out how many "weeks" of saving reach the goal.
**Watch for:** all-spend; not linking SAVE to the goal.
> **If the child is stuck:** 1) keep it warm; 2) name *why* — both pots; 3) split a fixed 1-and-2 together; 4) prompt: "one to enjoy now, some for the goal?"; 5) one split; 6) "Spend a little, save a little — clever!"
### ⏱ 30 min · RECAP
> **Say:** "Can you spend *and* save the same money?" → *listen for:* "yes — some in each pot."
**Process praise:** "You ran your own spend and save pots — that's grown-up money sense."
**Revisit later:** Unit 6 check; final unit — thinking it through with good questions.
---
**Differentiation** — support: split into a fixed one-and-two. / stretch: work out 'weeks' to reach the goal.
**Extension / if the child finishes early:** plan a spend/save split for five coins.
**Assessment evidence:** child splits money into spend-now and save.

> **End-of-unit check (Unit 6):** Child explains saving keeps money for later, chooses a want to save for, and splits a small amount into spend-now and save.

## Lesson 19 — The question words that open doors
**Life Skills · Year 2 · Unit 7 (Critical thinking)** | **Duration:** 25 min | **Objective:** "By the end, I can ask a good question using who, what, when, where, why or how."
**You will need:** a mystery bag and object, question-word cards.
**Prior knowledge to retrieve:** curiosity; talking and listening.
**Key vocabulary:** **question**, **who, what, when, where, why, how**, **find out**.
**What to emphasize:** **good questions help us find things out** — and there are *no silly questions*. Warm, encouraging.
---
### ⏱ 0–3 min · HOOK
> **Say:** "I've hidden a mystery object in this bag. You can ask me questions to work out what it is — but no peeking! What will you ask first?"
**Show:** the mystery bag.
**Ask (retrieve prior knowledge):** "How do we find things out?" → *listen for:* "ask / look / try."
### ⏱ 3–9 min · EXPLAIN (I do)
> **Say:** "Six magic words open doors to answers: **who, what, when, where, why, how**. A good question uses one of them. Watch: 'What shape is it?' 'Is it bigger than my hand?' Every question helps me learn — questions are *never* silly, they're how we figure things out."
**Do:** model 2–3 good questions about the bag object.
**Emphasize:** "**Who, what, when, where, why, how** — good questions find answers."
### ⏱ 9–14 min · EXAMPLE (we do)
> **Say:** "Ask me a 'what' or 'where' question about the bag with me."
**Do:** child asks a question using a question word.
**Ask:** "Name two question words that help you find out more." → *listen for:* any two of who/what/when/where/why/how.
### ⏱ 14–19 min · CHECK (reflective)
**Ask:** "Ask me a good question to guess the object — which question word did you use?"
- ✅ **Expected:** a question using a question word. — probe: *"Could a 'why' or 'how' question tell you even more?"*
- ❌ **If only yes/no with no question word:** → **fix:** "Start with a question word — 'what colour…?', 'where…?'"
- ❌ **If "I can't think of one":** → **fix:** "Point to a card — 'what' — now finish it: 'what is it made of?'"
### ⏱ 19–25 min · PRACTICE (you do)
**Do:** Child plays "20 questions" with the bag object, using who/what/where. Praise every question. Once secure, interleave: ask a "why" or "how" question, not just yes/no.
**Watch for:** random guesses instead of questions.
> **If the child is stuck:** 1) keep it playful; 2) name *why* — use a question word; 3) point to the word mat together; 4) prompt: "start with 'what'…"; 5) one question; 6) "Great question — questions are how we learn!"
### ⏱ 25 min · RECAP
> **Say:** "Name two question words." → *listen for:* two of who/what/when/where/why/how.
**Process praise:** "You asked brilliant questions to crack the mystery — curiosity is a superpower."
**Revisit later:** next — turning a 'wonder' into a way to find out.
---
**Differentiation** — support: use a question-word mat to point to. / stretch: ask a 'why'/'how' question, not yes/no.
**Extension / if the child finishes early:** set a mystery-bag for the tutor to question.
**Assessment evidence:** child asks a good question using a question word.

## Lesson 20 — Wondering and finding out
**Life Skills · Year 2 · Unit 7 (Critical thinking)** | **Duration:** 25 min | **Objective:** "By the end, I can turn something I wonder about into a question and a way to find out."
**You will need:** sticky notes, a "wonder wall" space.
**Prior knowledge to retrieve:** question words (L19).
**Key vocabulary:** **wonder**, **question**, **find out**, **ask/look/try/watch**.
**What to emphasize:** turn a **wonder** into a **question** and a **way to find out** (ask someone, look in a book, try it, watch closely). Curiosity is celebrated.
---
### ⏱ 0–3 min · HOOK
> **Say:** "I wonder… *why is the sky blue?* I really don't know! But how could we *find out* the answer? Wondering is the start of learning."
**Show:** the wonder-wall space and sticky notes.
**Ask (retrieve prior knowledge):** "Name a question word." → *listen for:* "why / how / what…"
### ⏱ 3–9 min · EXPLAIN (I do)
> **Say:** "A **wonder** is something you're curious about. I turn it into a **question** — 'Why is the sky blue?' — then think of **ways to find out**: ask a grown-up, look in a book, watch closely, or try it. Curiosity is a superpower — every wonder is worth exploring."
**Do:** write a wonder on a sticky note; brainstorm 2 ways to find out.
**Emphasize:** "Turn a **wonder** into a **question** + a **way to find out**."
### ⏱ 9–14 min · EXAMPLE (we do)
> **Say:** "Add your wonder to the wall with me. Now — one way we could find out the answer?"
**Do:** child posts a wonder and names a way to find out.
**Ask:** "You wonder how birds fly — name one way you could find out." → *listen for:* "ask a grown-up, look in a book, watch birds, look it up."
### ⏱ 14–19 min · CHECK (reflective)
**Ask:** "Turn a wonder into a question, then name a way to find out the answer."
- ✅ **Expected:** a wonder-question + a find-out method. — probe: *"Is there a *second* way to find out?"*
- ❌ **If just a statement, not a question:** → **fix:** "Make it a question — start with 'why' or 'how'."
- ❌ **If "you just know":** → **fix:** "How could we *find out* — ask? look? watch? try?"
### ⏱ 19–25 min · PRACTICE (you do)
**Do:** Child adds a wonder to the wall, makes it a question, and names a way to find out. Once secure, interleave: name two different ways to find the answer.
**Watch for:** statements instead of questions; no find-out method.
> **If the child is stuck:** 1) keep it playful; 2) name *why* — question + method; 3) pick a wonder from a list together; 4) prompt: "how could we check?"; 5) one method; 6) "Your wonder belongs on the wall — let's find out!"
### ⏱ 25 min · RECAP
> **Say:** "What can you do with something you wonder about?" → *listen for:* "make it a question and find out (ask/look/watch/try)."
**Process praise:** "You turned a wonder into a question *and* a plan to find out — that's a real thinker."
**Revisit later:** Unit 7 check; curiosity and questioning power every subject in Year 3.
---
**Differentiation** — support: choose a 'wonder' from a list. / stretch: name two ways to find the answer.
**Extension / if the child finishes early:** pick a wonder and start finding out today.
**Assessment evidence:** child turns a wonder into a question and a way to find out.

> **End-of-unit check (Unit 7):** Child asks a good question using a question word, and turns a 'wonder' into a question plus a way to find out.

## End-of-year outcomes
By the end of Year 2, a child reliably shows: **self-regulation** (spots a body signal, uses calming tools, pauses before acting); **empathy** (reads feelings, takes another's view, chooses a fitting kind act); **resilience with a plan** (treats stuck as learning, tries another strategy, asks for help bravely); **healthy habits** (good hand-washing, steady-energy snacks, a balanced day); **personal safety** (body autonomy, trusted adults, surprise-vs-secret, ask-first online and out); **saving** (money kept for a goal, split into spend/save); **curiosity** (good questions, turning a wonder into finding out); and a **kind, warm character** that treats feelings and mistakes as normal.
