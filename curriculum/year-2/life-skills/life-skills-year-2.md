## Year overview

Life Skills is where children practise being the kind of people the school's graduate profile
describes — **self-aware, kind, resilient, money-wise, planet-minded and globally minded**. In Year
2 children move from *naming* feelings (Year 1) to *understanding and managing* them: why feelings
happen, how thoughts change feelings, how to calm down and how to see a situation from someone
else's side. They learn practical ways to **sort out friendship problems fairly**, to tell the
difference between a one-off conflict and bullying, and to be an **upstander**. A **growth-mindset**
unit shows how brains get stronger with effort and mistakes. **Money sense** (timed alongside Maths
Unit 6) covers needs and wants, spending, saving and giving, and simple budgeting. **Sustainability**
turns into action with a school waste audit and a class project. **Global citizenship** introduces
children's rights, fairness and communities around the world, and ends with a **service project**.

Lessons are short, discussion-rich and practical — role play, stories, games and real projects —
with a named picture book in most units. There are no tests; progress is seen in what children say
and do, recorded through reflection journals and teacher observation.

**What Year 2 assumes from Year 1 (ages 6–7):** naming basic feelings; simple calming strategies;
taking turns and sharing; saying sorry; knowing that money is used to buy things and can be saved;
recycling basics; knowing some adults who help us.

**What Year 3 (ages 8–9) relies on from this year:** a toolkit of regulation strategies; the fair-fix
steps for conflict; growth-mindset language; spend/save/give and budgeting; an understanding of
children's rights; experience of planning a small action project.

## Unit 1 — Understanding my feelings

**Essential question:** Why do I feel the way I do — and what can I do about big feelings?

**Key vocabulary:** emotion, feeling, trigger, body clues, mixed feelings, thought, calm, regulate, strategy, empathy, perspective

### Lesson 1 — Feelings have causes
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can name a feeling and what caused it." (CASEL self-awareness; PSHE H17)
- **Hook:** *The Colour Monster* (Anna Llenas) — feelings sorted into coloured jars.
- **Key activity:** Build a class "feelings wheel" with more precise words (frustrated, nervous, proud, disappointed); scenario cards: "What might someone feel and why?"
- **Check for understanding:** "Why might two children feel differently about the same thing, like a test?" → They have different experiences and thoughts about it.
- **Differentiation:** support: picture feelings cards / stretch: use three precise feelings words in a real example.
- **Materials:** *The Colour Monster*, feelings-wheel poster, scenario cards, jars
- **Joy:** Colour jars — children add a coloured bead for their feeling today.

### Lesson 2 — Body clues
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can notice where I feel emotions in my body." (CASEL self-awareness)
- **Hook:** "Where do you feel butterflies?"
- **Key activity:** Body-outline mapping: colour where anger, worry and excitement show (hot face, fast heart, tight tummy); discuss early warning signs.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why is it useful to notice body clues early?" → You can use a calming strategy before the feeling gets too big.
- **Differentiation:** support: pre-drawn body outline with prompts / stretch: compare how two feelings can feel similar in the body (excitement and nervousness).
- **Materials:** body-outline sheets, coloured pencils
- **Joy:** Life-size body outline on the floor.

### Lesson 3 — Mixed and changing feelings
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can explain that we can feel more than one feeling at once." (CASEL self-awareness)
- **Hook:** "Moving to a new house: happy or sad?"
- **Key activity:** Discuss mixed feelings with stories (starting a new school, a goodbye party); "feelings smoothie" drawings with two or three colours.
- **Check for understanding:** "Give an example of when someone could feel excited and nervous at the same time." → e.g. performing in a concert.
- **Differentiation:** support: choose from picture scenarios / stretch: write a short diary entry with mixed feelings (English link).
- **Materials:** story cards, drawing paper
- **Joy:** "Feelings smoothie" art.

### Lesson 4 — Calming strategies toolkit
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can use three calming strategies and choose the right one." (CASEL self-management)
- **Hook:** A shaken glitter jar settling.
- **Key activity:** Practise: square breathing, counting to 10 backwards, "5-4-3-2-1" senses, squeezing a stress ball, asking for a break; make a personal toolkit card.
- **Check for understanding:** "Which strategy would you use if you were angry in the playground, and why?" → e.g. step away and breathe, because it calms the body quickly.
- **Differentiation:** support: picture toolkit / stretch: teach a strategy to a partner.
- **Materials:** glitter jar, stress balls, toolkit cards
- **Joy:** Make a personal glitter jar.

### Lesson 5 — Helpful and unhelpful thoughts
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can change an unhelpful thought into a helpful one." (CASEL self-management)
- **Hook:** *Silly Billy* (Anthony Browne) — Billy's worries and the worry dolls from Guatemala.
- **Key activity:** Sort thoughts ("I'll never be good at this" vs "I can get better with practice"); practise "flipping" thoughts; make worry dolls from pegs.
- **Check for understanding:** "How can changing a thought change a feeling?" → A kinder, more helpful thought can make us feel calmer or braver.
- **Differentiation:** support: choose the helpful thought from two / stretch: write their own flips.
- **Materials:** *Silly Billy*, thought cards, wooden pegs, wool
- **Joy:** Worry-doll making.

### Lesson 6 — Empathy: walking in someone's shoes
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can imagine how someone else feels and respond kindly." (CASEL social awareness)
- **Hook:** A pair of big shoes — "Step into these and become someone else."
- **Key activity:** Role play scenarios from different perspectives; practise empathetic responses ("That sounds hard. Can I help?").
- **Check for understanding:** "What's the difference between feeling sorry for someone and empathy?" → Empathy is trying to understand how they feel from their side.
- **Differentiation:** support: sentence stems / stretch: respond to a complex scenario.
- **Materials:** shoes, scenario cards
- **Joy:** "Shoe swap" role play.

**End-of-unit check (reflection):** In a journal entry or conversation, the child names a feeling, its cause and body clues, and explains which calming strategy they would use.

## Unit 2 — Friendship, conflict and kindness

**Essential question:** How can we be good friends — and fix things fairly when they go wrong?

**Key vocabulary:** friendship, conflict, compromise, apology, include, exclude, bullying, upstander, bystander, respect, fair, listen

### Lesson 7 — What makes a good friend?
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can describe qualities of a good friend and how to show them." (CASEL relationship skills; PSHE R1)
- **Hook:** *Beegu* (Alexis Deacon) — an alien who wants a friend.
- **Key activity:** Build a "friendship recipe" (ingredients: kindness, listening, sharing, forgiveness); discuss how Beegu felt.
- **Check for understanding:** "Which ingredient matters most to you, and why?" → A reasoned choice.
- **Differentiation:** support: picture ingredient cards / stretch: write a friendship poem.
- **Materials:** *Beegu*, recipe templates
- **Joy:** Friendship "recipe cards" displayed.

### Lesson 8 — Including everyone
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can notice when someone is left out and invite them in." (CASEL social awareness)
- **Hook:** *The Invisible Boy* (Trudy Ludwig) — Brian, who nobody notices.
- **Key activity:** Discuss how Brian becomes visible; playground "buddy bench" plan; role play inviting someone to play.
- **Check for understanding:** "What small thing can you do to include someone?" → e.g. say hello, invite them to play, sit with them.
- **Differentiation:** support: phrase cards / stretch: design a class inclusion idea.
- **Materials:** *The Invisible Boy*, phrase cards
- **Joy:** "Invite" role play.

### Lesson 9 — Both sides of the story
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can listen to both sides of a disagreement." (CASEL relationship skills)
- **Hook:** Two puppets arguing over a ball.
- **Key activity:** Retell a conflict from each side; notice each person's feelings and needs.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why is it important to hear both sides before deciding who is right?" → Each person may have a reason we didn't know.
- **Differentiation:** support: puppet with stems / stretch: mediate a puppet argument.
- **Materials:** puppets, scenario cards
- **Joy:** Puppet show.

### Lesson 10 — The fair-fix steps
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can use steps to solve a conflict: stop, say how I feel, listen, find a fair fix." (CASEL responsible decision-making)
- **Hook:** A "peace path" drawn on the floor.
- **Key activity:** Learn the steps (stop and calm down; use "I feel… when…"; listen; brainstorm fixes; choose one both agree on); practise on the peace path.
- **Check for understanding:** "What makes a fix 'fair'?" → Both people agree and it meets both of their needs.
- **Differentiation:** support: step cards on the path / stretch: coach others through the steps.
- **Materials:** floor path, step cards
- **Joy:** Walking the peace path.

### Lesson 11 — A real apology
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can give a sincere apology that takes responsibility and makes amends." (CASEL relationship skills)
- **Hook:** Two apologies: "Sorry… whatever" vs a real one.
- **Key activity:** Learn a real apology: say what you did, why it was wrong, how you'll make it right, what you'll do next time; practise.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why is 'sorry, but you started it' not a real apology?" → It blames the other person instead of taking responsibility.
- **Differentiation:** support: apology frame / stretch: role play accepting an apology gracefully.
- **Materials:** apology frame cards
- **Joy:** Apology role play.

### Lesson 12 — Bullying and being an upstander
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can tell the difference between conflict and bullying and know how to be an upstander." (PSHE R19; CASEL responsible decision-making)
- **Hook:** *Each Kindness* (Jacqueline Woodson) — a missed chance to be kind.
- **Key activity:** Define bullying (repeated, on purpose, hurtful, power imbalance) vs a one-off falling-out; upstander actions (support the person, tell an adult, don't join in).
- **Check for understanding:** "What makes something bullying rather than a one-off argument?" → It happens repeatedly and on purpose to hurt someone who finds it hard to defend themselves.
- **Differentiation:** support: sort scenario cards / stretch: create an upstander poster.
- **Materials:** *Each Kindness*, scenario cards
- **Joy:** "Ripple of kindness" — a stone dropped in water; each child names a kind act.

**End-of-unit check (reflection):** Walk through the fair-fix steps for a scenario and explain the difference between conflict and bullying and what an upstander does.

## Unit 3 — Growth mindset and resilience

**Essential question:** How does my brain grow — and how can mistakes help me learn?

**Key vocabulary:** brain, neuron, practise, effort, mistake, challenge, growth mindset, "yet", resilience, goal, step, feedback

### Lesson 13 — My elastic brain
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can explain that my brain gets stronger when I practise hard things." (CASEL self-management)
- **Hook:** *Your Fantastic Elastic Brain* (JoAnn Deak).
- **Key activity:** Model neurons connecting with pipe cleaners (connections grow with practice); share something they learned through practice (riding a bike, times tables).
- **Check for understanding:** "Why does practising something hard make it easier over time?" → Brain connections get stronger each time we practise.
- **Differentiation:** support: picture model / stretch: explain using the word 'neuron'.
- **Materials:** book, pipe cleaners
- **Joy:** Pipe-cleaner brains.

### Lesson 14 — The power of "yet" and mistakes
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can see mistakes as chances to learn and use the word 'yet'." (CASEL self-management)
- **Hook:** *The Girl Who Never Made Mistakes* (Mark Pett & Gary Rubinstein).
- **Key activity:** Discuss Beatrice's fear of mistakes; "mistake of the week" celebration; flip "I can't" to "I can't yet."
- **Check for understanding:** "How can a mistake help you learn?" → It shows what to change or practise next.
- **Differentiation:** support: sentence flip cards / stretch: describe a famous person who failed before succeeding.
- **Materials:** book, flip cards
- **Joy:** "Mistake of the week" award.

### Lesson 15 — Perseverance: the most magnificent thing
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can use strategies to keep going when something is hard." (CASEL self-management)
- **Hook:** *The Most Magnificent Thing* (Ashley Spires) — a girl whose invention keeps going wrong.
- **Key activity:** Build a tower or bridge from limited materials; when it fails, use strategies (take a break, try another way, ask for help).
- **Check for understanding:** "What did the girl do when she felt like giving up, and what did you do?" → Took a walk, came back and saw what was good; describes their own strategy.
- **Differentiation:** support: simpler build / stretch: improve design after a test.
- **Materials:** book, spaghetti, marshmallows or tape, card
- **Joy:** Build challenge.

### Lesson 16 — Goals in small steps
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can set a goal and break it into small steps." (CASEL self-management)
- **Hook:** A staircase drawn on the board — "You climb one step at a time."
- **Key activity:** Choose a personal goal (learn the 4× table, read a chapter book, skip 50 times); plan 3–4 steps; track weekly.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why break a big goal into steps?" → Each step is achievable and shows progress.
- **Differentiation:** support: goal menu / stretch: add a date and how to measure success.
- **Materials:** staircase goal sheets
- **Joy:** Staircase tracker with stickers.

### Lesson 17 — Feedback helps me grow
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can give and receive kind, specific, helpful feedback." (CASEL relationship skills)
- **Hook:** "Austin's Butterfly" — a famous example of a child's drawing improving through six drafts with feedback.
- **Key activity:** Watch or see the drafts; practise feedback "kind, specific, helpful" on a partner's drawing; improve their own.
- **Check for understanding:** "What makes feedback helpful rather than hurtful?" → It's kind, specific and suggests what to do next.
- **Differentiation:** support: feedback stems / stretch: give feedback on two drafts.
- **Materials:** Austin's Butterfly images, drawing paper
- **Joy:** Seeing their own drawing improve.

**End-of-unit check (reflection):** The child describes a time they persevered, uses "yet" appropriately, and shows progress on their stepped goal.

## Unit 4 — Money sense: needs, wants, saving and budgeting

**Essential question:** How can I make wise choices about money?

**Key vocabulary:** money, earn, spend, save, give, need, want, price, value, budget, goal, bank, interest, advert, choice

### Lesson 18 — Where does money come from?
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can explain that people earn money by working and use it to pay for things." (Financial education – earning)
- **Hook:** "Does money grow on trees? Where does it come from?"
- **Key activity:** Discuss jobs and earning; the flow of money (work → pay → spend/save); banks keep money safe; introduce currency from different countries (Maths Unit 6 link).
- **Check for understanding:** "Why do people go to work?" → To earn money to pay for needs and wants (and because work can be meaningful).
- **Differentiation:** support: picture flow chart / stretch: interview a family member about their job.
- **Materials:** job picture cards, play money, world coins
- **Joy:** "Job fair" role play.

### Lesson 19 — Needs and wants
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can sort needs and wants and explain the difference." (Financial education)
- **Hook:** *Those Shoes* (Maribeth Boelts) — Jeremy wants the shoes everyone has.
- **Key activity:** Sort items; discuss that needs can differ by situation; Jeremy's choice.
- **Check for understanding:** "Is a coat a need or a want? Can it be both?" → A warm coat is a need in cold weather; a designer coat is a want.
- **Differentiation:** support: picture sort / stretch: explain a tricky item.
- **Materials:** book, item cards
- **Joy:** "Desert island" choice game.

### Lesson 20 — Spend, save, give
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can divide money into spend, save and give and explain each." (Financial education)
- **Hook:** *A Chair for My Mother* (Vera B. Williams) — a family saves coins in a jar for a new chair after a fire.
- **Key activity:** Make three jars; share play money between them; discuss choices.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why did the family save instead of spending straight away?" → To buy something bigger and important that they couldn't afford at once.
- **Differentiation:** support: equal split / stretch: choose their own split and justify.
- **Materials:** book, jars, labels, play money
- **Joy:** Decorating spend/save/give jars.

### Lesson 21 — Saving for a goal
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can work out how many weeks it takes to save for something." (Financial education; Maths link)
- **Hook:** "A kite costs 12. You save 3 a week. When can you buy it?"
- **Key activity:** Calculate weeks with repeated addition/division; savings chart; discuss "interest" as a reward for saving in a bank.
- **Check for understanding:** "If you save 4 a week for a 20 toy, how long will it take? Explain." → 5 weeks, because 5 × 4 = 20.
- **Differentiation:** support: counters / stretch: include a birthday gift that speeds up saving.
- **Materials:** savings charts, counters, play money
- **Joy:** Savings thermometer.

### Lesson 22 — Adverts and choices
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can explain how adverts try to persuade us." (Financial education; digital literacy)
- **Hook:** A toy advert on video.
- **Key activity:** Spot persuasion tricks (music, famous people, "limited time"); compare advert with reality (the toy up close).
- **Check for understanding:** "Why do adverts make toys look so exciting?" → To persuade us to buy them.
- **Differentiation:** support: trick-spotting checklist / stretch: make a truthful advert.
- **Materials:** adverts, a toy
- **Joy:** "Honest advert" making.

### Lesson 23 — Budget challenge and unit reflection
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can plan spending within a budget." (Financial education; Maths link)
- **Hook:** "Plan a class picnic for 20."
- **Key activity:** Plan within budget; compare choices; reflect.
- **Check for understanding:** "What did you give up to stay within budget, and why?" → A trade-off with a reason.
- **Differentiation:** support: fewer items / stretch: include a 'give' element.
- **Materials:** price lists, play money
- **Joy:** Picnic planning.

**End-of-unit check (reflection):** Sort needs and wants, split money into spend/save/give with reasons, and plan a purchase within a budget.

## Unit 5 — Sustainability: our planet, our choices

**Essential question:** How do my everyday choices affect the planet — and what can we do together?

**Key vocabulary:** sustainability, resource, waste, reduce, reuse, recycle, compost, energy, pollution, plastic, habitat, audit, action

### Lesson 24 — Where does our rubbish go?
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can explain what happens to rubbish and why reducing waste matters." (UNESCO ESD; Geography link)
- **Hook:** A bag of (clean) classroom rubbish tipped out.
- **Key activity:** Sort into landfill, recycling, compost; discuss that some plastics take hundreds of years to break down; the "3 Rs" in order (reduce first).
- **Check for understanding:** "Why is 'reduce' better than 'recycle'?" → Reducing means less waste is made at all; recycling still uses energy.
- **Differentiation:** support: picture sort / stretch: explain what happens at a recycling plant.
- **Materials:** clean rubbish, sorting bins, gloves
- **Joy:** Rubbish sort.

### Lesson 25 — School waste audit
- **Duration:** 30 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can collect data on our school's waste and suggest improvements." (UNESCO ESD; Maths/Computing link)
- **Hook:** "Detectives: how much does our school throw away?"
- **Key activity:** Weigh or count a day's waste from classrooms; record in tables and charts; identify the biggest problem.
- **Check for understanding:** "What does our data tell us, and what should we change first?" → Uses data to choose an action.
- **Differentiation:** support: count items / stretch: weigh and compare classes.
- **Materials:** gloves, scales, tally sheets
- **Joy:** Waste detectives.

### Lesson 26 — Plastic and the ocean
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can explain how plastic reaches the ocean and harms wildlife." (UNESCO ESD; Geography link)
- **Hook:** *One Plastic Bag* (Miranda Paul) — Isatou Ceesay and the women of Gambia who recycled plastic bags into purses.
- **Key activity:** Trace plastic from street to sea (Geography rivers link); discuss the Gambia story as a real solution.
- **Check for understanding:** "How did Isatou Ceesay turn a problem into a solution?" → She and other women recycled plastic bags into purses to sell, cleaning up and earning money.
- **Differentiation:** support: sequence cards / stretch: design an upcycled product.
- **Materials:** book, sequence cards
- **Joy:** Upcycling craft.

### Lesson 27 — Saving water and energy
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can name ways to save water and energy at school and home." (UNESCO ESD)
- **Hook:** Measure how much water runs from a tap in one minute.
- **Key activity:** Measure water (Maths link: capacity); energy hunt (lights, screens left on); make "switch off" signs.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why does turning off the tap while brushing your teeth help?" → A running tap wastes several litres of clean water a minute.
- **Differentiation:** support: picture checklist / stretch: calculate water saved in a week.
- **Materials:** measuring jug, tap, timer, sign materials
- **Joy:** "Energy patrol" badges.

### Lesson 28 — Food waste and growing food
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can explain why food waste matters and how we can reduce it." (UNESCO ESD; Science link)
- **Hook:** Photos of wasted food — "About a third of the food produced in the world is lost or wasted."
- **Key activity:** Plan to reduce lunch waste; start a compost bin or grow herbs.
- **Check for understanding:** "What happens to food scraps in a compost bin?" → They break down into rich soil that helps plants grow.
- **Differentiation:** support: picture plan / stretch: measure lunch waste before and after.
- **Materials:** compost bin, seeds, pots
- **Joy:** Planting herbs.

### Lesson 29 — Class action project
- **Duration:** 30 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can plan and carry out a sustainability action with my class." (UNESCO ESD; CASEL responsible decision-making)
- **Hook:** *The Promise* (Nicola Davies) — a girl who plants acorns and turns a grey city green.
- **Key activity:** Vote on an action (litter pick, recycling campaign, planting); plan roles; carry it out; measure impact.
- **Check for understanding:** "How will we know our action made a difference?" → Measure before and after (e.g. waste weight, number of plants).
- **Differentiation:** support: clear role cards / stretch: lead a team.
- **Materials:** book, project materials
- **Joy:** Doing the project.

**End-of-unit check (reflection):** Present the class action project: what the problem was, what we did, what the data showed, and one habit each child will keep.

## Unit 6 — Global citizenship and service

**Essential question:** What does it mean to be a citizen of the world — and how can I help others?

**Key vocabulary:** citizen, community, rights, responsibilities, fair, equal, UN Convention on the Rights of the Child, culture, respect, fact, opinion, service, volunteer

### Lesson 30 — Rights of the child
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can name some rights every child has and why they matter." (UNCRC; global citizenship)
- **Hook:** *We Are All Born Free* (Amnesty International) — the Universal Declaration of Human Rights, illustrated by famous artists: "Rights for everyone — and there's a special list just for children."
- **Key activity:** Learn that the UN Convention on the Rights of the Child (1989) lists rights for every child; sort wants vs rights; discuss rights and responsibilities.
- **Check for understanding:** "What's the difference between a right and a want?" → A right is something every child needs to be safe and grow (like education); a want is nice to have.
- **Differentiation:** support: picture rights cards / stretch: link a right to a responsibility.
- **Materials:** book, rights cards
- **Joy:** Class "rights charter."

### Lesson 31 — Fair and equal
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can explain that fair doesn't always mean everyone gets the same." (Global citizenship; CASEL social awareness)
- **Hook:** The "plasters for everyone" activity — each child gets a plaster on the same spot, whatever their hurt.
- **Key activity:** Discuss fairness vs sameness; scenarios where people need different help.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why is it fair for someone with glasses to have them even if others don't?" → Fairness means everyone gets what they need to succeed.
- **Differentiation:** support: picture scenarios / stretch: find an example in school.
- **Materials:** plasters, scenario cards
- **Joy:** "Fair or not?" game.

### Lesson 32 — Communities around the world
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can describe how children in different communities live, noticing similarities and differences." (Global citizenship; Geography link)
- **Hook:** *Last Stop on Market Street* (Matt de la Peña) — CJ's bus ride through his city.
- **Key activity:** Compare daily lives of children in several countries (school, food, play); focus on what's shared; avoid stereotypes.
- **Check for understanding:** "What did you notice that children everywhere have in common?" → e.g. play, family, learning.
- **Differentiation:** support: picture cards / stretch: compare with partner-school children.
- **Materials:** book, photos from partner schools or reliable sources
- **Joy:** Letters to the partner school.

### Lesson 33 — Fact, opinion and respectful discussion
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can disagree respectfully and tell facts from opinions." (Global citizenship; critical thinking)
- **Hook:** "Pizza is the best food in the world." — fact or opinion?
- **Key activity:** Sort statements; debate a light topic with "I respectfully disagree because…".
- **Check for understanding:** "How can you disagree with someone and still be kind?" → Listen, use respectful words, focus on ideas not the person.
- **Differentiation:** support: sentence stems / stretch: summarise the other side.
- **Materials:** statement cards
- **Joy:** "Agree/disagree" line game.

### Lesson 34 — Planning a service project
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can plan a way to help others in our community." (Global citizenship; service)
- **Hook:** A visit (or video message) from a local helper — a librarian, a care-home worker or a food-bank volunteer: "Who in our community could use help?"
- **Key activity:** Brainstorm needs; vote on one project (collecting books for a children's hospital, making cards and songs for a care home, a food-bank collection); plan the steps, roles, timeline and how we'll tell people about it (a poster, a letter home — English link).
- **Check for understanding:** "Who will our project help, how will it help, and why does it matter?" → Names the group, the concrete benefit and a reason linked to fairness or kindness.
- **Differentiation:** support: choose a role from picture role cards (collector, designer, counter) / stretch: lead a planning team and write the letter to families.
- **Materials:** planning sheets, role cards, poster paper
- **Joy:** Voting for the project and designing the campaign poster.

### Lesson 35 — Service project and year reflection
- **Duration:** 30 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can carry out our service project and reflect on how I've grown this year." (Reflection)
- **Hook:** Project day — the collection boxes are full (or the cards are ready to deliver).
- **Key activity:** Count and pack the collection (Maths link) or deliver cards and songs; then a reflection circle and journal page looking back over the year: a feeling I can now manage, a friendship skill I use, a goal I reached, a money choice I made, a planet-friendly habit I kept.
- **Check for understanding:** "What difference did our project make, and what did you learn about yourself this year?" → Names a concrete result and a personal strength or change.
- **Differentiation:** support: reflection stems and picture prompts / stretch: give a short thank-you speech to helpers or families.
- **Materials:** collection boxes, cards, reflection journals
- **Joy:** Seeing the difference their kindness made.

**End-of-unit check (reflection):** Explain one child's right and one responsibility, and reflect on the service project and personal growth across the year.

## End-of-year mastery checks

1. **Emotional awareness** — names precise feelings, their causes and body clues. *Seen:* journal and conversations.
2. **Self-regulation** — uses a chosen calming strategy in real situations. *Seen:* teacher observation.
3. **Empathy** — responds kindly by considering others' perspectives. *Seen:* role play and daily behaviour.
4. **Conflict resolution** — uses the fair-fix steps and gives a real apology. *Seen:* observation.
5. **Upstanding** — knows the difference between conflict and bullying and what to do. *Seen:* scenario discussion.
6. **Growth mindset** — uses "yet," perseveres and acts on feedback. *Seen:* goal staircase and drafts.
7. **Money sense** — sorts needs and wants, uses spend/save/give and plans within a budget. *Seen:* budget challenge.
8. **Sustainability** — explains reduce/reuse/recycle and contributed to a measured class action. *Seen:* project presentation.
9. **Global citizenship** — names children's rights and responsibilities and took part in service. *Seen:* rights charter and service reflection.

## Teacher guidance

**A safe space first.** Agree class ground rules (listen, respect, no names in stories, the right to pass). Follow safeguarding procedures if a child discloses something worrying.

**Common misunderstandings (and the fix)**
- *"Some feelings are bad."* All feelings are okay; some actions are not. Focus on what we do with feelings.
- *"Fair means the same."* Use the plaster activity.
- *"Saying sorry fixes everything."* A real apology includes making amends and changing behaviour.
- *"Growth mindset means just trying harder."* It also means trying different strategies and asking for help.
- *"Recycling solves waste."* Reduce and reuse come first.

**Cultural and family sensitivity.** Families have different incomes, beliefs and traditions — use play money and hypothetical scenarios; never ask children to reveal family finances; celebrate many ways of giving and belonging.

**Links.** Unit 4 runs alongside Maths Unit 6 (money); Unit 5 links to Geography rivers and Science soils; Unit 1 links to PE & Health Unit 8.

**Vertical alignment.** Year 1 introduced feelings vocabulary, sharing, simple saving and recycling. Year 3 (ages 8–9) develops these into managing peer pressure, deeper conflict resolution, earning and simple enterprise, and larger community and sustainability projects.
