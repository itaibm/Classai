# Lesson 2 — Light hands: build-up lines
**Art & Design · Year 2 · Unit 1 (Sketchbook habits & line revisited)** · 30 min · **Delivery: 🤖 AI-led, with 🧑‍🏫 a grown-up for the drawing**
**Objective:** "By the end, I can draw lightly first and darken only the lines I want to keep."
*(Human view of [`lesson-02-light-hands-build-up-lines.json`](./lesson-02-light-hands-build-up-lines.json) — the JSON is the source of truth.)*

**Lay out before the lesson:** sketchbook or plain paper, a soft pencil (any pencil held gently works), a fine-liner or dark felt-tip/pencil for the keeper lines, and **one chunky, simple object** to draw — a mug, glue stick, toy block or small box. An eraser only if needed, used sparingly.

## Flow

- **Hook (0–3):** the ghost-line mystery — "which line comes first?" Two cards: 👻 pale ghost lines and 🖊️ a dark keeper line. Artists test softly first, then choose what to darken.
- **Explain (3–8):** the one new idea — **light first, then darken only the keeper lines**. Whiteboard: several soft searching lines, one chosen and inked. "Light enough to change, clear enough to help."
- **Example (8–13):** the tutor models a cup: look → feather-light build-up lines for the big shape → look again → pick the line that best matches → darken only that one; ghost lines stay visible as proof of thinking.
- **Check (13–16):** "Why draw lightly first?" → so you can fix the shape before committing to a dark line. Playbook: "so nobody can see it" (light ≠ invisible), "artists can't use dark lines" (they do — later), "so you don't need to look" (looking is the whole point).
- **Practice — your part (16–27):** the child draws the real object with ghost lines, then darkens only the keeper lines. Set the object where they can see it clearly and hand over the materials. If they press hard, ask for three feather-light test lines at the side; "it's messy" → "messy means searching — now choose"; early finisher adds one careful detail (rim, handle, bottom edge). **Hand back when the ghost lines are drawn and the keeper outline is darkened.**
- **Recap (27–30):** the assessment is **their drawing** — held up next to the object, the child points to a pale ghost line and a dark keeper line and says *why* they kept it ("it matched the side of the real cup"). Playbook: "I darkened all of them" → choose one now; "because it looks nice" → compare with the object; "no ghost lines, I pressed hard" → three light test lines together and redo one edge light-first.

**Practice bank (12 items, AI):** level 1 — purpose MC, "a light line is pale and easy to change" true/false, process order, outline/detail/light-line match; level 2 — *build-up* fillBlank, first-vs-later sort (pencil vs fine-liner), explain in your own words, "wobbly line → press harder?" trap; level 3 — pick the keeper among three lines, overlapping objects, write a self-check sentence (re-teach: sentence-starter steps), plan the page.

**Adaptivity:** start level 2 · two confident answers in a row → up · missing the purpose of light lines, reaching for the fine-liner too early, or no keeper explanation after one hint → down · mastery = can say, choose or *show* the process: look, light build-up lines, fix, choose keepers, darken only those.

**Video:** none — the time goes on real looking and drawing.

**Differentiation:** support — very soft pencil, a chunky object, "tickle the page", a small test corner of light lines first · stretch — two overlapping objects, darkening only the visible keeper edges and leaving hidden overlap lines pale.
**Evidence:** the child's own drawing shows pale build-up lines and a darker chosen outline (not every line darkened), and they can point to a ghost line and a keeper line and justify the keeper by comparing with the real object.
**Revisit:** next lesson, warm up with light build-up lines before adding detail or texture — big shape first, details after.
