# Year 2 Art & Design — Tutor Scripts (Lessons 1–18)

Complete tutor scripts for [Year 2 Art & Design](../art-design-year-2.md), built to the [scripting guide](../../../SCRIPTING-GUIDE.md) and grounded in `knowledge-base/subjects/art-music/art-ks1-ks2.md`. Art lessons run **demo → explore → create**, with the motto **make → look → improve**. Each beat ⏱ sums to the duration. Because art "checks" are often a *visible success criterion* rather than a single right answer, the CHECK beat names what to look for in the work plus a quick question; the "if the child is stuck" support still applies (step back to a smaller technique, never do it for them). Set up materials before the lesson and protect making time.

---

## Lesson 1 — My sketchbook is my workshop
**Art · Year 2 · Unit 1 (Sketchbook habits & line)** | **Duration:** 25 min | **Objective:** "By the end, I can set up and date a sketchbook page and explain it is for testing, not for perfect pictures."

**You will need:** a sketchbook, pencils, fine-liners, crayons, images of real artists' messy sketchbook pages.
**Prior knowledge to retrieve:** Year 1 mark-making (different lines).
**Key vocabulary:** **sketchbook**, **date**, **test page**, **experiment**, **mark-making**.
**What to emphasize (the heart of the lesson):** a sketchbook is a **workshop for testing and experimenting** — mess and "mistakes" are the **point**, not something to avoid. No rubbing out.

---

### ⏱ 0–3 min · HOOK
> **Say:** "Real artists' sketchbooks are full of crossings-out, half-ideas and wild experiments — and that's exactly why they're brilliant. Yours is going to be just as messy and just as useful."
**Show:** images of messy artist sketchbooks.  **Do:** flick through, pointing at scribbles and tests.
**Ask (retrieve prior knowledge):** "Last year we made different marks — give me one kind of line." → *listen for:* "zig-zag / wavy / dotted."

### ⏱ 3–8 min · EXPLAIN (I do — demo)
> **Say:** "Watch me set up a page like an artist. First I **date** it — top corner — so I can see how I grow. Then I make a 'warm-up' strip of marks: dots, zig-zags, swirls, thick, thin. No rubbing out — if I don't like one, I just make another. A sketchbook is for **testing**, not for perfect pictures."
**Show / Do:** date a page; fill a quick mark-making warm-up strip.
**Emphasize:** "This is a **workshop** — testing and mess are the *point*."

### ⏱ 8–12 min · EXAMPLE (we do)
> **Say:** "Let's start yours together — date the corner, then copy two of my marks and add one of your own."
**Do:** child dates the page and makes three marks.
**Ask:** "What is a sketchbook really for?" → *listen for:* "testing / experimenting / recording ideas — not perfect art."

### ⏱ 12–16 min · CHECK (look at the work + question)
**Ask:** "What is a sketchbook *for*, and why is it OK to make a mess?"
- ✅ **Look for / Expected:** a dated page, several varied marks, no rubbing out; "it's for testing." — probe: *"Which mark would you like to try more of?"*
- ❌ **If trying to make it 'perfect' / rubbing out:** perfection anxiety → **fix:** "There's no wrong mark here. Leave it and make another beside it — artists keep their tests."
- ❌ **If page undated:** → **fix:** "Date it first — that's how we'll see you getting better."

### ⏱ 16–22 min · PRACTICE (you do)
**Do:** Child fills a first **test page** with as many different marks as they can, dated, no erasing. Once flowing, interleave: add a tiny written note beside one mark ("I pressed hard").
**Watch for:** erasing; trying to draw a 'picture' instead of testing marks.
> **If the child is stuck (missed twice / frozen):** 1) don't do it for them; 2) name *why* — fear of mess; 3) shrink the task — "just three dots"; 4) one prompt: "what happens if you press harder?"; 5) build back up; 6) "There are no mistakes on a test page."

### ⏱ 22–25 min · RECAP
> **Say:** "Tell me one thing a sketchbook is for." → *listen for:* "testing / experimenting / ideas."
**Process praise:** "You filled the page bravely and didn't rub anything out — that's a real artist's sketchbook habit."
**Revisit later:** every art lesson now starts in the dated sketchbook.

---

**Differentiation**
- **Support (gentle):** pre-printed date box and a 6-square grid to fill.
- **Stretch (challenge):** add a written note next to one mark saying what they tried.

**Extension / if the child finishes early:** make a "marks menu" page naming five marks they invented.
**Assessment evidence:** child sets up and dates a test page and explains a sketchbook is for experimenting.

## Lesson 2 — Light hands: build-up lines
**Art · Year 2 · Unit 1 (Sketchbook habits & line)** | **Duration:** 30 min | **Objective:** "By the end, I can draw lightly first and darken only the lines I want to keep."

**You will need:** soft pencils, fine-liners, simple chunky objects (a cup, a mug), sketchbooks.
**Prior knowledge to retrieve:** sketchbook as a test space (Lesson 1).
**Key vocabulary:** **light line**, **ghost line**, **build-up**, **darken**, **keeper**.
**What to emphasize (the heart of the lesson):** draw **lightly first** ("ghost lines") so you can fix the shape, then **darken only the lines you want to keep**. Pre-empt pressing hard from the start.

---

### ⏱ 0–3 min · HOOK
> **Say:** "Artists have a secret: they don't press hard at first. They draw faint 'ghost lines' you can barely see — then darken only the best ones. Why would they do that?"
**Show:** a cup to draw.  **Do:** wave a pencil lightly above the page like a ghost.
**Ask (retrieve prior knowledge):** "Where do we test things first?" → *listen for:* "the sketchbook."

### ⏱ 3–9 min · EXPLAIN (I do — demo)
> **Say:** "Watch. I draw the cup with **light ghost lines** — barely touching — and if the shape's wrong, I just draw again, no rubbing. Once the shape looks right, I press a bit harder, or go over the **keeper** lines in fine-liner. Light first means I can fix mistakes before I commit."
**Show / Do:** sketch the cup faintly, adjust, then darken the keepers.
**Emphasize:** "**Light first, dark last** — fix the shape before you commit."

### ⏱ 9–14 min · EXAMPLE (we do)
> **Say:** "Let's start your cup together — ghost the round top with me, lightly… now is it the right shape? Adjust if not."
**Do:** child draws the light outline; tutor prompts checking the shape.
**Ask:** "Why draw lightly first?" → *listen for:* "so you can fix the shape before committing."

### ⏱ 14–18 min · CHECK (look + question)
**Ask:** "Why do artists draw lightly first?"
- ✅ **Look for / Expected:** faint build-up lines, then darkened keepers; "so you can fix it first." — probe: *"Which line did you decide to keep?"*
- ❌ **If pressing hard immediately:** can't adjust → **fix:** "Lift your hand light as a feather — ghost it first, dark only when you're sure."
- ❌ **If rubbing out instead of redrawing:** → **fix:** "Leave the ghost line, draw a better one beside it, darken the good one."

### ⏱ 18–26 min · PRACTICE (you do)
**Do:** Child draws one object with light build-up lines, then goes over the keepers in fine-liner. Once secure, interleave: draw a second object overlapping the first.
**Watch for:** heavy pressure; erasing rather than re-ghosting.
> **If the child is stuck (frozen / unhappy with shape):** 1) don't redraw it for them; 2) name *why* — pressing too hard; 3) shrink — "just ghost the outline shape"; 4) prompt: "is it taller or wider than that?"; 5) darken one keeper; 6) "Ghost lines mean nothing is a mistake yet."

### ⏱ 26–30 min · RECAP
> **Say:** "What does 'light first, dark last' help you do?" → *listen for:* "fix the shape before committing."
**Process praise:** "Your clean drawing came out of messy ghost lines — that patience is exactly how artists work."
**Revisit later:** light build-up underpins observational drawing in Year 3.

---

**Differentiation**
- **Support (gentle):** very soft pencil and a chunky simple object.
- **Stretch (challenge):** draw two objects overlapping.

**Extension / if the child finishes early:** ghost-draw a third object and ink only the keepers.
**Assessment evidence:** child uses light build-up lines then darkens chosen keeper lines.

## Lesson 3 — A line that shows movement (Van Gogh)
**Art · Year 2 · Unit 1 (Sketchbook habits & line)** | **Duration:** 35 min | **Objective:** "By the end, I can use swirling, energetic lines to show movement like Van Gogh."

**You will need:** dark paper, oil pastels or felt-tips, sketchbooks, an image of Van Gogh's *The Starry Night*.
**Prior knowledge to retrieve:** different marks/lines (Lessons 1–2).
**Key vocabulary:** **movement**, **swirl**, **spiral**, **energetic line**, **Van Gogh**.
**What to emphasize (the heart of the lesson):** **lines can show movement** — swirling, curving, repeated lines make a picture look like it's moving, as in Van Gogh's sky.

---

### ⏱ 0–4 min · HOOK
> **Say:** "Look at this sky by Vincent van Gogh. It doesn't sit still — it *swirls*, like the wind is alive. How did he make a flat painting look like it's *moving*?"
**Show:** *The Starry Night*.  **Do:** trace the swirls in the air with a finger.
**Ask (retrieve prior knowledge):** "Show me a swirly line in the air." → *listen for/watch:* the child making a spiral motion.

### ⏱ 4–10 min · EXPLAIN (I do — demo)
> **Say:** "**Vincent van Gogh** used **swirling, curving, repeated lines** to make movement. Watch me: a spiral here, a flowing wave there, repeated again and again. The repeating curves make your eye *move* across the sky. Straight, still lines wouldn't do that."
**Show / Do:** demo swirl, spiral and flowing lines building a moving sky on dark paper.
**Emphasize:** "**Swirling, repeated, curvy lines** = movement."

### ⏱ 10–15 min · EXAMPLE (we do)
> **Say:** "Practise three with me in your sketchbook: a swirl, a spiral, a flowing wave."
**Do:** child practises the three energetic line types.
**Ask:** "How did Van Gogh make his sky look like it's moving?" → *listen for:* "swirling, curvy, repeated lines."

### ⏱ 15–19 min · CHECK (look + question)
**Ask:** "What kind of lines make a sky look like it's moving?"
- ✅ **Look for / Expected:** confident swirls/spirals/flowing curves; "swirling repeated lines." — probe: *"Where does your eye travel on the page?"*
- ❌ **If stiff straight lines:** → **fix:** "Let your wrist loop and flow — curve it round and round like the wind."
- ❌ **If single marks, not repeated:** → **fix:** "Repeat the swirl again and again — repetition makes the movement."

### ⏱ 19–31 min · PRACTICE (you do — create)
**Do:** Child fills a night-sky strip with moving, Van-Gogh-style swirling lines (oil pastel on dark paper). Once flowing, interleave: add small dabbed star-marks among the swirls.
**Watch for:** stiff/straight lines; pressing pastels flat instead of looping.
> **If the child is stuck (frozen):** 1) don't draw it for them; 2) name *why* — too careful/stiff; 3) shrink — "one big loose spiral"; 4) prompt: "make the wind go round"; 5) repeat the loop; 6) "Loose and swirly — there's no wrong swirl."

### ⏱ 31–35 min · RECAP (look → improve)
> **Say:** "Look at your sky — point to where it feels most like it's moving. What made that work?" → *listen for:* "the repeated swirls."
**Process praise:** "You let your hand loop and flow — that looseness is exactly how Van Gogh made his sky come alive."
**Revisit later:** Unit 1 check; expressive line returns when we add colour-mood next unit.

---

**Differentiation**
- **Support (gentle):** trace over faint swirl guides first.
- **Stretch (challenge):** add small dabbed star-marks among the swirls.

**Extension / if the child finishes early:** turn the swirls into a whole windy landscape.
**Assessment evidence:** child uses swirling, repeated lines to convey movement and names Van Gogh's technique.

> **End-of-unit check (Unit 1):** Child shows a dated test page, draws one object using light build-up lines then darkens the keepers, and names one thing a sketchbook is for.

## Lesson 4 — Tints ladder revisited (lighter with white)
**Art · Year 2 · Unit 2 (Colour: tints and shades)** | **Duration:** 30 min | **Objective:** "By the end, I can make a smooth ladder of one colour from bright to palest using white."

**You will need:** ready-mix paint including white, brushes, water, paper, sketchbooks, a pre-drawn 5-box strip option.
**Prior knowledge to retrieve:** Year 1 tints (adding white).
**Key vocabulary:** **tint**, **lighter**, **white**, **ladder**, **step**.
**What to emphasize (the heart of the lesson):** add **white** to make a colour **lighter**; add a *little* more each step for a smooth ladder. Pre-empt jumping from bright to white with nothing between.

---

### ⏱ 0–3 min · HOOK
> **Say:** "How many *different* pinks do you think one red can make? Two? Five? Let's find out — with one secret ingredient: white."
**Show:** a blob of red and a blob of white.  **Do:** dab a little white into red to reveal pink.
**Ask (retrieve prior knowledge):** "What did we add last year to make a colour lighter?" → *listen for:* "white."

### ⏱ 3–9 min · EXPLAIN (I do — demo)
> **Say:** "I'm making a **tint ladder**. Box 1 is the pure colour. Box 2: add a *little* white. Box 3: a little more. Each step a bit lighter, ending palest. The trick is **a little white at a time** — small steps make a smooth ladder, not a big jump."
**Show / Do:** paint a 5-step ladder adding white each step.
**Emphasize:** "**A little white at a time** — small even steps."

### ⏱ 9–14 min · EXAMPLE (we do)
> **Say:** "Mix box 2 with me — pure colour plus a tiny bit of white. Lighter? Good — now box 3, a touch more."
**Do:** child mixes two steps of the ladder.
**Ask:** "How do you make a colour lighter?" → *listen for:* "add white."

### ⏱ 14–18 min · CHECK (look + question)
**Ask:** "How do you make a colour lighter, and why add only a little at a time?"
- ✅ **Look for / Expected:** five boxes getting evenly lighter; "add white, a bit at a time." — probe: *"Which box has the most white?"*
- ❌ **If jumps straight to white:** big leap → **fix:** "Too much white too soon. Start again — a *tiny* bit more each box."
- ❌ **If boxes uneven:** → **fix:** "Make each step the same size of change — small and steady."

### ⏱ 18–26 min · PRACTICE (you do — create)
**Do:** Child paints a 5-step tint ladder of one chosen colour, keeping steps even, into the sketchbook. Once secure, interleave: name each tint ("palest sky blue").
**Watch for:** uneven jumps; muddy brush (rinse between).
> **If the child is stuck:** 1) don't paint it for them; 2) name *why* — too much white / dirty brush; 3) shrink to a 3-step ladder; 4) prompt: "just a *speck* more white"; 5) build back up; 6) "Small steps — you're in control of the colour."

### ⏱ 26–30 min · RECAP
> **Say:** "What makes a colour lighter, and what's the trick to a smooth ladder?" → *listen for:* "white; a little at a time."
**Process praise:** "Your ladder softens so evenly — that careful, small-step mixing is real colour control."
**Revisit later:** tomorrow — the opposite: making colours *darker* with black.

---

**Differentiation**
- **Support (gentle):** a 3-step ladder.
- **Stretch (challenge):** name each tint (e.g. "palest sky blue").

**Extension / if the child finishes early:** make a tint ladder of a *second* colour.
**Assessment evidence:** child paints an even tint ladder and explains white lightens.

## Lesson 5 — Shades: darker with black (the secret ingredient)
**Art · Year 2 · Unit 2 (Colour: tints and shades)** | **Duration:** 35 min | **Objective:** "By the end, I can make a colour darker by adding a tiny bit of black."

**You will need:** paint including black, brushes, water, paper, sketchbooks.
**Prior knowledge to retrieve:** tints (lighter with white, Lesson 4).
**Key vocabulary:** **shade**, **darker**, **black**, **tiny speck**, **muddy**.
**What to emphasize (the heart of the lesson):** add a **tiny speck of black** to make a **shade** (darker); **too much black makes mud**. Pre-empt dumping in black.

---

### ⏱ 0–4 min · HOOK
> **Say:** "Here's a warning and a secret. A *tiny* speck of black can turn red into a rich, deep colour — but a *blob* of black makes horrible mud. Today we find the line between 'deep' and 'muddy'."
**Show:** red paint; a pinhead of black.  **Do:** add a speck → deep red; then add too much → mud, for contrast.
**Ask (retrieve prior knowledge):** "What made colours lighter yesterday?" → *listen for:* "white."

### ⏱ 4–11 min · EXPLAIN (I do — demo)
> **Say:** "To make a **shade**, I add black — but only a **tiny speck** on the very tip of the brush. See: red + speck = deep red. Each step, one more speck. Watch what happens with too much — mud! So the rule is: **less black than you think**."
**Show / Do:** build a 5-step shade ladder with specks of black; show the muddy mistake once.
**Emphasize:** "**A tiny speck of black** — too much makes mud."

### ⏱ 11–17 min · EXAMPLE (we do)
> **Say:** "Add a speck of black to your colour with me — just the tip. Darker? Good — one more speck for the next box."
**Do:** child mixes two darker steps.
**Ask:** "How do you make red into a darker shade?" → *listen for:* "add a little black."

### ⏱ 17–22 min · CHECK (look + question)
**Ask:** "How do you make a colour darker, and what happens with too much black?"
- ✅ **Look for / Expected:** boxes getting evenly darker, not muddy; "add a little black; too much = mud." — probe: *"How much black is just right?"*
- ❌ **If muddy:** too much black → **fix (KB):** "Less black! Start the box again with a *speck* on the brush tip."
- ❌ **If no visible change:** brush not loaded → **fix:** "Touch the very tip into the black, then into your colour."

### ⏱ 22–31 min · PRACTICE (you do — create)
**Do:** Child paints a 5-step shade ladder of one colour using specks of black. Once secure, interleave: paint a "too much" box beside a "just right" box and compare.
**Watch for:** mud; dirty water lightening instead of darkening.
> **If the child is stuck:** 1) don't fix it for them; 2) name *why* — too much black; 3) restart one box with a speck; 4) prompt: "tip of the brush only"; 5) build up; 6) "Less than you think — you've got this."

### ⏱ 31–35 min · RECAP
> **Say:** "What makes a colour darker, and what's the danger?" → *listen for:* "a little black; too much makes mud."
**Process praise:** "You resisted the urge to add loads of black — that restraint is what keeps the colour rich."
**Revisit later:** tomorrow we combine tints AND shades into one staircase.

---

**Differentiation**
- **Support (gentle):** a pre-mixed dark blob to dilute back toward the colour.
- **Stretch (challenge):** compare "too much black" vs "just right" and explain.

**Extension / if the child finishes early:** make a shade ladder of a second colour.
**Assessment evidence:** child makes a darker shade with a small amount of black and avoids mud.

---

## Lesson 6 — Tints-and-shades strip (one colour, full range)
**Art · Year 2 · Unit 2 (Colour: tints and shades)** | **Duration:** 35 min | **Objective:** "By the end, I can paint one colour across five tones from light to dark in order."

**You will need:** paint including white and black, brushes, water, paper with a pre-drawn 5-box strip option, sketchbooks.
**Prior knowledge to retrieve:** tints with white (L4), shades with black (L5).
**Key vocabulary:** **tint**, **shade**, **tone**, **lightest**, **darkest**, **order**.
**What to emphasize (the heart of the lesson):** one colour can span a **staircase of tones** — white on one side, black on the other, the pure colour in the middle — arranged **lightest → darkest**.

---

### ⏱ 0–4 min · HOOK
> **Say:** "Can you arrange one single colour like steps on a staircase — palest at the top, darkest at the bottom? Today you control a colour's whole range."
**Show:** an example tints-and-shades strip.  **Do:** run a finger down it, light to dark.
**Ask (retrieve prior knowledge):** "White does what? Black does what?" → *listen for:* "white lightens; black darkens."

### ⏱ 4–10 min · EXPLAIN (I do — demo)
> **Say:** "Watch my plan. I put the **pure colour in the middle box**. To the left, +white twice (tints) — lighter and lighter. To the right, +black twice (shades) — darker and darker. Five boxes: lightest on one end, darkest on the other, in order."
**Show / Do:** paint the middle, then tints left, shades right.
**Emphasize:** "Pure in the **middle**; **white side = light**, **black side = dark**; keep them **in order**."

### ⏱ 10–16 min · EXAMPLE (we do)
> **Say:** "Paint the middle box with me first — pure colour. Now which way for lighter?"
**Do:** child paints the middle and one tint box.
**Ask:** "Point to where the lightest box will go and the darkest." → *listen for/watch:* light end vs dark end correctly.

### ⏱ 16–21 min · CHECK (look + question)
**Ask:** "Point to your lightest and darkest boxes — why are they lightest/darkest?"
- ✅ **Look for / Expected:** five boxes reading smoothly light→dark; "lightest has most white, darkest has most black." — probe: *"Is each step an even change?"*
- ❌ **If order jumbled:** → **fix:** "Line them up palest to darkest — which has the most white? That's the top step."
- ❌ **If middle muddied:** → **fix:** "Keep the centre box pure — no white or black there."

### ⏱ 21–31 min · PRACTICE (you do — create)
**Do:** Child paints their own 5-box tints-and-shades strip and arranges/checks it reads lightest → darkest. Once secure, interleave: a second strip in a different colour to compare.
**Watch for:** uneven steps; mixing white into the black side.
> **If the child is stuck:** 1) don't fix for them; 2) name *why* — order or muddy; 3) do the middle + one each side together; 4) prompt: "white this way, black that way"; 5) build up; 6) "You control the whole staircase."

### ⏱ 31–35 min · RECAP (look → improve)
> **Say:** "Tell me how one colour can be both light and dark." → *listen for:* "add white for tints, black for shades."
**Process praise:** "Your staircase reads perfectly light to dark — that's full control of one colour."
**Revisit later:** next, we use these tones to give a picture a *feeling*.

---

**Differentiation**
- **Support (gentle):** a pre-drawn 5-box strip.
- **Stretch (challenge):** a second strip in a different colour and compare.

**Extension / if the child finishes early:** paint an object using three tones of one colour.
**Assessment evidence:** child paints a 5-tone strip reading lightest → darkest and explains white/black.

## Lesson 7 — Colour moods (warm/cool feelings)
**Art · Year 2 · Unit 2 (Colour: tints and shades)** | **Duration:** 35 min | **Objective:** "By the end, I can choose a warm or cool palette to give a picture a feeling."

**You will need:** paint, brushes, paper, sketchbooks, warm/cool colour cards.
**Prior knowledge to retrieve:** warm/cool colours (Year 1); tints and shades (L4–6).
**Key vocabulary:** **warm** (red/orange/yellow), **cool** (blue/green/purple), **mood**, **palette**, **feeling**.
**What to emphasize (the heart of the lesson):** **colour alone can carry a feeling** — warm = cosy/excited, cool = calm/sad — even with no faces or objects.

---

### ⏱ 0–4 min · HOOK
> **Say:** "Could you paint 'cosy' or 'lonely' using only *colour* — no faces, no objects? Artists do it all the time. Let's test it."
**Show:** warm/cool colour cards.  **Do:** hold up a warm swatch ('cosy?') then a cool one ('calm?').
**Ask (retrieve prior knowledge):** "Name a warm colour and a cool colour." → *listen for:* "red/orange; blue/green."

### ⏱ 4–11 min · EXPLAIN (I do — demo)
> **Say:** "**Warm** colours — red, orange, yellow — feel cosy, excited, even angry. **Cool** colours — blue, green, purple — feel calm, quiet, sometimes sad. Watch me paint a little warm patch — feels like a fire — and a cool patch — feels like a quiet lake. I'll use tints and shades for variety."
**Show / Do:** paint a warm 'mood' patch and a cool one, naming the feeling.
**Emphasize:** "**Colour carries feeling** — warm = cosy/excited, cool = calm."

### ⏱ 11–16 min · EXAMPLE (we do)
> **Say:** "Which palette would you pick for a calm bedtime picture — warm or cool? Let's start a cool patch together."
**Do:** child begins a cool-palette patch using two cool tones.
**Ask:** "Which palette feels calm — warm or cool?" → *listen for:* "cool."

### ⏱ 16–21 min · CHECK (look + question)
**Ask:** "Which palette feels calm, and which feels cosy or excited?"
- ✅ **Look for / Expected:** correct warm/cool choices; "cool = calm, warm = cosy." — probe: *"What feeling is your patch giving?"*
- ❌ **If mixes warm into a 'cool' picture by accident:** → **fix:** "Check your colours — is red warm or cool? Keep this one all cool."
- ❌ **If can't link colour to feeling:** → **fix:** "Imagine a cosy fire — what colours? Now a cold lake — what colours?"

### ⏱ 21–31 min · PRACTICE (you do — create)
**Do:** Child paints two small abstract pictures — one **warm feeling**, one **cool feeling** — using tints and shades for variety. Once secure, interleave: paint one scene (a campfire on a cold night) using both palettes on purpose.
**Watch for:** accidental palette mixing; flat single-tone work.
> **If the child is stuck:** 1) don't paint for them; 2) name *why* — palette/feeling link; 3) hand the matching colour cards; 4) prompt: "what feeling — then which colours?"; 5) start one patch together; 6) "Colour is doing the feeling for you."

### ⏱ 31–35 min · RECAP (look → improve)
> **Say:** "Tell me a feeling and the palette that shows it." → *listen for:* a feeling matched to warm/cool.
**Process praise:** "Your cool picture really *feels* calm — you let colour do the talking. That's expressive art."
**Revisit later:** Unit 2 check; the full colour wheel and harmonies come in Year 4.

---

**Differentiation**
- **Support (gentle):** choose from warm/cool colour cards.
- **Stretch (challenge):** paint one scene (campfire on a cold night) using both palettes on purpose.

**Extension / if the child finishes early:** title each picture with the feeling it shows.
**Assessment evidence:** child chooses a warm or cool palette to convey a named feeling.

> **End-of-unit check (Unit 2):** Child paints a 5-box tints-and-shades strip of one colour reading lightest → darkest, and explains white lightens / black darkens. Recorded in the sketchbook.

## Lesson 8 — Background first, then layers
**Art · Year 2 · Unit 3 (Layered & mixed-media collage)** | **Duration:** 35 min | **Objective:** "By the end, I can build a collage in layers, background first, foreground last."

**You will need:** coloured and torn papers, glue, backing sheets, sketchbooks.
**Prior knowledge to retrieve:** Year 1 collage (tearing/sticking).
**Key vocabulary:** **collage**, **layer**, **background**, **foreground**, **overlap**, **arrange**.
**What to emphasize (the heart of the lesson):** build **background first, foreground last**, so front pieces sit on top and overlap — and **arrange dry before gluing**. Pre-empt gluing the subject down first.

---

### ⏱ 0–4 min · HOOK
> **Say:** "Here's a puzzle: when collage artists make a sky with a bird in it, which do they glue first — the sky or the bird? And why does it matter?"
**Show:** torn paper pieces (sky, ground, a bird).  **Do:** hold the bird over the sky to show overlap.
**Ask (retrieve prior knowledge):** "What's it called when we tear and stick paper into a picture?" → *listen for:* "collage."

### ⏱ 4–10 min · EXPLAIN (I do — demo)
> **Say:** "I build in **layers**, back to front. First the **background** — the sky, then the ground. Next a middle layer. Last, the **foreground** subject on top. The front pieces **overlap** the back ones, which makes it look real — near things in front of far things. And I always **arrange everything dry first**, then glue."

## Lesson 16 — Score-and-slip: joins that hold

**Key vocabulary:** **join**, **score** (scratch), **slip** (watery clay), **press**, **smooth**.

---
### ⏱ 0–4 min · HOOK
> **Say:** "Why do clay arms and tails always seem to fall off? Because they weren't joined properly. Potters have a trick that makes joins really hold — score-and-slip. Let's learn it."

### ⏱ 4–11 min · EXPLAIN (I do)
> **Say:** "**Score-and-slip**, four steps. One: **score** — scratch criss-cross lines on *both* surfaces. Two: add **slip** — a little water or watery clay. Three: **press** them together. Four: **smooth** the join. Watch — now they hold!"
**Show / Do:** score, slip, press, smooth two pieces; tug to show it holds.
**Emphasize:** "**Score both sides, slip, press, smooth** — then it holds."
### ⏱ 11–17 min · EXAMPLE (we do)
> **Say:** "Score both pieces with me — criss-cross — now a dab of water, press, smooth."
**Do:** child scores, slips, presses and smooths a join.
**Ask:** "What are the steps to join clay so it holds?" → *listen for:* "score both sides, add slip/water, press and smooth."
### ⏱ 17–22 min · CHECK (look + question)
**Ask:** "What are the steps to join clay so it holds — and tug-test your join."
- ✅ **Look for / Expected:** scored, slipped, pressed, smoothed; join survives a gentle tug. — probe: *"Which step do people forget?"* (scoring).
- ❌ **If join falls apart:** skipped scoring/slip → **fix (KB):** "Scratch *both* sides and add a little water — then press and smooth."
- ❌ **If too much water (sloppy):** → **fix:** "Just a dab of slip — not a puddle."
### ⏱ 22–31 min · PRACTICE (you do — create)
**Do:** Child joins two coils/balls with score-and-slip and tug-tests. Once secure, interleave: join three pieces into a small structure.
**Watch for:** no scoring; too wet; not smoothing.
> **If the child is stuck:** 1) don't join for them; 2) name *why* — missed a step; 3) do the four steps together; 4) prompt: "did you score *both* sides?"; 5) re-join; 6) "Score, slip, press, smooth — it'll hold."
### ⏱ 31–35 min · RECAP
> **Say:** "Say the four steps to a join that holds." → *listen for:* "score, slip, press, smooth."
**Process praise:** "Your join passed the tug-test — that's proper potter's joining."
**Revisit later:** you'll use this to add limbs to your clay creature next.
---
**Differentiation** — support: pre-scored pieces. / stretch: join three pieces into a small structure.
**Extension / if the child finishes early:** add a scored-and-slipped handle to a pinch pot.
**Assessment evidence:** child joins clay with score-and-slip and the join holds a tug-test.

## Lesson 17 — Clay creature in the round
**Art · Year 2 · Unit 5 (Clay & 3-D form)** | **Duration:** 40 min | **Objective:** "By the end, I can mould a clay creature that holds together and looks good from all sides."
**You will need:** clay, tools, water, boards, sketchbooks.
**Prior knowledge to retrieve:** roll/pinch/coil (L15); score-and-slip (L16).
**Key vocabulary:** **in the round**, **form**, **texture**, **join**, **all sides**.
**What to emphasize:** a sculpture works **in the round** — it must look good from **front, side and back**; build the body, then add limbs/features with **score-and-slip**, turning as you work.
---
### ⏱ 0–4 min · HOOK
> **Say:** "You're a sculptor now. But here's the challenge: your creature must look great from the front, the side AND the back — because people walk all the way around a sculpture."
**Show:** a 3-D object viewed from several sides.  **Do:** turn it to show each face.
**Ask (retrieve prior knowledge):** "How do we join a limb so it won't drop off?" → *listen for:* "score-and-slip."
### ⏱ 4–10 min · EXPLAIN (I do)
> **Say:** "I plan a simple creature, build the **body** by pinching/coiling, then add limbs and features with **score-and-slip**. I keep **turning** it as I work, checking every side. Finally I press tools in for **texture** — scales or spots."
**Show / Do:** build a quick body, attach one limb with score-and-slip, add texture, turning it.
**Emphasize:** "Build, join securely, **turn and check every side**, add texture."
### ⏱ 10–16 min · EXAMPLE (we do)
> **Say:** "Plan your creature with me in one sentence, then start the body. How will we attach the legs?"
**Do:** child starts the body and names score-and-slip for limbs.
**Ask:** "Name two clay skills you'll use." → *listen for:* "coiling/pinching + score-and-slip."
### ⏱ 16–20 min · CHECK (look + question)
**Ask:** "Name two clay skills you're using, and turn your creature — does the back look finished too?"
- ✅ **Look for / Expected:** body built, a secure join, attention to all sides. — probe: *"Which side needs more work?"*
- ❌ **If only the front is worked:** → **fix:** "Turn it — the back is bare. Sculptors finish every side."
- ❌ **If limbs falling off:** → **fix:** "Score-and-slip the join — scratch both, add water, press, smooth."
### ⏱ 20–36 min · PRACTICE (you do — create)
**Do:** Child builds a creature: body by pinch/coil, limbs/features by score-and-slip, texture pressed in, turning as they work. Once secure, interleave: add one securely joined feature (tail/ears) and surface texture.
**Watch for:** front-only work; weak joins.
> **If the child is stuck:** 1) don't build for them; 2) name *why* — joins or one-sided; 3) re-join one limb together; 4) prompt: "turn it — what's the back like?"; 5) finish one side; 6) "Strong joins, all sides — you're sculpting in the round."
### ⏱ 36–40 min · RECAP (look → improve)
> **Say:** "Turn your creature and tell me which side you're proudest of and why." → *listen for:* reflection on the form in the round.
**Process praise:** "You joined every limb securely and finished the back — that's true 3-D sculpting."
**Revisit later:** larger sculpture and armatures come in Year 5.
---
**Differentiation** — support: one-lump creature with pressed features. / stretch: add a securely joined feature (tail, ears) and surface texture.
**Extension / if the child finishes early:** make a tiny base/stand for the creature.
**Assessment evidence:** child makes a clay creature using ≥2 skills incl. a secure join, finished in the round.

## Lesson 18 — Matisse cut-out paper sculpture finish
**Art · Year 2 · Unit 5 (Clay & 3-D form)** | **Duration:** 35 min | **Objective:** "By the end, I can decorate or build a 3-D form using bright cut-out shapes like Matisse."
**You will need:** bright coloured paper, child-safe scissors, glue, sketchbooks, an image of Matisse's *The Snail*, the clay creature's stand (or card to build on).
**Prior knowledge to retrieve:** cutting shapes; the clay creature (L17).
**Key vocabulary:** **cut-out**, **drawing with scissors**, **organic shape**, **arrange**, **bold**.
**What to emphasize:** Matisse "**drew with scissors**" — cutting bold shapes **straight from colour**, no drawing first — then arranging them. Be **bold and fearless** with the scissors.
---
### ⏱ 0–4 min · HOOK
> **Say:** "When **Henri Matisse** got too ill to paint, he didn't stop — he 'drew with scissors', cutting bright shapes straight out of coloured paper. Today we finish our work his fearless way."
**Show:** Matisse's *The Snail*.  **Do:** snip a bold shape from paper with no pencil line.
**Ask (retrieve prior knowledge):** "What did we make last lesson that needs finishing?" → *listen for:* "the clay creature / its stand."
### ⏱ 4–10 min · EXPLAIN (I do)
> **Say:** "'**Drawing with scissors**' means cutting the shape **directly** — no drawing it first. Watch me cut a bold leaf shape straight out, then a flowing wiggly one. Then I **arrange** them before gluing, like Matisse. Bold and free — wonky is good!"
**Show / Do:** cut two shapes freehand; arrange them on the stand/card.
**Emphasize:** "**Cut straight from colour** — no outline, be bold."
### ⏱ 10–15 min · EXAMPLE (we do)
> **Say:** "Cut one bold shape with me — straight out, no pencil. Now where shall it go?"
**Do:** child cuts a freehand shape and positions it.
**Ask:** "What did Matisse mean by 'drawing with scissors'?" → *listen for:* "cutting shapes directly from coloured paper instead of drawing them."
### ⏱ 15–19 min · CHECK (look + question)
**Ask:** "What does 'drawing with scissors' mean?"
- ✅ **Look for / Expected:** bold cut shapes, arranged; "cutting straight from colour." — probe: *"Did you need to draw it first?"* (no).
- ❌ **If drawing outlines first:** → **fix:** "Try Matisse's way — cut the shape straight out, no pencil. Bold!"
- ❌ **If shapes tiny/timid:** → **fix:** "Bigger, bolder — turn the paper as you cut."
### ⏱ 19–31 min · PRACTICE (you do — create)
**Do:** Child cuts bold simple shapes freehand and arranges/glues them to decorate their clay creature's stand, or builds a small standing cut-out sculpture. Once secure, interleave: cut a flowing organic shape with no outline.
**Watch for:** drawing-first; timid cuts.
> **If the child is stuck:** 1) don't cut for them; 2) name *why* — caution; 3) cut one bold shape together; 4) prompt: "straight from the colour"; 5) arrange it; 6) "Bold and free — there's no wrong shape."
### ⏱ 31–35 min · RECAP (look → improve)
> **Say:** "Tell me Matisse's trick — and show me your boldest shape." → *listen for:* "drawing with scissors / cutting straight from colour."
**Process praise:** "You cut boldly straight from the colour — fearless, just like Matisse."
**Revisit later:** Unit 5 check; bigger sculpture and modelling depth come in Year 5.
---
**Differentiation** — support: cut simple geometric shapes. / stretch: cut a flowing organic shape with no outline.
**Extension / if the child finishes early:** make a free-standing cut-out sculpture that stands up.
**Assessment evidence:** child decorates/builds a 3-D form with bold freehand cut-outs and explains Matisse's method.

> **End-of-unit check (Unit 5):** Child makes a clay form using at least two clay skills (incl. a score-and-slip join that holds), and shows it is finished from front, side and back.

## End-of-year outcomes

By the end of Year 2, a child's sketchbook/portfolio should show: a working **sketchbook** (dated tests, light build-up lines); a **tints-and-shades strip** (white lightens, black darkens); a **warm/cool colour-mood pair**; a **layered collage** (background→foreground, with overlap); a **mixed-media texture collage** (paper + ≥2 materials); a **designed repeating pattern** (a self-made tile + a printed two-block pattern with its rule); a **clay form** in the round with a secure score-and-slip join; and a **deeper artist study** inspired by **Matisse**, with revisits of **Van Gogh**, **Kandinsky** or **Kusama**, described using formal-element words.
