# Living, dead, or never alive

**Science · Year 2 · Unit 1 — Living things and their habitats** | **Lesson 1** | **30 min**
**ID:** `y2-science-u1-l01` · **Delivery:** human intro, then AI · **Kind:** lesson

> **Essential question:** How do we know if something is living, and how is each living thing suited to where it lives?

**Objective:** By the end, I can sort things into living, dead and never alive and give a reason.

## What must land (emphasize)
- The single test that decides every object: *"Does it, or did it ever, grow, feed and make babies?"*
- Pre-empt the trap "it moves, so it's alive" — repeat: *"Moving isn't enough — living things also grow, feed and have babies. A car moves but is never alive."*
- Three groups, not two: **dead** (once alive) is its own group, separate from **never alive**.

## Vocabulary
| Term | Kid-friendly meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| living | it does, or could, grow, feed and have babies | a butterfly and a sunflower are living |
| dead | it was once alive but isn't any more | a brown leaf that fell from a tree is dead |
| never alive | it was never living at all | a pebble was never alive |
| life process | a job all living things do | move, grow, feed, make babies |

## Delivery & materials
**Mode:** human_intro_then_ai. The parent runs the first ~15 minutes hands-on with real objects (hook, explore, naming the three groups into hoops); the AI character then takes over for the tricky check, adaptive practice and recap.

**Lay out before starting (human):** a real/pictured butterfly · a dead leaf (a real fallen one is best) · a pebble · a small potted plant (or cress/grass) · a teddy or plastic toy · three labelled hoops/plates ("living", "dead", "never alive") · a seashell (stretch) · optional wooden block + tree picture · a mixed practice pile (sock, feather, worm picture, sunflower).

**Digital (app renders):** the character's three-group anchor table and the adaptive practice blocks.

## Video (reinforce — unverified spec, no invented URL)
- **Channel:** SciShow Kids · **Search:** "living and nonliving things for kids"
- **Watch task:** Every time the video shows something that grows, feeds or has babies, whisper "living!" — count how many living things you spot.
- **After-check:** "The video showed a rock and a plant. Which was living, and what did it DO that told you?" → The plant — it grows and feeds (drinks sunlight); the rock never grows, feeds or has babies.

## Lesson arc (beats)

### 1. Hook · 0–4 min · human
Puzzle: a wooden block and a picture of a great big tree — both wood, but are they both alive? Retrieve Year 1: name one alive thing and one not-alive thing. *Leave the puzzle open.*

### 2. Explore (we-do) · 4–9 min · human
Be detectives. Hand the child each object; they say only what it **does or did** (no group name yet). Butterfly moves/eats/had a mummy → yes/yes/yes. Pebble → no/no/no. Leaf → came from a tree.

### 3. Explain (name it) · 9–15 min · human
Name the three groups while placing each real object in its hoop. Say the test slowly: *"Does it, or did it ever, grow, feed and make babies?"* Reinforce the trick: *moving isn't enough* — a car moves but is never alive.
**Handoff line (say as written):** "Now my friend on the screen has a really tricky one to test you with. Watch!"

### 4. Check · 15–20 min · AI
**Q:** A leaf on the ground that fell from a tree — living, dead or never alive?
- ✅ **Dead** — it grew on a living tree (once alive) but isn't growing now. Probe: "How do you know it isn't *never* alive?"
- ❌ *"Never alive"* — judging by "not moving now." → Where was the leaf before it fell? Was that tree alive?
- ❌ *"Living"* — confusing where it came from with being alive now. → Is it green and drinking water, or brown and dry?

*Anchor visual on screen: the three-group table (living / dead / never alive with examples).*

### 5. Practice (you do) · 20–26 min · AI
Child sorts mixed things into the three groups and must say **one reason** each. Watch for the dead-vs-never-alive slip (feather, shell). On a miss, hand over the real object (concrete step down). Director draws from the practice bank, starting at level 2.

### 6. Recap · 26–30 min · AI
Child is the teacher: name the three groups with one example of each. Process praise for using the test. Leave "is a car alive?" open as a hook for Lesson 2.

## Adaptivity
- **Start level:** 2. **Level up:** 2 correct in a row → up one level (interleave skills at level 3). **Level down:** 2 misses on a skill → re-teach, put the real object in hand, then a level-1 item.
- **Mastery signal:** 3+ correct across levels 2–3 including one once-living item placed as *dead* (leaf/shell/feather) and the car item → mastery ≥ 0.8.
- **Struggle protocol:** don't reveal → name the tricky bit (dead-vs-never-alive, or judging by movement) → go concrete / ask the yes/no "was it ever part of a living thing?" → one hint → re-ask smaller → keep it safe ("mistakes show us what to practise").
- **End on success** with specific process praise (used the test, caught the trap, gave a reason) — never "you're so clever".

## Differentiation, extension, assessment
- **Support:** sort only living vs never-alive first (clearest contrast) with real objects; add "dead" once secure.
- **Stretch:** the seashell/conker — "living, dead or never alive? Tell me why. Would it always be in that group?"
- **Extension:** "three-group hunt" — find one living, one dead, one never-alive thing and draw each with a reason.
- **Assessment evidence:** child sorts 3+ mixed objects correctly with a reason each, places the fallen leaf/shell/feather as *dead*, and states the test.
- **Revisit later:** bring back "is a car alive?" as a 1-minute retrieval at the top of Lesson 2 and again before Unit 2.
