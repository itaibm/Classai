> Built to [`../../PLANNING-BRIEF.md`](../../PLANNING-BRIEF.md). Standards drawn from

> Science programme for Year 3, and the US Next Generation Science Standards (NGSS) for Grades 2–3.

## Year overview

Year 2 science turns curious children into **careful investigators**. Every unit is built around
real investigations that follow the same cycle — **question → predict → plan a fair test →
measure → record → conclude** — so that by the summer children can plan a simple fair test on their
own, take measurements with standard units, record them in a table and bar chart (linking to
Maths Unit 7), and explain what their results show.

The content spans all four sciences. **Biology:** how plants work (the jobs of roots, stem, leaves
and flowers; water transport; pollination and seed dispersal) and how animals, including humans,
get nutrition and move (skeletons and muscles). **Chemistry / Earth science:** rocks, fossils and
soils — comparing rocks by their properties, how fossils form, what soil is made of. **Physics:**
light (we need light to see; reflection; shadows) and forces and magnets (pushes and pulls,
friction, magnetic attraction and repulsion). **Earth & space:** the Sun as our light source, Sun
safety, and how shadows change through the day because the Earth spins. Each unit meets a scientist
whose work matters — from Mary Anning's fossils to Ibn al-Haytham's experiments with light.

**What Year 2 assumes from Year 1 (ages 6–7):** naming common plants and animals and their basic
parts; knowing that plants need water, light and warmth; the basic needs of animals and humans
(food, water, air, exercise, hygiene); simple habitats and food chains; identifying and comparing
everyday materials and their uses; asking simple questions and observing closely.

**What Year 3 (ages 8–9) relies on from this year:** a secure fair-test routine; measurement with
standard units and tables; the idea that living things have parts with jobs (leading to digestion
and teeth); the properties of materials (leading to states of matter); and light and forces
(leading to sound and electricity).

## Unit 1 — Working scientifically: how scientists find out

**Essential question:** How can we find out if something is true — and make sure our test is fair?

**Key vocabulary:** scientist, question, predict, prediction, fair test, variable, change, keep the same, measure, observe, record, table, results, conclusion, evidence

### Lesson 1 — What do scientists do?
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can describe the steps scientists use to answer a question." (UK NC Y3 Working scientifically; NGSS Science & Engineering Practices)
- **Hook:** A mystery: two identical cups of ice — one wrapped in a scarf. "Which will melt first? Can we be sure?"
- **Key activity:** Set up the ice test; introduce the investigation cycle on a poster (question → predict → plan → test → measure → record → conclude); children make and record predictions with "because"; check the ice at the end of the lesson.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why is it not enough to just guess which will melt first?" → A guess could be wrong; a test gives evidence we can check.
- **Differentiation:** support: picture cards for each step of the cycle / stretch: suggest a second question the class could test next.
- **Materials:** ice cubes, identical cups, a wool scarf, timer, investigation-cycle poster, science journals
- **Joy:** The surprise — most children expect the scarf to melt the ice faster; it actually keeps it cold longer.

### Lesson 2 — Fair tests: change one thing
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can explain what makes a test fair: change one thing, keep everything else the same." (UK NC Y3 Working scientifically; NGSS 2-PS1-2 practices)
- **Hook:** Paper-helicopter race where one child cheats (throws theirs higher).
- **Key activity:** Discuss why the race was unfair; plan a fair helicopter test (change wing length only; keep paper, drop height and release the same); run it in groups, timing three drops each.
- **Check for understanding:** "If we changed the wing length and the paper type, what would go wrong?" → We wouldn't know which change caused the difference.
- **Differentiation:** support: a planning frame with "change / keep the same / measure" boxes with pictures / stretch: explain why we repeat the test three times.
- **Materials:** paper-helicopter templates, paper clips, scissors, stopwatches, metre sticks
- **Joy:** Helicopter drop from a safe height with a countdown.

### Lesson 3 — Measuring accurately and recording in tables
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can take measurements with standard units and record them in a table." (UK NC Y3 Working scientifically; NGSS 2-PS1-1 practices)
- **Hook:** "How far can a toy car roll down a ramp? Guess, then measure."
- **Key activity:** Groups roll a toy car from a fixed ramp, measure distance with a tape measure (cm), record three trials in a prepared table, and find the middle value.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why do we record the unit (cm) at the top of the column?" → So everyone knows what the numbers mean.
- **Differentiation:** support: pre-drawn table with units, metre stick marked every 10 cm / stretch: design their own table for a new question.
- **Materials:** toy cars, ramps (boards and books), tape measures, metre sticks, table templates
- **Joy:** "Ramp rally" — the class cheers each roll.

### Lesson 4 — Results, bar charts and conclusions
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can present results in a bar chart and write a simple conclusion." (UK NC Y3 Working scientifically; Maths link: statistics)
- **Hook:** Yesterday's helicopter data — "What does it tell us?"
- **Key activity:** Turn results into a bar chart (Maths link); write a conclusion using "We found that… This shows… because…"; compare with the prediction.
- **Check for understanding:** "What is the difference between a result and a conclusion?" → A result is what we measured; a conclusion explains what the results show.
- **Differentiation:** support: axes drawn and scale given, conclusion frame / stretch: suggest why one result didn't fit the pattern.
- **Materials:** squared paper, rulers, data from Lesson 2, conclusion frames
- **Joy:** Each group reveals its chart like a "science news" announcement.

**End-of-unit check:** Given a question ("Does a ball bounce higher on carpet or wood?"), say what to change, what to keep the same and what to measure, and draw the results table.

## Unit 2 — Plants: parts, water, flowers and seeds

**Essential question:** How does every part of a plant help it to survive and make new plants?

**Key vocabulary:** root, stem, trunk, leaf, flower, petal, seed, anchor, absorb, transport, nutrient, sunlight, pollen, pollination, pollinator, fertilisation, seed dispersal, germination, life cycle

### Lesson 5 — The parts of a flowering plant
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can name the parts of a flowering plant and describe what each one does." (UK NC Y3 Plants; NGSS 2-LS2-1)
- **Hook:** A whole plant (roots and all) lifted from a pot and shaken free of soil.
- **Key activity:** Observe with magnifiers; draw and label roots, stem, leaves, flower; match each part to its job (anchor and absorb, support and transport, make food using light, make seeds).
- **Check for understanding:** "What would happen to a plant with no roots, and why?" → It would fall over and couldn't take in water and nutrients from the soil.
- **Differentiation:** support: label cards to place on a real plant / stretch: compare the parts of a tree and a dandelion — which are the same jobs?
- **Materials:** potted plants (e.g. geraniums), hand lenses, newspaper, labelling sheets
- **Joy:** "Plant surgeons" — gently unpot and examine a plant.

### Lesson 6 — Investigation plan: what do plants need to grow well?
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can plan a fair test to find out what plants need." (UK NC Y3 Plants; Working scientifically; NGSS 2-LS2-1)
- **Hook:** Two identical bean seedlings: "One will get everything; one will miss something. What should we test?"
- **Key activity:** Groups choose one factor (light, water, or warmth) and plan a fair test with identical seedlings; set up; measure height on day 1; start a class results table to be updated weekly.
- **Check for understanding:** "In your test, what is the one thing you change, and what do you keep the same?" → e.g. change light (window vs cupboard); keep water, pot, soil and seedling type the same.
- **Differentiation:** support: planning frame with pictures / stretch: predict with a reason and suggest how to measure "growing well" (height, leaf count, colour).
- **Materials:** bean or cress seedlings, identical pots, compost, rulers, a dark cupboard, measuring cylinder for water
- **Joy:** Each group names and "adopts" its seedlings.

### Lesson 7 — Leaves: food factories
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can explain that leaves use light to make food for the plant." (UK NC Y3 Plants; NGSS 5-LS1-1 preview)
- **Hook:** A leaf half-covered with foil for a week (set up in advance) — "Why is the covered part paler?"
- **Key activity:** Observe the covered leaf; explain simply that green leaves use sunlight, air and water to make the plant's food (no need for the word photosynthesis to be mastered); compare leaf shapes collected outdoors.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why do plants in a dark cupboard go pale and weak?" → Without light the leaves can't make enough food.
- **Differentiation:** support: sort leaves by shape and colour first / stretch: explain why leaves are thin and flat (to catch light).
- **Materials:** foil-covered leaf on a potted plant, collected leaves, hand lenses
- **Joy:** Leaf rubbings with crayons.

### Lesson 8 — Water travels up the stem (investigation)
- **Duration:** 30 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can show how water moves up a stem to the leaves and flowers." (UK NC Y3 Plants – transport; NGSS 2-LS2-1)
- **Hook:** "Can we turn a white flower blue without painting it?"
- **Key activity:** Put white carnations or celery sticks in coloured water; predict; observe after 1 hour and next day; cut the stem across to see the coloured "tubes"; draw before and after.
- **Check for understanding:** "What does the coloured stem show us about how water travels?" → Water is pulled up through tubes in the stem to the leaves and flowers.
- **Differentiation:** support: observation sheet with pictures to circle / stretch: predict how temperature might change how fast colour rises and plan a test.
- **Materials:** white carnations or celery, food colouring, jars, water, knife (adult use), hand lenses
- **Joy:** Rainbow flowers — split a stem into two colours for a two-coloured flower.

### Lesson 9 — Seedling investigation: measuring and recording
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can measure plant growth accurately and record it over time." (UK NC Y3 Working scientifically)
- **Hook:** "Two weeks on — what has happened to our seedlings?"
- **Key activity:** Measure heights in mm/cm (Maths link); count leaves; record in the class table; describe changes.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why do we measure from the soil level every time?" → So all measurements are fair and can be compared.
- **Differentiation:** support: measuring with a paper strip then a ruler / stretch: calculate how much each seedling has grown.
- **Materials:** seedlings from L6, rulers, results tables
- **Joy:** "Growth reveal" — who has grown the most?

### Lesson 10 — Flowers: parts and purposes
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can name the parts of a flower and explain that flowers make seeds." (UK NC Y3 Plants – flowers; NGSS 3-LS1-1)
- **Hook:** A lily or tulip dissected under a visualiser.
- **Key activity:** Carefully take apart a flower; identify petals, stamens (pollen), stigma and ovary (where seeds form); stick parts on a card and label.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why do many flowers have bright petals and a sweet smell?" → To attract insects and other pollinators.
- **Differentiation:** support: large diagram with picture labels / stretch: compare an insect-pollinated flower with a wind-pollinated grass flower.
- **Materials:** large flowers (e.g. lilies — check for pollen allergies; lily pollen is toxic to cats), tweezers, hand lenses, card, sticky tape
- **Joy:** "Flower surgery" with tweezers.

### Lesson 11 — Pollination: bees, birds and wind
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can explain how pollen moves from flower to flower." (UK NC Y3 Plants – pollination; NGSS 2-LS2-2)
- **Hook:** A pipe cleaner "bee" dusted with cheese-puff "pollen."
- **Key activity:** Model pollination by moving the "bee" between paper flowers with powder; watch a short clip of bees, hummingbirds and bats pollinating; discuss wind pollination.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why do plants need pollinators, and what might happen if bees disappeared?" → Pollen must reach another flower for seeds to form; without bees many plants (and foods) would make fewer seeds and fruits.
- **Differentiation:** support: sequence picture cards of pollination / stretch: design a flower perfect for a particular pollinator.
- **Materials:** pipe cleaners, paper flowers, cocoa powder or cheese puffs, a short pollination video
- **Joy:** Pollinator role play in the playground.

### Lesson 12 — Seed dispersal
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can explain different ways seeds are spread and why it helps the plant." (UK NC Y3 Plants – dispersal; NGSS 2-LS2-2)
- **Hook:** A dandelion clock and a burr on a sock — "How do seeds travel?"
- **Key activity:** Sort real seeds by dispersal method (wind, animals — hooks and eating, water, explosion); test "sycamore" helicopters and burdock on fabric.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why is it good for seeds to land far from the parent plant?" → They won't compete for light, water and space with the parent.
- **Differentiation:** support: sort with picture cards / stretch: design a seed that would travel by water and explain its features.
- **Materials:** dandelion clocks, sycamore seeds, burrs, coconut (picture or real), fruits with seeds, fabric
- **Joy:** "Seed designer" — make a paper seed that stays in the air the longest.

### Lesson 13 — The life cycle of a flowering plant
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can describe the life cycle of a flowering plant from seed to seed." (UK NC Y3 Plants – life cycle; NGSS 3-LS1-1)
- **Hook:** A bean split open — "There's a baby plant inside!"
- **Key activity:** Observe soaked beans (seed coat, tiny root and shoot); sequence the life cycle (germination, growth, flowering, pollination, seed formation, dispersal) in a circular diagram.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why is it called a cycle and not a line?" → The seeds grow into new plants that make more seeds — it repeats.
- **Differentiation:** support: picture sequence to arrange / stretch: compare with an animal life cycle.
- **Materials:** soaked broad beans, hand lenses, life-cycle templates
- **Joy:** Germination jars — beans on wet paper towel against glass.

### Lesson 14 — Seedling investigation: conclusions
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can use my results to draw a conclusion about what plants need." (UK NC Y3 Working scientifically)
- **Hook:** Final measurement day.
- **Key activity:** Final measurements; bar chart of results; write a conclusion; compare groups' findings about light, water and warmth.
- **Check for understanding:** "What does your evidence show about light and plant growth?" → Plants with light grew taller, greener and healthier; without light they grew pale and weak.
- **Differentiation:** support: conclusion frame / stretch: evaluate the test — what would you improve?
- **Materials:** seedlings, rulers, squared paper
- **Joy:** "Plant awards" ceremony.

### Lesson 15 — Scientist spotlight: Janaki Ammal and Wangari Maathai
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can explain how two scientists' work with plants changed lives." (UK NC Y3 Working scientifically – scientists; global citizenship)
- **Hook:** A stick of sugarcane and a picture of a tree nursery.
- **Key activity:** Learn about Janaki Ammal (Indian botanist who bred sweeter sugarcane varieties for India) and Wangari Maathai (Kenyan scientist whose Green Belt Movement planted tens of millions of trees); discuss why plants matter to people.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why did planting trees help communities in Kenya?" → Trees provide wood, shade and food, protect soil and water, and give women work.
- **Differentiation:** support: picture-supported fact cards / stretch: compare the two scientists' goals.
- **Materials:** fact cards, photos, *Wangari's Trees of Peace* (Jeanette Winter)
- **Joy:** Plant a tree or seedling in the school grounds.

### Lesson 16 — Plants around the world
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can explain how some plants are adapted to where they live." (UK NC Y3 Plants; NGSS 3-LS4-3 preview)
- **Hook:** A cactus and a water lily side by side.
- **Key activity:** Compare plants from deserts, rainforests, ponds and mountains; match features to habitats (thick stems store water, waxy leaves, floating leaves).
- **Check for understanding:** "Why does a cactus have spines instead of big leaves?" → Spines lose less water and protect it from animals.
- **Differentiation:** support: matching cards / stretch: invent a plant for a new habitat with explained features.
- **Materials:** cactus, water plant, photos, world map
- **Joy:** "Plant-inventor" design challenge.

### Lesson 17 — End-of-unit check: plants
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can show what I know about plant parts, life cycles and investigations." (Assessment)
- **Hook:** "Plant detective" — a mystery plant with clues.
- **Key activity:** Label a plant and flower; sequence a life cycle; explain the celery result; answer a fair-test question.
- **Check for understanding:** "A plant in the classroom is wilting. Give two possible reasons and how you'd test one." → Not enough water or light; change one factor and keep others the same.
- **Differentiation:** support: questions read aloud, diagrams provided / stretch: an extended "explain why" question.
- **Materials:** check sheet, real plant, hand lenses
- **Joy:** Take home a seedling from the class investigation.

**End-of-unit check:** Label the parts of a plant and a flower with their jobs, sequence the plant life cycle, and explain what the coloured-celery test shows about water transport.

## Unit 3 — Animals including humans: nutrition, skeletons and muscles

**Essential question:** How do animals — including us — get what they need to grow and move?

**Key vocabulary:** nutrition, nutrient, carbohydrate, protein, fat, vitamin, mineral, fibre, water, balanced diet, skeleton, bone, skull, spine, ribs, joint, muscle, contract, relax, vertebrate, invertebrate, exoskeleton

### Lesson 18 — Animals can't make their own food
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can explain that animals, including humans, must eat to get nutrition, unlike plants." (UK NC Y3 Animals – nutrition; NGSS 3-LS1-1 link)
- **Hook:** "A plant makes its own lunch. Why can't you?"
- **Key activity:** Compare plants (make food using light) and animals (eat food); sort animals by what they eat (herbivore, carnivore, omnivore) with examples from different continents.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why does a lion need to eat, but a tree doesn't?" → The tree makes food in its leaves; the lion can't, so it must eat other living things.
- **Differentiation:** support: picture sort / stretch: build a food chain from a habitat.
- **Materials:** animal picture cards, sorting hoops
- **Joy:** "Who eats what?" guessing game.

### Lesson 19 — Nutrients and what they do
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can name the main nutrient groups and what each does for the body." (UK NC Y3 Animals – nutrition)
- **Hook:** A lunchbox — "What's this food doing for you?"
- **Key activity:** Sort real food packaging into carbohydrates (energy), proteins (growth and repair), fats (energy store — small amounts), vitamins and minerals (health), fibre (digestion), water.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why does a growing child need protein?" → Protein helps the body grow and repair itself.
- **Differentiation:** support: picture sorting with the eatwell-style plate / stretch: explain why some foods belong in more than one group.
- **Materials:** food packaging, food models, nutrition plate poster
- **Joy:** "Nutrient superheroes" — each group is a nutrient with a power.

### Lesson 20 — A balanced diet around the world
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can plan a balanced meal and explain my choices." (UK NC Y3 Animals – nutrition; PE & Health link)
- **Hook:** Photos of family meals from several countries — "What do they have in common?"
- **Key activity:** Identify nutrient groups in meals from different cultures (e.g. rice, dal and vegetables; injera and stew; pasta with beans); plan a balanced meal on a paper plate.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why is a meal of only crisps and sweets not balanced?" → It lacks protein, vitamins, minerals and fibre and has too much fat and sugar.
- **Differentiation:** support: plate template with sections / stretch: suggest a swap to improve an unbalanced meal.
- **Materials:** meal photos, paper plates, food cut-outs
- **Joy:** Design a "dream balanced meal."

### Lesson 21 — Reading food labels (investigation)
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can compare foods using nutrition labels." (UK NC Y3 Working scientifically; Maths link)
- **Hook:** "Which breakfast cereal has the most sugar?"
- **Key activity:** Read the sugar per 100 g from labels; compare in a bar chart; use sugar cubes (about 4 g each) to show amounts.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why do we compare per 100 g and not per packet?" → Packets are different sizes; per 100 g is fair.
- **Differentiation:** support: pre-found numbers / stretch: compare two nutrients.
- **Materials:** cereal boxes, sugar cubes, scales, squared paper
- **Joy:** Sugar-cube towers — surprising results.

### Lesson 22 — Skeletons: support, protect, move
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can explain the three jobs of the skeleton." (UK NC Y3 Animals – skeletons; NGSS 4-LS1-1 preview)
- **Hook:** A model skeleton ("Mr Bones") in a chair.
- **Key activity:** Feel own bones; identify skull (protects brain), ribs (protect heart and lungs), spine (support), leg bones (movement); label a skeleton.
- **Check for understanding:** "Which bones protect your heart and lungs, and how?" → The ribs form a cage around them.
- **Differentiation:** support: label cards on a model / stretch: explain why the skull has no joints that move (except the jaw).
- **Materials:** model skeleton, skeleton diagrams, X-ray images
- **Joy:** "Simon says" with bone names.

### Lesson 23 — Animal skeletons: vertebrates, exoskeletons and none
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can compare animals with internal skeletons, exoskeletons and no skeleton." (UK NC Y3 Animals – skeletons)
- **Hook:** A crab shell, a snail shell and a picture of a jellyfish.
- **Key activity:** Sort animals into endoskeleton (vertebrates), exoskeleton (insects, crabs), hydrostatic or none (worms, jellyfish); discuss how each moves and is protected.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why must a crab moult to grow?" → Its hard exoskeleton can't grow, so it sheds it and grows a new one.
- **Differentiation:** support: picture sort / stretch: explain the advantages of each type.
- **Materials:** shells, animal cards, X-ray pictures of animals
- **Joy:** Handling real crab and snail shells.

### Lesson 24 — Joints: how bones move
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can describe how joints let the skeleton move." (UK NC Y3 Animals – skeletons)
- **Hook:** "Try to pick up a pencil without bending your fingers."
- **Key activity:** Explore hinge joints (knee, elbow) and ball-and-socket joints (shoulder, hip) with body movements and simple models (a door hinge, a ball in a cup).
- **Check for understanding:** "Why can your shoulder move in more directions than your elbow?" → The shoulder is a ball-and-socket joint; the elbow is a hinge that bends one way.
- **Differentiation:** support: movement cards / stretch: find other joint types (the pivot joint in the neck).
- **Materials:** door hinge, ball and cup, joint diagrams
- **Joy:** "Robot vs human" movement challenge.

### Lesson 25 — Muscles work in pairs
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can explain that muscles pull bones by contracting and work in pairs." (UK NC Y3 Animals – muscles)
- **Hook:** "Make a muscle! Now feel what happens to the back of your arm."
- **Key activity:** Feel biceps and triceps as the arm bends and straightens; build a model arm from card, split pins and elastic bands.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why do muscles have to work in pairs?" → Muscles can only pull (contract), not push, so one bends the joint and the other straightens it.
- **Differentiation:** support: model with steps / stretch: explain which muscle contracts to straighten the arm.
- **Materials:** card strips, split pins, elastic bands, muscle diagrams
- **Joy:** Working model arms lift a paper cup.

### Lesson 26 — Investigation: does exercise change our heart rate? (plan)
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can plan a fair investigation on exercise and pulse." (UK NC Y3 Working scientifically; PE link)
- **Hook:** Feel your pulse at your wrist or neck.
- **Key activity:** Learn to count a pulse for 15 seconds (×4 for a minute); plan: resting pulse vs after 1 minute of star jumps; what to keep the same.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why should everyone do the same exercise for the same time?" → To keep the test fair.
- **Differentiation:** support: partner counts with a timer / stretch: plan a second question (different exercises).
- **Materials:** stopwatches, recording tables
- **Joy:** Finding your pulse for the first time.

### Lesson 27 — Investigation: carry out and conclude
- **Duration:** 30 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can collect pulse data and explain what it shows." (UK NC Y3 Working scientifically)
- **Hook:** Star-jump countdown.
- **Key activity:** Measure resting and after-exercise pulse; record; bar chart; conclude; discuss why the heart beats faster (muscles need more oxygen and energy).
- **Check for understanding:** "Why does your heart beat faster when you exercise?" → Muscles are working harder and need more oxygen and food, which the blood carries.
- **Differentiation:** support: conclusion frame / stretch: explain differences between classmates.
- **Materials:** stopwatches, squared paper
- **Joy:** Exercise circuit.

### Lesson 28 — Keeping bones and muscles healthy
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can explain how diet and exercise keep bones and muscles healthy." (UK NC Y3 Animals; PE & Health link)
- **Hook:** An astronaut returning from space — "Why do astronauts' bones get weaker?"
- **Key activity:** Discuss calcium, protein, exercise and sleep; design a "healthy bones" poster.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why do astronauts need to exercise in space?" → Without gravity their bones and muscles don't work as hard and get weaker.
- **Differentiation:** support: poster frame / stretch: explain the role of calcium.
- **Materials:** poster paper, food pictures
- **Joy:** Astronaut exercises.

### Lesson 29 — Scientist spotlight: Wilhelm Röntgen and X-rays
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can explain how X-rays help doctors see bones." (UK NC Y3 Working scientifically – scientists)
- **Hook:** Real X-ray images on a light box (window).
- **Key activity:** Learn that Wilhelm Röntgen discovered X-rays in 1895; identify bones in X-rays; discuss how doctors use them today.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why do bones show up white on an X-ray?" → Bones block more X-rays than soft body parts.
- **Differentiation:** support: matching X-rays to body parts / stretch: explain why X-rays must be used carefully.
- **Materials:** X-ray images, window or light box
- **Joy:** "Guess the X-ray" (including animal X-rays).

### Lesson 30 — End-of-unit check: nutrition, skeletons and muscles
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can show what I know about nutrition, skeletons and muscles." (Assessment)
- **Hook:** "Body detectives."
- **Key activity:** Label a skeleton; match nutrients to jobs; explain muscle pairs with a diagram; interpret a pulse table.
- **Check for understanding:** "Explain how the biceps and triceps bend and straighten your arm." → Biceps contracts to bend; triceps contracts to straighten.
- **Differentiation:** support: diagrams and word bank / stretch: an extended "why" question.
- **Materials:** check sheet, model arm
- **Joy:** "Body badge."

**End-of-unit check:** Name three nutrient groups and their jobs, label the skull, ribs and spine with their jobs, and explain with a diagram how a pair of muscles moves the arm.

## Unit 4 — Rocks, fossils and soils

**Essential question:** What is the ground beneath our feet made of — and how can rocks tell us stories from millions of years ago?

**Key vocabulary:** rock, igneous, sedimentary, metamorphic, property, hard, soft, permeable, impermeable, grain, crystal, fossil, sediment, mould, cast, palaeontologist, soil, humus, organic matter, sand, clay, loam, erosion

### Lesson 31 — What is under our feet?
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can explain that the Earth's surface is made of rock, often covered by soil." (UK NC Y3 Rocks; NGSS 2-ESS1-1 link)
- **Hook:** *The Street Beneath My Feet* unfolded (English link) — "What's under our school?"

- **Check for understanding:** "Explain the science that makes your game work." → Magnets attract steel objects through card / repel like poles.
- **Differentiation:** support: choose from template games / stretch: include both attraction and repulsion.
- **Materials:** card, magnets, paper clips, craft supplies
- **Joy:** Year 1 play-test day.

### Lesson 67 — Retrieval: forces and magnets
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can answer mixed questions about forces, friction and magnets." (Retrieval)
- **Hook:** "Force lab" stations.
- **Key activity:** Stations: force arrows, friction surfaces, magnetic sort, predict attract/repel, read a force meter.
- **Check for understanding:** "Two magnets push apart. What can you say about their poles?" → The facing poles are the same (N–N or S–S).
- **Differentiation:** support: picture cards and partner / stretch: bonus explain-why cards.
- **Materials:** force meters, ramps and toy cars, surface samples, bar magnets, mixed magnetic/non-magnetic objects, arrow cards
- **Joy:** "Force lab" badges for completing every station.

### Lesson 68 — End-of-unit check: forces and magnets
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can show what I know about forces and magnets." (Assessment)
- **Hook:** "Magnet mystery" — which object in a bag is magnetic?
- **Key activity:** Practical: test and sort six objects, predict and test attract/repel for labelled magnets, read a force meter to the nearest newton. Written: interpret a friction results table and answer a "why" question about compasses.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why did the car roll furthest on the smooth surface?" → The smooth surface caused the least friction, so the car was slowed down least.
- **Differentiation:** support: word bank and questions read aloud / stretch: "Explain how you could use a magnet to find which of two identical-looking bars is the magnet and which is plain steel." (Only a magnet can repel another magnet.)
- **Materials:** check sheet, bar magnets, objects bag, force meter
- **Joy:** "Forces expert" badge.

**End-of-unit check:** Sort six objects into magnetic and non-magnetic and explain one surprise, predict whether two magnets will attract or repel, and explain a friction ramp result.

## Unit 7 — Science fair and year review

**Essential question:** How can I plan my own investigation and share what I found?

**Key vocabulary:** investigation, question, prediction, fair test, method, results, conclusion, evaluate, present

### Lesson 69 — Plan and carry out my own investigation
- **Duration:** 30 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can plan and carry out my own fair test on a question I choose." (UK NC Y3 Working scientifically – independent)
- **Hook:** "Your question, your science."
- **Key activity:** Choose a question from the year (plants, rocks/soils, light, forces, magnets); plan with the class frame; carry out; record.
- **Check for understanding:** "What is your one change, and how do you keep it fair?" → States the variable and the fair-test controls.
- **Differentiation:** support: choose from 4 ready questions / stretch: an original question with repeated trials.
- **Materials:** equipment from the year's units, planning frames
- **Joy:** Freedom to follow their curiosity.

### Lesson 70 — Science fair
- **Duration:** 30 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can present my investigation to an audience and explain my conclusion." (UK NC Y3 Working scientifically – report; Spoken language)
- **Hook:** Tables set up like a real science fair; families or other classes visit.
- **Key activity:** Present question, prediction, method, results chart and conclusion; answer visitors' questions; reflect on what they'd improve.
- **Check for understanding:** "If you did it again, what would you change and why?" → An evaluation (e.g. more trials, measure more accurately).
- **Differentiation:** support: poster frame and a partner / stretch: explain a result that didn't match the prediction.
- **Materials:** poster paper, results, equipment for demonstrations
- **Joy:** The science fair itself, with "visitor's choice" awards.

**End-of-unit check:** An independent investigation poster with question, prediction, fair-test plan, results table/chart and conclusion.

## Teacher guidance

**Common misconceptions (and the fix)**
- *"Plants get their food from the soil."* Plants make their own food in their leaves using light, air and water; soil provides water and minerals. Use the foil-covered leaf and the dark-cupboard seedlings.
- *"We see because light comes out of our eyes."* Light travels from a source, reflects off objects and enters our eyes. Always draw arrows *into* the eye.
- *"The Moon is a light source."* It reflects the Sun's light.
- *"Shadows are reflections"* or *"shadows have details like faces."* A shadow is an area where light is blocked.
- *"All metals are magnetic."* Only some metals (iron, steel, nickel, cobalt) are; test aluminium foil and copper.
- *"Bigger magnets are always stronger."* Test it — shape and material matter more than size.
- *"Friction only happens with rough surfaces."* Smooth surfaces have less friction, but still some.
- *"Fossils are the actual bones."* Most fossils are rock that has replaced the original hard parts.
- *"The Sun moves around the Earth each day."* The Earth spins; the Sun appears to move.

**Safety.** Adult-only use of knives, heat and nails; wash hands after handling soil; check allergies before food activities (chocolate rock cycle, peanuts, lilies' pollen); never look at the Sun; supervise magnets around electronics and keep small magnets away from young children (swallowing risk).

**Local adaptation.** Choose plants, rocks and soils from your own region; replace the named buildings with local examples (a stone temple, mosque, church, market or wall near you); use your local sunrise/sunset times for the day-and-night lesson.

**Vertical alignment.** Year 1 introduced living things, habitats, plant needs, basic animal needs and everyday materials. Year 3 (ages 8–9) builds on this with classification keys, digestion and teeth, states of matter and the water cycle, sound, and electricity — each using the fair-test, measuring and recording routine secured this year.
