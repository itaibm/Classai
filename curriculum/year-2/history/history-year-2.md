# Year 2 History (ages 7–8) — Scope & Sequence

> Part of **the best international primary school** (see [`../../../SCHOOL-CHARTER.md`](../../../SCHOOL-CHARTER.md)).
> Built to [`../../PLANNING-BRIEF.md`](../../PLANNING-BRIEF.md). Drawn from the humanities knowledge
> base (`knowledge-base/subjects/history-geography/`, `_lesson-banks-humanities.md`) and the UK
> National Curriculum KS2 history (Stone Age to Iron Age; the earliest civilisations), with a
> deliberately worldwide lens.

## Year overview

Year 2 history is the story of **how humans went from living in small hunting bands to building
the first cities** — and of **how we can possibly know** about people who left no books. It is the
first year of long-range chronology: children learn to use BCE/CE (and BC/AD), to read and build
timelines spanning thousands of years, and to treat objects as **evidence**. Every unit asks
"How do we know?" and answers it with artefacts, sites, pictures and, later, the first writing.

**How the year sequences.** Unit 1 teaches the historian's toolkit — time, timelines, sources,
archaeology. Unit 2 follows humans from the first hunter-gatherers (Blombos Cave in South Africa,
the cave art of Lascaux) through the great change to **farming** (the Fertile Crescent, rice in
China, maize in Mexico) to **stone circles, bronze and iron** — using Göbekli Tepe, Çatalhöyük,
Skara Brae and Stonehenge as case studies. Unit 3 is a deep study of **Ancient Egypt**. Unit 4
compares three more early civilisations — **Sumer (Mesopotamia), the Indus Valley and Shang
China** — through the question "What do the first civilisations have in common?". Unit 5 puts the
whole year on one timeline and links it to the ancient history of the child's own country.

**What Year 2 assumes from Year 1 (ages 6–7):** the vocabulary of past and present; ordering events
and people within living memory and beyond on a simple timeline; learning about significant people
and events (explorers, nurses, a local study); asking "How do we know?" about photographs and
objects.

**What Year 3 (ages 8–9) relies on from this year:** secure BCE/CE chronology and long timelines;
comparing civilisations by the same headings (food, homes, beliefs, writing, rulers, legacy);
evaluating sources. Year 3 continues the story with **Ancient Greece**, **the Roman Empire**, **Kush** and **Ancient China**,
which grew from the world built by the civilisations studied here.

**How we teach history here:** a big question per unit, rich stories, real (replica) artefacts,
and a "detective" routine for every source — *What is it? What does it tell us? What can't it tell
us? What else would we need?* Lessons are 25–30 minutes, one per week.

## Unit 1 — The historian's toolkit: time, timelines and evidence

**Essential question:** How can we know about people who lived thousands of years ago?

**Key vocabulary:** past, present, century, millennium, decade, BCE, CE, BC, AD, timeline, chronological, era, source, evidence, artefact, archaeologist, excavate, museum, reliable

### Lesson 1 — How long ago is "long ago"?
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can use decade, century and millennium and place events on a long timeline." (UK NC KS2 History – chronology)
- **Hook:** A toilet-paper timeline unrolled down the corridor: one sheet = 100 years.
- **Key activity:** Mark "now," the children's birth years, the first aeroplane (1903), and the pyramids (about 4,500 years ago); discover how tiny our lives are on the roll; learn decade (10), century (100), millennium (1,000).
- **Check for understanding:** "Why did we need 45 sheets to reach the pyramids but less than one sheet for our own lives?" → The pyramids were built about 4,500 years ago — 45 centuries — while we have lived under 10 years.
- **Differentiation:** support: a shorter family timeline first / stretch: calculate how many centuries ago an event happened.
- **Materials:** toilet roll or till roll, marker pens, event cards
- **Joy:** Walking the corridor timeline "back in time."

### Lesson 2 — BCE and CE: counting years
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can explain BCE and CE and order dates on either side of year 1." (UK NC KS2 History – chronology)
- **Hook:** "Which is older: 3000 BCE or 300 BCE? The bigger number?"
- **Key activity:** Use a number line with year 1 in the middle; BCE counts backwards (like negative numbers), CE counts forwards; order a set of dates; note that BC/AD are older labels for the same system.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why is 3000 BCE older than 300 BCE?" → BCE years count backwards from year 1, so the bigger the BCE number, the further back in time.
- **Differentiation:** support: order three dates using a large number line / stretch: find how many years between 500 BCE and 500 CE (999, not 1,000 — there is no year 0).
- **Materials:** large number-line timeline, date cards, pegs
- **Joy:** "Time-travel line-up" — children holding dates arrange themselves.

### Lesson 3 — Sources: primary and secondary
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can explain the difference between a primary and secondary source." (UK NC KS2 History – evidence)
- **Hook:** Two objects: the teacher's childhood toy and a modern book about toys.
- **Key activity:** Sort sources (a diary, a photo, a textbook, a coin, a drawing made today of the past) into primary (made at the time) and secondary (made later); discuss usefulness of each.
- **Check for understanding:** "Is a painting of a Stone Age hunter made last year a primary source? Why?" → No — it was made long after the time, so it is secondary.
- **Differentiation:** support: picture sort with clear examples / stretch: explain why a secondary source can still be very useful.
- **Materials:** source cards, a real old object, a history book
- **Joy:** "Source museum" — each child brings an object from home to classify.

### Lesson 4 — Archaeology: history under the ground
- **Duration:** 30 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can explain how archaeologists find and study evidence." (UK NC KS2 History – evidence)
- **Hook:** A sand tray "dig site" with hidden broken pottery.
- **Key activity:** Excavate carefully with brushes; record where finds are found (layers); piece together pottery; discuss that deeper layers are usually older.
- **Check for understanding:** "If a coin is found below a pot, which is probably older? Why?" → The coin, because lower layers were usually laid down earlier.
- **Differentiation:** support: guided dig with a recording sheet / stretch: explain what could disturb the layers (animals, digging).
- **Materials:** sand trays, broken terracotta pots, brushes, spoons, finds bags, recording sheets
- **Joy:** The dig itself.

### Lesson 5 — Evidence detectives: what can an object tell us?
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can ask questions of an artefact and say what it can and can't tell us." (UK NC KS2 History – evidence)
- **Hook:** A mystery object (e.g. a replica flint hand axe).
- **Key activity:** Use the four detective questions (What is it? What does it tell us? What can't it tell us? What else would we need?) on replica artefacts in groups.
- **Check for understanding:** "What can't a hand axe tell us about the person who made it?" → Their name, language, feelings or beliefs.
- **Differentiation:** support: question cards with sentence starters / stretch: suggest what other evidence could fill a gap.
- **Materials:** replica artefacts or museum loan box, question cards
- **Joy:** "Mystery object" guessing game.

**End-of-unit check:** Put six dates (including BCE and CE) in order on a timeline, sort sources into primary and secondary, and ask the four detective questions about an artefact.

## Unit 2 — From hunters to farmers: the Stone, Bronze and Iron Ages

**Essential question:** How did learning to farm change the way humans lived forever?

**Key vocabulary:** prehistory, Stone Age, Palaeolithic, Neolithic, hunter-gatherer, nomad, flint, tool, cave art, farming, domesticate, settlement, village, monument, stone circle, Bronze Age, Iron Age, smelt, hillfort, trade

### Lesson 6 — The first humans and hunter-gatherers
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can describe how hunter-gatherers lived and how we know." (UK NC KS2 History – Stone Age)
- **Hook:** A replica flint and a photo of Blombos Cave in South Africa, where people made engraved ochre over 70,000 years ago.
- **Key activity:** Learn that modern humans first lived in Africa and spread across the world; explore how hunter-gatherers found food, moved with the seasons and made tools; handle flint, bone and antler replicas.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why did hunter-gatherers move from place to place?" → To follow animals and find plants that changed with the seasons.
- **Differentiation:** support: picture sequencing of a day in a hunter-gatherer band / stretch: explain what a tool can tell us about skills.
- **Materials:** replica tools, photos of Blombos Cave, world map
- **Joy:** Make a "tool" from clay and sticks and explain its use.

### Lesson 7 — Cave art: messages from the Ice Age
- **Duration:** 30 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can suggest what cave art tells us about people's lives and beliefs." (UK NC KS2 History – Stone Age; Art link)
- **Hook:** A darkened room, torchlight on projected images of Lascaux (France), about 17,000 years old.
- **Key activity:** Look at cave art from Lascaux and other continents (e.g. Australian rock art); suggest why it was made; paint with natural pigments on crumpled paper.
- **Check for understanding:** "What can cave paintings tell us, and what can't they?" → Which animals were important; not exactly why they were painted — we can only suggest.
- **Differentiation:** support: describe one picture with sentence starters / stretch: compare two interpretations of why cave art was made.
- **Materials:** projected images, torches, charcoal, ochre-coloured paints, crumpled brown paper
- **Joy:** Painting by torchlight in a "cave."

### Lesson 8 — The great change: farming begins
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can explain how and where farming began and why it changed people's lives." (UK NC KS2 History – Neolithic)
- **Hook:** A handful of wheat grains, rice and maize kernels.
- **Key activity:** Learn that farming began separately in several places — wheat and barley in the Fertile Crescent (around 11,000 years ago), rice in China, maize in Mexico; map them; discuss what changed (settled homes, more food, villages, new jobs).
- **Check for understanding:** "Why did farming mean people stopped moving around?" → They had to stay and look after crops and animals.
- **Differentiation:** support: before/after picture sort / stretch: explain one disadvantage of farming (hard work, disease, bad harvests).
- **Materials:** grains, world map, before/after cards
- **Joy:** Grind grain with two stones (quern) to make flour.

### Lesson 9 — Göbekli Tepe and Çatalhöyük: very old places
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can describe two very old sites and what archaeologists learned from them." (UK NC KS2 History – evidence)
- **Hook:** A photo of Göbekli Tepe's carved stone pillars — "Built before farming began?"
- **Key activity:** Göbekli Tepe (Turkey, about 11,500 years old) — huge carved pillars built by hunter-gatherers; Çatalhöyük (Turkey, about 9,000 years old) — a town with houses entered through the roof; compare.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why did Göbekli Tepe surprise archaeologists?" → It was built by hunter-gatherers, before people thought anyone could build such big monuments.
- **Differentiation:** support: picture fact cards / stretch: explain how archaeologists work out a site's age.
- **Materials:** photos, fact cards, a model roof-entry house
- **Joy:** Build a model Çatalhöyük house from a shoebox (entry through the roof!).

### Lesson 10 — Skara Brae: a Stone Age village
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can use evidence from Skara Brae to describe Neolithic life." (UK NC KS2 History – Neolithic)
- **Hook:** "A storm in 1850 uncovered a 5,000-year-old village in Orkney, Scotland."
- **Key activity:** Examine photos of stone "furniture" (beds, dressers, hearths); infer daily life; compare with Çatalhöyük.
- **Check for understanding:** "What does a stone dresser tell us about the people who lived there?" → They had things worth displaying or storing, and settled lives.
- **Differentiation:** support: labelled photos / stretch: compare what's the same and different in two villages.
- **Materials:** photos, a floor plan, comparison chart
- **Joy:** Design a Skara Brae house interior from modelling clay.

### Lesson 11 — Stonehenge and stone circles
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can suggest reasons why people built monuments like Stonehenge and weigh up evidence." (UK NC KS2 History – interpretation)
- **Hook:** "Some stones were dragged over 200 km from Wales. Why go to so much trouble?"
- **Key activity:** Look at theories (calendar and sun alignment, burial place, gathering place); sort evidence for each; hold a class vote with reasons.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why do historians still disagree about Stonehenge?" → There's no writing from the builders; we only have physical evidence, which can be read in different ways.
- **Differentiation:** support: theory cards with pictures / stretch: argue for one theory using two pieces of evidence.
- **Materials:** photos, theory cards, a model with blocks
- **Joy:** Move a heavy box on rollers (logs) to test how stones could be moved.

### Lesson 12 — The Bronze Age: metal changes everything
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can explain why bronze was important and how trade grew." (UK NC KS2 History – Bronze Age)
- **Hook:** A piece of copper wire and a photo of tin — "Melt these two metals together and you get…?" (Aside: most coins and "tin" cans today are not really made of copper or tin — a good reason to check.)
- **Key activity:** Learn that bronze is made by mixing copper and tin; that metal tools and weapons were stronger; that because copper and tin were often found far apart, trade grew; look at the Amesbury Archer's grave goods (buried near Stonehenge, whose teeth show he grew up in the Alps region).
- **Check for understanding:** "Why did the Bronze Age lead to more trade?" → Copper and tin came from different places, so people had to trade to get both.
- **Differentiation:** support: picture sequence of making bronze / stretch: explain how scientists can tell where someone grew up (chemistry of teeth).
- **Materials:** photos of bronze objects, copper wire, a trade map
- **Joy:** A trading game with "copper" and "tin" tokens.

### Lesson 13 — The Iron Age: hillforts and craft
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can describe life in the Iron Age and why iron replaced bronze." (UK NC KS2 History – Iron Age)
- **Hook:** A photo of a hillfort seen from the air.
- **Key activity:** Learn that iron ore was more common than copper and tin, so tools became cheaper; explore roundhouses, hillforts and Iron Age craft (e.g. Celtic metalwork) and, in West Africa, the Nok culture of Nigeria — early iron-smelters famous for their terracotta heads; note that Iron Ages began at different times in different places.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why might people build their homes on a hill with ditches around it?" → For protection from attack and to see enemies coming.
- **Differentiation:** support: labelled hillfort picture / stretch: compare a roundhouse with a Neolithic house.
- **Materials:** aerial photos, roundhouse model plans, metalwork images
- **Joy:** Build a roundhouse model with sticks, string and clay.

### Lesson 14 — Unit check: from hunters to farmers
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can explain how life changed from the Stone Age to the Iron Age and use evidence to show it." (Assessment)
- **Hook:** "Stone Age to Iron Age museum" — groups curate one object each.
- **Key activity:** Order the periods and sites on a timeline; write a "museum label" for a replica object; answer: "What was the most important change, and why?"
- **Check for understanding:** "What was the biggest change in how people lived between the Palaeolithic and the Neolithic? Give evidence." → Farming and settling in villages — e.g. Skara Brae, Çatalhöyük.
- **Differentiation:** support: label frame / stretch: argue that a different change (metal) was more important.
- **Materials:** replica objects, label cards, timeline
- **Joy:** Museum opening for another class.

**End-of-unit check:** Place hunter-gatherers, the first farming, Stonehenge and the Iron Age in order on a timeline, and explain with evidence how farming changed people's lives.

## Unit 3 — Ancient Egypt

**Essential question:** How did the River Nile make one of the greatest civilisations in history possible?

**Key vocabulary:** civilisation, Nile, flood, silt, pharaoh, dynasty, pyramid, tomb, mummy, afterlife, god, goddess, hieroglyphs, scribe, papyrus, Rosetta Stone, archaeologist, Egyptologist

### Lesson 15 — The gift of the Nile
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can explain why the River Nile was so important to Ancient Egypt." (UK NC KS2 History – earliest civilisations)
- **Hook:** A satellite photo of Egypt at night: a thin line of light along the Nile in a dark desert.
- **Key activity:** Locate Egypt; learn about the yearly flood, the rich black silt, farming, transport by boat; model a flood with sand, soil and water in a tray.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why did most Egyptians live close to the Nile?" → It gave water, fertile soil for crops, food (fish) and a way to travel, in the middle of a desert.
- **Differentiation:** support: labelled map and picture cards / stretch: explain what could happen in a year when the flood was too low.
- **Materials:** maps, satellite image, sand tray, soil, water
- **Joy:** The flood model.

### Lesson 16 — Pharaohs and society
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can describe the different groups in Egyptian society and the pharaoh's role." (UK NC KS2 History)
- **Hook:** A pyramid-shaped chart with a crown at the top.
- **Key activity:** Build a human "society pyramid" (pharaoh, officials and priests, scribes, craftspeople, farmers, servants); discuss who had power and why; meet female pharaohs such as Hatshepsut.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why were there far more farmers than scribes?" → Farming needed many workers to feed everyone; few people could read and write.
- **Differentiation:** support: picture role cards / stretch: explain why the pharaoh was seen as a god on Earth.
- **Materials:** role cards, pyramid chart, props
- **Joy:** The human society pyramid.

### Lesson 17 — Pyramids: how were they built?
- **Duration:** 30 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can explain what evidence tells us about how the pyramids were built and who built them." (UK NC KS2 History – evidence)
- **Hook:** "The Great Pyramid of Giza was built about 4,500 years ago. It was the tallest human-made structure on Earth for thousands of years."
- **Key activity:** Examine evidence: workers' villages and bakeries found near Giza, ramps, copper tools, papyrus logbooks describing transporting stone by boat; conclude that paid, organised workers (not slaves, as once claimed) built them; test moving a block with a sledge and water on sand.
- **Check for understanding:** "What evidence shows the pyramids were built by organised workers who were fed and housed?" → Workers' villages, bakeries and tombs near the pyramids.
- **Differentiation:** support: evidence picture cards / stretch: explain how new evidence changed an old interpretation.
- **Materials:** photos, evidence cards, a brick, sledge (tray), sand, water
- **Joy:** Pyramid-building challenge with sugar cubes.

### Lesson 18 — Mummies and the afterlife
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can explain why and how Egyptians mummified the dead." (UK NC KS2 History – beliefs)
- **Hook:** An apple slice "mummified" in salt and baking soda for a week (set up in advance).
- **Key activity:** Learn about beliefs in the afterlife and the process (around 70 days, natron salt, wrapping, canopic jars); examine the "mummified" apple vs a fresh one.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why did Egyptians go to so much trouble to preserve bodies?" → They believed the body was needed in the afterlife.
- **Differentiation:** support: picture sequence of the process / stretch: explain what the preserved apple shows about drying and decay (Science link).
- **Materials:** apple slices, salt, baking soda, cups, canopic jar pictures
- **Joy:** The apple mummy reveal.

### Lesson 19 — Gods, goddesses and temples
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can describe some Egyptian gods and what they show about Egyptian beliefs." (UK NC KS2 History – beliefs)
- **Hook:** Images of Ra, Osiris, Isis, Anubis and Thoth.
- **Key activity:** Match gods to what they looked after (sun, the dead, magic, writing); discuss why gods often had animal heads; design a temple wall.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why might a god of the dead (Anubis) have the head of a jackal?" → Jackals were seen near burial grounds, so they became linked with death and protecting the dead.
- **Differentiation:** support: matching cards / stretch: compare with gods from another ancient culture later in the year.
- **Materials:** god image cards, paper, gold paint
- **Joy:** Temple-wall art.

### Lesson 20 — Hieroglyphs and scribes
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can explain what hieroglyphs are and write my name in them." (UK NC KS2 History – writing)
- **Hook:** A cartouche with a mystery name.
- **Key activity:** Learn that scribes trained for years; hieroglyphs mix sound signs and picture signs; write names in cartouches on "papyrus" (brown paper).
- **Check for understanding:** "Why were scribes so important in Ancient Egypt?" → Very few people could write; scribes kept records of taxes, harvests and laws.
- **Differentiation:** support: simplified hieroglyph alphabet / stretch: explain that hieroglyphs are not simply an alphabet.
- **Materials:** hieroglyph charts, brown paper, reed pens or felt tips
- **Joy:** Name cartouches.

### Lesson 21 — The Rosetta Stone: cracking the code
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can explain how the Rosetta Stone helped people read hieroglyphs again." (UK NC KS2 History – evidence)
- **Hook:** A coded message and its translation side by side — "Crack the rest!"
- **Key activity:** Learn that the Rosetta Stone (found in 1799) has the same text in three scripts (hieroglyphs, demotic, ancient Greek); Jean-François Champollion used it to decipher hieroglyphs in 1822; children crack a code using a key.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why did having the same message in Greek help?" → Scholars could read Greek, so they could compare it to the hieroglyphs and work out the signs.
- **Differentiation:** support: a simple symbol code / stretch: explain why the Rosetta Stone is now in a London museum and whether it should return to Egypt (discuss both views).
- **Materials:** code sheets, a Rosetta Stone image
- **Joy:** Code-cracking race.

### Lesson 22 — Tutankhamun: discovery and debate
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can explain what the discovery of Tutankhamun's tomb taught us." (UK NC KS2 History – evidence and interpretation)
- **Hook:** "In 1922 an archaeologist peered into a tomb and said he could see 'wonderful things'."
- **Key activity:** Learn about the 1922 discovery by Howard Carter and a large team of Egyptian workers; examine objects (the golden mask, chariots, food); discuss what the tomb shows about a young pharaoh's life and death.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why is an untouched tomb such important evidence?" → Most tombs were robbed; an untouched one shows exactly what was buried and how.
- **Differentiation:** support: picture objects to describe / stretch: debate whether tombs should be opened at all.
- **Materials:** photos of the tomb and objects, fact cards
- **Joy:** "Treasure inventory" — groups catalogue replica "finds."

### Lesson 23 — Unit check: Ancient Egypt
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can explain how the Nile, beliefs and writing shaped Ancient Egypt, using evidence." (Assessment)
- **Hook:** "Ask an Egyptologist" — children are the experts.
- **Key activity:** Written task: why the Nile mattered; one piece of evidence about pyramids; why hieroglyphs could be read again; place Egypt on the class timeline.
- **Check for understanding:** "Cleopatra lived closer in time to us than to the building of the Great Pyramid. How can that be?" → Ancient Egypt lasted about 3,000 years; the Great Pyramid was built about 2,500 years before Cleopatra.
- **Differentiation:** support: frame and word bank / stretch: an extended answer with two sources.
- **Materials:** check sheet, timeline
- **Joy:** Egyptologist certificates.

**End-of-unit check:** Explain with evidence why the Nile made Egyptian civilisation possible and how we know about pyramids, mummies or hieroglyphs.

## Unit 4 — The first cities: Sumer, the Indus Valley and Shang China

**Essential question:** What do the first civilisations have in common — and what made each one special?

**Key vocabulary:** Mesopotamia, Sumer, Tigris, Euphrates, city-state, ziggurat, cuneiform, clay tablet, wheel, Indus, Harappa, Mohenjo-daro, drain, seal, Shang, Yellow River (Huang He), oracle bone, bronze, dynasty, compare, legacy

### Lesson 24 — Rivers and the first civilisations
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can locate four early river civilisations and explain why they grew near rivers." (UK NC KS2 History – earliest civilisations; Geography link)
- **Hook:** A world map with four rivers glowing: Nile, Tigris–Euphrates, Indus, Yellow River.
- **Key activity:** Locate each civilisation; compare with Egypt; build a class comparison chart with headings (river, dates, writing, buildings, rulers, inventions, beliefs) to fill in through the unit.
- **Check for understanding:** "What did all four civilisations need from their rivers?" → Water for crops and drinking, fertile soil, transport and trade.
- **Differentiation:** support: labelled map / stretch: suggest what problems rivers also caused (floods).
- **Materials:** world map, comparison chart, river cards
- **Joy:** "River race" — place the rivers on the floor map.

### Lesson 25 — Sumer: the first writing
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can explain how and why cuneiform writing began in Sumer." (UK NC KS2 History – Sumer)
- **Hook:** A clay tablet with wedge marks — "A shopping list from 5,000 years ago?"
- **Key activity:** Learn that cuneiform began over 5,000 years ago in cities like Uruk, first to record goods and trade; press wedge marks into clay with a lolly stick.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why might the first writing have been used for counting sheep and grain, not stories?" → People needed records for trade and taxes; stories came later.
- **Differentiation:** support: copy simple cuneiform signs / stretch: compare cuneiform and hieroglyphs.
- **Materials:** air-dry clay, lolly sticks, cuneiform charts
- **Joy:** Write a secret message in cuneiform.

### Lesson 26 — Sumer: cities, ziggurats and the wheel
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can describe a Sumerian city and explain the importance of the wheel." (UK NC KS2 History – Sumer)
- **Hook:** The Royal Game of Ur, one of the oldest board games in the world — "Let's play!"
- **Key activity:** Explore city-states, ziggurats and inventions (wheeled carts, the potter's wheel, counting in 60s, which gives us 60 minutes in an hour); play the Royal Game of Ur (printable board).
- **Check for understanding:** "Look at a clock. Explain how it connects to Sumer 5,000 years ago." → The Sumerians (and later Babylonians) counted in 60s, and we still split an hour into 60 minutes and a minute into 60 seconds.
- **Differentiation:** support: picture cards of inventions / stretch: explain why counting in 60s is useful (many factors).
- **Materials:** Royal Game of Ur boards, counters, dice, ziggurat pictures
- **Joy:** The Royal Game of Ur tournament.

### Lesson 27 — The Indus Valley: planned cities
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can describe the planned cities of the Indus Valley and what they show." (UK NC KS2 History – Indus Valley)
- **Hook:** A plan of Mohenjo-daro's grid streets — "What do you notice?"
- **Key activity:** Explore Harappa and Mohenjo-daro (about 4,500 years ago, in today's Pakistan and north-west India): grid streets, covered drains, standard-sized bricks, the Great Bath; compare with a modern town plan.
- **Check for understanding:** "What does a city with drains and same-sized bricks tell us about its people?" → They were organised, planned together and cared about clean water and health.
- **Differentiation:** support: labelled plan / stretch: suggest who might have organised this planning.
- **Materials:** city plans, photos, same-size building blocks
- **Joy:** Build a grid city with drains from blocks and straws.

### Lesson 28 — The Indus mystery: an unread script
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can explain why there's still a lot we don't know about the Indus Valley." (UK NC KS2 History – evidence)
- **Hook:** An Indus seal with an animal and symbols — "Nobody can read this. Yet."
- **Key activity:** Examine seals; learn the script is undeciphered and there's no "Rosetta Stone" for it; list what we know (from objects) and what we don't (names, rulers, beliefs); make a clay seal.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why can't historians be sure who ruled the Indus cities?" → We can't read their writing and haven't found palaces or royal tombs to tell us.
- **Differentiation:** support: know/don't-know sorting cards / stretch: suggest what evidence would help crack the script.
- **Materials:** seal images, clay, carving tools
- **Joy:** Carve and stamp your own "seal."

### Lesson 29 — Shang China: bronze and oracle bones
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can describe what Shang China is famous for and how we know." (UK NC KS2 History – Shang Dynasty)
- **Hook:** A picture of an oracle bone with cracks and writing.
- **Key activity:** Learn about the Shang dynasty (about 3,600–3,000 years ago) on the Yellow River: bronze vessels, oracle bones used to ask ancestors questions, the earliest Chinese writing; compare Chinese characters then and now.
- **Check for understanding:** "What do oracle bones tell us about Shang beliefs and writing?" → They asked ancestors questions (beliefs), and the inscriptions are the earliest Chinese writing.
- **Differentiation:** support: picture cards / stretch: find a modern Chinese character that grew from a Shang symbol.
- **Materials:** images of bronzes and oracle bones, character charts
- **Joy:** Write a question on a "bone" (card) and "crack" it.

### Lesson 30 — Comparing the first civilisations
- **Duration:** 30 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can compare four early civilisations and find similarities and differences." (UK NC KS2 History – compare)
- **Hook:** The class comparison chart, now full.
- **Key activity:** Groups use the chart to answer: What did all have? (rivers, farming, cities, writing, rulers, beliefs) What was unique? (pyramids, drains, cuneiform, oracle bones); present findings.
- **Check for understanding:** "Name one thing all four civilisations had and explain why." → e.g. writing — to record trade, laws and beliefs as societies grew larger.
- **Differentiation:** support: chart with picture icons / stretch: rank the civilisations by one criterion and justify.
- **Materials:** comparison chart, sticky notes
- **Joy:** "Civilisation Top Trumps" cards made by the class.

### Lesson 31 — Legacy: what did they give the world?
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can explain how ideas from early civilisations still affect our lives." (UK NC KS2 History – legacy)
- **Hook:** Everyday objects: a clock, a calendar, a wheel, a pen.
- **Key activity:** Link objects to ancient inventions (writing, the wheel, 60 minutes, calendars, planned drains); debate which legacy is most important.
- **Check for understanding:** "Which ancient invention would you find hardest to live without, and why?" → A reasoned answer.
- **Differentiation:** support: matching cards / stretch: write a persuasive paragraph.
- **Materials:** everyday objects, legacy cards
- **Joy:** "Invention auction" — bid with tokens for the most valuable legacy.

### Lesson 32 — Unit check: the first cities
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can compare early civilisations using evidence." (Assessment)
- **Hook:** "Time-traveller's guidebook."
- **Key activity:** Write a guidebook page comparing two civilisations with two pieces of evidence; place all four on the timeline.
- **Check for understanding:** "How do we know about the Indus Valley even though we can't read their writing?" → From cities, buildings, seals, pots and other objects archaeologists have found.
- **Differentiation:** support: frame / stretch: a third civilisation.
- **Materials:** guidebook templates, timeline
- **Joy:** Guidebooks swapped with a partner.

**End-of-unit check:** Compare two early civilisations under three headings with evidence, and explain one thing historians still don't know and why.

## Unit 5 — The big timeline and ancient history near me

**Essential question:** How does everything we learned this year fit together — and what happened where I live?

**Key vocabulary:** timeline, overlap, at the same time, period, local, heritage, evidence, museum

### Lesson 33 — The big timeline
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can place all the year's periods and civilisations on one timeline and spot overlaps." (UK NC KS2 History – chronology)
- **Hook:** "When the Great Pyramid was being built, what was happening at Stonehenge?"
- **Key activity:** Build a class timeline with parallel rows for different places; notice overlaps (Stonehenge's big stones and the Great Pyramid are roughly the same age; the Shang and later Egyptian dynasties overlap).
- **Check for understanding:** "Use our timeline to prove that Shang China and Ancient Egypt overlapped. Did the people know about each other?" → Both rows cover about 1600–1050 BCE, so they existed at the same time; they almost certainly did not know about each other — overlapping in time does not mean meeting.
- **Differentiation:** support: place cards on a prepared timeline / stretch: calculate time gaps between events.
- **Materials:** long paper, event cards, string
- **Joy:** A wall-length "world timeline."

### Lesson 34 — Ancient history near me
- **Duration:** 30 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can find out about an ancient site or object from my own country or region." (UK NC KS2 History – local history)
- **Hook:** A photo of an ancient site or museum object from the local area (worked example: for a school in the UK, a local Iron Age hillfort; in Kenya, the rock art of Mfangano Island on Lake Victoria; in Mexico, Teotihuacan; in Israel, Tel Megiddo).
- **Key activity:** Research a local site or museum object with the four detective questions; add it to the big timeline; if possible, visit.
- **Check for understanding:** "How does your local site fit on the big timeline?" → Places it and compares it with a civilisation studied.
- **Differentiation:** support: guided fact sheet / stretch: compare the local site with one studied this year.
- **Materials:** local photos and sources, museum website, timeline
- **Joy:** Visit (or virtual tour of) the site or museum.

### Lesson 35 — Year review: history showcase
- **Duration:** 30 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can explain what I've learned about the ancient world and how historians know it." (Assessment)
- **Hook:** "History museum" run by the class.
- **Key activity:** Children present a favourite artefact (replica or made) with its museum label and the question "How do we know?"; reflect on the year's big question.
- **Check for understanding:** "What is the most important thing a historian must do before believing something about the past?" → Look for evidence and check it with other sources.
- **Differentiation:** support: label frame / stretch: explain two interpretations of the same evidence.
- **Materials:** artefacts, labels, timeline
- **Joy:** Museum opening for families.

**End-of-unit check:** Place the year's periods on one timeline, noting two overlaps, and explain how evidence tells us about one ancient site near you.

## Teacher guidance

**Common misconceptions (and the fix)**
- *"Cavemen lived with dinosaurs."* Dinosaurs (except birds) died out about 66 million years ago; humans appeared only about 300,000 years ago. Use the corridor timeline — the gap is enormous.
- *"All Stone Age people lived in caves."* Most lived in shelters and camps; caves were used for some activities and survive well, so we find more there.
- *"The pyramids were built by slaves."* Current evidence (workers' villages, tombs, records) points to paid and conscripted workers. Present this as an example of how new evidence changes interpretations.
- *"Egypt was the only ancient civilisation."* Always compare with Sumer, the Indus and Shang China.
- *"BCE numbers work like normal numbers."* Bigger BCE numbers are further back; use a number line with year 1 in the middle.
- *"History is just facts."* Model "we think… because…" and "historians disagree about…".

**Sensitivity.** Treat beliefs about death and the afterlife respectfully; discuss the ethics of displaying human remains and of objects held far from their country of origin (the Rosetta Stone) with both views presented.

**International and local.** Swap case studies to include ancient sites from your own country in Unit 5 and wherever possible throughout (e.g. Great Zimbabwe is later but can be previewed on the timeline; Caral in Peru is one of the oldest cities in the Americas).

**Vertical alignment.** Year 1 introduced chronology within and beyond living memory, significant people and local history. Year 3 continues the story to **Ancient Greece, Rome, the Kingdom of Kush and Ancient China** (which deepens this year's single Shang lesson into a full unit) — the comparison headings, BCE/CE timelines and evidence routines built this year carry straight into those units.
