# Year 2 Geography — Tutor Scripts (Lessons 1–6)

Complete, minute-by-minute scripts for [Year 2 Geography](../geography-year-2.md), built to the [scripting guide](../../../SCRIPTING-GUIDE.md) and grounded in `knowledge-base/subjects/history-geography/geography-ks1.md`. Every beat ⏱ sums to the lesson's duration, with verbatim Say / Show / Do / Ask, a real check with wrong-answer fixes, the "if the child is stuck" protocol, what to emphasize, differentiation and assessment evidence. Pin every place-fact to a **wall map and globe**. Personalise the comparison place (Unit 2) to a contrasting hot location you have good photos of.

## Lesson 1 — From above again: drawing a plan view
**Geography · Year 2 · Unit 1 (Mapmaker skills)** | **Duration:** 30 min | **Objective:** "By the end, I can draw a simple bird's-eye plan of objects on a table and explain it shows the view from above."

**You will need:** a tray of household objects (cup, pencil case, book), paper and pencils, a paired side-photo and aerial-photo of the same scene.
**Prior knowledge to retrieve:** the bird's-eye idea from Year 1 (looking straight down).
**Key vocabulary:** **bird's-eye view**, **plan**, **from above**, **top (not the front)**.
**What to emphasize (the heart of the lesson):** a plan shows the view from **straight above** — the **tops** of things, not the fronts. Pre-empt drawing the sides/fronts of objects.

---

### ⏱ 0–4 min · HOOK
> **Say:** "Put your cup, pencil case and book on the table. Now — become a bird, hovering right above, looking straight down. What shapes can you see from up there? Not the fronts — the *tops*."
**Show:** the tray of objects.  **Do:** hold a phone/hand high above and 'look down'.
**Ask (retrieve prior knowledge):** "What's a *bird's-eye view*?" → *listen for:* "looking straight down from above."

### ⏱ 4–10 min · EXPLAIN (I do)
> **Say:** "A **plan** is a drawing of the view from straight above. Watch: from above, the cup is a **circle**, the book is a **rectangle**, the pencil case is a long oval. I don't draw the sides or the front — only what the bird sees looking *down*. That's how mapmakers draw."
**Show / Do:** draw the plan of two objects, narrating each top-shape; show the side vs aerial photo pair.
**Emphasize:** "A plan shows the **tops**, the view from straight above — never the fronts."

### ⏱ 10–16 min · EXAMPLE (we do)
> **Say:** "Help me add the cup to our plan — what shape is it from above? Now the book?"
**Do:** child names and draws the top-shapes of two objects onto the shared plan.
**Ask:** "Does a plan show the front of things or the top of things?" → *listen for:* "the top — the view from above."

### ⏱ 16–22 min · CHECK
**Ask:** "Does a plan show the front of things or the top of things — and what shape is the cup from above?"
- ✅ **Expected:** the top (view from straight above); a circle — probe: *"How do you know it's a circle and not a cup shape?"*
- ❌ **If draws the side/front of objects:** side-view error → **fix (KB):** "Be the bird — look *straight down*. Can you see the handle from above, or just the round top?"
- ❌ **If "the front":** → **fix:** "A plan is from *above* — point your eyes at the ceiling-down view."

### ⏱ 22–27 min · PRACTICE (you do)
**Do:** Child draws a bird's-eye plan of 3–4 objects on their own table, labelling each. Once secure, interleave: add the chair tucked under the desk to the plan.
**Watch for:** drawing fronts; adding detail a bird couldn't see.
> **If the child is stuck (missed twice):** 1) don't reveal; 2) name *why* — side vs top; 3) hold the object and look down together; 4) one hint: "Only what the bird sees"; 5) re-ask 'what shape from above?'; 6) "Mistakes show us what to practise — smaller step."

### ⏱ 27–30 min · RECAP
> **Say:** "What does a plan show — and from where?" → *listen for:* "the tops of things, from straight above."
**Process praise:** "You drew the tops, not the fronts — that's exactly how a real mapmaker sees the world."
**Revisit later:** tomorrow — symbols, a key, and how maps shrink the world.

---

**Differentiation**
- **Support (gentle):** trace round objects placed on the paper.
- **Stretch (challenge):** draw the plan of the whole desk, including where the chair tucks in.

**Extension (if time allows):** draw a plan of your own bedroom from above.
**Assessment evidence:** child draws a recognisable plan view and states it shows the tops/view from above.

## Lesson 2 — Symbols, a key, and shrinking the world
**Geography · Year 2 · Unit 1 (Mapmaker skills)** | **Duration:** 30 min | **Objective:** "By the end, I can use symbols and a key on a map and explain that a map is shrunk (smaller than real life)."

**You will need:** a simple local map with a key, symbol cards (blue=water, green=park, tree=woods), coloured pencils.
**Prior knowledge to retrieve:** plan view (Lesson 1); symbols from Year 1.
**Key vocabulary:** **symbol**, **key**, **shrink/smaller**, **scale** (informal), **decode**.
**What to emphasize (the heart of the lesson):** a map is **shrunk** — everything is drawn much **smaller than real life** so a whole town fits on a page; the **key** tells you what each **symbol** stands for. Pre-empt thinking symbols are the real thing's size.

---

### ⏱ 0–4 min · HOOK
> **Say:** "A real park is HUGE — you could run around it for ages. But look, here it is, fitting neatly on one page! How on earth did the mapmaker make a whole park fit?"
**Show:** the local map.  **Do:** spread arms wide ('real park') then point to the tiny map park.
**Ask (retrieve prior knowledge):** "On a map, what does blue usually mean?" → *listen for:* "water."

### ⏱ 4–10 min · EXPLAIN (I do)
> **Say:** "Two big ideas. One: a map is **shrunk** — everything is drawn much smaller than real life, so a whole town fits on a page. Two: little **symbols** stand for real things, and the **key** tells you what each means — blue = water, green = park, a tree = woods. Watch me decode this corner: blue here means a river, not paint!"
**Show / Do:** point to the key; decode 3 symbols on the map; stress 'shrunk'.
**Emphasize:** "A map is **shrunk smaller than real life**, and the **key** unlocks the symbols."

### ⏱ 10–16 min · EXAMPLE (we do)
> **Say:** "Decode with me — what's this green area? Check the key. And why does the whole town fit here?"
**Do:** child reads two symbols using the key and explains the map is shrunk.
**Ask:** "Why can a whole town fit onto one map?" → *listen for:* "because it's shrunk — drawn much smaller than real life."

### ⏱ 16–22 min · CHECK
**Ask:** "Why can a whole town fit onto one map, and what tells you what a symbol means?"
- ✅ **Expected:** the map is shrunk (everything smaller than real life); the key tells you the symbols — probe: *"So is the real river as small as the blue line?"* (no).
- ❌ **If "the town is small":** thinks it's actual size → **fix (KB):** "The real town is huge — the *map* is shrunk. The blue river is really wide."
- ❌ **If ignores the key:** → **fix:** "What does this colour mean? Check the *key* — that's the map's dictionary."

### ⏱ 22–27 min · PRACTICE (you do)
**Do:** Child decodes a small local map using its key and adds two new symbols to the key. Once secure, interleave: invent a sensible symbol for a school and add it.
**Watch for:** thinking symbols are real-size; not using the key.
> **If the child is stuck (missed twice):** 1) don't reveal; 2) name *why* — scale or key; 3) point from symbol to key together; 4) one hint: "It's shrunk — is the real thing bigger?"; 5) re-ask 'what does blue mean?'; 6) "smaller step."

### ⏱ 27–30 min · RECAP
> **Say:** "Tell me why a town fits on a map, and what the key is for." → *listen for:* "it's shrunk; the key explains the symbols."
**Process praise:** "You used the key like a real map-reader and understood that maps shrink the world — clever geography."
**Revisit later:** next, giving and following compass routes.

---

**Differentiation**
- **Support (gentle):** match six symbol cards to their meanings.
- **Stretch (challenge):** invent a sensible new symbol (e.g. for a school) and add it to the key.

**Extension (if time allows):** design a symbol for your own house and add it to the key.
**Assessment evidence:** child decodes symbols with the key and explains a map is shrunk.

## Lesson 3 — Following and giving compass routes
**Geography · Year 2 · Unit 1 (Mapmaker skills)** | **Duration:** 30 min | **Objective:** "By the end, I can follow and give a simple route using North, South, East and West."

**You will need:** a floor grid (tape squares), N/E/S/W signs (big N at the 'top' of the room), route cards, a hidden "treasure."
**Prior knowledge to retrieve:** the compass from Year 1; symbols/keys (Lesson 2).
**Key vocabulary:** **North, South, East, West**, **route**, **direction**, **step**.
**What to emphasize (the heart of the lesson):** the compass directions are **fixed** (North stays North wherever you face) and a **route** is a clear sequence of directions. Pre-empt "North is the way I'm facing."

---

### ⏱ 0–4 min · HOOK
> **Say:** "I've hidden a treasure in this room! Follow my directions *exactly* — North two steps, East three — or you'll walk right past it. Listen carefully, explorer!"
**Show:** the floor grid and N/E/S/W signs.  **Do:** point to the big N.
**Ask (retrieve prior knowledge):** "Which way is North in our room?" → *listen for:* the child pointing to the fixed N sign.

### ⏱ 4–10 min · EXPLAIN (I do)
> **Say:** "Our compass: **N**orth, **E**ast, **S**outh, **W**est — 'Never Eat Soggy Weetabix' going clockwise. The big N is at the top of the room and it **stays North** even when I turn around. A **route** is a list of directions: 'North 2, East 3.' Watch me follow my own route to the treasure."
**Show / Do:** walk a route on the grid calling each direction; turn around to show North stays put.
**Emphasize:** "North is **fixed** — it doesn't change when you turn. A route is a clear list of directions."

### ⏱ 10–16 min · EXAMPLE (we do)
> **Say:** "Let's send you to the treasure together — I'll call, you step: North two… East three…"
**Do:** child follows a 3–4 step compass route to the object.
**Ask:** "If you turn around, which way is North now?" → *listen for:* "still the same way (it's fixed)."

### ⏱ 16–22 min · CHECK
**Ask:** "Give me a route from the door to my desk using compass directions."
- ✅ **Expected:** a sensible compass route, e.g. "North to the window, then East to the desk" — probe: *"Is your North the same as mine?"* (yes — fixed).
- ❌ **If "North is where I'm facing":** relative-North error → **fix (KB):** "Point to our big N. Turn around — it's *still* there. North doesn't move."
- ❌ **If uses left/right only:** → **fix:** "Use the compass words — which way is the window, N or E?"

### ⏱ 22–27 min · PRACTICE (you do)
**Do:** Child writes a short route for the tutor to follow, then follows the tutor's route to a new spot. Once secure, interleave: write a four-step route *and* the return route.
**Watch for:** treating North as 'forwards'; mixing E and W.
> **If the child is stuck (missed twice):** 1) don't reveal; 2) name *why* — fixed North or direction mix; 3) stand them facing N and rehearse; 4) one hint: "N stays at the top sign"; 5) re-ask a two-step N/E route; 6) "smaller step."

### ⏱ 27–30 min · RECAP
> **Say:** "Tell me the four compass directions, and what happens to North when you turn." → *listen for:* "N, E, S, W; North stays the same."
**Process praise:** "You kept North fixed even when you turned — that's a true navigator's skill."
**Revisit later:** Unit 1 check; then we compare our home with a hot place far away.

---

**Differentiation**
- **Support (gentle):** follow two-step routes (just N and E).
- **Stretch (challenge):** write a four-step route and a return route.

**Extension (if time allows):** make a treasure map with a compass route for the tutor to follow.
**Assessment evidence:** child gives and follows a compass route and knows North is fixed.

**End-of-unit check (Unit 1):** Draw a bird's-eye plan with a key, explain that a map is shrunk, and give or follow a compass route. **Misconception watch:** a plan shows tops not fronts; symbols *stand for* real things; North is fixed, not "the way I'm facing."

## Lesson 4 — Human and physical features around me
**Geography · Year 2 · Unit 2 (Comparing places)** | **Duration:** 30 min | **Objective:** "By the end, I can sort features of a place into human-made and physical (natural)."

**You will need:** photos of the local area, two labelled sorting hoops (Human-made / Physical), an aerial photo to annotate.
**Prior knowledge to retrieve:** map and place vocabulary so far.
**Key vocabulary:** **human feature** (people made it), **physical feature** (natural, already there), **hill, river, beach, road, bridge, shop**.
**What to emphasize (the heart of the lesson):** **human features** are built by people; **physical features** are natural and were there before people. Pre-empt calling a natural thing (river) human-made.

---

### ⏱ 0–4 min · HOOK
> **Say:** "Look out of the window. Some things people *built* — and some were here long before any people arrived. Can you spot one of each?"
**Show:** the window / local photos.  **Do:** point to a building, then to a tree/hill.
**Ask (retrieve prior knowledge):** "Did people build that tree, or was it already there?" → *listen for:* "already there / nature."

### ⏱ 4–10 min · EXPLAIN (I do)
> **Say:** "Two kinds of feature. **Human features** are made by people — houses, shops, roads, bridges. **Physical features** are **natural** — hills, rivers, trees, beaches, even the weather. They were there before people came. Watch me sort: a road → human; a river → physical."
**Show / Do:** place a few photos into the two hoops, thinking aloud.
**Emphasize:** "People built it = **human**; nature made it = **physical**."

### ⏱ 10–16 min · EXAMPLE (we do)
> **Say:** "Sort these with me — a bridge? A hill? A shop? A beach?"
**Do:** child sorts four photos into the human/physical hoops.
**Ask:** "Is a river a human feature or a physical feature?" → *listen for:* "physical (natural)."

### ⏱ 16–22 min · CHECK
**Ask:** "Is a river a human feature or a physical feature — and what about a road?"
- ✅ **Expected:** river = physical (natural); road = human-made — probe: *"How do you decide?"* (did people build it?).
- ❌ **If "river is human-made":** → **fix (KB):** "Did people *make* the river, or was it flowing before anyone came? Nature made it — physical."
- ❌ **If muddles the words:** → **fix:** "Human = people made it. Physical = nature made it. Which is the road?"

### ⏱ 22–27 min · PRACTICE (you do)
**Do:** Child sorts a pile of local photos into human/physical and labels one of each on the aerial photo. Once secure, interleave: find a feature that's "a bit of both" (a reservoir) and explain.
**Watch for:** natural things called human-made; uncertainty on weather (physical).
> **If the child is stuck (missed twice):** 1) don't reveal; 2) name *why* — the made-by test; 3) ask "did people build it?" together; 4) one hint: "Was it here before people?"; 5) re-ask 'tree: human or physical?'; 6) "smaller step."

### ⏱ 27–30 min · RECAP
> **Say:** "Give me one human feature and one physical feature outside." → *listen for:* a built thing and a natural thing.
**Process praise:** "You used the 'did people build it?' test every time — that's clear geographical sorting."
**Revisit later:** next we use these words to describe a hot place far away.

---

**Differentiation**
- **Support (gentle):** a two-box picture sort with support.
- **Stretch (challenge):** find a feature that is "a bit of both" (e.g. a reservoir) and explain.

**Extension (if time allows):** draw your street and label two human and two physical features.
**Assessment evidence:** child sorts features into human-made vs physical with reasons.

## Lesson 5 — A hot place far away: locating and describing it
**Geography · Year 2 · Unit 2 (Comparing places)** | **Duration:** 35 min | **Objective:** "By the end, I can find a contrasting hot place on the globe and describe its weather and features."

> **Tutor note:** pick a contrasting hot place near the Equator you have good photos of (e.g. a village in Kenya or a Caribbean island).

**You will need:** a globe and UK map, a photo set of the chosen hot place, sticky labels.
**Prior knowledge to retrieve:** the globe, the Equator and hot/cold places (Year 1); human/physical features (Lesson 4).
**Key vocabulary:** **Equator**, **climate**, **hot**, **continent**, **human/physical features**.
**What to emphasize (the heart of the lesson):** places near the **Equator** are **hot**; locate the place on the **globe**, name its **continent**, and describe its climate and features. Link "hot" to "near the Equator."

---

### ⏱ 0–4 min · HOOK
> **Say:** "In some places it's hot enough to swim *every single day* of the year — and the children there have never seen snow! Let's pack our bags and visit one on the globe."
**Show:** the globe; the photo set.  **Do:** spin to the chosen place.
**Ask (retrieve prior knowledge):** "Where on the globe is it hottest — near the middle line or near the top and bottom?" → *listen for:* "near the middle — the Equator."

### ⏱ 4–12 min · EXPLAIN (I do)
> **Say:** "Here's our place. Watch me find it on the **globe** — it's in the continent of ___, near the **Equator**, the middle line. That's why its **climate** is **hot**. Now its features from the photos: physical — beaches, hills; human — homes, a market. The UK is much further from the Equator, so it's cooler."
**Show / Do:** locate the place and the UK on the globe; show both relative to the Equator; describe features from photos.
**Emphasize:** "Near the **Equator** → **hot**. The UK is far from it → cooler."

### ⏱ 12–20 min · EXAMPLE (we do)
> **Say:** "Find our place on the globe with me — which continent? Is it nearer the Equator than the UK?"
**Do:** child locates the place, names the continent, and compares its distance from the Equator to the UK's.
**Ask:** "Is our chosen place hotter or cooler than the UK, and how do you know from the globe?" → *listen for:* "hotter — it's much nearer the Equator."

### ⏱ 20–27 min · CHECK
**Ask:** "Is our place hotter or cooler than the UK — and how does the globe show it?"
- ✅ **Expected:** hotter; it's much nearer the Equator than the UK — probe: *"Which continent is it in?"*
- ❌ **If "cooler / can't tell":** Equator link missing → **fix (KB):** "Find the Equator. Which is closer to it — our place or the UK? Closer = hotter."
- ❌ **If can't locate it:** → **fix:** "Let's put a finger on the UK, then travel to our place — count how much closer to the middle it is."

### ⏱ 27–32 min · PRACTICE (you do)
**Do:** Child labels the place's continent and two features (one human, one physical) and says why it's hot. Once secure, interleave: "Why does being near the Equator make it hot?" (link to Year 1).
**Watch for:** missing the Equator link; mixing human/physical.
> **If the child is stuck (missed twice):** 1) don't reveal; 2) name *why* — locating or Equator link; 3) trace UK → place → Equator together; 4) one hint: "closer to the middle = hotter"; 5) re-ask 'hotter or cooler?'; 6) "smaller step."

### ⏱ 32–35 min · RECAP
> **Say:** "Tell me where our hot place is and why it's hot." → *listen for:* continent + "near the Equator."
**Process praise:** "You used the globe and the Equator to *explain* the heat — that's real geographical reasoning."
**Revisit later:** next, we compare this place with home — same and different.

---

**Differentiation**
- **Support (gentle):** find the place with a sticker already half-placed; name "hot or cold."
- **Stretch (challenge):** explain why it's hot there using the Equator (link to Year 1).

**Extension (if time allows):** draw a postcard from the hot place naming two features.
**Assessment evidence:** child locates a contrasting hot place, names its continent, and links its heat to the Equator.

## Lesson 6 — Same and different: two places, one comparison
**Geography · Year 2 · Unit 2 (Comparing places)** | **Duration:** 30 min | **Objective:** "By the end, I can give two ways my hometown and the hot place are similar and two ways they are different."

**You will need:** paired photos of both places (school, home, food, weather, clothes, animals), a two-column compare mat (Same / Different).
**Prior knowledge to retrieve:** the hot place's location and features (Lesson 5); human/physical features.
**Key vocabulary:** **compare**, **similar/same**, **different**, **climate**, **daily life**, **fair**.
**What to emphasize (the heart of the lesson):** places are **alike AND different** — children everywhere go to school, have families and play (same), while weather, houses, food and clothes differ. Crucially: **"different" does not mean "worse."**

---

### ⏱ 0–4 min · HOOK
> **Say:** "Children there go to school and play games, just like you. But what's on their plate and what's outside their window can be very different. Let's find what's the same — and what's different."
**Show:** the paired photos.  **Do:** put one 'same' pair (two schools) and one 'different' pair (snow vs sun) on the mat.
**Ask (retrieve prior knowledge):** "Is our hot place hotter or cooler than home?" → *listen for:* "hotter — near the Equator."

### ⏱ 4–10 min · EXPLAIN (I do)
> **Say:** "Let's **compare** fairly. **Same**: children go to school, have families and homes, and play. **Different**: the weather/climate, the houses, the food, the clothes, the animals. Watch — 'both go to school' → Same; 'one has snow, one has sun' → Different. And remember the fair rule: different is **not** worse, just *different* — and interesting!"
**Show / Do:** sort a few photo pairs into Same / Different, thinking aloud; state the fair rule.
**Emphasize:** "Alike *and* different — and **different never means worse**."

### ⏱ 10–16 min · EXAMPLE (we do)
> **Say:** "Sort these with me — homes, food, going to school, the weather."
**Do:** child places photo pairs into Same and Different columns.
**Ask:** "Give one way the two places are the same and one way they're different." → *listen for:* same: school/family/play; different: weather/food/houses.

### ⏱ 16–22 min · CHECK
**Ask:** "Give one way the two places are the same and one way they are different."
- ✅ **Expected:** same — both have homes/schools/families/play; different — weather, houses, food, clothes — probe: *"Does 'different' mean one is *worse*?"* (no).
- ❌ **If "their place is worse/poor/sad":** unfair judgement → **fix (KB):** "Different isn't worse — just different. What do you both *share*? School, family, play."
- ❌ **If can't find a 'same':** → **fix:** "Do children there go to school and play? Then that's a same."

### ⏱ 22–27 min · PRACTICE (you do)
**Do:** Child completes a two-each Same/Different compare with photos and states them in sentences. Once secure, interleave: "Why does the *climate* cause some differences (clothes, houses)?"
**Watch for:** value judgements ('worse'); only spotting differences.
> **If the child is stuck (missed twice):** 1) don't reveal; 2) name *why* — fairness or finding a 'same'; 3) point to a shared photo (two schools); 4) one hint: "What do both have?"; 5) re-ask 'one same?'; 6) "smaller step."

### ⏱ 27–30 min · RECAP
> **Say:** "Tell me something surprisingly *the same* about a child living far away — and the fair rule about 'different'." → *listen for:* a shared thing; "different isn't worse."
**Process praise:** "You spotted what we *share* with children far away and stayed fair-minded — that's exactly how a global citizen thinks."
**Revisit later:** Unit 2 check; KS2 geography next year goes global with latitude and longitude.

---

**Differentiation**
- **Support (gentle):** match three "same" and three "different" picture pairs.
- **Stretch (challenge):** explain why the *climate* causes some of the differences (clothes, houses).

**Extension (if time allows):** draw "same and different" — your window vs theirs.
**Assessment evidence:** child gives two similarities and two differences and states that different ≠ worse.

**End-of-unit check (Unit 2):** Sort human vs physical features; locate a contrasting hot place on the globe and describe it; give two similarities and two differences with home. **Misconception watch:** "different" doesn't mean "worse"; hot places are hot because they're near the Equator; people everywhere share much in common.

## Bridge to Year 3 (Key Stage 2)

KS2 geography begins with the **global grid** (latitude, longitude, hemispheres, time zones) to pinpoint any place, deeper **UK regions, settlements and physical/human geography**, and **rivers** as physical geography (linking to the river civilisations in history). The Year 2 skills scripted here — plan views, keys/scale, the compass, human vs physical features, and fair-minded place comparison — are the foundation.

## End-of-year mastery checks (Geography)

A Year 2 child who has mastered this year can:
1. **(Map skills)** Draw a bird's-eye plan with a key, explain that a map is shrunk, and give/follow a compass route (N/E/S/W).
2. **(Plan view)** Draw a plan of objects on a table and explain it shows the view from straight above.
3. **(Features)** Sort features of a place into human-made and physical (natural).
4. **(Locating a place)** Locate a contrasting hot place on the globe, name its continent, and describe its climate.
5. **(Comparison)** Give two similarities and two differences between a hot place and their hometown.
6. **(Fair-mindedness)** Explain that "different" doesn't mean "worse," and that people everywhere share much in common.
