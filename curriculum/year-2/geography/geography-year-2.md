> Built to [`../../PLANNING-BRIEF.md`](../../PLANNING-BRIEF.md). Drawn from the humanities knowledge

## Year overview

Year 2 geography widens the lens from "my place and a contrasting place" to **the whole planet and
the forces that shape it**. Children learn to find any place on a globe using the **equator,
hemispheres, the Prime Meridian and simple latitude and longitude**, and to explain why it is
night in Tokyo when it is morning in Lima (**time zones**). They sharpen **map skills** — the
eight compass points, map symbols and **four-figure grid references** — and carry out
**fieldwork** in their own school grounds. They then study two great physical systems: **rivers**
(from source to mouth, and why cities grow beside them — linking to the river civilisations in
History) and **mountains, volcanoes and earthquakes** (why the Earth shakes and why people still
live near volcanoes). The year ends with a study of **their own country's regions and
settlements**, written so any school in any country can use it (a worked example is given).

**What Year 2 assumes from Year 1 (ages 6–7):** naming the continents and oceans; simple maps with
symbols and a key; the four compass points; comparing their home area with a contrasting place;
basic geographical vocabulary (hill, river, beach, city, farm, port).

**What Year 3 (ages 8–9) relies on from this year:** confident use of the globe grid,
eight-point compass and four-figure grid references; the vocabulary of rivers and mountains; and
the habit of asking "Where? Why there? How is it changing?" Year 3 builds on this with the **water
cycle, climate zones and biomes**, and deeper **fieldwork** (collecting and graphing data).

**How we teach geography here:** a map or globe is out in every lesson; every unit includes
fieldwork or a hands-on model; places are chosen from every continent; and sustainability — how
people change places and how places change people — is discussed in every unit.

## Unit 1 — Our world: the globe, hemispheres and time zones

**Essential question:** How can we describe exactly where any place on Earth is — and why isn't it the same time everywhere?

**Key vocabulary:** globe, atlas, continent, ocean, equator, hemisphere, northern, southern, eastern, western, North Pole, South Pole, Prime Meridian, latitude, longitude, Tropic of Cancer, Tropic of Capricorn, Arctic Circle, Antarctic Circle, time zone

### Lesson 1 — Continents and oceans revisited
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can name and locate the seven continents and five oceans on a globe and a world map." (UK NC KS2 Geography – locational knowledge)
- **Hook:** An inflatable globe thrown around the circle — "Where is your right thumb touching?"
- **Key activity:** Play "globe catch" (name the continent or ocean under your thumb); label a blank world map; compare the globe and a flat map and notice how shapes change (Greenland looks huge on many flat maps).
- **Check for understanding:** "Why does Greenland look bigger than Africa on some flat maps, when Africa is really about 14 times bigger?" → Flattening a round Earth stretches places near the poles.
- **Differentiation:** support: labelled map to copy from / stretch: estimate what fraction of the Earth's surface is ocean (about 70%) from the globe-catch results.
- **Materials:** inflatable globes, blank world maps, atlases
- **Joy:** Globe catch — and the class tallies how often thumbs land on water.

### Lesson 2 — The equator and hemispheres
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can locate the equator and the four hemispheres and say which ones my country is in." (UK NC KS2 Geography – locational knowledge)
- **Hook:** An orange cut in half around its "equator."
- **Key activity:** Mark the equator on oranges and globes; divide into northern/southern and (using the Prime Meridian) eastern/western hemispheres; locate countries in each; find their own country.
- **Check for understanding:** "Name a country in both the southern and the western hemispheres." → e.g. Brazil, Argentina, Chile, Peru.
- **Differentiation:** support: coloured hemisphere maps / stretch: find a country that lies in all four hemispheres (e.g. Kiribati) or crossed by the equator (e.g. Kenya, Ecuador, Indonesia, Brazil).
- **Materials:** oranges, marker pens, globes, hemisphere maps
- **Joy:** Eat the "hemispheres" afterwards.

### Lesson 3 — Lines around the Earth: tropics and polar circles
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can locate the tropics and polar circles and describe the climate near each." (UK NC KS2 Geography – locational knowledge)
- **Hook:** Photos: a rainforest, a desert, a snowfield — "Which lines are they near?"
- **Key activity:** Add the Tropics of Cancer and Capricorn and the Arctic and Antarctic Circles to maps; match photos to zones (hot near the equator, cold near the poles); torch-on-globe demo showing sunlight spreading out near the poles.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why is it generally hotter near the equator?" → The Sun's rays hit it more directly, so the heat is more concentrated.
- **Differentiation:** support: colour zones on a map / stretch: explain why mountains near the equator can still have snow (height — link to Unit 4).
- **Materials:** world maps, globes, torches, photo cards
- **Joy:** Torch-and-globe experiment.

### Lesson 4 — Latitude and longitude: the Earth's grid
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can explain latitude and longitude and use them to find a place roughly." (UK NC KS2 Geography – locational knowledge)
- **Hook:** A beach ball with lines drawn on it — "How could a pilot describe exactly where they are?"
- **Key activity:** Latitude lines run east–west and measure north/south of the equator (0°–90°); longitude lines run pole to pole and measure east/west of the Prime Meridian (0°–180°); play "battleships" on a simplified world grid.
- **Check for understanding:** "Which line is 0° latitude and which is 0° longitude?" → The equator is 0° latitude; the Prime Meridian (through Greenwich, London) is 0° longitude.
- **Differentiation:** support: only north/south of the equator / stretch: find a capital city from given coordinates (e.g. roughly 0°, 78° W — Quito).
- **Materials:** beach balls, world grid maps, atlases
- **Joy:** "World battleships."

### Lesson 5 — Day and night around the world
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can explain why some places have day while others have night." (UK NC KS2 Geography – time zones; Science link)
- **Hook:** A video call (or a recorded message) from a school in another time zone.
- **Key activity:** Torch-and-globe demo; stickers on four cities; rotate the globe to see sunrise travel west-to-east across them; recap Science Unit 5.
- **Check for understanding:** "When it's noon here, why is it night on the opposite side of the world?" → That side is facing away from the Sun as the Earth spins.
- **Differentiation:** support: two cities only / stretch: explain why places further east see the sunrise first.
- **Materials:** globe, torch or lamp, stickers
- **Joy:** Connecting with a partner school.

### Lesson 6 — Time zones
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can use a time-zone map to work out the time in another city." (UK NC KS2 Geography – time zones; Maths link: time)
- **Hook:** Five clocks on the wall labelled with cities showing different times.
- **Key activity:** Learn that the world is divided into about 24 standard time zones (some countries use half-hours); calculate times in other cities using whole-hour differences; plan a call to a friend abroad.
- **Check for understanding:** "It's 10:00 here and Tokyo is 8 hours ahead. What time is it in Tokyo?" → 18:00.
- **Differentiation:** support: time-zone map with clocks drawn / stretch: find times across midnight (the date changes).
- **Materials:** time-zone map, geared clocks, city cards
- **Joy:** "World clock wall" set by the class.

### Lesson 7 — Unit check: our world
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can show I can locate places using the globe's lines and explain time zones." (Assessment)
- **Hook:** "Pilot's mission" — navigate a plane to five mystery cities.
- **Key activity:** Label continents, oceans, equator, tropics and hemispheres; locate places by clues; one time-zone calculation.
- **Check for understanding:** "Is your country north or south of the equator? East or west of the Prime Meridian? How do you know?" → Uses the globe lines correctly.
- **Differentiation:** support: word bank and partly labelled map / stretch: coordinates challenge.
- **Materials:** check sheet, atlases, globe
- **Joy:** Pilot's wings certificate.

**End-of-unit check:** Label the equator, tropics, Prime Meridian and the hemispheres on a world map, locate three countries by hemisphere, and work out the time in a city two to eight hours ahead.

## Unit 2 — Map skills and fieldwork: compass, symbols and grid references

**Essential question:** How do maps help us find our way — and how do geographers collect information outdoors?

**Key vocabulary:** map, plan, key, symbol, scale, compass, north, north-east, east, south-east, south, south-west, west, north-west, grid, grid reference, easting, northing, aerial photograph, fieldwork, survey, land use, tally

### Lesson 8 — The eight points of the compass
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can use the eight compass points to give and follow directions." (UK NC KS2 Geography – map skills)
- **Hook:** "Never Eat Shredded Wheat" — and a real compass.
- **Key activity:** Learn N, NE, E, SE, S, SW, W, NW; find north outdoors with a compass (Science link: magnets); a directions game in the playground.
- **Check for understanding:** "If you face north and turn a quarter turn clockwise, which way are you facing?" → East.
- **Differentiation:** support: 4 points first with a floor compass / stretch: give directions using 8 points and numbers of steps.
- **Materials:** compasses, chalk, floor compass mat
- **Joy:** "Compass treasure hunt."

### Lesson 9 — Symbols and keys on real maps
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can read symbols on a real map using its key." (UK NC KS2 Geography – map skills)
- **Hook:** A local street map or a national mapping agency map of the area.
- **Key activity:** Find symbols (church, school, bus stop, woodland, river); design own symbols for school features; discuss why maps use symbols.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why do maps use symbols instead of drawings of everything?" → Symbols are simple, save space and are easy to recognise with a key.
- **Differentiation:** support: map with 6 symbols / stretch: compare symbols on maps from two different countries.
- **Materials:** local maps, symbol cards
- **Joy:** Design a symbol for the school's best feature.

### Lesson 10 — Four-figure grid references
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can find a square on a map using a four-figure grid reference." (UK NC KS2 Geography – map skills; Maths link: coordinates)
- **Hook:** "Along the corridor and up the stairs!"
- **Key activity:** Learn to read eastings (along) then northings (up); find squares on a grid map; give references for features.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why do we always read along first, then up?" → So everyone finds the same square — it's an agreed rule.
- **Differentiation:** support: 2-figure references on a small grid / stretch: 4-figure references on a real map.
- **Materials:** grid maps, local map with a grid
- **Joy:** "Grid-reference bingo."

### Lesson 11 — Aerial photos and maps
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can match an aerial photograph to a map." (UK NC KS2 Geography – map skills)
- **Hook:** A satellite image of the school — "Can you find us?"
- **Key activity:** Compare the aerial image and map of the local area; identify features; draw a simple map from an aerial photo.
- **Check for understanding:** "What can an aerial photo show that a map can't, and vice versa?" → Photos show real colours and details; maps show names, symbols and hidden features clearly.
- **Differentiation:** support: matched pairs / stretch: draw a map with a key from an unfamiliar aerial photo.
- **Materials:** aerial photos, maps, tracing paper
- **Joy:** Find your own house on a satellite image (with permission).

### Lesson 12 — Scale and distance
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can explain what a scale is and measure a simple distance on a map." (UK NC KS2 Geography – map skills; Maths link)
- **Hook:** "How far is the park? The map says 1 cm = 100 m."
- **Key activity:** Measure distances on a map with a ruler; convert using the scale; compare with the real walk.
- **Check for understanding:** "If 1 cm on the map is 100 m, how far is 5 cm?" → 500 m.
- **Differentiation:** support: whole-cm distances / stretch: plan a route and estimate total distance.
- **Materials:** local maps with scale bars, rulers, string
- **Joy:** Walk the route to check.

### Lesson 13 — Fieldwork: planning a land-use survey
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can plan a fieldwork survey of land use around the school." (UK NC KS2 Geography – fieldwork)
- **Hook:** "What is our local area used for? Let's find out like real geographers."
- **Key activity:** Agree categories (homes, shops, green space, roads, services); plan a route; prepare tally sheets and a base map.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why do we agree categories before we go out?" → So everyone records in the same way and results can be compared.
- **Differentiation:** support: pre-made tally sheet / stretch: add a question (e.g. noise level).
- **Materials:** base maps, tally sheets, clipboards
- **Joy:** "Geographer" lanyards.

### Lesson 14 — Fieldwork: carrying out the survey
- **Duration:** 30 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can collect and record land-use data in the field." (UK NC KS2 Geography – fieldwork)
- **Hook:** The walk begins.
- **Key activity:** Walk the route (supervised); tally land use; colour the base map; take photos.
- **Check for understanding:** "What was the most common land use, and did it surprise you?" → Based on tallies.
- **Differentiation:** support: work in a pair with an adult / stretch: record a sketch map.
- **Materials:** clipboards, tally sheets, base maps, coloured pencils, camera
- **Joy:** Fieldwork walk.

### Lesson 15 — Fieldwork: presenting results and unit check
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can present fieldwork results in a map and chart and draw a conclusion." (Assessment)
- **Hook:** "Report to the town council."
- **Key activity:** Make a bar chart of land use (Maths link); write two conclusions and one suggestion to improve the area; map-skills check (compass, symbols, grid references).
- **Check for understanding:** "What does your chart show, and what would you change in our local area?" → A data-based conclusion and a suggestion (e.g. more green space).
- **Differentiation:** support: chart template / stretch: compare with an aerial photo from years ago.
- **Materials:** squared paper, results
- **Joy:** Presentation to the head teacher as "council."

**End-of-unit check:** Give directions using the eight compass points, find three features using four-figure grid references, and present one conclusion from the land-use survey with a chart.

## Unit 3 — Rivers of the world

**Essential question:** How does a river change on its journey from mountain to sea — and why have people always lived beside rivers?

**Key vocabulary:** source, mouth, tributary, confluence, channel, bank, meander, waterfall, estuary, delta, erosion, deposition, flood, floodplain, drainage basin, upstream, downstream, dam

### Lesson 16 — What is a river?
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can describe a river's journey from source to mouth." (UK NC KS2 Geography – rivers)
- **Hook:** Pour water on a crumpled-paper "mountain" coloured with felt pens — watch it flow.
- **Key activity:** Observe where water flows and joins; label source, tributary, confluence, mouth; sequence a river's journey.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why do rivers always flow downhill to the sea?" → Gravity pulls water down, from high land to low land.
- **Differentiation:** support: labelled diagram / stretch: explain what a drainage basin is.
- **Materials:** paper, felt pens, spray bottles, trays
- **Joy:** Crumpled-paper river model.

### Lesson 17 — Upper, middle and lower course
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can describe how a river changes along its course." (UK NC KS2 Geography – rivers)
- **Hook:** Photos: a fast mountain stream, a meandering river, a wide estuary.
- **Key activity:** Sort photos into upper, middle, lower course; describe width, speed and features (waterfalls, meanders, deltas).
- **Check for understanding:** "Why is a river usually wider near its mouth?" → More water from tributaries has joined, and the land is flatter.
- **Differentiation:** support: picture sort with labels / stretch: explain how a waterfall forms (hard rock over soft rock).
- **Materials:** photo cards, a river profile diagram
- **Joy:** "River journey" drama — move like the river in each course.

### Lesson 18 — Erosion and deposition (stream-tray model)
- **Duration:** 30 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can explain erosion and deposition using a model." (UK NC KS2 Geography – rivers)
- **Hook:** A stream tray with sand and a gentle hose/jug.
- **Key activity:** Pour water; watch the river carve a channel (erosion) and drop sand (deposition) at bends and the "sea"; draw before and after.
- **Check for understanding:** "Where did the sand get dropped, and why?" → Where the water slowed down — on inside bends and at the mouth.
- **Differentiation:** support: guided observation sheet / stretch: change the slope and predict the difference.
- **Materials:** stream tray, sand, jug, water, towels
- **Joy:** The stream tray.

### Lesson 19 — Great rivers of the world
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can locate and compare major rivers on different continents." (UK NC KS2 Geography – locational knowledge)
- **Hook:** "The Nile and the Amazon: which is longer? Scientists argue!"
- **Key activity:** Locate the Nile, Amazon, Yangtze, Mississippi, Ganges, Danube and Murray on a world map; compare length, countries crossed and uses; note the Amazon carries the most water.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why might scientists disagree about which river is longest?" → It depends on where you decide the source and mouth are, and how you measure.
- **Differentiation:** support: fact cards for three rivers / stretch: bar chart comparing lengths.
- **Materials:** world map, river fact cards, string for measuring
- **Joy:** "River Top Trumps."

### Lesson 20 — Why do people live near rivers?
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can explain why settlements grow near rivers." (UK NC KS2 Geography – settlements; History link)
- **Hook:** Map of the world's big cities — "What do most of them have in common?"
- **Key activity:** Find cities on rivers (Cairo, London, Baghdad, Shanghai, Kolkata, New Orleans); list reasons (water, food, transport, trade, power, defence); link to Egypt, Sumer, Indus and Shang in History.
- **Check for understanding:** "Give two reasons why ancient Egypt and modern Cairo both grew beside the Nile." → Water for farming and drinking; transport and trade.
- **Differentiation:** support: reason cards / stretch: explain a disadvantage (floods).
- **Materials:** world city map, reason cards
- **Joy:** "City founder" game — choose the best place for a new settlement.

### Lesson 21 — Floods: living with rivers
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can explain why rivers flood and how people protect themselves." (UK NC KS2 Geography – rivers; sustainability)
- **Hook:** Photos of flooding in Bangladesh and houses on stilts.
- **Key activity:** Model floods in a tray (heavy rain, concrete vs grass); discuss flood defences (levees, planting trees, stilt houses) and why floods can also help farmland (silt).
- **Check for understanding:** "Why does paving over fields make floods worse?" → Water can't soak into concrete, so it runs quickly into rivers.
- **Differentiation:** support: picture sequence / stretch: evaluate one flood defence.
- **Materials:** trays, sponges (grass), plastic (concrete), watering can
- **Joy:** Design a flood-proof house.

### Lesson 22 — Rivers and sustainability
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can describe how people pollute and protect rivers." (UK NC KS2 Geography – human impact; Life skills link)
- **Hook:** A jar of "polluted river" water (with safe additives).
- **Key activity:** Discuss plastic, sewage and farm chemicals; how rivers carry pollution to the sea; case study of a river clean-up; make a pledge.
- **Check for understanding:** "How can plastic dropped in a town end up in the ocean?" → It washes into drains and streams that flow into rivers and then the sea.
- **Differentiation:** support: cause–effect picture cards / stretch: write a letter to a local council about a river.
- **Materials:** jars, safe "pollutants" (oil, glitter, paper), filters
- **Joy:** Filter challenge — clean the water with a home-made filter.

### Lesson 23 — Unit check: rivers
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can explain a river's features and why people live near rivers." (Assessment)
- **Hook:** "River explorer's log."
- **Key activity:** Label a river diagram; explain erosion/deposition; locate three world rivers; give reasons for river settlements.
- **Check for understanding:** "What is a meander, and why does sand build up on its inside bend?" → A bend in a river; water flows slower on the inside, so sand is deposited.
- **Differentiation:** support: word bank / stretch: extended explanation.
- **Materials:** check sheet, world map
- **Joy:** "River explorer" badge.

**End-of-unit check:** Label a river from source to mouth with five features, explain erosion and deposition, and give two reasons why cities grow beside rivers.

## Unit 4 — Mountains, volcanoes and earthquakes

**Essential question:** Why does the ground shake and erupt — and why do people still choose to live in dangerous places?

**Key vocabulary:** mountain, peak, range, summit, crust, mantle, core, tectonic plate, fault, volcano, magma, lava, crater, erupt, active, dormant, extinct, earthquake, epicentre, tsunami, Ring of Fire

### Lesson 24 — Mountains of the world
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can locate major mountain ranges and describe mountain features." (UK NC KS2 Geography – mountains)
- **Hook:** Photos of Everest, Kilimanjaro, the Andes and the Alps — "Which is highest?"
- **Key activity:** Locate the Himalayas, Andes, Rockies, Alps, Atlas and Kilimanjaro; learn summit, ridge, slope, valley; Everest is about 8,849 m (link: place value).
- **Check for understanding:** "Why is there snow on Kilimanjaro even though it's near the equator?" → It is very high, and air gets colder the higher you go.
- **Differentiation:** support: labelled map / stretch: compare heights in a bar chart.
- **Materials:** world physical map, photos, height cards
- **Joy:** Build a salt-dough mountain range.

### Lesson 25 — Inside the Earth and tectonic plates
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can describe the Earth's layers and explain that the crust is broken into plates." (UK NC KS2 Geography – physical processes; Science link)
- **Hook:** A peach (or hard-boiled egg) cut in half — crust, mantle, core.
- **Key activity:** Label the Earth's layers; a puzzle of plate pieces on a globe; show plates moving slowly (a few cm a year — about as fast as fingernails grow).
- **Check for understanding:** "What happens where two plates push together?" → The land can crumple up into mountains, and earthquakes can happen.
- **Differentiation:** support: labelled layer diagram / stretch: explain how the Himalayas formed (India pushing into Asia).
- **Materials:** peaches or eggs, plate map, jigsaw pieces
- **Joy:** "Plate puzzle" globe.

### Lesson 26 — Volcanoes
- **Duration:** 30 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can explain how volcanoes form and erupt and label a volcano." (UK NC KS2 Geography – volcanoes)
- **Hook:** A baking-soda-and-vinegar volcano eruption (outdoors).
- **Key activity:** Label magma chamber, vent, crater, lava; sort active, dormant, extinct; locate the Ring of Fire around the Pacific.
- **Check for understanding:** "What is the difference between magma and lava?" → Magma is melted rock underground; lava is magma that has come out onto the surface.
- **Differentiation:** support: labelled diagram / stretch: explain why so many volcanoes are around the Pacific.
- **Materials:** model volcano, baking soda, vinegar, food colouring, maps
- **Joy:** The eruption!

### Lesson 27 — Earthquakes and tsunamis
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can explain what causes earthquakes and how people stay safe." (UK NC KS2 Geography – earthquakes; PE & Health link)
- **Hook:** A jelly "town" on a tray that gets shaken.
- **Key activity:** Plates sliding and catching cause earthquakes; tsunamis form from undersea earthquakes; practise "drop, cover, hold on"; test sugar-cube buildings on jelly.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why should you get under a table in an earthquake?" → To protect yourself from falling objects.
- **Differentiation:** support: picture sequence / stretch: explain why some buildings survive earthquakes (design).
- **Materials:** jelly, tray, sugar cubes, toothpicks
- **Joy:** Earthquake-proof building challenge.

### Lesson 28 — Living near a volcano: Iceland, Japan and Italy
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can explain why people live near volcanoes despite the danger." (UK NC KS2 Geography – human and physical)
- **Hook:** A photo of people swimming in a geothermal pool in Iceland.
- **Key activity:** Case studies: Iceland (geothermal energy, heating), Japan (hot springs, careful building, warning systems), Italy near Vesuvius (fertile soil, farming, tourism); sort benefits and risks.
- **Check for understanding:** "Give one benefit and one risk of living near a volcano." → Benefit: fertile soil or geothermal energy; risk: eruptions.
- **Differentiation:** support: benefit/risk cards / stretch: decide and justify whether you would live there.
- **Materials:** case study cards, photos, world map
- **Joy:** "Would you live there?" debate.

### Lesson 29 — Ready for disaster: helping communities
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can describe how communities prepare for and recover from natural hazards." (UK NC KS2 Geography – human geography; Life skills link)
- **Hook:** An emergency grab-bag — "What would you pack?"
- **Key activity:** Discuss warnings, drills and aid; pack a grab-bag with priorities; learn how people help after disasters.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why do countries like Japan practise earthquake drills often?" → So people know what to do instantly and stay safe.
- **Differentiation:** support: picture-choice grab-bag / stretch: design a warning poster for a town.
- **Materials:** grab-bag items (torch, water, first-aid kit), picture cards
- **Joy:** Grab-bag packing challenge.

### Lesson 30 — Unit check: mountains, volcanoes and earthquakes
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can explain how mountains, volcanoes and earthquakes form and affect people." (Assessment)
- **Hook:** "Volcano expert."
- **Key activity:** Label a volcano and Earth's layers; locate the Ring of Fire; explain one benefit and one risk; safety question.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why are earthquakes and volcanoes common in the same places?" → Both happen mostly at the edges of tectonic plates.
- **Differentiation:** support: word bank / stretch: extended answer.
- **Materials:** check sheet, maps
- **Joy:** Expert badge.

**End-of-unit check:** Label a volcano and the Earth's layers, mark the Ring of Fire, and explain why people live near volcanoes despite the risks.

## Unit 5 — My country: regions, settlements and land use

**Essential question:** What makes my country's regions different — and how do people use the land?

**Key vocabulary:** country, region, capital, city, town, village, hamlet, settlement, population, rural, urban, land use, farming, industry, physical feature, human feature

> **Written for any country.** The teacher uses their own country. Worked example given for the UK; the same lessons work for, say, Kenya (counties, Nairobi, the Rift Valley, Lake Victoria) or Mexico (states, Mexico City, the Sierra Madre, the Yucatán).

### Lesson 31 — Regions of my country
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can name and locate the main regions and cities of my country." (UK NC KS2 Geography – locational knowledge)
- **Hook:** A jigsaw map of the country.
- **Key activity:** Assemble the jigsaw; label regions, the capital and three big cities (UK example: England, Scotland, Wales, Northern Ireland; London, Edinburgh, Cardiff, Belfast); find where we live.
- **Check for understanding:** "What is the capital of our country, and why might capitals often be large cities?" → Names it; government and businesses gather there, attracting people.
- **Differentiation:** support: labelled map / stretch: locate regions using grid references.
- **Materials:** country jigsaw map, atlases, blank maps
- **Joy:** Jigsaw race.

### Lesson 32 — Physical and human features of my country
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can identify key physical and human features of my country on a map." (UK NC KS2 Geography – human and physical)
- **Hook:** Photos of the country's famous places — "Natural or made by people?"
- **Key activity:** Sort photos (UK example: Ben Nevis, the River Thames, Lake District vs the Houses of Parliament, ports, motorways); mark on a map using symbols.
- **Check for understanding:** "Is a reservoir a physical or human feature? Explain." → Human — it's made by people, even though it holds natural water.
- **Differentiation:** support: picture sort / stretch: explain how one physical feature affects where people live.
- **Materials:** photos, maps, symbol stickers
- **Joy:** "Feature bingo."

### Lesson 33 — Settlements: hamlet to city
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can order settlements by size and explain why they grew where they did." (UK NC KS2 Geography – settlements)
- **Hook:** "Our school's town: why is it here?"
- **Key activity:** Order hamlet, village, town, city by size and services; investigate why our settlement grew (river crossing, hill, coast, crossroads, resources) using an old map.
- **Check for understanding:** "Give one reason why our town grew where it is." → A reason based on the map (e.g. river crossing).
- **Differentiation:** support: settlement hierarchy picture cards / stretch: compare an old and a modern map to find changes.
- **Materials:** settlement cards, old and modern local maps
- **Joy:** "Settlement builder" game.

### Lesson 34 — Land use: urban and rural
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can compare land use in urban and rural areas of my country." (UK NC KS2 Geography – land use)
- **Hook:** Two photos: a city centre and farmland.
- **Key activity:** Compare land use (housing, industry, farming, parks) in urban and rural areas; link to the fieldwork survey; discuss how land use is changing.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why is farming more common in rural areas?" → There is more open land and fewer buildings.
- **Differentiation:** support: sort pictures / stretch: argue whether a field should be turned into houses.
- **Materials:** photos, maps, fieldwork results
- **Joy:** "Town planner" debate.

### Lesson 35 — Year review: geography showcase
- **Duration:** 30 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can use this year's map skills and knowledge to present a place." (Assessment)
- **Hook:** "Geography fair."
- **Key activity:** Pairs present a place they've learned about (a river city, a volcano town, a region of our country) with a map, location using the globe's lines, and one human and one physical feature.
- **Check for understanding:** "Describe exactly where your place is using at least two of: continent, hemisphere, compass direction, grid reference." → Uses them correctly.
- **Differentiation:** support: poster frame / stretch: include time difference and a sustainability issue.
- **Materials:** poster paper, atlases, globes
- **Joy:** Geography fair.

**End-of-unit check:** Locate your country's capital and regions, explain why your own settlement grew where it did, and compare urban and rural land use.

## End-of-year mastery checks

1. **Globe knowledge** — locates continents, oceans, the equator, tropics, polar circles, Prime Meridian and hemispheres. *Seen:* labelled world map.
2. **Latitude, longitude and time zones** — explains the grid and works out a time difference in whole hours. *Seen:* pilot's mission and world clocks.
3. **Map skills** — uses eight compass points, map symbols and keys, four-figure grid references and a simple scale. *Seen:* map-skills check.
4. **Fieldwork** — plans, collects, records and presents fieldwork data with a conclusion. *Seen:* land-use survey.
5. **Rivers** — describes a river from source to mouth, explains erosion and deposition, and why people settle by rivers. *Seen:* stream tray and river check.
6. **Mountains, volcanoes, earthquakes** — explains how they form, where they happen (plate edges) and how people live with the risks. *Seen:* Unit 4 check.
7. **My country** — names regions, capital and major cities and explains why the local settlement grew where it did. *Seen:* Unit 5 work.
8. **Sustainability** — gives an example of people harming and protecting a place (e.g. river pollution, flood defence). *Seen:* river pledge and discussions.

## Teacher guidance

**Common misconceptions (and the fix)**
- *"North is always up on the page / north means 'up the hill'."* North is a direction on Earth; use a compass outdoors.
- *"Rivers flow from north to south."* Rivers flow downhill, in any direction (the Nile flows north).
- *"The equator is a real line / it's hot everywhere on the equator."* It's an imaginary line; high places on the equator can be cold (Kilimanjaro, the Andes of Ecuador).
- *"Flat maps show true sizes."* Compare the globe and map (Greenland vs Africa).
- *"Volcanoes are everywhere / earthquakes happen randomly."* Most occur along plate boundaries.
- *"Grid references: up first."* Always along the corridor, then up the stairs.
- *"All of Africa is hot and dry."* Use the rich variety of climates, cities and landscapes on every continent.

**Safety.** Fieldwork needs a risk assessment, adult ratios and parental consent; the volcano model is done outdoors with eye protection; handle food allergies for the orange/peach and jelly activities.

**Local adaptation.** Units 2 and 5 are built around your own school and country; swap the UK worked example for your own. Choose river and volcano case studies from your region where possible.

**Vertical alignment.** Year 1 introduced continents, oceans, simple maps and comparing places. Year 3 (ages 8–9) builds on this year's globe grid, map skills and rivers with the water cycle, climate zones and biomes, and more data-heavy fieldwork.
