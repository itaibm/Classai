# Year 2 Music — Tutor Scripts (Lessons 1–15)

Complete tutor scripts for [Year 2 Music](../music-year-2.md), built to the [scripting guide](../../../SCRIPTING-GUIDE.md) and grounded in `knowledge-base/subjects/art-music/music-ks1-ks2.md`. Music is active: every lesson runs **listen/imitate → practise → perform** and cycles **Listen → Perform → Compose**. The CHECK is often an *audible success criterion* plus a question. Each beat ⏱ sums to the duration; the "if the child is stuck" support steps back to body/voice and a smaller pattern. No instruments are required (voice, body and household sounds work); percussion makes it richer. Use the named listening pieces for the year-long appraising thread.

---

## Lesson 1 — Welcome back: pulse and rhythm together
**Music · Year 2 · Unit 1 (Pulse & rhythm)** | **Duration:** 25 min | **Objective:** "By the end, I can keep a steady pulse with my feet and clap a word-rhythm on top."

**You will need:** a steady-tempo recording, optional claves, space to tap feet.
**Prior knowledge to retrieve:** Year 1 — feet = the steady beat (pulse), hands = the words (rhythm).
**Key vocabulary:** **pulse** (steady heartbeat), **rhythm** (changing long-short pattern), **beat**.
**What to emphasize (the heart of the lesson):** **pulse is steady; rhythm changes** — and you can do **both at once** (feet pulse, hands rhythm). Pre-empt clapping the pulse instead of the word-rhythm.

---

### ⏱ 0–3 min · HOOK
> **Say:** "Last year your feet learned the heartbeat of music and your hands learned the words on top. Bet you can still do both at once — let's prove it!"
**Show/Do:** start tapping a steady foot pulse to the recording; invite the child to join.
**Ask (retrieve prior knowledge):** "Which is steady — pulse or rhythm?" → *listen for:* "pulse."

### ⏱ 3–8 min · EXPLAIN (I do)
> **Say:** "Watch: my **feet** keep the **pulse** — steady, never changing — and my **hands** clap the **rhythm** of the words: 'I-like-piz-za' has its own long-short pattern. Pulse underneath, rhythm on top. Two jobs at once!"
**Show/Do:** demo feet pulse + clap the rhythm of "I like pizza."
**Emphasize:** "Feet = steady **pulse**; hands = the **rhythm** of the words."

### ⏱ 8–13 min · EXAMPLE (we do)
> **Say:** "Feet with me first — steady. Now add the claps for 'ba-na-na bread' on top."
**Do:** child keeps foot pulse and claps a word-rhythm.
**Ask:** "Which is the steady one, and which changes?" → *listen for:* "pulse steady; rhythm changes (long-short)."

### ⏱ 13–17 min · CHECK (audible + question)
**Ask:** "Keep your feet on the pulse and clap 'I like pizza' — then tell me which was steady."
- ✅ **Listen for / Expected:** steady feet + a word-rhythm on top; "pulse was steady." — probe: *"Did your feet ever change speed?"* (no).
- ❌ **If claps the pulse, not the rhythm:** → **fix:** "Clap the *words* — say them and clap each part: I-like-piz-za."
- ❌ **If feet drift/stop:** → **fix:** "Feet keep marching no matter what your hands do."

### ⏱ 17–22 min · PRACTICE (you do)
**Do:** Child keeps the pulse through a whole nursery rhyme while clapping the word-rhythm; then swap leader with the tutor (one takes the pulse, one takes the rhythm). Once secure, interleave a new phrase.
**Watch for:** hands and feet collapsing into the same thing.
> **If the child is stuck:** 1) don't do both for them; 2) name *why* — pulse/rhythm merged; 3) feet only first, then add hands; 4) prompt: "say the words as you clap"; 5) rebuild both; 6) "Two jobs — feet steady, hands on the words."

### ⏱ 22–25 min · RECAP
> **Say:** "Tell me the difference between pulse and rhythm." → *listen for:* "pulse steady; rhythm is the long-short word pattern."
**Process praise:** "You held a steady pulse *and* clapped the words — two things at once, like a real musician."
**Revisit later:** tomorrow we give those long and short sounds their names.

---

**Differentiation** — support: feet first, then add hands. / stretch: keep both through a whole nursery rhyme.
**Extension / if the child finishes early:** lead the tutor in a two-jobs round.
**Assessment evidence:** child sustains a steady pulse while clapping a word-rhythm and explains the difference.

## Lesson 2 — Ta and ti-ti (naming the notes)
**Music · Year 2 · Unit 1 (Pulse & rhythm)** | **Duration:** 30 min | **Objective:** "By the end, I can say and clap 'ta' for a long sound and 'ti-ti' for two quick sounds."

**You will need:** food picture cards (cat, tiger, pizza, banana), optional claves.
**Prior knowledge to retrieve:** pulse vs rhythm (L1).
**Key vocabulary:** **ta** (one beat / crotchet), **ti-ti** (two quick / quavers), **beat**.
**What to emphasize (the heart of the lesson):** one beat = "**ta**"; two quick sounds squeezed into one beat = "**ti-ti**." Keep the pulse steady underneath. Pre-empt making ti-ti take two beats.

---

### ⏱ 0–3 min · HOOK
> **Say:** "Musicians have secret nicknames for sounds. One beat is '**ta**'. Two quick sounds squished into one beat are '**ti-ti**'. Say them with me — ta… ti-ti!"
**Show/Do:** clap a slow 'ta' and a quick 'ti-ti'.
**Ask (retrieve prior knowledge):** "Feet keep the…?" → *listen for:* "pulse."

### ⏱ 3–9 min · EXPLAIN (I do)
> **Say:** "Watch and listen. **Ta** is one clap on one beat — 'cat' = ta. **Ti-ti** is two quick claps in the *same* one beat — 'ti-ger' = ti-ti, 'piz-za' = ti-ti. Both fit in one beat — ti-ti is just squeezed in. Feet keep the steady pulse the whole time."
**Show/Do:** map food cards to ta / ti-ti; clap "ta ti-ti ta ta" over a foot pulse.
**Emphasize:** "**Ta = one beat; ti-ti = two squeezed into one beat.**"

### ⏱ 9–15 min · EXAMPLE (we do)
> **Say:** "Sort these foods with me — 'cat' is…? 'tiger' is…? Now clap them over your pulse."
**Do:** child labels foods ta/ti-ti and claps a 4-beat string.
**Ask:** "Clap and say 'ta ti-ti ta ta'." → *listen for/watch:* one, two-quick, one, one — in time.

### ⏱ 15–20 min · CHECK (audible + question)
**Ask:** "Clap and say 'ta ti-ti ta ta', keeping your feet on the pulse."
- ✅ **Listen for / Expected:** correct long/two-quick/long/long in time. — probe: *"How many sounds in ti-ti, and how many beats?"* (two sounds, one beat).
- ❌ **If ti-ti takes two beats:** stretched it → **fix:** "Squeeze both into *one* beat — ti-ti, quick-quick, then move on."
- ❌ **If loses the pulse:** → **fix:** "Feet steady — clap the names on top."

### ⏱ 20–26 min · PRACTICE (you do)
**Do:** Child claps several 4-beat ta/ti-ti strings from food cards over a steady pulse. Once secure, interleave: invent a 4-beat ta/ti-ti pattern and say it.
**Watch for:** ti-ti stretched; pulse lost.
> **If the child is stuck:** 1) don't clap it for them; 2) name *why* — ti-ti timing; 3) just ta, then just ti-ti; 4) prompt: "two quick in one beat"; 5) rebuild the string; 6) "Ta long, ti-ti quick-quick — you've got it."

### ⏱ 26–30 min · RECAP
> **Say:** "What's 'ta' and what's 'ti-ti'?" → *listen for:* "ta = one beat; ti-ti = two quick in one beat."
**Process praise:** "You squeezed ti-ti neatly into one beat — that's real rhythm reading."
**Revisit later:** next, a beat with *no* sound — the rest.

---

**Differentiation** — support: clap just ta then just ti-ti. / stretch: invent a 4-beat ta/ti-ti pattern and say it.
**Extension / if the child finishes early:** turn your name into ta/ti-ti.
**Assessment evidence:** child says and claps ta and ti-ti correctly over a steady pulse.

## Lesson 3 — The silent beat (rest)
**Music · Year 2 · Unit 1 (Pulse & rhythm)** | **Duration:** 25 min | **Objective:** "By the end, I can leave a silent beat (a rest) in a rhythm and still keep the pulse."

**You will need:** none (body percussion).
**Prior knowledge to retrieve:** ta and ti-ti (L2); steady pulse.
**Key vocabulary:** **rest** (a silent beat), **pulse**, **shh**.
**What to emphasize (the heart of the lesson):** on a **rest** you make **no sound — but the pulse keeps going**. Pre-empt stopping the pulse during the rest.

---

### ⏱ 0–3 min · HOOK
> **Say:** "What if the music suddenly goes *shh* — a beat where you make no sound at all, but the steady pulse keeps marching underneath? That silent beat is called a **rest**."
**Show/Do:** clap "ta, ta, ta, (shh)" with open hands on the rest.
**Ask (retrieve prior knowledge):** "What's a quick two-sound beat called?" → *listen for:* "ti-ti."

### ⏱ 3–8 min · EXPLAIN (I do)
> **Say:** "A **rest** is a silent beat. I show it with quiet open hands — 'shh'. But — important — my feet keep the pulse going right through the silence. Watch: ta, ta, ta, *shh* — the silence still takes one beat."
**Show/Do:** demo clap-clap-clap-shh, feet steady through the rest.
**Emphasize:** "On a rest: **no sound, but the pulse keeps going.**"

### ⏱ 8–13 min · EXAMPLE (we do)
> **Say:** "Do clap–clap–clap–shh with me — keep your feet marching on the shh."
**Do:** child performs the pattern with a rest, feet steady.
**Ask:** "What happens on a rest?" → *listen for:* "you make no sound but the beat keeps going."

### ⏱ 13–17 min · CHECK (audible + question)
**Ask:** "Clap 'ta ta shh ta' with your feet on the pulse — what did you do on the shh?"
- ✅ **Listen for / Expected:** silence on the rest, pulse unbroken; "no sound, beat keeps going." — probe: *"Did your feet stop?"* (no).
- ❌ **If stops/pauses everything:** froze the pulse → **fix:** "Hands rest, but feet march on — the silence still counts a beat."
- ❌ **If fills the rest with a clap:** → **fix:** "Open your hands and stay quiet for that beat — shh."

### ⏱ 17–22 min · PRACTICE (you do)
**Do:** Child plays patterns mixing ta/ti-ti with a rest (rest at the end, then in the middle), feet steady. Once secure, interleave: two rests in one 4-beat line.
**Watch for:** pulse stopping on the rest; rest skipped.
> **If the child is stuck:** 1) don't do it for them; 2) name *why* — pulse stops; 3) march feet only through a 'shh'; 4) prompt: "feet keep going"; 5) add hands; 6) "Silent hands, marching feet."

### ⏱ 22–25 min · RECAP
> **Say:** "On a rest, what do your hands do — and your feet?" → *listen for:* "hands silent; feet keep the pulse."
**Process praise:** "You kept the pulse marching right through the silence — that's tricky and you nailed it."
**Revisit later:** tomorrow we *write down* ta, ti-ti and rests.

---

**Differentiation** — support: one rest at the end of a pattern. / stretch: a rest in the middle of a pattern.
**Extension / if the child finishes early:** compose a 4-beat pattern with a surprise rest.
**Assessment evidence:** child performs a rest while keeping the pulse and explains it.

## Lesson 4 — Draw the rhythm (graphic notation)
**Music · Year 2 · Unit 1 (Pulse & rhythm)** | **Duration:** 30 min | **Objective:** "By the end, I can write a 4-beat rhythm with my own symbols and clap someone else's."

**You will need:** paper and pens, a symbol key (dash = ta, two joined dots = ti-ti, box = rest).
**Prior knowledge to retrieve:** ta, ti-ti, rest (L2–3).
**Key vocabulary:** **notation**, **symbol**, **read**, **write**, **rhythm**.
**What to emphasize (the heart of the lesson):** **notation** lets you **write music down so it can be played again the same way**. (First taste of notation → Year 3.)

---

### ⏱ 0–3 min · HOOK
> **Say:** "Here's a problem. You make up a brilliant rhythm today — how could a friend play it *tomorrow* if you didn't tell them? You'd have to **write it down**! That's what musicians do."
**Show/Do:** show the symbol key.
**Ask (retrieve prior knowledge):** "Name our three rhythm sounds." → *listen for:* "ta, ti-ti, rest."

### ⏱ 3–9 min · EXPLAIN (I do)
> **Say:** "We'll invent **symbols**: a long **dash** = ta, two **joined dots** = ti-ti, a blank **box** = rest. Watch me write a 4-beat line — dash, dots, dash, box — then clap exactly what I wrote. The writing *is* the music."
**Show/Do:** write a 4-beat line and clap it precisely.
**Emphasize:** "**Notation = writing music down** so it's played the same again."

### ⏱ 9–15 min · EXAMPLE (we do)
> **Say:** "Read my line with me — clap what each symbol says."
**Do:** child claps a written symbol line accurately.
**Ask:** "What is notation for?" → *listen for:* "to write music down so it can be played the same again."

### ⏱ 15–20 min · CHECK (audible + question)
**Ask:** "Clap this written line, then tell me why we write rhythms down."
- ✅ **Listen for / Expected:** claps the symbols correctly; "so it can be played again the same." — probe: *"Which symbol is the rest?"*
- ❌ **If ignores the symbols / claps freely:** → **fix:** "Read left to right — dash = ta, dots = ti-ti, box = silent."
- ❌ **If muddles ti-ti/ta symbols:** → **fix:** "Two dots joined = two quick sounds = ti-ti."

### ⏱ 20–27 min · PRACTICE (you do — compose → read)
**Do:** Child writes their own 4-beat line, swaps with the tutor, and they clap each other's exactly. Once secure, interleave: write an 8-beat line with a rest.
**Watch for:** symbols that don't match the clap; skipping the rest box.
> **If the child is stuck:** 1) don't write it for them; 2) name *why* — symbol/sound link; 3) match one symbol to one sound together; 4) prompt: "what does a dash say?"; 5) build the line; 6) "Your symbols, your music — write what you hear."

### ⏱ 27–30 min · RECAP
> **Say:** "Why is it useful to write music down?" → *listen for:* "to play it again the same way."
**Process praise:** "You wrote a rhythm a friend could read and play — that's composing *and* notating."
**Revisit later:** Unit 1 check; proper notation grows in Year 3.

---

**Differentiation** — support: copy and clap a given symbol line. / stretch: write an 8-beat line with a rest.
**Extension / if the child finishes early:** notate a favourite phrase ("happy birthday to you").
**Assessment evidence:** child writes and reads a 4-beat rhythm in graphic notation.

**Listening thread (Unit 1):** **"Mango Walk"** (Caribbean folk) — clap the ti-ti-rich word-rhythm, then *notice → feel → judge*: name the bouncy pulse and say whether it makes you want to move, and why.

> **End-of-unit 1 check:** Child claps a 4-beat pattern mixing ta, ti-ti and a rest while keeping the pulse, and writes/reads a short rhythm in graphic notation.

## Lesson 5 — Warm up and match the note
**Music · Year 2 · Unit 2 (Pitch & singing voice)** | **Duration:** 25 min | **Objective:** "By the end, I can warm up my voice and sing back a single note in tune."
**You will need:** a chime bar (or app) for a clear starting note.
**Prior knowledge to retrieve:** high/low pitch from Year 1.
**Key vocabulary:** **pitch**, **in tune**, **match**, **warm-up**, **siren**.
**What to emphasize:** good posture and breath, then **slide your voice until it matches** the note exactly. Pre-empt shouting instead of tuning.
---
### ⏱ 0–3 min · HOOK
> **Say:** "Singers tune their voices like a guitar gets tuned. Can your voice find the *exact* note I sing? Let's warm up and try."
**Do:** stand tall, take a breath, do a gentle 'siren' up and down.
**Ask (retrieve prior knowledge):** "Show me a high sound and a low sound with your voice." → *listen for/watch:* a clear high and low.
### ⏱ 3–8 min · EXPLAIN (I do)
> **Say:** "First, posture — tall, relaxed — and a breath. Sirens up and down warm the voice. Now **pitch-matching**: I sing a note, and you *slide* your voice up or down until it locks onto mine. Not louder — just the same *height*. I'll show my hand at the note's height."
**Do:** model a siren, then sing a mid note and match it, hand showing the height.
**Emphasize:** "**Slide to match the height** — tuning, not shouting."
### ⏱ 8–13 min · EXAMPLE (we do)
> **Say:** "Slide your voice to my note with me — up a bit… there, locked on!"
**Do:** child slides to match a mid note.
**Ask:** "Sing back this note." → *listen for:* a reasonably accurate pitch match.
### ⏱ 13–17 min · CHECK (audible + question)
**Ask:** "Sing back the note I play — and is matching about being *louder* or the same *height*?"
- ✅ **Listen for / Expected:** a close pitch match; "same height/pitch." — probe: *"Slide up or down to lock on?"*
- ❌ **If shouts:** loud ≠ in tune → **fix:** "Softer — slide the *height* of your voice to meet mine."
- ❌ **If far off:** → **fix:** "Start on my note (hum it), then sing it back."
### ⏱ 17–22 min · PRACTICE (you do)
**Do:** Child matches three different notes, using a hand at the matched height. Once secure, interleave a higher note.
**Watch for:** shouting; not adjusting toward the note.
> **If the child is stuck:** 1) don't sing it for them as 'the answer'; 2) name *why* — sliding/listening; 3) hum the note together first; 4) prompt: "higher or lower to match?"; 5) match one comfortable note; 6) "Listen, then slide to lock on."
### ⏱ 22–25 min · RECAP
> **Say:** "What does it mean to sing 'in tune'?" → *listen for:* "your voice matches the note's height."
**Process praise:** "You listened and slid your voice right onto the note — that's true pitch-matching."
**Revisit later:** next, how tunes *move* — steps and leaps.
---
**Differentiation** — support: match one comfortable mid note. / stretch: match three notes including a high one.
**Extension / if the child finishes early:** match a note then sing it back a beat later (memory).
**Assessment evidence:** child warms up and matches a sung note reasonably in tune.

## Lesson 6 — Steps and leaps (how tunes move)
**Music · Year 2 · Unit 2 (Pitch & singing voice)** | **Duration:** 25 min | **Objective:** "By the end, I can hear and sing a tune that steps (next-door notes) versus one that leaps."
**You will need:** chime bars/xylophone; recordings of "Frère Jacques" (steps) and "Twinkle Twinkle" (leaps).
**Prior knowledge to retrieve:** pitch matching (L5); high/low.
**Key vocabulary:** **step** (next-door notes), **leap** (a jump), **melody/tune**.
**What to emphasize:** tunes move by **steps** (small, next-door) or **leaps** (big jumps); your hand traces small steps vs big jumps.
---
### ⏱ 0–3 min · HOOK
> **Say:** "Some tunes tiptoe up the stairs, one step at a time. Some jump around like a frog! Listen — can you hear which is which?"
**Do:** sing a step-wise phrase, then a leaping one.
**Ask (retrieve prior knowledge):** "Was that high or low?" → *listen for:* a pitch response.
### ⏱ 3–8 min · EXPLAIN (I do)
> **Say:** "A **step** goes to the next-door note — small, smooth, like stairs. A **leap** jumps over notes — big, like a frog. 'Frère Jacques' starts with steps; 'Twinkle Twinkle' (twin-kle = a leap up) jumps. Watch my hand: little steps up, then a big jump."
**Do:** sing/play steps then leaps, tracing with the hand.
**Emphasize:** "**Steps = next-door; leaps = big jumps.**"
### ⏱ 8–13 min · EXAMPLE (we do)
> **Say:** "Trace with me — steps up the stairs… now a frog leap. Sing each."
**Do:** child sings a stepping phrase and a leaping one, hand tracing.
**Ask:** "Did that tune step or leap?" → *listen for:* correct identification.
### ⏱ 13–17 min · CHECK (audible + question)
**Ask:** "Listen — did this phrase step or leap? How could you tell?"
- ✅ **Listen for / Expected:** correct step/leap; "small next-door = step; big jump = leap." — probe: *"Show it with your hand."*
- ❌ **If confuses them:** → **fix:** "Steps are tiny and smooth; leaps jump far. Trace the size of the move."
- ❌ **If only hears high/low:** → **fix:** "Listen to the *size* of the move between notes, not just up/down."
### ⏱ 17–22 min · PRACTICE (you do)
**Do:** Child invents a 3-note "steps" tune and a "leap" tune on chime bars/voice. Once secure, interleave: name step vs leap while singing.
**Watch for:** mixing high/low with step/leap.
> **If the child is stuck:** 1) don't play it for them; 2) name *why* — size of move; 3) play next-door bars (step) vs far bars (leap) together; 4) prompt: "small move or big jump?"; 5) copy one of each; 6) "Steps small, leaps big."
### ⏱ 22–25 min · RECAP
> **Say:** "What's a step and what's a leap?" → *listen for:* "step = next-door; leap = a jump."
**Process praise:** "You made your voice frog-jump on the leaps — you can really hear how tunes move now."
**Revisit later:** next, put pitch, pulse and tune together in a whole song.
---
**Differentiation** — support: just copy steps then leaps. / stretch: name step vs leap while singing.
**Extension / if the child finishes early:** turn a step tune into a leap tune.
**Assessment evidence:** child distinguishes and sings stepping vs leaping phrases.

## Lesson 7 — Sing a song in tune and in time
**Music · Year 2 · Unit 2 (Pitch & singing voice)** | **Duration:** 30 min | **Objective:** "By the end, I can sing a short song from memory, in tune and in time, with a clear chorus."
**You will need:** a clear verse-chorus song recording (e.g. "This Little Light of Mine" or "Down by the Bay").
**Prior knowledge to retrieve:** pitch-matching (L5); pulse (L1).
**Key vocabulary:** **verse**, **chorus**, **in tune**, **in time**, **breath**.
**What to emphasize:** sing **in tune AND in time**, with a stronger **chorus** using **breath, not shouting**.
---
### ⏱ 0–3 min · HOOK
> **Say:** "Today you become the whole band — voice, beat and tune all at once — and we'll make the chorus shine. Listen first and tap the pulse."
**Do:** play the song; tap the pulse.
**Ask (retrieve prior knowledge):** "To sing louder, should we shout?" → *listen for:* "no."
### ⏱ 3–9 min · EXPLAIN (I do)
> **Say:** "I learn it line by line, **echoing**: I sing a line, you sing it back. The **verse** tells the story; the **chorus** repeats and we sing it a little **stronger** — using **breath**, not shouting. Shouting goes out of tune. Watch the chorus: full breath, clear, still in tune."
**Do:** model echo-singing a line and a strong-but-controlled chorus.
**Emphasize:** "**In tune AND in time**; chorus = breath, not shout."
### ⏱ 9–15 min · EXAMPLE (we do)
> **Say:** "Echo this line after me… now let's do the chorus together, breath ready."
**Do:** child echoes lines and joins the chorus.
**Ask:** "Should you shout to sing louder?" → *listen for:* "no — use breath and stay in tune."
### ⏱ 15–20 min · CHECK (audible + question)
**Ask:** "Sing the chorus stronger — using breath, not shouting. Then: what keeps it in tune?"
- ✅ **Listen for / Expected:** controlled stronger chorus, in tune and time; "breath, not shouting." — probe: *"In time with the pulse?"*
- ❌ **If shouts/goes flat:** → **fix:** "Big breath, open mouth — power from breath keeps the tune."
- ❌ **If rushes/drags:** → **fix:** "Feel the pulse — sing with the beat."
### ⏱ 20–27 min · PRACTICE (you do — perform)
**Do:** Child sings the whole song from memory, in tune and in time, chorus stronger. Once secure, interleave: lead the song as caller.
**Watch for:** shouting; timing drift.
> **If the child is stuck:** 1) don't sing it all for them; 2) name *why* — tune or timing; 3) chorus only, hum verses; 4) prompt: "breathe, then sing"; 5) rebuild; 6) "Breath power, with the beat."
### ⏱ 27–30 min · RECAP
> **Say:** "How do you sing a strong chorus without shouting?" → *listen for:* "use breath; stay in tune."
**Process praise:** "Your chorus was strong *and* in tune — breath power, not shouting. Lovely singing."
**Revisit later:** Unit 2 check; singing grows into rounds in Year 5.
---
**Differentiation** — support: sing chorus only, hum verses. / stretch: lead the song as caller.
**Extension / if the child finishes early:** add a body-percussion pulse while singing.
**Assessment evidence:** child sings a verse-chorus song from memory, in tune and in time.

**Listening thread (Unit 2):** **"The Aquarium"** (Saint-Saëns) — *notice* the high, sparkly pitch and gliding melody, *feel* calm or busy, *judge* with a "because."

> **End-of-unit 2 check:** Child matches a sung note, tells a stepping tune from a leaping one, and sings a short song from memory in tune and in time.

## Lesson 8 — Forte and piano (the volume words)
**Music · Year 2 · Unit 3 (Dynamics & tempo)** | **Duration:** 25 min | **Objective:** "By the end, I can play forte (loud) and piano (quiet) on cue and name them."
**You will need:** a drum or shaker; F and P cards.
**Prior knowledge to retrieve:** keeping a pulse.
**Key vocabulary:** **dynamics**, **forte** (loud), **piano** (quiet), **conduct**.
**What to emphasize:** **dynamics = how loud or quiet**; **forte** = loud, **piano** = quiet — control them on cue.
---
### ⏱ 0–3 min · HOOK
> **Say:** "Musicians use secret Italian words to boss the music around: **forte** means LOUD, **piano** means quiet. Learn them and you can control any sound!"
**Do:** clap a forte then a piano clap.
**Ask (retrieve prior knowledge):** "Keep a steady pulse for me." → *watch:* steady tapping.
### ⏱ 3–8 min · EXPLAIN (I do)
> **Say:** "**Dynamics** means how loud or quiet music is. **Forte** = loud; **piano** = quiet. Watch me conduct: big hands = forte, small hands = piano. When I show the **F** card, play loud; **P** card, play quiet."
**Do:** play the pulse switching forte/piano to the cards; conduct big/small.
**Emphasize:** "**Dynamics = loud/quiet; forte = loud, piano = quiet.**"
### ⏱ 8–13 min · EXAMPLE (we do)
> **Say:** "Watch my cards — play the pulse forte… now piano. Follow my hands."
**Do:** child plays forte/piano to the cards/conducting.
**Ask:** "What does dynamics mean?" → *listen for:* "how loud or quiet the music is."
### ⏱ 13–17 min · CHECK (audible + question)
**Ask:** "Play forte, then piano on my cue — and tell me what 'dynamics' means."
- ✅ **Listen for / Expected:** clear loud vs quiet on cue; "how loud/quiet." — probe: *"Which is forte?"*
- ❌ **If forte = faster:** mixing with tempo → **fix:** "Forte is *louder*, not faster — same speed, bigger sound."
- ❌ **If muddles the words:** → **fix:** "Forte = forceful = loud; piano = gentle = quiet."
### ⏱ 17–22 min · PRACTICE (you do — perform)
**Do:** Child plays a song's pulse switching forte/piano on cue, then conducts the tutor. Once secure, interleave: three levels (quiet, medium, loud).
**Watch for:** loud-means-fast; ignoring the cue.
> **If the child is stuck:** 1) don't play for them; 2) name *why* — loud vs fast; 3) clap same-speed loud then quiet together; 4) prompt: "same speed, just louder"; 5) two levels only; 6) "Volume, not speed."
### ⏱ 22–25 min · RECAP
> **Say:** "What do forte and piano mean?" → *listen for:* "loud; quiet."
**Process praise:** "You took the music from a whisper to a roar on cue — total dynamic control."
**Revisit later:** tomorrow — the *other* dimension: tempo (speed).
---
**Differentiation** — support: just two levels loud/quiet. / stretch: three levels (quiet, medium, loud).
**Extension / if the child finishes early:** add a getting-louder (crescendo) over four beats.
**Assessment evidence:** child plays forte/piano on cue and defines dynamics.

## Lesson 9 — Tempo: fast, slow, and changing
**Music · Year 2 · Unit 3 (Dynamics & tempo)** | **Duration:** 25 min | **Objective:** "By the end, I can keep a fast pulse and a slow pulse and gradually speed up or slow down."
**You will need:** a fast recording and a slow recording.
**Prior knowledge to retrieve:** steady pulse; dynamics (L8).
**Key vocabulary:** **tempo**, **fast**, **slow**, **getting faster** (accelerando), **getting slower**, **even**.
**What to emphasize:** **tempo = how fast or slow**; you can change it **gradually and evenly**, not jerkily.
---
### ⏱ 0–3 min · HOOK
> **Say:** "A lullaby crawls along; a race gallops. Same heartbeat — different **speed**. That speed is called **tempo**. Let's control it."
**Do:** tap a slow pulse, then a fast one.
**Ask (retrieve prior knowledge):** "Forte means…?" → *listen for:* "loud."
### ⏱ 3–8 min · EXPLAIN (I do)
> **Say:** "**Tempo** is how fast or slow the pulse goes. Watch: a slow pulse (lullaby), a fast pulse (race). Now I can *gradually* speed up — like a train leaving the station — and slow down to a stop. Keep it **even**, not jumpy."
**Do:** tap slow, fast, then a smooth speed-up and slow-down.
**Emphasize:** "**Tempo = speed; change it gradually and evenly.**"
### ⏱ 8–13 min · EXAMPLE (we do)
> **Say:** "Tap slow with me to this song… now fast to this one. Now let's be a train speeding up."
**Do:** child taps slow, fast, and a gradual accelerando.
**Ask:** "What does tempo mean?" → *listen for:* "how fast or slow the music is."
### ⏱ 13–17 min · CHECK (audible + question)
**Ask:** "Tap a slow pulse, then speed up gradually — what does tempo mean?"
- ✅ **Listen for / Expected:** controlled slow→fast, even; "how fast/slow." — probe: *"Was your speed-up smooth or jumpy?"*
- ❌ **If jumps speed suddenly:** → **fix:** "A little faster each beat — like a train pulling away, smooth."
- ❌ **If confuses with louder:** → **fix:** "Faster is *speed*, not volume — keep it the same loudness."
### ⏱ 17–22 min · PRACTICE (you do — perform)
**Do:** Child keeps a fast and a slow pulse to recordings, then performs a smooth speed-up and slow-down. Once secure, interleave: speed up *then* slow to a stop in one go.
**Watch for:** jerky changes; speed/volume confusion.
> **If the child is stuck:** 1) don't tap for them; 2) name *why* — evenness; 3) count the gradual change together; 4) prompt: "tiny bit faster each beat"; 5) just fast vs slow; 6) "Smooth, like a train."
### ⏱ 22–25 min · RECAP
> **Say:** "What's tempo, and how do you change it smoothly?" → *listen for:* "speed; a little at a time."
**Process praise:** "Your train sped up beautifully smoothly — real tempo control."
**Revisit later:** next — proving loud isn't the same as fast.
---
**Differentiation** — support: just fast vs slow. / stretch: smooth gradual speed-up then slow-down.
**Extension / if the child finishes early:** conduct the tutor's tempo with your hands.
**Assessment evidence:** child controls fast/slow pulses and a gradual tempo change.

## Lesson 10 — The trick: loud isn't fast
**Music · Year 2 · Unit 3 (Dynamics & tempo)** | **Duration:** 25 min | **Objective:** "By the end, I can prove dynamics and tempo are different by playing loud-and-slow and quiet-and-fast."
**You will need:** drum/body percussion; sorting cards (loud/quiet, fast/slow).
**Prior knowledge to retrieve:** dynamics (L8), tempo (L9).
**Key vocabulary:** **dynamics**, **tempo**, **loud-slow**, **quiet-fast**, **separate**.
**What to emphasize:** **dynamics (volume) and tempo (speed) are separate** — you can be loud AND slow, or quiet AND fast.
---
### ⏱ 0–3 min · HOOK
> **Say:** "Here's a challenge I bet you can't do: play LOUD but SLOW… and then quiet but super FAST! Can you keep them separate?"
**Do:** stamp loud-slow giant steps; then tiptoe quiet-fast.
**Ask (retrieve prior knowledge):** "Forte means loud; what does tempo mean?" → *listen for:* "speed."
### ⏱ 3–8 min · EXPLAIN (I do)
> **Say:** "**Dynamics** (loud/quiet) and **tempo** (fast/slow) are *different controls*. Watch: **loud-slow** = giant booming footsteps; **quiet-fast** = tiny scurrying mouse feet. I can mix them any way: loud-fast, quiet-slow too. They don't have to match!"
**Do:** demo all four combos, naming each with both words.
**Emphasize:** "**Volume and speed are separate** — mix them freely."
### ⏱ 8–13 min · EXAMPLE (we do)
> **Say:** "Do loud-slow giant steps with me… now quiet-fast mouse feet."
**Do:** child performs two contrasting combos, naming both words.
**Ask:** "Can music be loud and slow at the same time?" → *listen for:* "yes — dynamics and tempo are different."
### ⏱ 13–17 min · CHECK (audible + question)
**Ask:** "Play loud-and-slow, then quiet-and-fast — so are volume and speed the same thing?"
- ✅ **Listen for / Expected:** clear loud-slow and quiet-fast; "no, they're separate." — probe: *"Name both words for your last one."*
- ❌ **If loud drifts faster:** → **fix:** "Keep it slow — loud doesn't mean rushing. Big *and* slow."
- ❌ **If can't separate:** → **fix:** "One control at a time: set the speed slow, *then* make it loud."
### ⏱ 17–22 min · PRACTICE (you do — perform)
**Do:** Child plays the four combinations and sorts sound clips into a loud/quiet box and a fast/slow box. Once secure, interleave: invent and label all four combos.
**Watch for:** loud→fast coupling.
> **If the child is stuck:** 1) don't do it for them; 2) name *why* — coupling; 3) lock the tempo with a metronome/clap, then change volume; 4) prompt: "speed stays, volume changes"; 5) two combos; 6) "Two separate dials."
### ⏱ 22–25 min · RECAP
> **Say:** "Are loud and fast the same? Prove it." → *listen for:* "no — e.g. loud-slow giant steps."
**Process praise:** "You kept volume and speed totally separate — that's a sophisticated bit of musicianship."
**Revisit later:** Unit 3 check; these dimensions feed your composition in Unit 5.
---
**Differentiation** — support: do two combos. / stretch: invent and label all four.
**Extension / if the child finishes early:** describe a song you know using both a dynamics and a tempo word.
**Assessment evidence:** child performs loud-slow and quiet-fast and explains dynamics ≠ tempo.

**Listening thread (Unit 3):** **"In the Hall of the Mountain King"** (Grieg) — track how it gets *both* louder (dynamics) and faster (tempo); *notice → feel → judge* the building excitement.

> **End-of-unit 3 check:** Child controls forte/piano and fast/slow on cue, performs a gradual change, and explains that dynamics (volume) and tempo (speed) are separate.

## Lesson 11 — Same note, different sound (timbre)
**Music · Year 2 · Unit 4 (Timbre & instrument families)** | **Duration:** 25 min | **Objective:** "By the end, I can name 'timbre' and describe sounds as bright, dull, ringing or buzzy."
**You will need:** household sound-makers (metal pan, cushion, glass of water, comb-and-paper); timbre word cards.
**Prior knowledge to retrieve:** pitch (same note can sound different).
**Key vocabulary:** **timbre**, **bright**, **dull**, **ringing**, **buzzy**.
**What to emphasize:** **timbre** is the *character/colour* of a sound — why a drum and a flute sound different even on the same note.
---
### ⏱ 0–3 min · HOOK
> **Say:** "A drum and a flute can play the *exact same note* — so why don't they sound the same? That special difference is called **timbre**. Let's hunt for timbres."
**Do:** tap a metal pan (ring) then a cushion (dull thud).
**Ask (retrieve prior knowledge):** "Was the pan high or low?" → *listen for:* a pitch answer (then note both can be same pitch, different timbre).
### ⏱ 3–8 min · EXPLAIN (I do)
> **Say:** "**Timbre** is the *colour* of a sound. Same action, different colour: the pan is **bright and ringing**; the cushion is **dull and soft**; the glass goes **clear-ting**; the comb-and-paper is **buzzy**. We describe timbre with words like bright, dull, ringing, buzzy."
**Do:** play each sound-maker, naming its timbre word.
**Emphasize:** "**Timbre = the character/colour of a sound.**"
### ⏱ 8–13 min · EXAMPLE (we do)
> **Say:** "Match a word to this sound with me — is the glass *ringing* or *dull*?"
**Do:** child matches two sounds to timbre words.
**Ask:** "What is timbre?" → *listen for:* "the type/character of a sound — what makes instruments sound different."
### ⏱ 13–17 min · CHECK (audible + question)
**Ask:** "Describe two of these sounds with timbre words — and what does 'timbre' mean?"
- ✅ **Listen for / Expected:** apt words (ringing/dull/buzzy); "the character of a sound." — probe: *"Could two of these be the same pitch but different timbre?"* (yes).
- ❌ **If describes pitch (high/low) instead:** → **fix:** "Not high or low — the *colour*: bright? dull? buzzy?"
- ❌ **If no word fits:** → **fix:** "Listen again — does it ring on, or stop dead? Buzz or sing?"
### ⏱ 17–22 min · PRACTICE (you do — explore)
**Do:** Child plays the "kitchen band," describing each timbre; builds a word bank. Once secure, interleave: invent their own timbre word for a sound.
**Watch for:** pitch words used for timbre.
> **If the child is stuck:** 1) don't label for them; 2) name *why* — pitch vs timbre; 3) compare two very different sounds; 4) prompt: "ring or thud? buzz or clear?"; 5) match one; 6) "Timbre is the *colour*, not the height."
### ⏱ 22–25 min · RECAP
> **Say:** "What is timbre, in your own words?" → *listen for:* "the character/colour of a sound."
**Process praise:** "Your kitchen band found bright, dull, ringing and buzzy — sharp listening for timbre."
**Revisit later:** next, sorting instruments into families by their sound.
---
**Differentiation** — support: match two sounds to two words. / stretch: invent your own timbre word.
**Extension / if the child finishes early:** find three timbres around the room.
**Assessment evidence:** child names timbre and describes sounds with timbre words.

## Lesson 12 — The four families
**Music · Year 2 · Unit 4 (Timbre & instrument families)** | **Duration:** 30 min | **Objective:** "By the end, I can sort instruments into percussion, strings, woodwind and brass by how they make sound."
**You will need:** instrument clips (violin, recorder/flute, trumpet, drum) — e.g. BBC Ten Pieces; family sort cards.
**Prior knowledge to retrieve:** timbre (L11).
**Key vocabulary:** **percussion** (hit), **strings** (bowed/plucked), **woodwind** (blown), **brass** (buzzed lips), **family**.
**What to emphasize:** instruments group into **families by HOW the sound is made** — hit, bowed, blown, or buzzed. Pre-empt sorting by size/looks.
---
### ⏱ 0–3 min · HOOK
> **Say:** "Instruments live in families — some are **hit**, some **bowed**, some **blown**, some **buzzed**. Detective question: which family is a trumpet in?"
**Do:** play a quick clip of each family.
**Ask (retrieve prior knowledge):** "What word means the colour of a sound?" → *listen for:* "timbre."
### ⏱ 3–9 min · EXPLAIN (I do)
> **Say:** "We sort by **how** the sound is made. **Percussion** — you **hit** it (drum). **Strings** — you **bow or pluck** (violin). **Woodwind** — you **blow** down it (recorder/flute). **Brass** — you **buzz your lips** (trumpet). Watch out for tricksters: a recorder is woodwind, not brass; a piano's strings are *struck*."
**Do:** play each clip and place it in its family by the *how*.
**Emphasize:** "Sort by **how** the sound is made — hit, bowed, blown, buzzed."
### ⏱ 9–15 min · EXAMPLE (we do)
> **Say:** "This one's bowed — which family? This one you blow — which family?"
**Do:** child sorts four clear examples by the how.
**Ask:** "Which family is a trumpet in?" → *listen for:* "brass."
### ⏱ 15–20 min · CHECK (audible + question)
**Ask:** "Sort these four by how they're played — and which family is the trumpet?"
- ✅ **Listen for / Expected:** correct sort by method; "brass." — probe: *"How do you know it's brass, not woodwind?"* (buzzed lips).
- ❌ **If sorts by size/looks:** → **fix:** "Not by size — by *how* you play it. Do you hit, bow, blow or buzz?"
- ❌ **If recorder → brass:** → **fix:** "You *blow* a recorder gently — that's woodwind; brass you *buzz*."
### ⏱ 20–27 min · PRACTICE (you do — categorise)
**Do:** Child sorts picture/sound cards into the four families. Once secure, interleave: explain the rule (hit/blown/bowed/buzzed) and catch a trickster.
**Watch for:** size-based sorting; brass/woodwind mix.
> **If the child is stuck:** 1) don't sort for them; 2) name *why* — method; 3) mime hit/bow/blow/buzz together; 4) prompt: "how do you make it sound?"; 5) sort one clear card; 6) "Sort by the *how*."
### ⏱ 27–30 min · RECAP
> **Say:** "Name the four families and one way to tell them apart." → *listen for:* the four + "by how they're played."
**Process praise:** "You sorted by *how* they're played, not how they look — true instrument-detective work."
**Revisit later:** Unit 4 check; instruments of the orchestra deepen in Year 4.
---
**Differentiation** — support: sort four clear examples. / stretch: explain the rule (hit/blown/bowed/buzzed).
**Extension / if the child finishes early:** add one more instrument to each family.
**Assessment evidence:** child sorts instruments into the four families by sound-production method.

**Listening thread (Unit 4):** **"The Young Person's Guide to the Orchestra"** (Britten) — pick out a family by its timbre as it enters; *notice → feel → judge* which timbre you liked and why.

> **End-of-unit 4 check:** Child describes three sounds with timbre words and sorts instruments into the four families by how they make sound.

## Lesson 13 — Plan a sound piece on paper
**Music · Year 2 · Unit 5 (Compose, notate & perform)** | **Duration:** 30 min | **Objective:** "By the end, I can plan a 3-part piece in graphic notation, choosing dynamics and tempo."
**You will need:** paper with three boxes, a symbol key, mixed percussion/household sounds.
**Prior knowledge to retrieve:** graphic notation (L4); dynamics & tempo (Unit 3).
**Key vocabulary:** **compose**, **structure**, **beginning–middle–end**, **dynamics (F/P)**, **tempo**.
**What to emphasize:** composers **plan/sketch before playing**; a piece has a **beginning, middle and end**, each with chosen **dynamics and tempo**.
---
### ⏱ 0–3 min · HOOK
> **Say:** "Composers don't just bash about — they *sketch* the music first, like a map. Today we draw a whole piece before we make a single sound."
**Show:** the three-box plan and symbol key.
**Ask (retrieve prior knowledge):** "Name a loud word and a fast word." → *listen for:* "forte; fast."
### ⏱ 3–9 min · EXPLAIN (I do)
> **Say:** "We plan a 'weather journey' in three boxes. **Beginning**: quiet, slow — light rain. **Middle**: loud, fast — a storm! **End**: quiet, slow, fading away. I draw symbols in each box and write **F or P** (dynamics) and **fast/slow** (tempo) underneath. Now it's a map I can play."
**Do:** fill the three boxes with symbols + F/P + tempo.
**Emphasize:** "Plan **beginning–middle–end**, each with chosen **dynamics and tempo**."
### ⏱ 9–15 min · EXAMPLE (we do)
> **Say:** "Let's plan the storm middle together — loud or quiet? fast or slow? Draw it."
**Do:** child fills the middle box with symbols and labels.
**Ask:** "Name the three parts a piece should have." → *listen for:* "beginning, middle, end."
### ⏱ 15–20 min · CHECK (look + question)
**Ask:** "Show me your three parts and the dynamics/tempo for each."
- ✅ **Look for / Expected:** three boxes with symbols + F/P + tempo; names B-M-E. — probe: *"How will the storm sound different from the rain?"*
- ❌ **If only one idea / no contrast:** → **fix:** "Make the middle *different* — louder and faster than the beginning."
- ❌ **If no dynamics/tempo labels:** → **fix:** "Write F or P and fast/slow under each box so you can play it the same."
### ⏱ 20–27 min · PRACTICE (you do — compose)
**Do:** Child plans the full 3-part piece in graphic notation with dynamics and tempo. Once secure, interleave: add a repeating pattern that returns.
**Watch for:** no contrast between sections; missing labels.
> **If the child is stuck:** 1) don't plan it for them; 2) name *why* — structure/contrast; 3) decide one box together; 4) prompt: "what's the weather here — loud or quiet?"; 5) fill one box; 6) "Three parts, each different."
### ⏱ 27–30 min · RECAP
> **Say:** "What three parts does your piece have, and what changes between them?" → *listen for:* B-M-E + dynamics/tempo contrast.
**Process praise:** "You mapped your whole piece before playing — that's exactly how composers work."
**Revisit later:** tomorrow you *play* your plan — twice the same.
---
**Differentiation** — support: tutor scribes, child decides. / stretch: add a repeating pattern that returns.
**Extension / if the child finishes early:** add a fourth idea or a surprise rest.
**Assessment evidence:** child plans a 3-part piece in graphic notation with chosen dynamics and tempo.

## Lesson 14 — Play your plan twice the same
**Music · Year 2 · Unit 5 (Compose, notate & perform)** | **Duration:** 30 min | **Objective:** "By the end, I can perform my planned piece the same way twice, with deliberate dynamics and tempo."
**You will need:** the child's written plan; percussion/household sounds.
**Prior knowledge to retrieve:** the plan (L13); controlling dynamics/tempo.
**Key vocabulary:** **compose**, **perform**, **repeat**, **deliberate**, **timbre choice**.
**What to emphasize:** real music can be **played again the same way** — choose timbres, follow your plan, control dynamics and tempo **on purpose**.
---
### ⏱ 0–3 min · HOOK
> **Say:** "Here's what makes it *real music* and not random noise: a composer can play it AGAIN, exactly. Can you play yours the same way twice?"
**Show:** the child's plan.
**Ask (retrieve prior knowledge):** "What are your three parts?" → *listen for:* B-M-E recall.
### ⏱ 3–9 min · EXPLAIN (I do)
> **Say:** "I choose a **timbre** for each box — a soft shaker for rain, a loud drum for the storm. Then I **rehearse**, following my plan, controlling **forte/piano** and **fast/slow** on purpose. Watch — I'll play it once, then again the *same* way."
**Do:** choose instruments, perform the plan twice, matching.
**Emphasize:** "Follow the plan; **same dynamics and tempo each time**."
### ⏱ 9–15 min · EXAMPLE (we do)
> **Say:** "Let's choose the storm's sound together and rehearse the middle — loud and fast."
**Do:** child selects timbres and rehearses one section to the plan.
**Ask:** "What does composing mean?" → *listen for:* "making up your own music you can play the same way again."
### ⏱ 15–20 min · CHECK (audible + question)
**Ask:** "Play your beginning — quiet and slow as planned. Why can a composer repeat a piece exactly?"
- ✅ **Listen for / Expected:** matches the plan's dynamics/tempo; "because it's planned/written, not random." — probe: *"Did it match your plan?"*
- ❌ **If ignores the plan:** → **fix:** "Read your boxes — what did you write for this part? Play that."
- ❌ **If dynamics/tempo random:** → **fix:** "Check your F/P and fast/slow labels and play them on purpose."
### ⏱ 20–27 min · PRACTICE (you do — perform)
**Do:** Child performs the whole piece **twice**, following the plan with deliberate dynamics and tempo, and checks the two match. Once secure, interleave: perform from memory of the plan.
**Watch for:** drift between the two plays; ignoring labels.
> **If the child is stuck:** 1) don't perform for them; 2) name *why* — following the plan; 3) point to each box as they play; 4) prompt: "what did you plan here?"; 5) one section twice; 6) "Same again — that's what makes it a composition."
### ⏱ 27–30 min · RECAP
> **Say:** "How is your piece different from random noise?" → *listen for:* "I can play it the same again — it's planned."
**Process praise:** "You played it the same way twice — that *proves* it's a real composition. Brilliant."
**Revisit later:** next — perform for an audience and become a music critic.
---
**Differentiation** — support: tutor points to each box as a guide. / stretch: perform from memory of the plan.
**Extension / if the child finishes early:** teach the tutor to play your piece from your plan.
**Assessment evidence:** child performs a planned piece twice the same with deliberate dynamics and tempo.

## Lesson 15 — Our concert and appraise
**Music · Year 2 · Unit 5 (Compose, notate & perform)** | **Duration:** 30 min | **Objective:** "By the end, I can perform for an audience and appraise a piece with notice → feel → judge."
**You will need:** composed pieces / learned songs; a "stage" space; instruments.
**Prior knowledge to retrieve:** performing the plan (L14); dimension words (pulse/tempo, dynamics, pitch, timbre).
**Key vocabulary:** **perform**, **audience**, **appraise**, **notice**, **feel**, **judge** (because).
**What to emphasize:** perform confidently for an audience, then **appraise** a piece in three steps — **notice** (a dimension word), **feel** (a mood), **judge** ("I liked … **because** …").
---
### ⏱ 0–3 min · HOOK
> **Say:** "It's showtime! You'll perform your piece — and then become the **music critic**: what did you notice, how did it feel, and what did you think? Take a breath, performer."
**Do:** set up the 'stage'.
**Ask (retrieve prior knowledge):** "Give me one dimension word — tempo, dynamics, pitch or timbre." → *listen for:* one of them.
### ⏱ 3–9 min · EXPLAIN (I do — model performing + appraising)
> **Say:** "Two skills today. **Performing**: stand ready, play in time with your chosen dynamics, take a bow. **Appraising** in three steps — I'll model on a piece: *Notice* — 'the tempo got faster'. *Feel* — 'it felt exciting'. *Judge* — 'I liked it **because** the fast bit built up tension.' Always give the *because*."
**Do:** perform a short example; model notice → feel → judge with a reason.
**Emphasize:** "Appraise = **notice → feel → judge with a *because*.**"
### ⏱ 9–15 min · EXAMPLE (we do)
> **Say:** "Let's appraise my piece together — what did you *notice*? How did it *feel*?"
**Do:** child gives a notice word and a feeling for a performance.
**Ask:** "Give an opinion about that piece with a reason." → *listen for:* a dimension word + a *because*.
### ⏱ 15–20 min · CHECK (audible + question)
**Ask:** "Appraise this performance: notice something, say how it felt, and judge it with a *because*."
- ✅ **Listen for / Expected:** e.g. "I noticed it was loud and fast, it felt exciting, I liked it because the storm built up." — probe: *"Which dimension word did you use?"*
- ❌ **If "it was good" only:** no reason → **fix:** "Add a *because* — what made it good? Use a music word."
- ❌ **If retells the story, no opinion:** → **fix:** "Now your *opinion* — did you like it, and why?"
### ⏱ 20–27 min · PRACTICE (you do — perform → appraise)
**Do:** Child performs their piece (or a learned song) in time with chosen dynamics, takes a bow; then appraises the tutor's performance (or a recording) using all three steps. Once secure, interleave: appraise a *contrasting* piece.
**Watch for:** opinions with no reason; performance nerves (keep it warm).
> **If the child is stuck (appraising):** 1) don't supply the verdict; 2) name *why* — missing a step; 3) do one step at a time together; 4) prompt: "finish 'I liked it because…'"; 5) just a *notice* word first; 6) "Notice, feel, then the because."
### ⏱ 27–30 min · RECAP
> **Say:** "Give me the three steps of appraising." → *listen for:* "notice → feel → judge (with a because)."
**Process praise:** "You performed for a real audience *and* gave a real critic's verdict with a reason — a complete musician today."
**Revisit later:** appraising and music history deepen in Year 4 and beyond.
---
**Differentiation** — support: just say one *notice* word. / stretch: do all three steps unaided.
**Extension / if the child finishes early:** write a one-line concert review.
**Assessment evidence:** child performs for an audience and appraises a piece with notice → feel → judge and a reason.

**Listening thread (Unit 5):** a **West African djembe** ensemble and a quiet **lullaby** side by side — appraise each with *notice → feel → judge*, comparing tempo, dynamics and timbre.

> **End-of-unit 5 check:** Child plans a 3-part piece in graphic notation, performs it twice the same with deliberate dynamics and tempo, and appraises a performance using notice → feel → judge with a reason.

## Listening & appraising (year-long thread)
Every unit carries a short, **named**, diverse piece — Caribbean folk (Mango Walk), orchestral (Saint-Saëns, Grieg, Britten), West African drumming, and lullaby/choral singing. The response grows to full **notice → feel → judge with a reason**, using dimension words (pulse/tempo, dynamics, pitch, timbre). Liking or disliking is fine; **explaining** is the skill. History and genre comparison continue → Year 4/6.

## End-of-year outcomes (what a Year 2 child can do)
Read & write rhythm (ta, ti-ti, rest in graphic notation); keep pulse and rhythm together; sing in tune and in time with a strong chorus; control dynamics (forte/piano, crescendo); control tempo (fast/slow, accelerando) knowing it differs from dynamics; describe timbre and sort the four instrument families; compose, notate and perform a 3-part piece twice the same; and appraise diverse music with notice → feel → judge.
