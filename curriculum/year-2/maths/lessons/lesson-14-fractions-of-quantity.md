## Flow (all AI, "fair-share café" frame)

- **Hook (0–3):** "12 sweets — and you get a QUARTER of them. How many is that?" Retrieval: the one-to-each dealing from Lesson 11's division.
- **Explain (3–8):** the anchor — **"the bottom number tells me how many plates."** Deal all 12 into 4 plates (3 each), count ONE plate → ¼ of 12 = 3. The division link made explicit: "fractions of amounts are division wearing a party hat" (¼ of 12 = 12 ÷ 4). Steps card: bottom number → plates · deal one-to-each · count the plates the TOP number asks for.
- **Example (8–13):** child finds **¾ of 12** on the same plates — top number 3 means count THREE plates: 3, 6, 9. Traps handled live: "3" (stopped at one plate) and "8" (did 12 − 4).
- **Check (13–17):** ¾ of 12 fresh, no plates on screen — catches both the **subtract-the-denominator** crowd (12 − 4 = 8) and the **one-plate stoppers** (¼ instead of ¾). Probe: "how did the plates help?"
- **Practice (17–21):** adaptive 10-item bank (levels 1–3) — ½ of 6 and ¼ of 4 (level 1), ¼ of 8/12, ¾ of 8, "how many plates for ⅓?" and a fraction-family match (level 2), and stretch: ⅓ of 9 (new denominator), the **backwards puzzle** ("¼ of my number is 5 — what's the whole?"), and "a friend says ¼ of 12 = 8 — what went wrong?" Child says the recipe aloud before each answer: "share into __, count __."
- **Recap (21–24):** child states their recipe for ¾ of a number; process praise ("you dealt one-to-each instead of guessing").

**Adaptivity:** start level 2 · 2 right → up · 2 misses on a skill → scripted re-teach with the dealing animated one counter at a time + level 1 · mastery = 4+ correct incl. one unit fraction, one non-unit fraction (¾) and the routine spoken aloud.

**Optional video (reinforce, unverified — search the channel):** BBC Bitesize, search "find a quarter of an amount KS1". Task: say how many plates BEFORE the video shares each amount — the bottom number gives it away.

**Differentiation:** support — physically deal into the denominator's piles, label plates 1–4, say the recipe aloud every time · stretch — ⅓ of 9, ¾ of 8, and the backwards "¼ of ? = 5" puzzle.
**Evidence:** finds unit and non-unit fractions of amounts by sharing, links ¼ to ÷ 4, avoids the subtract-the-denominator error.
