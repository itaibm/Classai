# Lesson 2 — Partitioning numbers
**Maths · Year 2 · Unit 1 (Place value to 100)** · 22 min · **Delivery: 🤖 fully AI**
**Objective:** "By the end, I can partition a two-digit number into tens and ones and put it back together."
*(Human view of [`lesson-02-partitioning.json`](./lesson-02-partitioning.json) — the JSON is the source of truth. No preparation needed from you.)*

## Flow (all AI, "smash and rebuild" game frame)

- **Hook (0–3):** "I'll SMASH a number like a Lego model, then rebuild it — no piece missing." Retrieval: tens and ones in 43.
- **Explain (3–8):** animated whiteboard slides the 4 rods away from the 3 cubes — parts named by VALUE: "forty and three, not four and three." Recombine to show nothing is lost.
- **Example (8–13):** child smashes 76, fills `76 = ___ + 6`. Trap handled: "7 and 6" → count the rods in tens.
- **Check (13–16):** the sneaky **50** — must be 50 + 0, not "5 and 0." Wrong-answer playbook included (5+0 digits trap, 25+25 halving trap).
- **Practice (16–20):** adaptive 9-item bank — teen-number smashes (level 1), 38/64/90 with rebuild checks (level 2), and stretch: reversed rebuilds (80+3 vs 38), **non-standard partitions** (43 = 30 + 13 — the pre-seed for regrouping in Lesson 6), and "a friend says 64 = 6 + 4 — what went wrong?"
- **Recap (20–22):** child states the rule + explains 50; process praise ("you rebuilt every number to check").

**Adaptivity:** start level 2 · 2 right → up · 2 misses on a skill → scripted re-teach + separated-rods drawing + level 1 · mastery = 3+ correct incl. a zero-ones case and a recombine.
