# Lesson 8 — Inverse: addition and subtraction check each other
**Maths · Year 2 · Unit 2 (Calculation)** · 20 min · **Delivery: 🤖 fully AI**
**Objective:** "By the end, I can use an addition fact to check a subtraction (and the reverse)."

## Flow (all AI, "maths detective" frame)

- **Hook (0–3):** "you finished a sum… are you SURE? Today you prove it like a detective." Retrieval: the opposite of adding.
- **Explain (3–8):** part-whole bar model on the whiteboard — whole 63, parts 38 and 25. Removing the 25 lands exactly back on 38 → 63 − 25 = 38. Reveal the treasure: **same three numbers, four facts** (the fact family); the biggest number is always the whole.
- **Example (8–12):** prove last lesson's 27 + 46 = 73: child PREDICTS the landing spot first, then computes 73 − 46 = 27. Traps handled: "46" (gave the removed part), "119" (added).
- **Check (12–15):** "38 + 25 = 63, so 63 − 25 = ?" — no working out allowed → 38. Wrong-answer playbook: "25" (parts/whole swapped) and "88" (added). Probe: "how did you know without calculating?"
- **Practice (15–18):** adaptive 10-item bank — within-20 families (level 1), pick-the-checking-fact, matchPairs, add-back checks (level 2), stretch: missing number □ + 25 = 63, fact-family sort (gatecrashers 63+25=88 and 38−25=13), prove 64 − 28 = 36 in words.
- **Recap (18–20):** child explains how subtraction proves an addition; process praise ("you proved instead of trusting").

**Adaptivity:** start level 2 · 2 right → up · 2 misses on a skill → bar-model re-teach + within-20 level 1 · mastery = 3+ correct incl. an add-back check and a full family.

**Video:** none — the bar-model animation carries the idea better than any clip.

**Differentiation:** support — bar model stays visible, fact-family triangle · stretch — all four facts unaided, missing numbers.
**Evidence:** states the matching inverse fact without recalculating and writes a correct four-fact family.
