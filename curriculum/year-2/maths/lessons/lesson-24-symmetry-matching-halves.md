# Lesson 24 — Symmetry: matching halves
**Maths · Year 2 · Unit 7 (Statistics, shape and symmetry)** · 22 min · **Delivery: 👤 human intro → 🤖 AI**
**Objective:** "By the end, I can find a line of symmetry by folding so both halves match."
*(Human view of [`lesson-24-symmetry-matching-halves.json`](./lesson-24-symmetry-matching-halves.json) — the JSON is the source of truth.)*

## Your part (first ~8 minutes, real paper)

**Lay out before the lesson:** paper cut-outs of a square, a rectangle, a circle and a deliberately **wonky (scalene) triangle**; a butterfly outline; paint + brush (kept back for practice); scissors; optional small mirror. Keep the shapes within reach afterwards — the AI sends the child back to fold them.

1. **Hook (0–3):** fold the butterfly down the middle — "the wings land exactly on top!" Retrieve: what did the halves have to be in our fractions folding? *(equal / the same)*.
2. **Explain (3–8):** name it — **a line of symmetry is a fold where both halves land EXACTLY on top**. Fold the square all four ways (edge-to-edge ×2, corner-to-corner ×2) → **4 lines**. Then the wonky triangle: fold any way, never matches → **no lines at all**. "Only an exact landing counts — not just nearly."
3. **Handoff line (say as written):** *"Now my friend on the screen is going to draw what we found. Watch!"* → AI takes over.

## AI part (8–22 min)

- **Example (8–13):** character draws the square's 4 lines; the child folds the REAL rectangle — the two straight-across folds work, then the sneaky **corner-to-corner fold FAILS** (two equal triangles, but the corners miss). "The fold never lies!"
- **Check (13–17):** the unit's key question — "Does a square have a line of symmetry? **Prove it by folding.**" Traps handled: "any line through it is fine" (test, don't assume), sloppy folds read as "no", and carrying the square's diagonal over to the rectangle.
- **Practice (17–20):** adaptive 10-item bank (levels 1–3) — the fold test itself, counting lines (square 4 / rectangle 2), the **diagonal-trap** true/false, a symmetry sort (butterfly, heart, square vs wonky triangle, letter F), "how many MORE lines has the square?", explain-the-test, and "every shape has one" (false!). Finale: the **paint-fold butterfly** (paint one half, fold, press, open).
- **Recap (20–22):** child states the rule on their own butterfly — fold, and both halves must land exactly on top; process praise ("you tested by folding instead of guessing").

**Adaptivity:** start level 2 · 2 right → up · 2 misses on a skill → scripted re-teach + fold the real shape on that exact line + level 1 · mastery = 3+ correct incl. a rectangle-diagonal item, with the fold test stated in the child's own words.

**Optional video (reinforce, unverified — search the channel):** BBC Bitesize, search "lines of symmetry KS1". Task: vote symmetry-or-not with an imaginary fold before the video tests each shape.

**Differentiation:** support — fold to test every line, start with the square and butterfly · stretch — draw ALL lines on rectangle vs square (2 vs 4) and explain why the square's diagonals work.
**Evidence:** finds lines of symmetry by folding, rejects non-matching lines (incl. the rectangle's diagonal), states the exact-landing rule.
