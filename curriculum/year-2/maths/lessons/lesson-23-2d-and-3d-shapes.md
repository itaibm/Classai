# Lesson 23 — Properties of 2-D and 3-D shapes
**Maths · Year 2 · Unit 7 (Statistics, shape and symmetry)** · 22 min · **Delivery: 👤 human intro → 🤖 AI**
**Objective:** "By the end, I can describe 2-D shapes by sides/vertices and 3-D shapes by faces/edges/vertices."
*(Human view of [`lesson-23-2d-and-3d-shapes.json`](./lesson-23-2d-and-3d-shapes.json) — the JSON is the source of truth.)*

## Your part (first ~8 minutes, real solids)

**Lay out before the lesson:** a dice (any cube), a cereal box (cuboid), a ball (sphere), a tin (cylinder), a paper pentagon cut-out, and sticky dots or paper bits + tape — one per face. Keep the solids within reach afterwards — the AI sends the child back to them during practice.

1. **Hook (0–3):** hold the dice STILL — "without turning it, how many faces? You can only see three… are more hiding?" Retrieve: is a dice flat or solid, 2-D or 3-D?
2. **Explain (3–8):** pentagon by touch — **5 sides, 5 vertices** (flat shapes have SIDES). Then the trick that IS the lesson: **turn-and-dot** the cube — a dot on each face as you count to **6** ("three were hiding!"), then edges (**12**) and vertices (**8**). Solid shapes have FACES.
3. **Handoff line (say as written):** *"Now my friend on the screen is going to draw what we found. Watch!"* → AI takes over.

## AI part (8–22 min)

- **Example (8–13):** character redraws the pentagon (child supplies the vertices count) and the dotted cube with the famous 6-12-8; probe — "which of those could you NEVER count without turning it?"
- **Check (13–17):** the key diagnostic — **"how many faces does a cube have, including every hiding one?"** Traps handled: 3 or 4 (visible-only counting → back to the real dotted dice) and 8 (vertices given as faces). The method is probed, not just the number.
- **Practice (17–20):** adaptive 10-item bank (levels 1–3) — square and triangle sides (level 1), pentagon fill, cube faces/vertices and the faces-edges-vertices vocabulary (level 2), and stretch: the **cuboid cereal box** (same 6-12-8, rectangle faces), a **rolls-vs-flat** sort (sphere/cylinder vs cube/cuboid), the "4 faces because that's what you see" true/false, and "a friend counted 3 and stopped — what should they DO?" Child may grab the real solids any time.
- **Recap (20–22):** child states what solids have that flat shapes don't, and how hidden parts get caught ("turn it and mark each one"); process praise.

**Adaptivity:** start level 2 · 2 right → up · 2 misses on a skill → scripted re-teach + dot-and-turn the real solid + level 1 · mastery = 3+ correct incl. one full 3-D count with hidden parts included.

**Optional video (reinforce, after the lesson):** [3D Shapes Song For Kids — Math Songs by NUMBEROCK](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZnZYK83utu0) *(verified 2026-07-03)*. Task: shout each shape's name first — and catch the American "rectangular prism": in the UK we say **CUBOID**. After: "what do WE call the cereal-box shape, and how many faces?" → a cuboid, 6.

**Differentiation:** support — keep the dotted dice in hand, count faces in opposite pairs (top+bottom, front+back, left+right) · stretch — cube vs cuboid (what changes, what doesn't), rolls/stacks sorting, hunt for a shape with MORE than 6 faces.
**Evidence:** states sides/vertices of 2-D shapes (pentagon 5/5) and faces/edges/vertices of a cube and cuboid (6/12/8), explicitly counting hidden faces by turning.
