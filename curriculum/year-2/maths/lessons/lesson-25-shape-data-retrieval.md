## Flow (all AI, quick-fire quiz frame — no new idea, pure fluency)

- **Hook (0–3):** "Quiz challenge! How many can you fire back without a pause?" Guaranteed-win warm-up: triangle sides.
- **Explain (3–6):** the two champion rules modelled on the whiteboard — **charts → key first** (key = 2, three apples → 2, 4, 6) and **solids → count the hidden parts** (I see 3 faces, a cube has 6).
- **Example (6–9):** pentagon sides & vertices (5 and 5), then read "most" from a live bar chart (dogs 6 / cats 4 / birds 2).
- **Check (9–12):** pentagon sides AND vertices — catches the 2-D/3-D word mix ("faces and edges") and miscounts.
- **Practice (12–17):** quick-fire 11-item bank with a visible streak — tally gates of 5, keyed pictograms (key = 5, row of 4 → 20), block-diagram "how many more", cube faces (hidden-face trap), hexagon/rectangle facts, fold-test symmetry; stretch: half-symbol pictogram (key = 2, 3½ symbols → 7) and "is any middle line a line of symmetry?"
- **Recap (17–20):** honest self-assessment — easiest vs needs-practice; the named weak spot becomes the next warm-up. Process praise: "you read the key before every chart question."

**Adaptivity:** start level 2 · 2 right → up (interleave at 3) · 2 misses on a skill → picture-in-view reteach + level 1 · mastery = 5+ mixed correct incl. a keyed pictogram and a hidden-faces fact.

**No video** — retrieval lesson; every minute is quiz reps.

**Differentiation:** support — picture stays in view, slower pace · stretch — explain *why*, timed no-look streak.
**Evidence:** fluent recall of shape properties, keyed chart reads, and the fold test.
