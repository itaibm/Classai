# Lesson 9 — Multiplication: equal groups and arrays
**Maths · Year 2 · Unit 2 (Calculation: add, subtract, multiply, divide)** · 24 min · **Delivery: 🤖 fully AI**
**Objective:** "By the end, I can show a multiplication as equal groups and as an array."

## Flow (all AI, "lazy genius / array chef" frame)

- **Hook (0–3):** 4 plates of 5 grapes — "how many WITHOUT counting them all?" Child rediscovers skip-counting in 5s from Lesson 5 (5, 10, 15, 20).
- **Explain (3–9):** the × sign means **"groups of"**; groups tidy into an **array** (3 rows of 5). The heart of the lesson: **turn the array a quarter-turn** — same 15 dots, so **3 × 5 = 5 × 3**. Anchor: "Same dots, same total — turn it and check." Animated whiteboard shows both counts (5, 10, 15 and 3, 6, 9, 12, 15).
- **Example (9–14):** child builds 2 × 5 as array chef (groups? in each? total?), then predicts 5 × 2 with **no recounting allowed** — "did any dot escape?"
- **Check (14–17):** 3 × 5 (trap: "8" = × read as +), plus the commutativity probe — "if 2 × 10 = 20, what's 10 × 2, and HOW do you know?"
- **Practice (17–21):** adaptive 10-item bank (levels 1–3) — counting equal groups and skip-count fills (level 1), 3 × 5 / 4 × 2 / array-reading / both-facts matching (level 2), and stretch: the 5 × 5 square array, sorting **equal vs unequal groups** ("can you even multiply it?"), and "Leo says 3 × 5 = 8 — what went wrong?"
- **Recap (21–24):** child states what × means and why 3 × 5 = 5 × 3; teaser for Lesson 10 ("two facts for the price of one makes the tables twice as easy").

**Adaptivity:** start level 2 · 2 right → up · 2 misses on a skill → scripted re-teach with groups rebuilt as separate plates + level 1 (2 groups of 2) · mastery = 3+ correct incl. one array item and one commutativity item stated without recounting.

**Optional video (reinforce, unverified — search the channel):** Numberblocks, search "Numberblocks arrays making arrays episode". Task: freeze at every array and say the TWO facts it shows; after — "did turning the rectangle change the number of blocks?"

**Differentiation:** support — skip-count with one finger per row, keep groups on separate plates until arrays feel safe · stretch — both facts unaided, "would turning ANY array keep the total?", the 5 × 5 square and its twin.
**Evidence:** shows a multiplication as equal groups AND an array, totals by skip-counting, writes both commutative facts.
