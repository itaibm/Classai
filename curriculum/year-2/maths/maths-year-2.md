## Year overview

Year 2 is the year numbers grow into the **hundreds** and children meet the two tools that will
carry them through primary maths: the **column method** and the **bar model**. It is taught in
the Singapore / Maths-No-Problem mastery tradition: every lesson moves **concrete → pictorial →
abstract** (base-10 blocks and place-value counters first, then drawings and bar models, then
digits), the class moves through the content together, and big ideas get many lessons —
introduce, practise, apply, retrieve — never one.

**What Year 2 assumes from Year 1 (ages 6–7):** place value to 100; number bonds to 10 and 20
known fluently; adding and subtracting two-digit numbers with and without regrouping using
base-10; the 2, 5 and 10 times tables and the idea of equal groups and arrays; halves, quarters
and thirds of shapes and small quantities; telling time to 5 minutes; coins and notes; measuring
in cm, kg and litres; tally charts, pictograms and block diagrams; naming 2-D and 3-D shapes.

**How the year sequences.** Term 1 builds **place value to 1000** (Unit 1), then **mental
addition and subtraction** (Unit 2) before the **formal column method with exchanging** and the
first **part–whole and comparison bar models** (Unit 3). Term 2 is multiplicative: the **3, 4
and 8 times tables** learned through arrays, doubling and patterns (Unit 4), then **2-digit ×
1-digit and 2-digit ÷ 1-digit** with partitioning, the grid and remainders (Unit 5), then
**money** — adding, subtracting and giving change in real currencies (Unit 6) — and **statistics**
with scaled pictograms and bar charts (Unit 7). Term 3 turns to measure and shape: **length and
perimeter** (Unit 8), **fractions** — tenths, fractions of amounts, equivalence, comparing and
adding within one whole (Unit 9), **time to the minute, the 24-hour clock and Roman numerals**
(Unit 10), **angles, lines and shape** (Unit 11), **mass and capacity** (Unit 12), and a final
**consolidation and problem-solving** unit (Unit 13). Each term closes with retrieval lessons, and
every unit ends with a short mastery check.

**What Year 3 (ages 8–9) relies on from this year:** secure three-digit place value and the
column method (extended to four digits and to decimals), the 3, 4 and 8 tables known by heart
(all tables to 12 × 12 are completed in Year 3), the bar model as the default way to represent a
word problem, tenths as a fraction (the bridge to decimals), perimeter (the bridge to area), and
confident reading of scales and clocks.

**Daily shape of a lesson (20–30 min):** 3-minute fluency warm-up (number facts, counting in
steps, a times-table song) → anchor task with manipulatives → guided practice ("we do") →
independent practice ("you do") → a reasoning question that asks *why*. Fluency sprints and
times-table games also run as 5-minute starters outside the maths lesson.

## Unit 1 — Place value to 1000

**Essential question:** How can the same digit be worth 3, 30 or 300 — and why does zero matter so much?

**Key vocabulary:** hundreds, tens, ones, digit, place value, partition, recombine, placeholder, exchange, three-digit, compare, order, greater than, less than, number line, estimate, one more, ten more, one hundred more, multiple

### Lesson 1 — Counting in hundreds
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can count in 100s to 1000 and show any number of hundreds with blocks." (UK NC Y3 PV; CCSS 2.NBT.1a–b)
- **Hook:** A bag of 1000 dried beans is tipped out — "How could we count these without losing track?"
- **Key activity:** Concrete: bundle beans or straws into tens, then ten tens into a "hundred" cup; swap for base-10 flats. We do: count flats aloud 100, 200 … 1000; notice ten hundreds make a thousand (the big cube). You do: show 400, 700, 1000 with flats and write each number.
- **Check for understanding:** "Mo says 10 hundreds is 1100. Build it and explain." → 10 flats = 1000 because ten hundreds exchange for one thousand cube.
- **Differentiation:** support: count hundreds as "1 hundred, 2 hundreds" using flats only, with a 100-square underneath each / stretch: count back in 100s from 1000 and from 950 (950, 850…) and explain what stays the same.
- **Materials:** base-10 flats and a thousand cube, bundles of 10 straws with elastic bands, dried beans, paper cups
- **Joy:** "Thousand tower" — the class stacks ten flats and checks it is exactly as tall as the thousand cube.

### Lesson 2 — Hundreds, tens and ones
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can build, draw and write any three-digit number and say what each digit is worth." (UK NC Y3 PV; CCSS 2.NBT.1)
- **Hook:** "I'm thinking of a number with 3 flats, 4 rods and 6 cubes — what could it be? Could it be 436?"
- **Key activity:** Concrete: build numbers on a hundreds/tens/ones place-value mat with base-10. Pictorial: draw them as squares, sticks and dots. Abstract: write the numeral and the value of each digit (346 = 300 + 40 + 6). I do → we do → you do with six numbers.
- **Check for understanding:** "In 474, are the two 4s worth the same? Explain." → No: one is 400, one is 4, because of their place.
- **Differentiation:** support: numbers with no zeros, blocks always available, place-value mat with column pictures / stretch: "Make the largest and smallest number using 3 digit cards; what is the difference in hundreds?"
- **Materials:** base-10 blocks, place-value mats (H/T/O), digit cards 0–9, mini-whiteboards
- **Joy:** "Mystery bag" — pull blocks out one at a time; the class writes the number and races to update it.

### Lesson 3 — Zero as a placeholder
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can read and write numbers like 305 and 540 and explain what the zero does." (UK NC Y3 PV; CCSS 2.NBT.3)
- **Hook:** A "robot" (teacher) writes "three hundred and five" as 3005 — "What went wrong?"
- **Key activity:** Concrete: build 305 with base-10 — see the empty tens column. Pictorial: draw on the mat and write 0 in the empty place. Abstract: read and write numbers in words and numerals (e.g. 608, 850, 700). Sort "zero numbers" by which place is empty.
- **Check for understanding:** "What is the difference between 35 and 305? Show with blocks." → 305 has 3 hundreds; the 0 holds the tens place empty so the 3 is worth 300, not 30.
- **Differentiation:** support: only one zero per number; build before writing / stretch: write 5 numbers between 100 and 1000 with two zeros and one with a zero in the ones and tens (e.g. 900) and explain each.
- **Materials:** base-10 blocks, place-value mats, number-word cards, arrow cards (Gattegno place-value cards)
- **Joy:** "Fix the robot" — children correct the robot's silly numbers and the robot "reboots" with a beep for each fix.

### Lesson 4 — Partitioning in more than one way
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can partition a three-digit number in different ways, like 342 = 300 + 30 + 12." (UK NC Y3 PV; CCSS 2.NBT.3)
- **Hook:** "The shop has run out of hundreds flats. Can you still pay 342 in blocks?"
- **Key activity:** Concrete: build 342, then exchange a ten for ten ones, a hundred for ten tens, recording each new partition. Pictorial: part–whole models with 2 and 3 parts. Abstract: complete missing-part equations (342 = 200 + ___ + 2).
- **Check for understanding:** "Is 342 = 2 hundreds + 14 tens + 2 ones? Prove it." → Yes: 200 + 140 + 2 = 342 (14 tens is 140).
- **Differentiation:** support: two-part partitions only (hundreds and the rest) using blocks / stretch: find five different partitions of 500 using only tens and hundreds and explain the pattern.
- **Materials:** base-10 blocks, place-value counters (100s, 10s, 1s), part–whole mats
- **Joy:** "Exchange market" — a bank table where children swap blocks and the "banker" checks every exchange is fair.

### Lesson 5 — Numbers on a number line to 1000
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can place and estimate three-digit numbers on a number line." (UK NC Y3 PV; CCSS 2.MD.6)
- **Hook:** A 10-metre rope along the corridor labelled 0 and 1000 — "Where do you think 500 lives? 250? 990?"
- **Key activity:** Concrete: peg number cards on a washing line/rope 0–1000. Pictorial: number lines 0–1000 marked in 100s, then 300–400 marked in 10s. Abstract: read marked points and estimate unmarked ones, explaining with the nearest hundreds.
- **Check for understanding:** "An arrow is just past halfway between 600 and 700. Is it 605, 655 or 695? Why?" → 655, because halfway is 650.
- **Differentiation:** support: lines marked in 100s with every interval labelled / stretch: estimate on a line with no middle markings, then measure to check how close.
- **Materials:** long rope or washing line, pegs, number cards, printed number lines, metre sticks
- **Joy:** "Human number line" — children hold numbers and arrange themselves along the corridor; a surprise number is handed out last.

### Lesson 6 — 1, 10 and 100 more or less
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can find 1, 10 or 100 more or less than a three-digit number and explain which digit changes." (UK NC Y3 PV; CCSS 2.NBT.8)
- **Hook:** A "number machine" box: 456 goes in, 556 comes out — "What does the machine do?"
- **Key activity:** Concrete: add or remove one cube, rod or flat on a place-value mat. Pictorial: record on a three-row table. Abstract: work across boundaries (395 + 10 = 405; 908 − 10 = 898), rebuilding with blocks when a column empties.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why does 395 + 10 change both the tens and hundreds digits?" → Nine tens plus one ten makes ten tens, which exchange for a hundred.
- **Differentiation:** support: no boundary crossings; always build first / stretch: invent machines "+110" and "−101" and predict outputs across boundaries.
- **Materials:** base-10 blocks, place-value mats, a cardboard "number machine" box, 1000-grids (ten 100-squares)
- **Joy:** Children design their own secret number machine; partners guess the rule from three input/output pairs.

### Lesson 7 — Counting in 50s and 100s from any number
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can count on and back in 50s and 100s from any number and spot the pattern." (UK NC Y3 PV; CCSS 2.NBT.2)
- **Hook:** A stepping-stone game drawn in chalk: each stone is worth 50 — "How many stones to get from 150 to 600?"
- **Key activity:** Concrete: counting sticks and 50-value place-value counters; count 50, 100, 150… Pictorial: jumps on number lines. Abstract: continue sequences forwards and backwards (870, 820, 770…) and explain the repeating digit pattern.
- **Check for understanding:** "Will 725 appear if I count in 50s from 25? Explain." → Yes: from 25, the ones digit stays 5 and tens alternate 2 and 7, so 725 is reached.
- **Differentiation:** support: count in 100s first, then 50s from multiples of 50, with a 1000-bead string / stretch: find the missing terms in 1000, ___, ___, 850, ___ and write a rule.
- **Materials:** counting stick, 1000-bead string, chalk, place-value counters
- **Joy:** Chalk stepping-stone hopscotch outdoors — call the number on each stone to jump.

### Lesson 8 — Counting in 4s and 8s (and 3s)
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can count in 3s, 4s and 8s from 0 and see links between the patterns." (UK NC Y3 PV; CCSS 3.OA.9)
- **Hook:** An octopus picture: "How many legs have 5 octopuses? Can counting in 4s help?"
- **Key activity:** Concrete: build towers of 3, 4 and 8 multilink cubes and count along. Pictorial: colour multiples on a 100-square (4s red, 8s blue). Abstract: notice every 8s number is a 4s number and every 8s number is even; say sequences to 40, 48 and 96.
- **Check for understanding:** "Is every multiple of 4 a multiple of 8? Show me." → No: 4, 12 and 20 are multiples of 4 but not 8; every second one is.
- **Differentiation:** support: count in 4s with cube towers and a 100-square to 40 / stretch: predict whether 100 is in the 3s, 4s and 8s sequences and explain.
- **Materials:** multilink cubes, 100-squares, coloured pencils, counting stick
- **Joy:** "Octopus and dog legs" song — counting legs in 8s and 4s with actions.

### Lesson 9 — Comparing three-digit numbers
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can compare two three-digit numbers using <, > and = and explain which place I looked at first." (UK NC Y3 PV; CCSS 2.NBT.4)
- **Hook:** Two treasure chests with "coins": 489 and 512 — "Which chest has more? The one with bigger digits?"
- **Key activity:** Concrete: build both numbers; compare hundreds first, then tens, then ones. Pictorial: place both on a number line. Abstract: fill <, >, = between pairs, including tricky ones (398 ___ 401; 670 ___ 607).
- **Check for understanding:** "Ria says 89 > 100 because 8 and 9 are big digits. Is she right?" → No: 100 has one hundred; 89 has no hundreds.
- **Differentiation:** support: use blocks for every pair; compare only numbers with different hundreds / stretch: missing-digit problems (4_5 > 47_) — list every digit that works.
- **Materials:** base-10 blocks, place-value mats, symbol cards (<, >, =), "crocodile mouth" card
- **Joy:** "Higher or lower" card game with three digit cards each — highest number wins the round.

### Lesson 10 — Ordering numbers
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can order a set of three-digit numbers from smallest to largest and explain my method." (UK NC Y3 PV; CCSS 2.NBT.4)
- **Hook:** Heights of famous waterfalls or towers written in metres on cards — "Put them in order."
- **Key activity:** Concrete: place-value counters for each number in rows. Pictorial: place numbers on a 0–1000 line. Abstract: order five numbers including ones with the same hundreds digit (345, 354, 305, 435, 543).
- **Check for understanding:** "Why is it wrong to put 99 after 900 when ordering smallest to largest?" → 99 has no hundreds, so it is smaller than any number in the hundreds.
- **Differentiation:** support: order three numbers with different hundreds / stretch: make and order all six numbers from digits 3, 5, 0 (only valid three-digit ones) and explain why 035 is not a three-digit number.
- **Materials:** place-value counters, number line 0–1000, number cards, a real data card set (e.g. building heights in metres)
- **Joy:** "Tallest to shortest" — ordering real world landmarks (e.g. Eiffel Tower about 330 m, Statue of Liberty about 93 m including pedestal).

### Lesson 11 — Rounding to the nearest 10 and 100 (intro)
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can say which ten or hundred a number is closest to using a number line." (CCSS 3.NBT.1; UK NC Y3 PV – estimation)
- **Hook:** "A newspaper says 'about 400 people came to the festival.' Did exactly 400 come?"
- **Key activity:** Concrete: a "number hill" drawn on paper with multiples of ten at the bottoms; a counter "rolls down" to the nearest ten. Pictorial: number lines between two tens and between two hundreds. Abstract: round numbers to the nearest 10 and 100; agree the convention that a number exactly halfway (e.g. 45, 350) rounds up.
- **Check for understanding:** "Round 349 to the nearest hundred. Why isn't it 400?" → 300, because 349 is less than halfway (350) to 400.
- **Differentiation:** support: nearest 10 only for two-digit numbers with the number line always drawn / stretch: list every whole number that rounds to 200 to the nearest hundred (150–249).
- **Materials:** printed "number hill" sheets, counters, number lines, a newspaper headline with a rounded number
- **Joy:** "Roll down the hill" — a small ball rolled on a paper hill made of curved card.

### Lesson 12 — Estimating quantities and positions
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can make a sensible estimate of a quantity or a position and improve it using a known amount." (UK NC Y3 PV; CCSS 2.NBT.4)
- **Hook:** A jar of pasta shapes — "Guess how many. Could it be 10? 10,000?"
- **Key activity:** Concrete: count out a benchmark of 100 pieces and compare its size with the whole jar. Pictorial: estimate positions on unmarked lines. Abstract: record estimate, improved estimate and actual count; find how close each was.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why is your second estimate better than your first?" → I compared with a known 100 and judged how many hundreds fit.
- **Differentiation:** support: jar of about 50–100; benchmark of 10 / stretch: estimate beans in a larger jar using a 100-bean benchmark and give a range, not a single number.
- **Materials:** clear jars, dried pasta or beans, small cups, blank number lines
- **Joy:** Estimation jar competition — the closest estimate gets to name the next jar's challenge.

### Lesson 13 — Place-value problems and reasoning
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can solve 'digit puzzles' by reasoning about place value." (UK NC Y3 PV – reasoning; CCSS 2.NBT.1)
- **Hook:** "My number has 7 hundreds, more tens than hundreds and fewer ones than tens. What could it be?"
- **Key activity:** Concrete: digit cards and place-value mats to test possibilities. Pictorial: record possibilities in a table. Abstract: work systematically to find all solutions to constraint puzzles.
- **Check for understanding:** "How do you know you've found all the answers?" → I worked systematically (tens 8, then 9) and listed the ones for each.
- **Differentiation:** support: puzzles with one answer and only two clues / stretch: write their own puzzle with exactly three solutions for a partner.
- **Materials:** digit cards 0–9, place-value mats, puzzle cards, base-10 blocks for checking
- **Joy:** "Safe cracker" — solve the three-digit code to open a padlocked box of stickers.

### Lesson 14 — Place value in real life: 1000 in the world
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can find and explain three-digit numbers in the world around me." (UK NC Y3 PV – application)
- **Hook:** A short walk around school (or photos): bus numbers, page numbers, prices, heights on maps.
- **Key activity:** Concrete: collect numbers on clipboards (or from real objects: a 500-page book, a 250 g packet). Pictorial: sort findings on a large 0–1000 line. Abstract: for each number, write it in words and in expanded form and say what 100 more would be.
- **Check for understanding:** "A packet says 450 g. Is that closer to half a kilogram or to 1 kilogram? Explain." → Half a kilogram (500 g), because 450 is 50 away from 500 but 550 away from 1000.
- **Differentiation:** support: collect five numbers and build each with blocks / stretch: find a number above 1000 and explain what the fourth digit means.
- **Materials:** clipboards, food packets, a thick book, a map with elevations, base-10 blocks
- **Joy:** Number hunt with a class "number museum" display of what they find.

### Lesson 15 — Retrieval: place value to 1000
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can use everything I know about place value to answer a mixed set of questions." (UK NC Y3 PV – retrieval)
- **Hook:** "Place value bingo" — the caller describes numbers ("4 hundreds, 0 tens, 9 ones").
- **Key activity:** Mixed retrieval: counting in 50s and 100s, partitioning, 10/100 more, comparing and rounding, first with blocks available, then without. Self-mark and choose one type to re-practise.
- **Check for understanding:** "Which of these is wrong and why: 607 = 6 hundreds + 7 tens?" → Wrong: 607 has 0 tens and 7 ones; 6 hundreds + 7 tens = 670.
- **Differentiation:** support: blocks on the table throughout; fewer, simpler items / stretch: explain two answers in writing using "because".
- **Materials:** bingo cards, base-10 blocks, mini-whiteboards
- **Joy:** Bingo with a small prize for the first full line — and a "reasoning bonus" for explaining a card.

### Lesson 16 — End-of-unit check: place value
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can show my teacher I understand three-digit place value." (UK NC Y3 PV – assessment)
- **Hook:** "Show what you know" — a short, calm quiz followed by a place-value challenge.
- **Key activity:** Low-stakes check: 8 items (write in numerals and words, partition two ways, 10/100 more, compare, order, round, a number-line estimate) then a reasoning puzzle. Teacher confers individually and notes gaps for flex slots.
- **Check for understanding:** "Explain, using blocks or a drawing, why 1000 is ten hundreds." → Ten flats exchange for one thousand cube.
- **Differentiation:** support: items read aloud; blocks available / stretch: an extension puzzle — "the difference between my number and 1000 is 125" (875).
- **Materials:** printed check sheet, base-10 blocks, number line
- **Joy:** Each child finishes by making a "place-value poster" of their favourite number shown every way they know.

**End-of-unit check:** Write 507 in words, partition it two ways, find 100 less, place it on a 0–1000 line, round it to the nearest 100, and explain why the 0 is needed.

## Unit 2 — Mental addition and subtraction

**Essential question:** When can I calculate in my head — and which strategy is the smartest?

**Key vocabulary:** add, subtract, sum, difference, total, bond, multiple of 10, bridge, compensate, adjust, inverse, near double, related fact, mental strategy

### Lesson 17 — Related facts: from 3 + 4 to 300 + 400
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can use a known fact to add and subtract ones, tens and hundreds." (UK NC Y3 Add/Sub mental; CCSS 2.NBT.8)
- **Hook:** "If 6 + 3 = 9, what else do I know for free?"
- **Key activity:** Concrete: model 6 + 3 with cubes, 60 + 30 with rods, 600 + 300 with flats. Pictorial: fact-family triangles. Abstract: generate families of related facts (70 − 40, 700 − 400, 900 − 600).
- **Check for understanding:** "Why is 500 + 700 not 1200 tens?" → It is 12 hundreds, which is 1200 — 5 hundreds + 7 hundreds = 12 hundreds.
- **Differentiation:** support: bonds to 10 scaled to tens only, with rods / stretch: bonds to 1000 in 100s and 50s (650 + ___ = 1000).
- **Materials:** multilink cubes, base-10 rods and flats, fact-family triangle cards
- **Joy:** "Fact multiplier" — a card flip where the same fact appears in ones, tens and hundreds as fast as possible.

### Lesson 18 — Adding and subtracting ones to a three-digit number
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can add and subtract ones to a three-digit number mentally, including crossing a ten." (UK NC Y3 Add/Sub mental; CCSS 2.NBT.7)
- **Hook:** "I'm on page 347 of a book and read 6 more pages. Which page am I on?"
- **Key activity:** Concrete: base-10 on a mat, exchanging when ones pass 10. Pictorial: number line jumps via the next ten (347 + 3 + 3). Abstract: mental practice with and without crossing.
- **Check for understanding:** "Show why 402 − 5 is not 403." → 402 − 2 = 400, then 3 more back is 397.
- **Differentiation:** support: no crossing first; number line provided / stretch: crossing a hundred (598 + 7; 603 − 8) and explaining.
- **Materials:** base-10 blocks, place-value mats, blank number lines, a chapter book with numbered pages
- **Joy:** "Page race" — roll a die to "read" pages; first reader to pass page 400 wins.

### Lesson 19 — Adding and subtracting tens
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can add and subtract multiples of 10 to a three-digit number mentally." (UK NC Y3 Add/Sub mental; CCSS 2.NBT.8)
- **Hook:** A lift/elevator with 10 floors per button press — "From floor 250, press +40..."
- **Key activity:** Concrete: add and remove rods. Pictorial: jumps of 10 on a 1000-grid. Abstract: 253 + 60 (tens cross into hundreds), 427 − 50.
- **Check for understanding:** "Which digit never changes when you add tens? Why?" → The ones digit, because only tens are added.
- **Differentiation:** support: tens not crossing the hundred / stretch: 385 + 90 as + 100 − 10 and explain why it works.
- **Materials:** base-10 rods and flats, 1000-grid, place-value mats
- **Joy:** "Elevator operator" role play — the operator calls floors, passengers compute.

### Lesson 20 — Adding and subtracting hundreds
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can add and subtract hundreds to a three-digit number and say why only one digit changes." (UK NC Y3 Add/Sub mental; CCSS 2.NBT.7)
- **Hook:** A bank account game: "You have 245 coins; a dragon gives you 300 more."
- **Key activity:** Concrete: place-value counters, add/remove 100s. Pictorial: arrow diagrams. Abstract: mixed practice, including subtracting hundreds to reach zero hundreds (345 − 300 = 45).
- **Check for understanding:** "Is 872 − 400 = 432 or 472? Prove it." → 472: only the hundreds digit changes.
- **Differentiation:** support: counters always available / stretch: find missing numbers: ___ − 300 = 519 and explain using the inverse.
- **Materials:** place-value counters, gold-coin tokens, place-value mats
- **Joy:** Dragon treasure game — cards say "the dragon steals 200" or "gives 400."

### Lesson 21 — Bridging through 10 and 100
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can add by making the next ten or hundred first (e.g. 68 + 7 = 68 + 2 + 5)." (UK NC Y3 Add/Sub mental; CCSS 2.NBT.9)
- **Hook:** "Why do shops love round numbers?"
- **Key activity:** Concrete: ten-frames and rods — fill the ten first. Pictorial: number line jumps split across the boundary. Abstract: 68 + 7, 195 + 8, 470 + 50, 132 − 5.
- **Check for understanding:** "Explain why 195 + 8 is easier as 195 + 5 + 3." → 195 + 5 makes 200, then 3 more is 203.
- **Differentiation:** support: two-digit bridging through 10 with ten-frames / stretch: bridging through a hundred with tens (380 + 70) and inventing a word problem.
- **Materials:** ten-frames, counters, base-10 rods, blank number lines
- **Joy:** "Fill the box" — boxes hold exactly 10 eggs; complete the boxes then place the rest.

### Lesson 22 — Compensating: add 9 as add 10 subtract 1
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can add or subtract near-multiples of 10 by rounding and adjusting." (UK NC Y3 Add/Sub mental; CCSS 2.NBT.9)
- **Hook:** "Adding 99 in one second — watch me: 245 + 99." Teacher answers instantly.
- **Key activity:** Concrete: add a rod and remove one cube. Pictorial: number line with a big jump and a small jump back. Abstract: + 9, + 19, + 99, − 9, − 29 practice.
- **Check for understanding:** "For 356 − 19, Sam did 356 − 20 then subtracted 1 more. What went wrong?" → He took away 1 too many; he must add 1 back: 337.
- **Differentiation:** support: + 9 and − 9 only, with rods and cubes / stretch: + 98, − 101 and explain the adjustment in words.
- **Materials:** base-10 rods and cubes, number lines, mini-whiteboards
- **Joy:** "Beat the calculator" — one child uses a calculator, the other mental compensation; see who is faster.

### Lesson 23 — Near doubles and adding several numbers
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can add three or four numbers by spotting bonds and doubles first." (UK NC Y3 Add/Sub mental; CCSS 2.NBT.6)
- **Hook:** Four children's pocket money: 15, 25, 30, 5 — "Who can add them fastest, and how?"
- **Key activity:** Concrete: coins or cubes, grouping bonds to 10. Pictorial: loop bond pairs. Abstract: 35 + 36 as double 35 + 1; 17 + 8 + 13 + 2 by pairing.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why is it clever to add 17 + 13 first in 17 + 8 + 13?" → 17 + 13 = 30, a multiple of ten.
- **Differentiation:** support: three single-digit numbers, then tens / stretch: four two-digit numbers and an "odd-one-out" set that has no helpful pairs.
- **Materials:** coins or tokens, multilink cubes, number cards
- **Joy:** "Target 100" card game — draw cards and try to make exactly 100.

### Lesson 24 — Inverse and checking
- **Duration:** 25 min

### Lesson 27 — Column addition without exchange
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can add two three-digit numbers in columns and explain why I line up the digits." (UK NC Y3 Add/Sub formal; CCSS 2.NBT.7)
- **Hook:** Two classes collected bottle tops: 234 and 152 — "How many altogether? Can we write it neatly?"

### Lesson 28 — Column addition with one exchange (ones to tens)
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can add in columns when the ones make more than ten and show the exchange." (UK NC Y3 Add/Sub formal; CCSS 2.NBT.7)
- **Hook:** "248 + 135: the ones make 13. Where does the extra ten go?"

### Lesson 29 — Column addition with exchanges in two columns

- **Hook:** A theme-park queue counter: 367 + 258 people — "Two exchanges at once?"

- **Check for understanding:** "In 367 + 258 the tens make 12 (6 + 5 + 1). Where does each part go?" → 2 tens stay; 10 tens become 1 hundred in the hundreds column (answer 625).

### Lesson 30 — Estimate first, then add
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can estimate an answer by rounding and use it to catch mistakes." (UK NC Y3 Add/Sub – estimate; CCSS 3.NBT.1)
- **Hook:** "My friend says 312 + 489 = 1501. Before calculating, is that believable?"
- **Key activity:** Round each number to the nearest hundred (or ten), estimate, then calculate in columns and compare. Pictorial: number lines showing the estimate and the exact answer close together.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why is 1501 impossible for 312 + 489?" → About 300 + 500 = 800; 1501 is far too big (exact 801).

### Lesson 31 — Column subtraction without exchange
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can subtract a three-digit number in columns when no exchange is needed." (UK NC Y3 Add/Sub formal; CCSS 2.NBT.7)
- **Hook:** A library has 587 books; 243 are borrowed. "How many are left on the shelves?"

### Lesson 32 — Column subtraction with exchange from tens
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can exchange a ten for ten ones when there aren't enough ones to subtract." (UK NC Y3 Add/Sub formal; CCSS 2.NBT.7)
- **Hook:** "352 − 127: I need to take 7 ones but I only have 2. Stuck?"

### Lesson 33 — Column subtraction with exchange from hundreds
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can exchange a hundred for ten tens and subtract with exchanges in two columns." (UK NC Y3 Add/Sub formal; CCSS 2.NBT.7)
- **Hook:** A mountain climber is at 725 m on a 900 m climb — "How far to go?"

### Lesson 34 — Subtracting across zeros
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can subtract from numbers like 400 and 305 by exchanging through the zero." (UK NC Y3 Add/Sub formal; CCSS 2.NBT.7)
- **Hook:** "400 − 1 is easy in my head. Why is 400 − 163 tricky on paper?"
- **Key activity:** Concrete: exchange 1 hundred → 10 tens, then 1 ten → 10 ones. Pictorial: record 3 hundreds, 9 tens, 10 ones. Abstract: compare with a mental method, counting up (163 → 200 → 400 = 237).
- **Check for understanding:** "Why does 400 become 3 hundreds, 9 tens and 10 ones?" → It is still 400; we exchanged so every column has enough to subtract.
- **Differentiation:** support: 100 − two-digit numbers with blocks / stretch: choose column or counting-up for 1000 − 998 and 1000 − 257 and justify.

### Lesson 35 — Checking with the inverse
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can check a column calculation using the inverse operation." (UK NC Y3 Add/Sub – inverse; CCSS 2.NBT.9)
- **Hook:** "A shopkeeper's till says 842 − 367 = 485. Should we trust the till?"
- **Key activity:** For each subtraction, add the answer to the part subtracted; for each addition, subtract. Pictorial: part–whole models show why. Children find and fix errors.
- **Check for understanding:** "How does 485 + 367 tell us the till is wrong?" → It makes 852, not 842, so the answer should be 475.

### Lesson 36 — Introducing the bar model: part–whole
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can draw a bar model to show a whole and its parts and write the calculations it shows." (Singapore bar model; UK NC Y3 Add/Sub problems; CCSS 2.OA.1)
- **Hook:** Cuisenaire rods: an orange rod (10) is matched exactly by two yellows (5 + 5) — "The rods tell a story."
- **Key activity:** Concrete: Cuisenaire rods and paper strips to show whole and parts. Pictorial: draw bars with a bracket for the whole; label known numbers and "?". Abstract: write both the addition and the subtraction the bar shows (245 + ? = 400; 400 − 245 = ?).
- **Check for understanding:** "The whole is 400 and one part is 245. Which calculation finds the other part, and why?" → 400 − 245 = 155, because a part is the whole minus the other part.
- **Differentiation:** support: Cuisenaire rods with small numbers before drawing / stretch: from one bar model, write four different number sentences it shows.
- **Materials:** Cuisenaire rods, paper strips, rulers, squared paper
- **Joy:** "Rod stories" — children build a rod picture and a partner invents the matching story.

### Lesson 37 — Bar models for addition and subtraction problems
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can use a bar model to decide whether a word problem needs addition or subtraction." (Singapore bar model; CCSS 2.OA.1)
- **Hook:** "A zoo had some penguins. 36 more arrived. Now there are 125. How many were there at first?"

### Lesson 38 — Comparison bar models: how many more?

## Unit 4 — Multiplication and division: the 3, 4 and 8 tables

**Essential question:** How do equal groups, arrays and doubling help me know facts I have never learned by heart?

### Lesson 48 — The 3 times table: building it

### Lesson 50 — Dividing by 3

- **Objective:** "By the end, I can divide by 8 using known facts and halving three times." (UK NC Y3 Mult/Div – ÷8; CCSS 3.OA.6)

- **Key activity:** Concrete: group counters into 8s. Pictorial: bar model with 8 equal parts. Abstract: 40 ÷ 8 via 5 × 8 = 40; halve, halve, halve (40 → 20 → 10 → 5).
- **Check for understanding:** "Why does halving three times divide by 8?" → 2 × 2 × 2 = 8 equal parts.
- **Differentiation:** support: counters for grouping / stretch: 96 ÷ 8 by halving three times.
- **Materials:** counters, paper strips, plastic spiders
- **Joy:** "Spider count" mystery — legs drawn under a sheet; children find the number of spiders.

### Lesson 55 — Linking 2, 4 and 8
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can explain how the 2, 4 and 8 times tables are linked and use the link to solve facts." (UK NC Y3 Mult/Div – reasoning; CCSS 3.OA.9)
- **Hook:** Three counting sticks side by side, 2s, 4s and 8s — "What do you notice?"

- **Check for understanding:** "Priya says 8 × 6 is double 3 × 8. Is she right?" → Yes: 6 is double 3, so 8 × 6 = 2 × 24 = 48.

- **Joy:** "Doubling dominoes" game matching 2×, 4× and 8× facts.

### Lesson 56 — Unit bar models for multiplication
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can draw a bar model with equal units to solve a multiplication problem." (Singapore bar model; CCSS 3.OA.3)
- **Hook:** "A ticket costs 8 coins. How much for 6 tickets?"
- **Key activity:** Concrete: 6 identical Cuisenaire rods end to end. Pictorial: a bar with 6 equal units labelled 8, bracket "?". Abstract: 6 × 8 = 48.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why must every unit in the bar be the same length?" → Each unit is the same value (8); equal groups.
- **Differentiation:** support: rods first; factors 3 and 4 / stretch: "A costs 4 times as much as B; B is 8. Find A and A + B."

### Lesson 57 — Bar models for division problems
- **Duration:** 25 min

- **Hook:** "32 stickers shared equally between 4 friends. How many each?"
- **Key activity:** Pictorial: bar of 32 split into 4 units, one labelled "?". Contrast with grouping: "32 stickers, 4 on each page — how many pages?" Abstract: both 32 ÷ 4 = 8.
- **Check for understanding:** "In the grouping problem, what does the 8 mean?" → 8 pages (groups), not 8 stickers each.

### Lesson 58 — Multiplying three numbers and using facts flexibly
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can multiply three small numbers in any order to make it easier." (UK NC Y3 Mult/Div – mental; CCSS 3.OA.5)
- **Hook:** "3 boxes, each has 2 packets, each packet has 4 pens. How many pens?"

- **Check for understanding:** "Why is 5 × 7 × 2 easiest as 5 × 2 first?" → 5 × 2 = 10, and 10 × 7 is easy (70).

### Lesson 59 — Multiplying by 10 and multiples of 10
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can multiply a one-digit number by 10 and by multiples of 10 (e.g. 4 × 30)." (UK NC Y3 Mult/Div; CCSS 3.NBT.3)
- **Hook:** "3 × 4 = 12 — so what is 3 × 40?"

- **Check for understanding:** "Why is 'add a zero' a risky rule to say?" → Digits move one place to the left because each is worth 10 times more; saying "12 tens" explains why (and the rule fails for decimals later).

### Lesson 60 — Times-table fluency games

### Lesson 61 — Solving multiplication and division word problems
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can decide whether a problem needs ×, ÷, + or − and solve it with a bar model." (UK NC Y3 Mult/Div – problems; CCSS 3.OA.3, 3.OA.8)
- **Hook:** A picnic shopping list with packs of 4 and 8.
- **Key activity:** Mixed one- and two-step problems; draw bar models; justify operations.

### Lesson 62 — Scaling problems: "times as many"

- **Hook:** A tall giraffe cut-out next to a child cut-out: "How many times taller?"

### Lesson 63 — Term 2 retrieval: tables and division facts

## Unit 5 — Multiplying and dividing bigger numbers

**Essential question:** How can I use tables I know to multiply and divide numbers I have never seen?

**Key vocabulary:** partition, grid method, multiply, product, distributive, divide, share, group, remainder, left over, inverse, estimate, round, exchange

### Lesson 65 — 2-digit × 1-digit: partitioning with base-10
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can multiply a 2-digit number by a 1-digit number by partitioning into tens and ones." (UK NC Y3 Mult/Div – written; CCSS 3.NBT.3, 3.OA.5)
- **Hook:** "23 children each bring 4 conkers/stones. How many altogether?"
- **Key activity:** Concrete: 4 rows of 23 in base-10; group the tens (80) and ones (12). Pictorial: place-value counter rows. Abstract: 23 × 4 = 20 × 4 + 3 × 4 = 80 + 12 = 92.
- **Check for understanding:** "Mia says 23 × 4 = 812. What mistake did she make?" → She wrote 2 × 4 and 3 × 4 side by side; the 2 is 20, so 80 + 12 = 92.
- **Differentiation:** support: no exchange (e.g. 21 × 3) with blocks / stretch: 47 × 8 and explain each step.
- **Materials:** base-10 blocks, place-value counters, place-value mats
- **Joy:** Collect a real class set of small stones to check the total.

### Lesson 66 — The grid method
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can use the grid method to multiply 2-digit × 1-digit numbers." (UK NC Y3 Mult/Div – written; CCSS 3.NBT.3)
- **Hook:** A tiled floor 34 tiles long and 3 tiles wide — "Count fast?"
- **Key activity:** Concrete: base-10 arranged as a rectangle 3 × 34 split into 3 × 30 and 3 × 4. Pictorial: the grid. Abstract: fill in grids and total them (90 + 12 = 102).
- **Check for understanding:** "Why does the grid split 34 into 30 and 4?" → To use facts we know (3 × 3 tens and 3 × 4) and keep place value.
- **Differentiation:** support: grids pre-drawn with the partition shown / stretch: missing numbers inside the grid (___ × 6 = 240 in the tens box).
- **Materials:** base-10 blocks, squared paper, grid templates
- **Joy:** "Floor tilers" — build a tile rectangle with sticky notes and verify with the grid.

### Lesson 67 — Multiplication with exchange: towards the short method
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can multiply 2-digit by 1-digit with an exchange and record it in a column layout." (UK NC Y3 Mult/Div – written; CCSS 3.NBT.3)
- **Hook:** "Grid, then shorter: can we write 26 × 3 in one tidy column?"
- **Key activity:** Concrete: place-value counters, 3 rows of 26, exchange 18 ones for 1 ten and 8 ones. Pictorial: counters beside an expanded column (18 + 60 = 78). Abstract: expanded column method only (short multiplication is Year 3).
- **Check for understanding:** "In the expanded method for 26 × 3, why do we write 18 and 60 on separate lines?" → They are 6 × 3 and 20 × 3, which add to 78.
- **Differentiation:** support: grid method remains / stretch: compare grid and expanded; which is quicker for 48 × 7?
- **Materials:** place-value counters, place-value mats, squared paper
- **Joy:** "Method race" — two children use different methods and check they agree.

### Lesson 68 — Estimating products
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can estimate a product by rounding and check my answer is reasonable." (UK NC Y3 Mult/Div – estimate; CCSS 3.OA.8)
- **Hook:** "38 × 4 = 1502? Believable?"
- **Key activity:** Round the 2-digit number to the nearest 10, multiply, compare with the exact product.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why is 38 × 4 close to 160?" → 38 is close to 40 and 40 × 4 = 160 (exact 152).
- **Differentiation:** support: estimates with 20, 30, 40 using rods / stretch: will the estimate be more or less than the exact product? Explain.
- **Materials:** base-10 rods, number lines, "Believable?" cards
- **Joy:** "Price is right" — estimate the cost of 4 identical toys before seeing the exact total.

### Lesson 69 — Dividing 2-digit numbers by partitioning
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can divide a 2-digit number by a 1-digit number by partitioning into easy parts." (UK NC Y3 Mult/Div – written; CCSS 3.OA.7)
- **Hook:** "69 shells shared between 3 rock pools."
- **Key activity:** Concrete: share base-10 among 3 plates — 6 tens then 9 ones. Pictorial: place-value counters in groups. Abstract: 69 ÷ 3 = 60 ÷ 3 + 9 ÷ 3 = 20 + 3 = 23.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why partition 72 ÷ 4 into 40 and 32, not 70 and 2?" → 70 and 2 don't divide by 4 exactly; 40 and 32 both do (10 + 8 = 18).
- **Differentiation:** support: no exchange (e.g. 48 ÷ 4) with blocks / stretch: 96 ÷ 8 by choosing the partition.
- **Materials:** base-10 blocks, paper plates, place-value counters
- **Joy:** "Rock-pool share" with real shells or pasta shells.

### Lesson 70 — Dividing with exchange
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can divide when a ten must be exchanged for ones, e.g. 52 ÷ 4." (UK NC Y3 Mult/Div – written; CCSS 3.OA.7)
- **Hook:** "52 cards dealt to 4 players. We run out of tens!"
- **Key activity:** Concrete: share 5 tens among 4 — one ten left, exchange for ten ones, share 12 ones → 13 each. Pictorial: record exchanges. Abstract: partition 52 = 40 + 12.
- **Check for understanding:** "Explain why each player gets 13, not 12." → 1 ten each (10) plus 12 ones shared into 4 = 3 each → 13.
- **Differentiation:** support: exchanges with blocks and a partner / stretch: 84 ÷ 6 and explain the exchange.
- **Materials:** base-10 blocks, a pack of playing cards, paper plates
- **Joy:** Deal a real 52-card pack to 4 players to check.

### Lesson 71 — Remainders
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can divide and explain what the remainder means." (UK NC Y3 Mult/Div – remainders; CCSS 3.OA.3)
- **Hook:** Pinczes' A Remainder of One — a beetle soldier left out of the parade.
- **Key activity:** Concrete: group counters and see what is left. Pictorial: jumps on number line with a short final gap. Abstract: 25 ÷ 4 = 6 r 1.
- **Check for understanding:** "Can a remainder be bigger than the divisor? Why not?" → No: if 5 are left when grouping in 4s, you can make another group.
- **Differentiation:** support: counters for every question / stretch: find all numbers under 40 that leave remainder 1 when divided by 4 and describe the pattern.
- **Materials:** counters, number lines, A Remainder of One (Elinor J. Pinczes)
- **Joy:** Children march as the parade in rows and spot who's left over.

### Lesson 72 — Interpreting remainders in stories
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can decide whether to round up, round down or keep the remainder in a real problem." (UK NC Y3 Mult/Div – problems; CCSS 3.OA.3)
- **Hook:** "31 children, cars take 4 each. How many cars? Can we leave one child behind?"
- **Key activity:** Solve three story types with the same calculation (31 ÷ 4 = 7 r 3): cars needed (8), full boxes made (7), sweets left over (3). Discuss each answer.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why is 7 r 3 the right answer to only one of the three stories?" → The question decides: cars needed rounds up, full boxes rounds down, leftovers asks for the remainder.
- **Differentiation:** support: act out with counters and toy cars / stretch: write stories for 50 ÷ 8 that give answers 6, 7 and 2.
- **Materials:** counters, toy cars, story cards
- **Joy:** "Road trip" — plan a class trip and decide the number of cars/minibuses.

### Lesson 73 — Multiplication and division problems with bar models
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can use bar models to solve two-step problems involving multiplication and division." (Singapore bar model; CCSS 3.OA.8)
- **Hook:** "A school buys 6 boxes of 24 pencils and shares them among 4 classes."
- **Key activity:** Draw bar models in stages; choose methods; check with estimation.
- **Check for understanding:** "Which step comes first and why?" → Find the total (6 × 24 = 144) before sharing among 4 (36 each).
- **Differentiation:** support: one-step with smaller numbers / stretch: create a two-step problem whose answer is 48.
- **Materials:** paper strips, squared paper, base-10 blocks
- **Joy:** "Class supplies manager" — children plan an order for the classroom.

### Lesson 74 — Correspondence problems
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can find how many combinations there are, like 3 tops and 4 trousers." (UK NC Y3 Mult/Div – correspondence)
- **Hook:** Paper dolls with 3 T-shirts and 4 skirts/trousers — "How many outfits?"
- **Key activity:** Concrete: dress paper cut-outs. Pictorial: tables and tree diagrams. Abstract: 3 × 4 = 12 outfits.
- **Check for understanding:** "If we add one more T-shirt, how many more outfits? Why?" → 4 more (one per pair of trousers): 16.
- **Differentiation:** support: 2 × 3 with physical cut-outs / stretch: 3 ice cream flavours × 2 cones × 2 toppings.
- **Materials:** paper cut-out clothes, table templates
- **Joy:** "Ice-cream shop" — design every combination on a menu board.

### Lesson 75 — Multiplication and division investigation
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can investigate a multiplication puzzle and explain the pattern I found." (UK NC Y3 Mult/Div – reasoning)
- **Hook:** "Using digits 2, 3 and 4 once each, make ___ × ___ (2-digit × 1-digit). Which gives the largest product?"
- **Key activity:** Systematic listing of all six arrangements, grid calculation, and explanation.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why is 32 × 4 bigger than 42 × 3?" → 32 × 4 = 128 and 42 × 3 = 126; putting the largest digit as the multiplier multiplies both tens and ones by 4.
- **Differentiation:** support: 2 × 1-digit cards with blocks / stretch: generalise for any three digits.
- **Materials:** digit cards, grid templates, calculators for checking only
- **Joy:** "Product champion" leaderboard.

### Lesson 76 — End-of-unit check: bigger multiplication and division
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can show how I multiply and divide 2-digit numbers by 1-digit numbers." (Assessment)
- **Hook:** "Teach the robot" — explain one method to a toy robot in writing.
- **Key activity:** Written check: 3 multiplications, 3 divisions (one with remainder), one remainder story, one bar-model two-step problem.
- **Check for understanding:** "Show two different ways to calculate 24 × 5." → Grid (100 + 20 = 120) or 24 × 10 ÷ 2 = 120.
- **Differentiation:** support: blocks and grids available / stretch: extra "which is wrong?" reasoning item.
- **Materials:** check sheet, base-10 blocks, grid templates
- **Joy:** Children choose a toy robot "student" to teach at the end.

## Unit 6 — Money

**Essential question:** How do main units and small units (like dollars and cents) work together — and how do I know I got the right change?

**Key vocabulary:** currency, coin, note, main unit, subunit, cent, penny, total, cost, price, change, exchange, estimate, budget, cheaper, dearer

> Worked examples use a currency with 100 subunits (dollars and cents, euros and cents, pounds and pence, rupees and paise). Teach with **your own country's coins**; the maths is identical. See Teacher guidance for currencies with no subunit.

### Lesson 77 — Coins, notes and 100 subunits
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can explain that 100 cents make 1 dollar (or 100 pence make 1 pound) and show amounts in different ways." (UK NC Y3 Money; CCSS 2.MD.8)
- **Hook:** A coin collection from several countries — "What do they have in common?"
- **Key activity:** Concrete: exchange 100 one-cent coins (or 10 ten-cent coins) for a 1-unit coin. Pictorial: coin drawings in a place-value-style table. Abstract: write 3 dollars and 45 cents as $3.45 and say it aloud, noting the point separates the units (no decimal teaching yet).
- **Check for understanding:** "Is 5 dollars and 5 cents written $5.5 or $5.05? Why?" → $5.05; cents always need two digits because 100 cents make a dollar.
- **Differentiation:** support: amounts under one main unit only with real coins / stretch: show $2.60 with the fewest coins and notes, and with the most.
- **Materials:** real or realistic plastic coins and notes of the local currency, a world-coin collection, coin-sorting trays
- **Joy:** "World coin museum" — handle and sort coins from different countries.

### Lesson 78 — Adding amounts of money
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can add two amounts of money by adding main units and subunits separately and exchanging 100 subunits." (UK NC Y3 Money; CCSS 2.MD.8)
- **Hook:** A café menu: juice 1.45, muffin 2.80 — "Can I buy both with 5?"
- **Key activity:** Concrete: pay with coins, combine, exchange 100 cents for 1 dollar. Pictorial: partitioned bar model (dollars | cents). Abstract: add units, add cents, exchange (1.45 + 2.80 = 3 + 125 cents = 4.25).
- **Check for understanding:** "Why is 1 dollar 45 plus 2 dollars 80 not '3 dollars 125 cents' in the final answer?" → 100 cents exchange for 1 dollar, so it is 4 dollars 25 cents.
- **Differentiation:** support: whole-unit amounts, then cents only below 100 / stretch: three items and a budget limit.
- **Materials:** play money, café menu cards, bar-model strips
- **Joy:** "Class café" — children take orders and total the bill.

### Lesson 79 — Subtracting money and finding change
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can find change by counting up from the price." (UK NC Y3 Money – change; CCSS 2.MD.8)
- **Hook:** "I pay with a 5 note for a book costing 3.65. How much change?"
- **Key activity:** Concrete: shopkeeper counts up with coins (3.65 → 3.70 → 4.00 → 5.00). Pictorial: number-line jumps. Abstract: record the jumps and add them (5 + 30 + 100 cents = 1.35).
- **Check for understanding:** "Why is counting up easier than subtracting 5.00 − 3.65 in columns?" → Jumping to round amounts avoids exchanging through zeros.
- **Differentiation:** support: whole-unit prices and 10-cent steps / stretch: paying with 20 for items totalling 13.47.
- **Materials:** play money, price labels, blank number lines
- **Joy:** "Shopkeeper challenge" — shopkeepers must count change aloud to customers.

### Lesson 80 — Money problems with bar models
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can solve one- and two-step money problems using bar models." (Singapore bar model; CCSS 2.MD.8)
- **Hook:** "Ana has 10. She buys a kite for 6.50 and a ball for 2.75. Does she have enough left for an ice cream costing 1.20?"
- **Key activity:** Bar models for totals, change and comparisons; decide the steps; check by estimation.
- **Check for understanding:** "How does rounding 6.50 and 2.75 to 7 and 3 help?" → Total about 10, so very little left — likely not enough (exact change 0.75).
- **Differentiation:** support: whole-unit amounts / stretch: problems comparing two shops' prices.
- **Materials:** paper strips, play money, problem cards
- **Joy:** "Budget birthday party" — plan a party within a fixed budget.

### Lesson 81 — Multiplying and dividing money
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can find the cost of several identical items and share an amount equally." (UK NC Y3 Money; CCSS 3.OA.3)
- **Hook:** "Four cinema tickets cost 32. How much is one? How much would 8 cost?"
- **Key activity:** Unit bar models; use 4× and 8× facts; convert to subunits where needed (3 × 45 cents = 135 cents = 1.35).
- **Check for understanding:** "If 4 pens cost 2.00, how can you find the cost of 8 pens without finding one pen?" → 8 is double 4, so double the cost: 4.00.
- **Differentiation:** support: whole-unit amounts with coins / stretch: best-value comparison (3 for 1.20 or 4 for 1.50).
- **Materials:** play money, paper strips, supermarket leaflets
- **Joy:** "Best-buy detectives" with real supermarket leaflets.

### Lesson 82 — Money around the world
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can compare prices in one currency and explain why different countries use different money." (UK NC Y3 Money – application; global citizenship link)
- **Hook:** "A snack costs 2 dollars in one country and 150 rupees in another. Which is more expensive?"
- **Key activity:** Discuss currencies (euro, rupee, yen, shilling, peso, dollar); compare prices *within* one currency only; make a class chart of "what 5 buys" at a local shop. (Exchange rates are not taught — emphasise we can't compare different currencies by number alone.)
- **Check for understanding:** "Why can't we say 150 rupees is more than 2 dollars just because 150 > 2?" → They are different units; you need to know how many rupees make a dollar.
- **Differentiation:** support: pictures of coins from 3 countries / stretch: research one currency's coins and present its subunit.
- **Materials:** world banknote pictures, a globe, local shop price list
- **Joy:** Design your own currency with a name, coins and a note.

### Lesson 83 — Budgeting project (part 1)
- **Duration:** 30 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can plan spending within a budget and record my choices." (UK NC Y3 Money – application; life-skills link)
- **Hook:** "You have 20 to run a class stall for a charity. What will you buy?"
- **Key activity:** In pairs, choose items from a price list, total them, check within budget, and adjust. Record with a table and bar model.
- **Check for understanding:** "Your plan costs 21.50. What two ways could you fix it?" → Remove an item worth at least 1.50, or swap for cheaper items.
- **Differentiation:** support: whole-unit prices, 10 budget / stretch: include a "profit" target (sell for more than you spent).
- **Materials:** price lists, play money, planning tables
- **Joy:** Planning a real charity stall (lemonade or crafts).

### Lesson 84 — Budgeting project (part 2): the stall
- **Duration:** 30 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can buy and sell, give change and count what we made." (UK NC Y3 Money – application)
- **Hook:** The stall opens — real or pretend customers arrive.
- **Key activity:** Run the stall with play money; record sales in a tally; count takings; calculate profit (takings − cost).
- **Check for understanding:** "We spent 12 and took 19.50. How much profit, and how do you know?" → 7.50, because profit is takings minus spending.
- **Differentiation:** support: fixed whole-unit prices / stretch: price change halfway (sale) and recalculate.

### Lesson 85 — End-of-unit check: money
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can show I can add, subtract and find change with money." (Assessment)

- **Materials:** 100 g and 1 kg weights, balance scales, food packets with masses printed
- **Joy:** Weigh school bags and convert — who carries the heaviest?

### Lesson 153 — Capacity in millilitres and litres
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can measure capacity in ml and l with a measuring jug." (UK NC Y3 Measure – capacity; CCSS 3.MD.2)
- **Hook:** "How many cups of water fill a 1 litre bottle? Guess first."
- **Key activity:** Concrete: pour water between cups, bottles and 1 l jugs. Pictorial: read printed jugs. Abstract: record in ml; 1 l = 1000 ml.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why must you read the jug at eye level?" → Looking from above or below makes the water level look higher or lower than it is.
- **Differentiation:** support: jugs marked in 100 ml / stretch: jugs marked in 50 ml or 25 ml.
- **Materials:** measuring jugs, bottles and cups of different shapes, water trays, towels
- **Joy:** Outdoor water station — "Which container holds the most? Tall or wide?"

### Lesson 154 — Converting and comparing capacities
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can convert between litres and millilitres and compare capacities." (UK NC Y3 Measure – conversion)
- **Hook:** "A bottle holds 250 ml. How many bottles to fill a 1 litre jug? A 2 l jug?"
- **Key activity:** Concrete: fill 1 l with 250 ml cups. Pictorial: bar model — 1000 ml as four 250 ml units. Abstract: conversions and comparisons (1 l 200 ml vs 1020 ml).
- **Check for understanding:** "Why do 4 cups of 250 ml fill a 1 l jug?" → 4 × 250 = 1000 ml, which is 1 l.
- **Differentiation:** support: whole litres and half litres / stretch: mixed-unit ordering and "how many 200 ml cups in 3 l?"
- **Materials:** measuring jugs, 250 ml cups, bottles
- **Joy:** Make a jug of lemonade or fruit squash to exact measures.

### Lesson 155 — Adding and subtracting mass and capacity
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can add and subtract masses and capacities, converting units first." (UK NC Y3 Measure – calculation)
- **Hook:** "My suitcase limit is 20 kg. My case is 23 kg 500 g. What must I take out?"
- **Key activity:** Bar models; convert to one unit; calculate with mental or column methods.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why convert 1 kg 200 g to 1200 g before subtracting 450 g?" → You can only subtract amounts in the same unit: 1200 − 450 = 750 g.
- **Differentiation:** support: same-unit problems with scales / stretch: two-step mixed-unit problems.
- **Materials:** suitcase or bag, bathroom and kitchen scales, jugs, paper strips
- **Joy:** "Pack the suitcase" — choose items (with printed masses) to stay under the limit.

### Lesson 156 — Scaling recipes
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can double and halve quantities in a recipe." (UK NC Y3 Measure – problems)
- **Hook:** A recipe for 4 people — "Our class has 8 guests coming!"
- **Key activity:** Concrete: measure ingredients for the original recipe. Pictorial: bar models doubling each quantity. Abstract: double and halve g and ml amounts.
- **Check for understanding:** "4 people need 250 g of flour. How much for 8 people and how much for 2? Explain." → 500 g (double) and 125 g (half).
- **Differentiation:** support: doubling only with easy numbers / stretch: a recipe for 6 (1.5 times — find half and add).
- **Materials:** recipe cards (e.g. flatbread or pancakes), kitchen scales, measuring jugs
- **Joy:** Cook the recipe (with an adult) and share it.

### Lesson 157 — Temperature
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can read a thermometer in °C and compare temperatures." (UK NC Y3 Measure; science link)
- **Hook:** Today's temperatures in five cities on different continents.
- **Key activity:** Concrete: read thermometers in warm and cold water and outdoors. Pictorial: thermometer scales in 1s, 2s and 5s. Abstract: compare and find differences; water freezes at 0 °C and boils at 100 °C at sea level.
- **Check for understanding:** "Nairobi is 24 °C and Oslo is 6 °C. Which is warmer, and by how much?" → Nairobi, by 18 degrees.
- **Differentiation:** support: thermometers marked in 1s / stretch: scales in 2s and 5s, and temperatures below zero read from a picture.
- **Materials:** classroom thermometers, cups of warm and cold water (safe temperature), world weather data
- **Joy:** "World weather reporter" — present the forecast to the class like a TV presenter.

### Lesson 158 — Measurement investigation
- **Duration:** 30 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can plan and carry out a fair measurement investigation." (UK NC Y3 Measure – application; science link)
- **Hook:** "Which paper towel soaks up the most water?"
- **Key activity:** Plan a fair test in groups; measure water absorbed (ml left in the jug); record in a table; present in a bar chart.
- **Check for understanding:** "How did you make your test fair, and why does it matter?" → Same size towel, same water amount, same time — so only the towel type changes.
- **Differentiation:** support: guided plan with steps / stretch: design your own measurement question and test it.
- **Materials:** three brands of paper towel, measuring jugs, water, timers, squared paper
- **Joy:** A satisfyingly messy experiment with a class "winner" towel.

### Lesson 159 — End-of-unit check: mass and capacity
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can show I can measure, read scales and convert mass and capacity." (Assessment)
- **Hook:** "Kitchen challenge" stations.
- **Key activity:** Practical stations (weigh, pour, read a thermometer) and written items (read scales with different intervals, convert, a bar-model problem).
- **Check for understanding:** "A scale goes up in 200s with one small mark between each label. What is each mark worth? Why?" → 100, because one mark splits each 200 into two equal spaces.
- **Differentiation:** support: equipment available, questions read aloud / stretch: extra mixed-unit problem.
- **Materials:** scales, jugs, thermometers, check sheet
- **Joy:** Earn the "chef" badge.

## Unit 13 — Year-end consolidation and problem solving

**Essential question:** How can I use everything I know to solve problems I have never seen before?

**Key vocabulary:** strategy, reason, explain, prove, systematic, pattern, estimate, check, efficient, bar model, work backwards, generalise

### Lesson 160 — Place value review: number of the day
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can use place value to describe, compare and round any number to 1000." (Retrieval)
- **Hook:** "Number of the day: 608 — how many true things can we say?"
- **Key activity:** Build 608 with base-10, then list facts: partitions, 10/100 more, rounding, odd/even, place on a line, multiples. Pairs repeat with their own number.
- **Check for understanding:** "What is the value of each digit in 608, and what would happen without the 0?" → 600, 0 tens, 8 ones; without the 0 it would be 68.
- **Differentiation:** support: base-10 blocks and a prompt card / stretch: choose a four-digit number and include thousands.
- **Materials:** base-10 blocks, number cards, large paper
- **Joy:** Each child's "number of the day" poster goes on the class wall.

### Lesson 161 — Addition and subtraction review: method duel
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can choose an efficient method to add and subtract and explain my choice." (Retrieval)
- **Hook:** "Method duel": two children, two methods, one calculation — who is faster and still right?
- **Key activity:** Mixed calculation cards; for each, decide mental or column, solve, then check with the inverse.
- **Check for understanding:** "Which method for 1000 − 199, and why?" → Mental: 1000 − 200 + 1 = 801, because 199 is 1 less than 200.
- **Differentiation:** support: base-10 blocks and strategy menu / stretch: missing-digit column puzzles.
- **Materials:** calculation cards, base-10 blocks, strategy menu poster
- **Joy:** The duel with a class commentator.

### Lesson 162 — Multiplication and division review: tables tournament
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can recall the 2, 3, 4, 5, 8 and 10 tables and use them in problems." (Retrieval)
- **Hook:** "Tables tournament" — knockout rounds with dice.
- **Key activity:** Fluency games, then bar-model problems including one with a remainder.
- **Check for understanding:** "How does knowing 8 × 7 help with 80 × 7?" → 8 × 7 = 56, and 80 is ten times 8, so 80 × 7 = 560.
- **Differentiation:** support: a tables grid for checking / stretch: two-step problems with remainders to interpret.
- **Materials:** dice (6- and 8-sided), fact cards, paper strips
- **Joy:** Tournament bracket on the board.

### Lesson 163 — Fractions review: fraction pizza shop
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can use fractions of shapes and amounts, equivalence and comparison." (Retrieval)
- **Hook:** "The fraction pizza shop is open — customers order in fractions!"
- **Key activity:** Role play: customers order (e.g. "3/8 of a pizza", "1/4 of 12 olives"); cooks build with fraction circles and counters; the cashier checks.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why is 1/3 of 12 greater than 1/4 of 12?" → Thirds are bigger parts than quarters: 4 is more than 3.
- **Differentiation:** support: fraction circles and counters / stretch: orders with equivalent fractions ("2/4 please — is that the same as half?").
- **Materials:** fraction circles, counters, paper-plate pizzas
- **Joy:** Running the pizza shop.

### Lesson 164 — Measurement review: measure the school
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can choose units and tools to measure length, mass, capacity and time." (Retrieval)
- **Hook:** "The head teacher needs facts about our school — measure everything!"
- **Key activity:** Groups visit stations outdoors and indoors: measure a path (m), weigh a bag (kg/g), fill a bucket (l), time a run (s), and record with units.
- **Check for understanding:** "Which unit would you use for the mass of an apple, and why not kg?" → Grams; an apple is much lighter than 1 kg, so grams give a useful number.
- **Differentiation:** support: tools chosen for them / stretch: convert each measurement to a second unit.
- **Materials:** tape measures, trundle wheel, scales, buckets and jugs, stopwatches
- **Joy:** Outdoor measuring expedition.

### Lesson 165 — Problem-solving strategy: work systematically
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can solve a problem by listing possibilities in an organised way." (Reasoning)
- **Hook:** "How many ways can you pay exactly 50 cents using only 10c and 20c coins?"
- **Key activity:** Concrete: coins. Pictorial: an ordered table (most 20c coins first). Abstract: find all solutions and prove none are missing.
- **Check for understanding:** "How do you know you have found every way?" → I started with the most 20c coins (2) and reduced by one each time: 2, 1, 0 → three ways.
- **Differentiation:** support: coins and a partly filled table / stretch: add 5c coins and find all ways to make 50c.
- **Materials:** coins, recording tables
- **Joy:** "Coin puzzle" challenge board with class solutions.

### Lesson 166 — Problem-solving strategy: work backwards
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can solve a problem by working backwards with inverse operations." (Reasoning)
- **Hook:** "I think of a number, add 25, double it and get 90. I'm a mind reader — are you?"
- **Key activity:** Concrete: function-machine boxes. Pictorial: bar models and arrow diagrams. Abstract: undo each step in reverse order.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why do we halve before subtracting 25?" → We undo the last step first: 90 ÷ 2 = 45, then 45 − 25 = 20.
- **Differentiation:** support: one-step machines / stretch: create a three-step puzzle for a partner.
- **Materials:** cardboard function machines, number cards, paper strips
- **Joy:** "Mind reader" — children astonish families at home with the trick.

### Lesson 167 — Problem-solving strategy: spot a pattern
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can find and extend a pattern and use it to predict." (Reasoning)
- **Hook:** Lolly-stick squares in a row: 1 square uses 4 sticks, 2 squares use 7...
- **Key activity:** Concrete: build the first four terms. Pictorial: table of squares vs sticks. Abstract: predict the 10th term and describe the rule in words.
- **Check for understanding:** "How many sticks for 10 squares in a row? Explain." → 31: the first square needs 4 and each extra square needs 3 more (4 + 9 × 3).
- **Differentiation:** support: build every step to 6 / stretch: triangles in a row, and explain why the rule differs.
- **Materials:** lolly sticks, recording tables
- **Joy:** Build the longest pattern the tables will hold.

### Lesson 168 — Maths in the real world project: plan the picnic
- **Duration:** 30 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can use maths to plan a real event within a budget and a timetable." (Application)
- **Hook:** "You are in charge of the end-of-year picnic."
- **Key activity:** Groups plan food quantities (multiplication), cost (money), schedule (time) and a data survey of favourite games; present the plan.
- **Check for understanding:** "How did you make sure you didn't go over budget?" → We totalled costs, compared with the budget and swapped items where needed.
- **Differentiation:** support: fixed menu with 3 choices / stretch: compare two suppliers and justify the best value.
- **Materials:** price lists, blank timetables, survey sheets, play money
- **Joy:** The picnic actually happens using the winning plan.

## End-of-year mastery checks

1. **Place value to 1000** — builds, reads and writes any three-digit number (including zeros), partitions it two ways, and rounds to the nearest 10 and 100. *Seen:* builds 607 and 670 with base-10 and explains the difference.
2. **Column addition and subtraction** — adds and subtracts three-digit numbers with exchanges (including across zeros) and checks with the inverse. *Seen:* solves 703 − 246 and explains each exchange with blocks.
3. **Bar models** — draws part–whole and comparison bar models for one- and two-step problems. *Seen:* models and solves "Asha has 245 stamps, 80 more than Ben..."
4. **Times tables** — recalls 2, 3, 4, 5, 8 and 10 times tables and matching division facts within about 3 seconds each, and derives unknown facts (8× by doubling 4×). *Seen:* a 3-minute mixed sprint and an explanation of 7 × 8.
5. **Bigger multiplication and division** — multiplies 2-digit × 1-digit (grid/partitioning) and divides 2-digit ÷ 1-digit with remainders, interpreting remainders in context. *Seen:* 36 × 4 and "45 eggs in boxes of 6."
6. **Fractions** — finds unit and non-unit fractions of amounts, recognises tenths, shows equivalence with a fraction wall, compares, and adds/subtracts same-denominator fractions within one whole. *Seen:* 3/4 of 28; explains why 1/8 < 1/4.
7. **Money** — adds and subtracts amounts and finds change by counting up. *Seen:* change from a 10 note for 6.35.
8. **Measurement** — measures in mm/cm/m, g/kg, ml/l; reads scales with various intervals; converts; finds perimeter of rectangles and rectilinear shapes. *Seen:* perimeter of an L-shape with a missing side.
9. **Time** — tells time to the minute (including Roman numerals), converts 12- and 24-hour times, and finds durations. *Seen:* the duration from 10:40 to 12:15.
10. **Shape and data** — identifies right, acute and obtuse angles, parallel and perpendicular lines, describes 2-D and 3-D shapes, and reads/draws scaled bar charts and pictograms. *Seen:* reads a bar between gridlines on a scale of 5.

## Teacher guidance

**Common misconceptions to watch for (and the fix)**
- *Digits, not values* — 23 × 4 written as "812"; 96 ÷ 4 as 9 ÷ 4 and 6 ÷ 4. Fix: always say the value ("twenty times four") and use base-10 or the grid.
- *Smaller-from-larger* in column subtraction (52 − 27 → 35). Fix: build only the first number; "not enough ones — exchange a ten."
- *"Add a zero" to multiply by 10.* Fix: digits move one place left because each is worth ten times more; this rule will fail with decimals in Year 3.
- *Bigger denominator = bigger fraction.* Fix: fraction tiles side by side; more equal parts means smaller parts.
- *Adding denominators* (2/5 + 1/5 = 3/10). Fix: the bar is still in fifths; count the fifths.
- *1 m = 10 cm.* Fix: lay out a metre stick; 100 cm fit in 1 m and 10 mm in 1 cm.
- *Measuring from the end of the ruler instead of 0.* Fix: always place 0 at the start of the object.
- *3 p.m. = 13:00.* Fix: 1 p.m. = 13:00; add 12 to the p.m. hour.
- *Longer arms = bigger angle.* Fix: angle-makers — lengthen the strips; the turn doesn't change.
- *Reading every gridline as 1.* Fix: read the scale interval first.

**Rule edge cases stated exactly:** a number exactly halfway (45, 350) rounds up by convention; a remainder must be smaller than the divisor; 12 noon is written 12:00 in 24-hour time and midnight is 00:00; many clocks use IIII instead of IV for 4 — both are seen; leap years in this period are every 4 years (2024, 2028), with century exceptions taught later.

**Money outside 100-subunit currencies.** Where the local currency has no subunit in daily use (e.g. Japanese yen), use whole-number prices up to 1000 and teach "change" identically; introduce a 100-subunit currency (euro or dollar) as a travel context so every child meets the two-part notation.

**Vertical connections.** Year 1 gave two-digit place value, 2/5/10 tables and simple fractions; if a child is shaky on bonds to 20 or two-digit regrouping, use the flex slots to re-teach before Unit 3. Year 3 immediately extends place value to 10,000, column methods to four digits, all tables to 12 × 12 (the 6, 7, 9, 11 and 12 tables build directly on this year's doubling and derived-fact work), decimals from tenths, and area from perimeter — so tenths, derived facts and the bar model are the three things never to rush.

**Daily fluency outside the lesson (5 minutes).** Counting in 3s, 4s, 8s, 50s and 100s; number bonds to 100 and 1000; times-table songs; "number of the day." Retrieval little and often beats long revision sessions.

**Assessment is by looking and listening.** Use the checks for understanding to decide who needs a same-day "keep-up" session; the end-of-unit checks inform re-teaching, never ranking.
