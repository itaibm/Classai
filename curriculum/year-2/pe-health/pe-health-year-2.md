# Year 2 PE & Health (ages 7–8) — Scope & Sequence

> Part of **the best international primary school** (see [`../../../SCHOOL-CHARTER.md`](../../../SCHOOL-CHARTER.md)).
> Built to [`../../PLANNING-BRIEF.md`](../../PLANNING-BRIEF.md). Drawn from the UK National
> Curriculum KS2 Physical Education (including swimming and water safety), UK Relationships and
> Health Education guidance, US SHAPE America grade-level outcomes (Grade 2–3) and the WHO
> physical-activity guidance for children (an average of 60 minutes of moderate-to-vigorous
> activity a day).

## Year overview

Year 2 PE & Health turns the fundamental movements of Year 1 (running, jumping, throwing,
catching, balancing) into **real game, gymnastics and dance skills**, while giving children the
knowledge and habits to look after their **bodies and minds**. It is **mostly delivered by a
teacher, coach or parent in person and is active from start to finish** — the Classai tutor's role
is to explain, demonstrate on video, count, time and help children reflect, never to replace
movement with screen time. Each practical lesson follows **warm-up → skill teaching → practice
game → cool-down and reflection**; health lessons are shorter, discussion-based and practical.

**How the year sequences.** Term 1 builds **fitness and fundamental movement**, then applies it to
**invasion games** (passing, moving into space, attacking and defending) and a **health unit on
food, water, sleep and activity** (linked to Science Unit 3). Term 2 is **gymnastics** (balances,
rolls, jumps and sequences on the floor and apparatus) and **dance from around the world**. Term 3
brings **striking and fielding and net games**, **water safety and swimming** (pool sessions where
facilities allow; dry-land water safety for all), a **health unit on body safety, feelings, hygiene
and getting help**, and **athletics and outdoor adventure**, ending with **sports day**.

**What Year 2 assumes from Year 1 (ages 6–7):** running, hopping, skipping and jumping with control;
throwing and catching a large ball; simple balances; moving safely in space; following simple game
rules; naming healthy foods and knowing that exercise and sleep matter.

**What Year 3 (ages 8–9) relies on from this year:** accurate passing and receiving in small-sided
games; a 6–8 movement gymnastics sequence; a performed dance with counts; basic striking; water
confidence and floating (swimming distance targets continue); and the language to talk about
feelings, body safety and healthy choices.

**Safety first.** Every practical lesson needs a checked space (no obstacles, safe surface), a
warm-up, appropriate footwear or bare feet for gymnastics, hair tied back, no jewellery, water
available, and adult supervision. Pool lessons need qualified instructors and correct ratios.

## Unit 1 — Fit to move: fundamental movement and fitness

**Essential question:** What happens to my body when I move — and how can I move better?

**Key vocabulary:** warm-up, cool-down, heart rate, breathing, stamina, speed, agility, balance, coordination, strength, flexibility, space, personal best

### Lesson 1 — Warming up and moving safely in space
- **Duration:** 30 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can warm up properly and move safely in a shared space." (UK NC KS2 PE; SHAPE S1.E1)
- **Hook:** "Why do cars need to warm up in the cold — and do our muscles too?"
- **Key activity:** Adult-led: pulse-raiser (jogging, side-steps, skips), dynamic stretches (arm circles, leg swings), "traffic lights" movement game (green = jog, amber = walk, red = freeze); children feel heart and breath change.
- **Check for understanding:** "What changed in your body during the warm-up, and why does it help?" → Heart beat faster and breathing deepened; muscles get warm and ready, which helps prevent injury.
- **Differentiation:** support: walk/jog version with a partner / stretch: lead the warm-up for a small group.
- **Materials:** cones to mark the space, whistle; **safety:** check floor/field for hazards, trainers on, water bottles available
- **Joy:** "Traffic lights" with surprise signals (purple = hop!).

### Lesson 2 — Running well: speed and technique
- **Duration:** 30 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can run with good arm and leg action and change speed." (UK NC KS2 PE – running; SHAPE S1.E1)
- **Hook:** Slow-motion video of a sprinter — "What are their arms doing?"
- **Key activity:** Teach running technique (arms bent, driving forward and back; head up; on the balls of the feet); relay races over 20 m; acceleration/deceleration drills.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why do sprinters pump their arms forward and back, not across their bodies?" → It helps drive the body forward and keeps it balanced.
- **Differentiation:** support: practise arm action standing still first / stretch: time a 20 m run and try to beat it.
- **Materials:** cones, stopwatch, relay batons; **safety:** run in lanes, stop well before walls
- **Joy:** Relay races.

### Lesson 3 — Jumping: for height and distance
- **Duration:** 30 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can take off and land safely when jumping for height and distance." (UK NC KS2 PE – jumping; SHAPE S1.E4)
- **Hook:** "How far can a kangaroo jump? How far can you?"
- **Key activity:** Teach take-off (two feet, one foot), arm swing, soft landing (bend knees); standing long jump and "jump and reach" for height; measure with tape measures (Maths link).
- **Check for understanding:** "Why do we bend our knees when we land?" → To absorb the force and protect our joints.
- **Differentiation:** support: jump onto a mat marker / stretch: combine hop, step and jump.
- **Materials:** mats, tape measures, chalk; **safety:** non-slip landing area
- **Joy:** "Kangaroo challenge" with personal bests.

### Lesson 4 — Throwing and catching
- **Duration:** 30 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can throw overarm and underarm accurately and catch with 'soft hands'." (UK NC KS2 PE; SHAPE S1.E13–16)
- **Hook:** Target hoops at different distances.
- **Key activity:** Teach underarm (step with opposite foot, swing), overarm (side-on, elbow high), catching (eyes on ball, hands ready, pull in); partner practice; target games.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why step forward with the opposite foot when you throw?" → It adds power and keeps you balanced.
- **Differentiation:** support: larger, softer balls; shorter distances / stretch: smaller balls, catching one-handed.
- **Materials:** bean bags, tennis balls, soft balls, hoops
- **Joy:** "Target splat" with points.

### Lesson 5 — Agility and coordination circuit
- **Duration:** 30 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can move quickly and change direction with control." (UK NC KS2 PE – agility; SHAPE S1.E2)
- **Hook:** "Can you move like a football player dodging a defender?"
- **Key activity:** Circuit stations: ladder footwork, cone zig-zag, skipping rope, balance beam, reaction ball; 1 minute each; record repetitions.
- **Check for understanding:** "What helps you change direction quickly without falling?" → Bend knees, lower your body, small quick steps.
- **Differentiation:** support: walk the stations first / stretch: timed challenge.
- **Materials:** agility ladders, cones, skipping ropes, low beam, reaction balls, stopwatch
- **Joy:** Circuit with music.

### Lesson 6 — Exercise and my heart (Science link)
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can explain how exercise affects my heart and breathing and why this is healthy." (Health; Science link)
- **Hook:** Measure resting pulse, then after 1 minute of star jumps.
- **Key activity:** Count pulse for 15 seconds × 4; compare; discuss why the heart is a muscle that gets stronger with exercise; recovery time.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why does your heart rate go back down after you stop?" → Muscles need less oxygen once they stop working hard.
- **Differentiation:** support: partner counts pulse / stretch: compare two activities.
- **Materials:** stopwatches, recording sheets
- **Joy:** "Heart detectives."

### Lesson 7 — Skipping and rhythm
- **Duration:** 30 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can skip with a rope continuously and learn a playground skipping rhyme." (UK NC KS2 PE; coordination)
- **Hook:** Playground skipping rhymes from around the world.
- **Key activity:** Individual rope skipping; long-rope group skipping with rhymes; count personal bests.
- **Check for understanding:** "What's the secret to skipping continuously?" → Small, even jumps and turning the rope with wrists in a steady rhythm.
- **Differentiation:** support: jump over a still rope, then a swinging one / stretch: tricks (crossover, double bounce).
- **Materials:** individual and long ropes
- **Joy:** Skipping rhymes.

### Lesson 8 — Fitness challenge and unit check
- **Duration:** 30 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can set a personal best and show safe warm-up and technique." (Assessment by observation)
- **Hook:** "Personal-best Olympics."
- **Key activity:** Children lead their own warm-up in groups; stations: 20 m run, standing jump, throw to target, skipping count; record personal bests to repeat in Unit 9.
- **Check for understanding:** Teacher observes technique; asks "Which skill improved most since Lesson 2, and why?" → Names a skill and a reason (practice, technique).
- **Differentiation:** support: fewer stations / stretch: coach a partner.
- **Materials:** stopwatches, tape measures, targets, ropes, record cards
- **Joy:** Personal-best certificates.

**End-of-unit check:** Lead a safe warm-up and demonstrate good technique in running, jumping and throwing (teacher observation with a simple checklist).

## Unit 2 — Invasion games: pass, move, score

**Essential question:** How can a team work together to keep the ball and score?

**Key vocabulary:** attack, defend, pass, receive, dribble, space, possession, marking, intercept, target, team, tactic, fair play

### Lesson 9 — Passing and receiving with hands
- **Duration:** 30 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can make a chest pass and a bounce pass to a partner and receive safely." (UK NC KS2 PE – invasion games; SHAPE S1.E16)
- **Hook:** "How many passes can your pair make in 60 seconds?"
- **Key activity:** Adult demonstration; partner passing; "piggy in the middle" in threes.
- **Check for understanding:** "When is a bounce pass better than a chest pass?" → When a defender is in the way — the ball can go under their arms.
- **Differentiation:** support: larger ball and shorter distance / stretch: pass on the move.
- **Materials:** netballs or basketballs, cones
- **Joy:** 60-second pass count challenge.

### Lesson 10 — Moving into space
- **Duration:** 30 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can move into space to receive a pass." (UK NC KS2 PE – tactics)
- **Hook:** "Where would you stand to get the ball?"
- **Key activity:** "Space invaders" game: 3 v 1 keep-ball in a square; freeze to discuss where the space is.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why should you move away from a defender to receive?" → So the passer has a clear line and the defender can't intercept.
- **Differentiation:** support: 4 v 1 / stretch: 3 v 2.
- **Materials:** cones, bibs, balls
- **Joy:** Keep-ball.

### Lesson 11 — Dribbling with hands (basketball skills)
- **Duration:** 30 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can dribble a ball with my hand while moving and looking up." (UK NC KS2 PE; SHAPE S1.E17)
- **Hook:** Basketball highlights from a professional women's or men's game.
- **Key activity:** Stationary dribble with fingertips; walking dribble; cone slalom; "dribble tag."
- **Check for understanding:** "Why should you look up when dribbling?" → To see teammates, defenders and space.
- **Differentiation:** support: larger bouncy ball, stationary / stretch: switch hands while moving.
- **Materials:** basketballs, cones
- **Joy:** Dribble tag.

### Lesson 12 — Dribbling with feet (football skills)
- **Duration:** 30 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can dribble a football with close control using both feet." (UK NC KS2 PE; SHAPE S1.E18)
- **Hook:** "Can you keep the ball 'glued' to your feet?"
- **Key activity:** Dribble with inside/outside of the foot; stop the ball; "traffic lights" dribble; 1 v 1 to a gate.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why use small touches when dribbling in a crowd?" → The ball stays close so you keep control.
- **Differentiation:** support: walking pace / stretch: weaker-foot challenge.
- **Materials:** footballs, cones, bibs
- **Joy:** Gate-dribbling challenge.

### Lesson 13 — Passing with feet and shooting at a target
- **Duration:** 30 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can pass along the ground and shoot at a target." (UK NC KS2 PE)
- **Hook:** Target goals made from cones.
- **Key activity:** Inside-foot pass (non-kicking foot beside the ball); passing gates; shooting at small goals.
- **Check for understanding:** "Where should your standing foot go for an accurate pass?" → Beside the ball, pointing at the target.
- **Differentiation:** support: stationary ball / stretch: first-time pass.
- **Materials:** footballs, cones, small goals
- **Joy:** "Gate points" game.

### Lesson 14 — Defending: marking and intercepting
- **Duration:** 30 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can mark a player and try to intercept fairly." (UK NC KS2 PE – tactics)
- **Hook:** "Be a shadow."
- **Key activity:** Shadowing game; 2 v 2 with a defender marking; rules of fair play (no contact).
- **Check for understanding:** "Where should a defender stand to stop a pass?" → Between the attacker and the ball, or in the passing line.
- **Differentiation:** support: 2 v 1 / stretch: switch attack/defence quickly.
- **Materials:** bibs, balls, cones
- **Joy:** "Shadow" game.

### Lesson 15 — Tag rugby: running with the ball
- **Duration:** 30 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can run with the ball, pass backwards and remove a tag." (UK NC KS2 PE – invasion games)
- **Hook:** Tag belts on — "No tackling, just tags!"
- **Key activity:** Tag-belt games; backward passing rule; small-sided tag rugby.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why must you pass backwards in rugby?" → It's a rule of the game; it makes teammates support from behind.
- **Differentiation:** support: walking version / stretch: 4 v 3.
- **Materials:** tag belts, rugby balls, cones; **safety:** no contact, tags only
- **Joy:** Tag rugby.

### Lesson 16 — Small-sided games: using tactics
- **Duration:** 30 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can use a simple tactic in a small-sided game." (UK NC KS2 PE – tactics)
- **Hook:** "Your team has a secret plan."
- **Key activity:** 3 v 3 or 4 v 4 end-ball or mini-football; teams plan one tactic (spread out, pass quickly); timeouts to review.
- **Check for understanding:** "What tactic did your team use, and did it work?" → Names it and evaluates.
- **Differentiation:** support: fewer rules / stretch: two tactics.
- **Materials:** bibs, balls, cones
- **Joy:** Team huddles.

### Lesson 17 — Fair play and leadership
- **Duration:** 30 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can play fairly, be a good winner and loser, and help run a game." (UK NC KS2 PE; Life skills link)
- **Hook:** Video of famous sporting kindness (e.g. athletes helping a fallen rival).
- **Key activity:** Children act as referees, coaches and players in rotation; reflect on fair play.
- **Check for understanding:** "What does a good loser say and do?" → Congratulate the other team, shake hands, think about how to improve.
- **Differentiation:** support: player role / stretch: referee role.
- **Materials:** bibs, whistles, balls
- **Joy:** Being the referee.

### Lesson 18 — Invasion games festival and unit check
- **Duration:** 30 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can show passing, moving into space and defending in games." (Assessment by observation)
- **Hook:** Mini-tournament.
- **Key activity:** Round-robin of small-sided games; teacher observes skills with a checklist.
- **Check for understanding:** "What was your best moment and what skill did it show?" → Links a moment to a skill.
- **Differentiation:** support: mixed-ability teams with support roles / stretch: captain role.
- **Materials:** bibs, balls, cones, score board
- **Joy:** Tournament.

**End-of-unit check:** Teacher observation in small-sided games: accurate passing, moving into space, dribbling with control and fair defending.

## Unit 3 — Health: food, water, sleep and activity

**Essential question:** What does my body need every day to be healthy — and how can I make good choices?

**Key vocabulary:** healthy, balanced diet, energy, hydration, sugar, sleep, routine, screen time, active, habit, choice

### Lesson 19 — Fuel for my body
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can explain how food gives my body energy and nutrients." (Health; Science link)
- **Hook:** A car with no fuel vs a full tank.
- **Key activity:** Recap food groups from Science; plan an "active day" menu; discuss treats as sometimes foods.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why is breakfast important before a PE lesson?" → It gives energy for the body and brain.
- **Differentiation:** support: picture menu / stretch: explain why some foods give longer-lasting energy (wholegrains).
- **Materials:** food pictures, plate templates
- **Joy:** "Menu designer."

### Lesson 20 — Water and hydration
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can explain why my body needs water and how to stay hydrated." (Health)
- **Hook:** A wilted plant and a watered plant.
- **Key activity:** Discuss that a large part of the body is water; signs of thirst; compare sugar in drinks (sugar-cube model); water-bottle habit chart.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why is water better than fizzy drinks after exercise?" → It hydrates without sugar that harms teeth.
- **Differentiation:** support: picture sort of drinks / stretch: read sugar on drinks labels.
- **Materials:** drink cartons, sugar cubes, water bottles
- **Joy:** Fruit-infused water tasting.

### Lesson 21 — Sleep: my body's recharge
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can explain why sleep matters and plan a good bedtime routine." (Health)
- **Hook:** A phone battery at 5% — "What happens if we don't recharge?"
- **Key activity:** Learn children aged 6–12 need about 9–12 hours of sleep; what helps (routine, no screens before bed, dark room); design a bedtime routine.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why can screens before bed make sleep harder?" → Bright light and exciting content keep the brain awake.
- **Differentiation:** support: picture routine / stretch: track sleep for a week.
- **Materials:** routine templates, sleep diary
- **Joy:** "Dream routine" poster.

### Lesson 22 — Active every day
- **Duration:** 30 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can name ways to be active for 60 minutes a day and try new ones." (Health; WHO guidance)
- **Hook:** "60 minutes a day — where can we find them?"
- **Key activity:** Make a "movement menu" (walking to school, playground games, dancing, cycling); try 5 quick activities; plan an active week.
- **Check for understanding:** "Give three ways you could be active that aren't PE lessons." → e.g. walk, play tag, dance.
- **Differentiation:** support: picture menu / stretch: plan for a family.
- **Materials:** activity cards, timers
- **Joy:** "Activity dice" game.

### Lesson 23 — Screen time balance
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can make a balanced plan for screen time and active time." (Health; Computing link)
- **Hook:** "How many hours did you spend on screens yesterday?"
- **Key activity:** Discuss the good and not-so-good of screens; make a personal balance plan.
- **Check for understanding:** "What could you do instead of a screen when you're bored?" → A named active or creative alternative.
- **Differentiation:** support: choose from pictures / stretch: family agreement.
- **Materials:** plan templates
- **Joy:** "Screen-free challenge" day.

### Lesson 24 — Healthy habits check
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can explain three healthy habits and set a goal." (Assessment)
- **Hook:** "Healthy habits wheel."
- **Key activity:** Complete a habit wheel (food, water, sleep, activity, screens); choose one goal; share.
- **Check for understanding:** "Which habit will you improve, and how?" → A specific, realistic goal.
- **Differentiation:** support: sentence starters / stretch: plan how to track progress.
- **Materials:** habit wheels
- **Joy:** Goal stars on the class board.

**End-of-unit check:** Explain why food, water, sleep and activity matter, and set one specific healthy-habit goal with a plan.

## Unit 4 — Gymnastics: balance, roll, jump, sequence

**Essential question:** How can I link movements together to make a smooth, controlled sequence?

**Key vocabulary:** balance, point, patch, stretch, tuck, straddle, pike, roll, travel, jump, land, sequence, level, direction, apparatus, control, extension

### Lesson 25 — Points and patches balances
- **Duration:** 30 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can hold balances on different points and patches for 3 seconds." (UK NC KS2 PE – gymnastics; SHAPE S1.E7)
- **Hook:** "Can you balance on 3 points? 2? Just 1?"
- **Key activity:** Explore balances on large patches (back, tummy) and small points (hands, feet); hold for 3 counts with tension; partner feedback.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why is a balance on 2 points harder than on 4?" → Less contact with the floor means it's harder to stay still.
- **Differentiation:** support: 4- and 3-point balances / stretch: 1-point balances and changing between balances smoothly.
- **Materials:** mats; **safety:** bare feet or gym shoes, no jewellery, mats spaced, adult spotting
- **Joy:** "Statue" freeze game.

### Lesson 26 — Shapes: tuck, straddle, pike, star, straight
- **Duration:** 30 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can make five gymnastic shapes with extension and control." (UK NC KS2 PE – gymnastics)
- **Hook:** Photos of gymnasts in shapes.
- **Key activity:** Learn shapes on the floor and in jumps; "shape tag."
- **Check for understanding:** "What makes a shape look controlled?" → Pointed toes, straight legs/arms where needed, holding still.
- **Differentiation:** support: shapes lying down / stretch: shapes in the air from a jump.
- **Materials:** mats, shape cards
- **Joy:** Shape tag.

### Lesson 27 — Rolls: log, egg and forward roll
- **Duration:** 30 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can perform log, egg and (with support) forward rolls safely." (UK NC KS2 PE – gymnastics)
- **Hook:** "Roll like a log, rock like an egg."
- **Key activity:** Teach log roll, egg roll, then forward roll from crouch (chin tucked, hands flat, roll on back of the head/shoulders) on a wedge mat with adult support.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why do we tuck our chin for a forward roll?" → To protect the neck and roll smoothly on the back.
- **Differentiation:** support: log and egg rolls only / stretch: forward roll to stand without hands.
- **Materials:** mats, wedge (incline) mat; **safety:** adult spotting for forward rolls; never roll on hard floor
- **Joy:** Rolling "train."

### Lesson 28 — Jumps and safe landings
- **Duration:** 30 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can perform different jumps and land safely." (UK NC KS2 PE – gymnastics)
- **Hook:** "Jump like a gymnast — land like a cat."
- **Key activity:** Straight, tuck, star and half-turn jumps; land with bent knees, arms forward, feet together.
- **Check for understanding:** "What does a safe gymnastics landing look like?" → Feet together, knees bent, arms out front, holding still.
- **Differentiation:** support: small jumps / stretch: jumps from a low box.
- **Materials:** mats, low box; **safety:** landing mats
- **Joy:** "Stick it!" landing challenge.

### Lesson 29 — Travelling and linking
- **Duration:** 30 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can link a balance, a travel and a roll smoothly." (UK NC KS2 PE – sequences)
- **Hook:** "Make your movements flow like a river."
- **Key activity:** Travel in different ways (walk on toes, bunny hop, crab walk); link three moves with smooth transitions.
- **Check for understanding:** "What makes a link smooth?" → No stops or extra steps between moves.
- **Differentiation:** support: two-move links / stretch: add a change of level.
- **Materials:** mats
- **Joy:** "River flow" sequences.

### Lesson 30 — Partner balances and mirroring
- **Duration:** 30 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can perform mirror and matching movements and a simple partner balance." (UK NC KS2 PE – gymnastics)
- **Hook:** "Be each other's mirror."
- **Key activity:** Mirroring; matching; counter-balance (hold hands, lean back) with safety rules.
- **Check for understanding:** "What do you need to do to make a counter-balance safe?" → Communicate, lean slowly, keep a firm grip, agree when to stop.
- **Differentiation:** support: mirroring only / stretch: two partner balances.
- **Materials:** mats; **safety:** similar-size partners, adult supervision
- **Joy:** Mirror games.

### Lesson 31 — Apparatus: balances and jumps
- **Duration:** 30 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can use benches, boxes and mats safely to balance, travel and jump." (UK NC KS2 PE – apparatus)
- **Hook:** Apparatus set up as an "obstacle course."
- **Key activity:** Carry and place apparatus safely (team lifting); travel along benches; jump off low boxes; balance on apparatus.
- **Check for understanding:** "How do we carry a bench safely?" → Four people, lift with bent knees, look where you're going.
- **Differentiation:** support: floor-level apparatus / stretch: add a roll off the apparatus onto a mat.
- **Materials:** benches, low boxes, mats; **safety:** adult checks all apparatus
- **Joy:** Apparatus obstacle course.

### Lesson 32 — Creating a sequence
- **Duration:** 30 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can create a 6–8 movement sequence with a beginning and end." (UK NC KS2 PE – sequences)
- **Hook:** An Olympic gymnastics floor routine clip.
- **Key activity:** Plan a sequence (start shape, balance, travel, roll, jump, end shape); record on a card; practise.
- **Check for understanding:** "How does your sequence show a change of level or direction?" → Points to the moves.
- **Differentiation:** support: 4-move sequence with picture cards / stretch: 8 moves with apparatus.
- **Materials:** mats, sequence cards
- **Joy:** Planning a routine.

### Lesson 33 — Performance and unit check
- **Duration:** 30 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can perform my sequence with control and give feedback." (Assessment by observation)
- **Hook:** "Judges' table."
- **Key activity:** Perform sequences; peer "judges" give feedback on control, links and shapes.
- **Check for understanding:** "What would you improve in your sequence, and how?" → A specific improvement.
- **Differentiation:** support: perform with a partner / stretch: include a partner balance.
- **Materials:** mats, feedback cards
- **Joy:** Performance.

**End-of-unit check:** Perform a 6–8 movement sequence including a balance, a roll, a jump and a travel with clear start and end shapes (teacher observation).

## Unit 5 — Dance around the world

**Essential question:** How do people tell stories and celebrate through dance?

**Key vocabulary:** rhythm, beat, count, formation, canon, unison, mirror, gesture, motif, travel, level, dynamics, performance, culture

### Lesson 34 — Moving to the beat
- **Duration:** 30 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can move in time with music and count 8-beat phrases." (UK NC KS2 PE – dance)
- **Hook:** Music with a strong beat — "Count to 8 with me!"
- **Key activity:** Clap and step on the beat; count 8-counts; follow-the-leader moves in 8s.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why do dancers count in 8s?" → Most music is grouped in phrases of 8 beats; counting keeps everyone together.
- **Differentiation:** support: 4-counts / stretch: lead an 8-count.
- **Materials:** music player, speakers
- **Joy:** Follow-the-leader dance.

### Lesson 35 — Bollywood dance
- **Duration:** 30 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can perform a short Bollywood-style routine with expressive hands." (UK NC KS2 PE – dance; culture)
- **Hook:** A joyful Bollywood film dance clip.
- **Key activity:** Learn signature moves (light-bulb hands, shoulder shimmies, hip-step); an 8×4 count routine.
- **Check for understanding:** "How do hand gestures help tell the story?" → They show feelings and actions.
- **Differentiation:** support: simplified steps / stretch: add a travelling section.
- **Materials:** music, scarves
- **Joy:** Performing with colourful scarves.

### Lesson 36 — West African dance and drumming
- **Duration:** 30 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can dance to live drumming with energy and grounded movements." (UK NC KS2 PE – dance; culture; Music link)

## Unit 7 — Water safety and swimming

**Essential question:** How can I stay safe in and around water — and move confidently in it?

### Lesson 52 — Water safety code
- **Duration:** 25 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can explain the water safety code: stop and think, stay together, float, call for help." (Health; UK NC KS2 PE – water safety)

### Lesson 53 — Float to live and getting help

## Teacher guidance

**Delivery.** PE is practical and adult-led; the tutor supports with demonstrations, timers, counting and reflection questions but never replaces movement with screen time. Aim for children to be moving for most of every practical lesson.

**Safety.** Risk-assess every space; warm up and cool down; bare feet or gym shoes on mats; no jewellery; long hair tied back; water available; adult spotting for rolls and apparatus; follow local rules for swimming ratios and instructor qualifications.

**Common difficulties (and the fix)**
- *Watching the thrower's hands, not the ball, when catching:* "eyes on the ball, fingers spread, pull it in."
- *Everyone chasing the ball in invasion games* ("bee-hive"): freeze and ask "Where is the space?"; use zones.
- *Landing with straight legs:* practise "soft knees" landings every lesson.
- *Rushing sequences:* hold each balance for 3 counts; smooth transitions.
- *Unsafe forward rolls:* chin tucked, hands flat, on a wedge mat with spotting — or teach alternative rolls.

**Health lessons with care.** Inform families before body-safety lessons; follow the school safeguarding policy; if a child discloses, listen, don't promise secrecy, and report to the designated safeguarding lead. Be sensitive to different family food cultures, body shapes and abilities — never weigh children or label foods as "good" or "bad"; talk about "everyday" and "sometimes" foods.

**Inclusion.** Adapt equipment (larger balls, lower nets), roles (referee, scorer, coach) and distances so every child is active and successful.

**Vertical alignment.** Year 1 built fundamental movement and basic health knowledge. Year 3 (ages 8–9) extends to more tactical team games, longer gymnastics and dance compositions, swimming distance targets (towards 25 m by the end of primary), and health topics including puberty basics in later years and online wellbeing.
