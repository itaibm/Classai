# Year 5 PE & Health (ages 10–11) — Scope & Sequence

> Part of **the best international primary school** (see [`../../../SCHOOL-CHARTER.md`](../../../SCHOOL-CHARTER.md)).
> Built to [`../../PLANNING-BRIEF.md`](../../PLANNING-BRIEF.md). Standards reference: UK National
> Curriculum KS2 Physical Education, UK Relationships, Sex and Health Education (health strand), and
> SHAPE America National PE Standards (Grade 5).

## Year overview

PE & Health in Year 5 aims for **competent, confident, active young people who understand their
bodies**. Lessons are **mostly delivered by a teacher, coach or parent in a hall, playground, field or
pool** — every Key activity describes what pupils physically do — and the Classai tutor supports the
thinking parts (rules, tactics, health knowledge, reflection) rather than replacing movement.

The year moves from **fitness** (the components of fitness and a personal plan, linked to the science
unit on the heart) through **invasion games** (passing, space, attacking and defending tactics),
**gymnastics** (balances, rolls, flight and partner sequences), **dance from around the world**
(choreography inspired by Bollywood, West African dance, capoeira and Māori stick games), **net and wall
games** (badminton and volleyball skills), **swimming and water safety** (including 'Float to Live' and
self-rescue), **athletics** (running, jumping, throwing, measured and improved — maths link), and
**outdoor and adventurous activities** (orienteering with maps — geography link — team challenges and
basic first aid).

A dedicated **health unit** covers growing up and puberty (taught with care, following the school's
policy and informing parents), hygiene, sleep, nutrition and mental health. Healthy habits are also
woven into every unit: warm-ups and cool-downs, hydration, and noticing how exercise affects mood.

Every practical lesson follows the same shape: **warm-up → skill teaching (I do, we do, you do) →
applied practice or game → cool-down and reflection**. Pupils assess themselves and each other with
simple success criteria, and teachers assess through observation.

**Safety is built in:** every lesson's Materials line includes safety notes; teachers check the space,
equipment, clothing, jewellery removal, hydration and pupils' medical needs (e.g. asthma inhalers)
before starting.

**What this year assumes (from Year 4):** fundamental movement skills (running, jumping, throwing,
catching, balancing) performed with control; simple games with rules; basic gymnastic shapes and rolls;
simple dance phrases; swimming 25 m (or working towards it); understanding that exercise and food
affect health.

**What Year 6 relies on:** applying tactics in small-sided games; linking gymnastic and dance
movements into longer sequences; measuring and improving personal performance; understanding the
components of fitness; water-safety knowledge; and accurate knowledge of puberty and wellbeing — ready
for more formal sports, leadership roles (e.g. young leaders), and deeper health education.

## Unit 1 — Fitness and healthy bodies

**Essential question:** What does it mean to be fit, and how can I improve my own fitness?

**Key vocabulary:** stamina, strength, speed, flexibility, agility, balance, coordination, heart rate, pulse, warm-up, cool-down, circuit, repetition, personal best, hydration

### Lesson 1 — What is fitness?
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can name the components of fitness and match activities to them." (UK NC KS2 PE / SHAPE America Standard 3)
- **Hook:** "Is a marathon runner fitter than a gymnast? Or are they fit in different ways?"
- **Key activity:** Teacher-led practical: pupils try a station for each component (shuttle runs — stamina/speed; plank — strength; sit-and-reach — flexibility; zig-zag cones — agility; one-leg stand — balance; ball juggling — coordination), then discuss which component each uses.
- **Check for understanding:** "Which components of fitness does a basketball player need most, and why?" → e.g. agility and speed to change direction, coordination for dribbling, stamina for a long game.
- **Differentiation:** support: stations with picture cards and simplified tasks / stretch: rank the components for three different sports and justify.
- **Materials:** cones, mats, stopwatches, balls, station cards. Safety: clear space, mats for floor work, pupils' medical needs checked.
- **Joy:** "Fitness Olympics" rotation.

### Lesson 2 — Warming up and cooling down
- **Duration:** 35 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can lead a safe warm-up and cool-down and explain why they matter." (UK NC KS2 PE / SHAPE America Standard 3)
- **Hook:** "Why do athletes warm up — even for a short race?"
- **Key activity:** Teacher models the three phases of a warm-up (raise heart rate, mobilise joints, dynamic stretches) and a cool-down (slow jog, static stretches); small groups design and lead a warm-up for the class.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why should a warm-up start gently and build up?" → To gradually raise heart rate and warm the muscles, reducing the risk of injury.
- **Differentiation:** support: warm-up cards to sequence / stretch: design a sport-specific warm-up.
- **Materials:** warm-up cards, music, cones. Safety: no bouncing in stretches; hold static stretches gently.
- **Joy:** pupil-led warm-ups to music.

### Lesson 3 — Heart rate and exercise
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can measure my heart rate before and after exercise and explain the changes (science link)." (UK NC KS2 PE / SHAPE America Standard 3)
- **Hook:** "Your heart is a muscle. What happens when it has to work harder?"
- **Key activity:** Take resting pulse; do 2 minutes of moderate activity (skipping), then vigorous activity (shuttle runs); measure after each and during recovery; record and compare (links to science Lesson 12).
- **Check for understanding:** "Why does your heart rate go up during exercise?" → Muscles need more oxygen and energy, so the heart pumps faster to deliver more blood.
- **Differentiation:** support: pulse-taking partner and a simple record sheet / stretch: estimate maximum heart rate with the rough formula 220 − age and discuss why it is only an estimate.
- **Materials:** stopwatches, skipping ropes, record sheets. Safety: pupils with heart or asthma conditions follow their care plans; stop if dizzy.
- **Joy:** "Heart-rate race" — who recovers fastest?

### Lesson 4 — Circuit training
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can complete a circuit with good technique and record my scores." (UK NC KS2 PE / SHAPE America Standard 3)
- **Hook:** "Can you beat yourself, not your friends?"
- **Key activity:** Teacher demonstrates correct technique for each station (step-ups, star jumps, wall sit, bicep curls with light bottles, speed bounce, modified press-ups); 30 seconds per station, 30 seconds rest; partners count and check technique.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why is good technique more important than doing lots of repetitions?" → Poor technique can cause injury and trains the wrong muscles.
- **Differentiation:** support: easier versions at each station (e.g. wall press-ups) / stretch: design an extra station targeting a specific component.
- **Materials:** benches, mats, water bottles (as weights), cones, station cards, score sheets. Safety: check bench stability; controlled movements.
- **Joy:** personal-best score cards.

### Lesson 5 — Flexibility and balance
- **Duration:** 35 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can perform and improve flexibility and balance exercises safely." (UK NC KS2 PE / SHAPE America Standard 1)
- **Hook:** "Why do footballers do yoga?"
- **Key activity:** Yoga-inspired flow led by the teacher (tree, warrior, downward dog, child's pose); balance challenges on one foot with eyes closed; measure sit-and-reach at start and end of the unit.
- **Check for understanding:** "How does flexibility help in sport and everyday life?" → A wider range of movement, fewer injuries and better posture.
- **Differentiation:** support: hold poses with a wall for support / stretch: create a short balance sequence of four poses.
- **Materials:** mats, sit-and-reach box (or ruler and bench). Safety: stretch only to mild tension, never pain.
- **Joy:** "Statue challenge" — longest balance.

### Lesson 6 — Fitness games
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can take part in active games that build stamina and agility." (UK NC KS2 PE / SHAPE America Standard 5)
- **Hook:** "Can getting fit feel like play?"
- **Key activity:** Teacher runs high-energy games: 'fitness bingo', 'capture the flag', 'agility relays'; pupils note how their bodies feel.
- **Check for understanding:** "Which game raised your heart rate most, and which component of fitness did it train?" → A named game and a matching component.
- **Differentiation:** support: shorter intervals and a buddy / stretch: suggest a rule change to make a game more demanding.
- **Materials:** bibs, cones, flags, bingo cards. Safety: clear boundaries; non-contact rules.
- **Joy:** capture the flag.

### Lesson 7 — My fitness plan
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can set a fitness goal and plan activities to reach it." (UK NC KS2 PE / SHAPE America Standard 3)
- **Hook:** "Health guidance suggests children aim for an average of at least 60 minutes of moderate-to-vigorous activity a day. How close are you?"
- **Key activity:** Pupils log their activity over a typical day; choose a fitness component to improve; write a 4-week plan (e.g. skipping 3 times a week); plan to retest in Lesson 64 (athletics) or at unit end.
- **Check for understanding:** "What will you measure to know if your plan has worked?" → A specific test (e.g. skips in one minute, sit-and-reach distance).
- **Differentiation:** support: plan template with activity choices / stretch: plan progression week by week.
- **Materials:** activity logs, plan templates.
- **Joy:** "Activity passport" stamps.

### Lesson 8 — Fitness retest and reflection
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can retest my fitness and explain what has changed and why." (UK NC KS2 PE / SHAPE America Standard 3)
- **Hook:** "Have your personal bests improved?"
- **Key activity:** Repeat selected circuit and flexibility tests; compare with earlier scores (maths link: difference, percentage); reflect on effort, sleep, food and consistency.
- **Check for understanding:** "Which score improved most, and what do you think caused it?" → A named score with a sensible reason (practice, consistency).
- **Differentiation:** support: comparison table ready / stretch: graph your scores.
- **Materials:** score sheets, circuit equipment. Safety: as Lesson 4.
- **Joy:** personal-best certificates.

**End-of-unit check:** Observed: the pupil leads a safe warm-up, performs circuit exercises with good technique, measures heart rate before and after exercise and explains the change, and sets a measurable fitness goal.

## Unit 2 — Invasion games

**Essential question:** How do teams create and deny space to score?

**Key vocabulary:** invasion game, attack, defence, possession, pass, receive, dribble, space, width, depth, marking, interception, support, tactic, formation, fair play

### Lesson 9 — Passing and receiving under pressure
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can pass and receive accurately while moving." (UK NC KS2 PE — invasion games / SHAPE America Standard 1)
- **Hook:** "Why do top teams pass so quickly?"
- **Key activity:** Teacher demonstrates chest and bounce passes (netball/basketball) and side-foot passes (football); drills in pairs then threes on the move; 'keep ball' 3 v 1.
- **Check for understanding:** "What should you do immediately after you pass?" → Move into space to receive again.
- **Differentiation:** support: stationary passing before moving / stretch: 3 v 2 with a time limit.
- **Materials:** netballs, basketballs, footballs, cones, bibs. Safety: space between groups; soft balls for beginners.
- **Joy:** 'keep ball' challenge.

### Lesson 10 — Finding space
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can move into space to support a teammate." (UK NC KS2 PE — tactics / SHAPE America Standard 2)
- **Hook:** "Where's the best place to be when you don't have the ball?"
- **Key activity:** Games in grids: 'end-zone ball' (score by catching in the zone); freeze-frame coaching to show good and poor positions; discuss width and depth.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why is standing right next to the ball carrier unhelpful?" → A defender can mark you both; moving away creates a passing lane.
- **Differentiation:** support: fewer players and larger grids / stretch: call out tactical instructions to teammates.
- **Materials:** cones, bibs, balls. Safety: non-contact rules.
- **Joy:** freeze-frame 'coach's eye'.

### Lesson 11 — Dribbling and ball control
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can dribble with control, changing speed and direction." (UK NC KS2 PE / SHAPE America Standard 1)
- **Hook:** "Can you keep the ball while someone tries to take it?"
- **Key activity:** Teacher demonstrates basketball and football dribbling (head up, close control, both hands/feet); slalom courses; 'dribble tag' in a grid.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why should you keep your head up while dribbling?" → To see teammates, defenders and space.
- **Differentiation:** support: larger balls and slower pace / stretch: use weaker hand/foot only.
- **Materials:** balls, cones. Safety: grids spaced to avoid collisions.
- **Joy:** dribble tag.

### Lesson 12 — Defending: marking and intercepting
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can mark an opponent and try to intercept safely." (UK NC KS2 PE — defending / SHAPE America Standard 2)
- **Hook:** "Defence wins games. How do you stop a team that passes well?"
- **Key activity:** Teacher demonstrates goal-side marking, staying between opponent and goal, watching the ball and the player; 2 v 2 and 3 v 3 games; interception without contact.
- **Check for understanding:** "Where should a defender stand when marking?" → Between the opponent and the goal they're attacking, close enough to intercept.
- **Differentiation:** support: 'shadow' a partner without the ball first / stretch: switch between man-marking and zone defence.
- **Materials:** bibs, balls, cones, small goals. Safety: clear no-contact rules.
- **Joy:** "Interception heroes" tally.

### Lesson 13 — Netball-style game
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can play a modified netball game, following rules on footwork and contact." (UK NC KS2 PE / SHAPE America Standard 4)
- **Hook:** "No running with the ball — how can you get it up the court?"
- **Key activity:** Teach key rules (footwork, three-second rule, no contact, positions); play high-five netball (5 v 5) with rotation.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why does the footwork rule make passing and moving into space even more important?" → You can't run with the ball, so the team must pass and get free to move it forward.
- **Differentiation:** support: fewer rules at first / stretch: play a specific position role with responsibilities.
- **Materials:** netballs, posts (adjustable height), bibs with positions. Safety: remove jewellery; check court surface.
- **Joy:** mini-tournament.

### Lesson 14 — Basketball-style game
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can play a modified basketball game using dribbling, passing and shooting." (UK NC KS2 PE / SHAPE America Standard 1)
- **Hook:** "BEEF: Balance, Eyes, Elbow, Follow-through."
- **Key activity:** Teacher demonstrates set shot technique (BEEF); shooting practice; 3 v 3 half-court games.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why does a bent-elbow shot with follow-through go in more often than a two-handed throw?" → It gives more control and accuracy with a consistent motion.
- **Differentiation:** support: lower hoops or larger targets / stretch: layup practice.
- **Materials:** basketballs (size 5), hoops (adjustable). Safety: space around hoops.
- **Joy:** "Around the world" shooting game.

### Lesson 15 — Football-style game
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can play a small-sided football game, using passing, space and teamwork." (UK NC KS2 PE / SHAPE America Standard 2)
- **Hook:** "Football is played in more countries than almost any other sport. What makes it work?"
- **Key activity:** Skills warm-up; 5 v 5 small-sided games with rotating positions; coaching points on spreading out and passing.
- **Check for understanding:** "What is one tactic your team used to create a scoring chance?" → e.g. a quick pass into space, using width.
- **Differentiation:** support: 'walking football' version / stretch: captain role — plan a tactic at half-time.
- **Materials:** footballs, bibs, small goals, cones. Safety: shin pads if available; no slide tackles.
- **Joy:** small-sided league.

### Lesson 16 — Tag rugby-style game
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can play tag rugby, passing backwards and evading tags." (UK NC KS2 PE / SHAPE America Standard 1)
- **Hook:** "In rugby you must pass backwards. How can you still go forwards?"
- **Key activity:** Teach tag belts, backward passing, running into space, the 'tag' rule; small-sided games.
- **Check for understanding:** "How can a team move forward if every pass goes backwards?" → The ball carrier runs forward; supporting players run behind and alongside to receive.
- **Differentiation:** support: slower walking version / stretch: plan a set move with a dummy pass.
- **Materials:** tag belts, rugby balls (size 4), cones. Safety: no contact; tags only.
- **Joy:** tag rugby match.

### Lesson 17 — Designing a game
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can design an invasion game with fair rules and explain its tactics." (UK NC KS2 PE / SHAPE America Standard 2)
- **Hook:** "Invent a new sport."
- **Key activity:** Groups design a game (equipment, scoring, rules, safety); teach it to another group; play and improve.
- **Check for understanding:** "Which rule did you add to make your game fair, and why?" → A rule with a clear fairness reason (e.g. everyone must touch the ball before scoring).
- **Differentiation:** support: a game-design template with options / stretch: include a rule that encourages a specific tactic.
- **Materials:** mixed equipment, design templates. Safety: risk-check each game before playing.
- **Joy:** playing each other's invented sports.

### Lesson 18 — Invasion games festival
- **Duration:** 45 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can apply skills and tactics in competitive games while showing fair play." (UK NC KS2 PE / SHAPE America Standard 4)
- **Hook:** "Tournament day — and a fair-play award."
- **Key activity:** Round-robin tournament of the unit's games; pupils referee in turn; peer-voted fair-play award; reflection on tactics.
- **Check for understanding:** "Give an example of good sportsmanship you saw today." → A specific action (e.g. helping an opponent up, accepting a referee's decision).
- **Differentiation:** support: roles such as scorer or timekeeper alongside playing / stretch: referee a game.
- **Materials:** all unit equipment, score sheets, whistles.
- **Joy:** the festival.

**End-of-unit check:** Observed in games: the pupil passes and receives on the move, moves into space to support, marks an opponent, follows rules, and explains one tactic their team used.

## Unit 3 — Gymnastics: balance, rotation and flight

**Essential question:** How can I link movements into a sequence that is controlled, creative and safe?

**Key vocabulary:** balance, base of support, point, patch, counter-balance, counter-tension, roll, rotation, flight, take-off, landing, sequence, canon, unison, mirror, symmetry, asymmetry, extension, control

### Lesson 19 — Balances: points and patches
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can hold balances on different points and patches with control and extension." (UK NC KS2 PE — gymnastics / SHAPE America Standard 1)
- **Hook:** "Can you balance on three points? Two? One?"
- **Key activity:** Teacher demonstrates; pupils explore balances on different bases, holding each for 3 seconds with tension and pointed toes; symmetrical vs asymmetrical shapes (maths link).
- **Check for understanding:** "Why is a balance on a small base harder than on a large one?" → A smaller base of support makes it harder to keep the centre of gravity over it.
- **Differentiation:** support: wide-base balances / stretch: balances on one hand and one foot.
- **Materials:** mats. Safety: mats spaced, no socks on smooth floors, hair tied back, jewellery removed.
- **Joy:** "Balance gallery" — hold your best shape while others walk round.

### Lesson 20 — Partner balances
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can perform counter-balances and counter-tension balances safely with a partner." (UK NC KS2 PE / SHAPE America Standard 4)
- **Hook:** "Two people can balance in ways one person can't."
- **Key activity:** Teacher demonstrates safe grips; pupils practise counter-balance (pushing towards each other) and counter-tension (pulling away); create three partner balances.
- **Check for understanding:** "What is the difference between counter-balance and counter-tension?" → Counter-balance: partners push against each other; counter-tension: partners pull away from each other.
- **Differentiation:** support: simple hand-to-hand counter-tension / stretch: a balance with three people.
- **Materials:** mats. Safety: partners of similar size, communication ('ready, go, down'), no standing on partners.
- **Joy:** photographing partner balances.

### Lesson 21 — Rolls
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can perform a range of rolls (log, egg, teddy bear, forward, backward) with control." (UK NC KS2 PE / SHAPE America Standard 1)
- **Hook:** "How do gymnasts roll without getting hurt?"
- **Key activity:** Teacher-led progression: rocking, log roll, egg roll, teddy-bear roll, forward roll (chin tucked, hands flat), backward roll or backward shoulder roll (only with teacher approval).
- **Check for understanding:** "Why must your chin be tucked in a forward roll?" → To roll on the back of the shoulders, not the head or neck.
- **Differentiation:** support: roll down a gentle wedge mat / stretch: forward roll to straddle or to standing without hands.
- **Materials:** mats, wedge mat. Safety: teacher supervises all inverted rolls; pupils with neck or back problems do alternatives.
- **Joy:** "Roll roulette" — spin a wheel to choose your roll.

### Lesson 22 — Flight: jumps and landings
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can perform shaped jumps with a safe take-off and landing." (UK NC KS2 PE / SHAPE America Standard 1)
- **Hook:** "The hardest part of a jump is landing it."
- **Key activity:** Teacher demonstrates safe landing (bend knees, arms forward, feet apart, 'stick' it); jumps: straight, tuck, star, straddle, half-turn; from floor then low apparatus.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why do gymnasts bend their knees when landing?" → To absorb the force and protect the joints.
- **Differentiation:** support: jumps from the floor only / stretch: jumps with a half-turn from a low box.
- **Materials:** mats, low benches or boxes, springboard (only with trained staff). Safety: apparatus checked; landing mats in place.
- **Joy:** "Stick the landing" competition.

### Lesson 23 — Apparatus
- **Duration:** 45 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can travel on, off and over apparatus with control." (UK NC KS2 PE / SHAPE America Standard 1)
- **Hook:** "Benches, boxes, bars: an obstacle course for gymnasts."
- **Key activity:** Teacher and pupils set out apparatus safely (lifting technique); pupils explore travelling, balancing and jumping on apparatus in rotations.
- **Check for understanding:** "What is the safe way to carry a bench with a partner?" → Bend knees, straight back, one at each end, facing the way you are walking.
- **Differentiation:** support: lower apparatus / stretch: combine a balance and a jump on the apparatus.
- **Materials:** benches, boxes, mats, wall bars. Safety: apparatus checked before use; one pupil at a time on high apparatus.
- **Joy:** apparatus circuit.

### Lesson 24 — Creating a sequence
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can create a sequence linking balances, rolls, jumps and travel with smooth transitions." (UK NC KS2 PE / SHAPE America Standard 1)
- **Hook:** "A great sequence has a clear start, a flowing middle and a strong finish."
- **Key activity:** Pairs plan a sequence (start and end shape, two balances, a roll, a jump, travel); write it as a sequence card; rehearse.
- **Check for understanding:** "How did you make the transition between your balance and roll smooth?" → A specific link movement (e.g. lowering into the roll from the balance).
- **Differentiation:** support: a sequence card template with choices / stretch: include canon and unison.
- **Materials:** mats, sequence cards. Safety: as above.
- **Joy:** sequence rehearsal with music.

### Lesson 25 — Refining with feedback
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can improve a sequence using peer feedback on control, extension and timing." (UK NC KS2 PE / SHAPE America Standard 4)
- **Hook:** "What would a judge notice?"
- **Key activity:** Pairs perform for another pair, who use a simple 'judging' card (control, extension, flow, originality); refine; video (with permission) for self-assessment.
- **Check for understanding:** "What feedback did you receive and what did you change?" → A specific comment and change.
- **Differentiation:** support: one feedback point to focus on / stretch: add a more difficult element.
- **Materials:** judging cards, tablets for video. Safety: as above.
- **Joy:** watching yourself on video.

### Lesson 26 — Gymnastics performance
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can perform my sequence with control and evaluate performances using gymnastics vocabulary." (UK NC KS2 PE — evaluate / SHAPE America Standard 1)
- **Hook:** "Welcome to the Year 5 Gymnastics Showcase."
- **Key activity:** Performances in groups; audience gives feedback using vocabulary; self-evaluation.
- **Check for understanding:** "Using the words control and extension, describe a strong moment in a classmate's sequence." → A specific, vocabulary-rich comment.
- **Differentiation:** support: perform with a partner / stretch: perform a solo sequence.
- **Materials:** mats, music, judging cards.
- **Joy:** the showcase.

**End-of-unit check:** Observed: the pupil performs balances, rolls and jumps with control and safe landings, links them into a partner sequence with smooth transitions, and evaluates using gymnastics vocabulary.

## Unit 4 — Health: growing up, sleep, food and feelings

**Essential question:** How can I look after my body and mind as I grow up?

**Key vocabulary:** puberty, hormone, growth spurt, hygiene, deodorant, period (menstruation), wet dream, voice change, body image, sleep, routine, nutrients, balanced diet, hydration, mental health, wellbeing, stress, trusted adult

### Lesson 27 — Growing up: how bodies change
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can describe the main physical changes of puberty and know they happen at different times for everyone." (UK RSHE — changing adolescent body)
- **Hook:** "Question box: write any question about growing up — anonymously."
- **Key activity:** Teacher-led, following the school's policy (parents informed beforehand): puberty usually begins between about 8 and 14; changes for all bodies (growth spurts, hair growth, sweat, skin changes, voice changes, emotions); changes specific to female and male bodies introduced with correct scientific vocabulary.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why is it normal for friends of the same age to be at very different stages of puberty?" → Puberty starts and progresses at different times for different people; there's a wide normal range.
- **Differentiation:** support: labelled diagrams and picture timelines / stretch: explain the role of hormones in simple terms.
- **Materials:** age-appropriate diagrams, anonymous question box. Safety/sensitivity: ground rules, right to pass, no personal questions; follow safeguarding procedures.
- **Joy:** anonymous questions answered honestly — no question is silly.

### Lesson 28 — Periods, wet dreams and staying comfortable
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can explain menstruation and wet dreams in simple, accurate terms and know how to manage them." (UK RSHE — changing adolescent body)
- **Hook:** "Every family has someone who has gone through this. It's normal biology."
- **Key activity:** Teacher explains the menstrual cycle simply (including period products and where to find them in school) and wet dreams as normal; everyone learns about both, so no one is embarrassed or unkind.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why should everyone — not just girls — learn about periods?" → So everyone understands, is kind and supportive, and nobody is teased for a normal part of growing up.
- **Differentiation:** support: simple diagrams and a word bank / stretch: write a supportive advice leaflet for a younger pupil.
- **Materials:** period products for demonstration, diagrams, question box. Sensitivity: follow school policy; parents informed.
- **Joy:** creating a 'Growing Up Kit' list together.

### Lesson 29 — Hygiene and personal care
- **Duration:** 35 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can plan a personal hygiene routine for a changing body." (UK RSHE — health and prevention)
- **Hook:** "After PE, what's the best way to stay fresh?"
- **Key activity:** Discuss sweat and bacteria (science link: microorganisms), daily washing, deodorant, clean clothes, dental care, skin care; design a morning and evening routine.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why does sweat start to smell more during puberty?" → Glands produce more sweat, and bacteria on the skin break it down, making an odour.
- **Differentiation:** support: routine picture cards / stretch: design a healthy-hygiene poster for a younger class.
- **Materials:** routine templates, toiletry examples.
- **Joy:** "Fresh routine" design challenge.

### Lesson 30 — Sleep: the secret superpower
- **Duration:** 35 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can explain why sleep matters and plan a healthy bedtime routine." (UK RSHE — health and prevention)
- **Hook:** "Children your age are advised to sleep about 9–12 hours a night. How much do you get?"
- **Key activity:** Sleep diary review (kept for a week before the lesson); what happens during sleep (growth, memory, mood); screens and sleep; design a 'wind-down' routine.
- **Check for understanding:** "Give two reasons why using a screen late at night can make sleep worse." → Bright light tells the brain it's still daytime; exciting content keeps the mind active; notifications wake you.

## Unit 7 — Swimming and water safety

**Essential question:** How can I stay safe in and around water and help others if they're in trouble?

**Key vocabulary:** stroke, front crawl, backstroke, breaststroke, treading water, float, survival, cold water shock, rip current, lifeguard, flags, self-rescue, reach and throw, emergency services

> Delivered at a pool with qualified instructors where available. If no pool is available, Lessons 51–53 and 56 are taught as land-based water-safety lessons and practical swimming is arranged with families or a local provider.

### Lesson 51 — Water safety knowledge
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can explain the dangers of open water and what safety flags and signs mean." (UK NC KS2 PE — swimming and water safety)
- **Hook:** "A calm-looking river or beach can still be dangerous. Why?"
- **Key activity:** Classroom/land lesson: dangers (cold water, currents, hidden objects, depth); beach flags (e.g. red and yellow = lifeguarded swimming area; red = danger, don't swim — check local systems); the water safety code (stop and think, stay together, float, call for help).
- **Check for understanding:** "Why is it safer to swim between the red and yellow flags on a lifeguarded beach?" → Lifeguards watch that area and it has been judged safest for swimming.
- **Differentiation:** support: safety-sign matching cards / stretch: plan a safe family beach trip.
- **Materials:** flag images, local water-safety signs, water-safety code posters.
- **Joy:** "Safety sign spotter" quiz.

### Lesson 52 — Float to live and cold water shock
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can explain and (in the pool) demonstrate how to float to survive cold water shock." (UK NC KS2 PE — water safety)
- **Hook:** "If you fall into cold water, your first instinct is to panic and thrash. What should you do instead?"
- **Key activity:** Teach 'Float to Live' (lean back, spread arms and legs, move hands to stay afloat, and float for about 60–90 seconds while cold water shock passes, then call for help or swim to safety); practise floating in the pool with instructors.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why should you float first rather than swim immediately when you fall into cold water?" → Cold water shock makes you gasp and breathe fast; floating keeps your head up until breathing calms.
- **Differentiation:** support: float with a buoyancy aid / stretch: float in different positions (star, mushroom) and change between them.
- **Materials:** pool, floats, qualified lifeguard/instructors. Safety: instructor ratios; medical conditions checked.
- **Joy:** "Starfish float" challenge.

### Lesson 53 — Rip currents and self-rescue
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can explain what to do if caught in a rip current and how to help someone else safely." (UK NC KS2 PE — water safety)
- **Hook:** "A rip current pulls you out to sea. Should you swim straight back?"
- **Key activity:** Explain rip currents with a diagram and tray model; advice: don't swim against it; if you can, swim parallel to the shore; if you can't, float and raise a hand and shout. Helping others: 'call, reach, throw, don't go' — never jump in; call emergency services (know your country's number, e.g. 112, 911, 999).
- **Check for understanding:** "Why shouldn't you jump in to rescue a friend in trouble?" → You could get into trouble too; it's safer to call for help and reach or throw something that floats.
- **Differentiation:** support: picture sequence for 'call, reach, throw' / stretch: plan what to throw from things found at a beach.
- **Materials:** tray, water, food colouring (rip model), throw lines or rope, rescue scenario cards.
- **Joy:** 'reach and throw' rescue practice with ropes.

### Lesson 54 — Stroke technique (1): front crawl and backstroke
- **Duration:** 45 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can improve my front crawl and backstroke technique, including breathing." (UK NC KS2 PE — swimming)
- **Hook:** "Why do Olympic swimmers keep their bodies so flat?"
- **Key activity:** Instructor-led drills: body position, kick, arm action and side breathing (front crawl); backstroke rhythm; swim widths or lengths at pupils' levels.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why does turning your head to the side to breathe help in front crawl?" → You can breathe without lifting your head and dropping your legs, keeping the body streamlined.
- **Differentiation:** support: kickboards and shallow water / stretch: bilateral breathing (every three strokes).
- **Materials:** pool, kickboards, pull buoys, instructors. Safety: ability groups; lifeguard present.
- **Joy:** "Silent swim" — smooth strokes without splash.

### Lesson 55 — Stroke technique (2): breaststroke and treading water
- **Duration:** 45 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can swim breaststroke with coordinated timing and tread water for 30 seconds or more." (UK NC KS2 PE — swimming)
- **Hook:** "Pull, breathe, kick, glide."
- **Key activity:** Instructor-led breaststroke drills (timing, glide) and treading water practice; aim to swim 25 m confidently with a range of strokes (the UK KS2 expectation).
- **Check for understanding:** "Why is the glide important in breaststroke?" → It lets you travel efficiently between strokes and conserve energy.
- **Differentiation:** support: breaststroke legs with a float / stretch: swim 50 m with a stroke change.
- **Materials:** pool, floats, instructors.
- **Joy:** treading-water challenge.

### Lesson 56 — Water safety challenge and review
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can demonstrate a sequence of survival skills and explain water safety rules." (UK NC KS2 PE — water safety / assessment)
- **Hook:** "The survival challenge: enter, float, tread, swim, exit."
- **Key activity:** In the pool: a survival sequence (safe entry, float, tread water, swim 25 m, exit safely); on land: water-safety quiz and scenario answers.
- **Check for understanding:** "You see a friend struggling in a lake. List your actions in order." → Stay calm, shout for help / call emergency services, reach or throw a floating object, never jump in.
- **Differentiation:** support: shorter distances with buoyancy aid / stretch: add a clothes-on swim (instructor-led).
- **Materials:** pool, instructors, quiz sheets.
- **Joy:** survival skills certificates.

**End-of-unit check:** The pupil explains the water safety code, Float to Live, rip-current advice and 'call, reach, throw, don't go', and (where a pool is available) swims 25 m and completes a survival sequence.

- **Hook:** "Beat your own record."
- **Key activity:** Retest chosen events and the fitness goal from Unit 1; calculate improvements (maths link: difference and percentage); reflect.
- **Check for understanding:** "By how much did you improve, and what caused it?" → A calculated difference and a reason (practice, technique).
- **Differentiation:** support: comparison table / stretch: graph progress across the year.
- **Materials:** equipment, previous results.
- **Joy:** personal-best celebration (and sports day preparation).

**End-of-unit check:** Observed and measured: the pupil sprints with good technique, completes a clean relay changeover, paces a longer run, jumps and throws with correct technique, and measures and explains improvement.

## Unit 9 — Outdoor and adventurous activities

**Essential question:** How can I solve problems, navigate and stay safe outdoors as part of a team?

**Key vocabulary:** orienteering, map, key, orientate, control point, compass, bearing (introduced), route, teamwork, communication, leadership, risk, first aid, recovery position, emergency

### Lesson 65 — Team challenges
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can solve physical problems as a team, communicating and taking turns to lead." (UK NC KS2 PE — OAA / SHAPE America Standard 4)
- **Hook:** "Get your whole team across the 'river' using only three mats — without touching the 'water'."
- **Key activity:** Teacher-led challenge stations (river crossing, blindfold trail with a guide, 'minefield' guided by voice); review teamwork after each.
- **Check for understanding:** "What made your team successful in the river crossing?" → e.g. planning before moving, listening to everyone, one clear leader at a time.
- **Differentiation:** support: fewer constraints / stretch: silent version (no talking).
- **Materials:** mats, blindfolds, cones, ropes. Safety: blindfolded pupils always guided by a partner; clear space.
- **Joy:** the river crossing.

### Lesson 66 — Maps and orientating
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can orientate a map and follow a route around the school grounds (geography link)." (UK NC KS2 PE — OAA)
- **Hook:** "A map is only useful if it's the right way round."
- **Key activity:** Make/read a school-grounds map with key; orientate it using landmarks; follow a star course (return to base after each control) with partners.
- **Check for understanding:** "How do you orientate a map using landmarks?" → Turn it so features on the map line up with the same features in front of you.
- **Differentiation:** support: a simplified map with large landmarks / stretch: a longer course with more controls.
- **Materials:** school-grounds maps, control markers with codes, recording cards. Safety: boundaries agreed; adults positioned.
- **Joy:** star-course race.

### Lesson 67 — Orienteering course
- **Duration:** 45 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can navigate a multi-control course using a map and compass directions." (UK NC KS2 PE — OAA)
- **Hook:** "Ten controls, one map, the clock is ticking."
- **Key activity:** Introduce the compass (north, orienting the map); line course in pairs; record control codes; time and check accuracy.
- **Check for understanding:** "How can a compass help you if you can't see any landmarks?" → The needle points north, so you can turn the map to match and choose the right direction.
- **Differentiation:** support: fewer controls, adult support / stretch: plan your own course for another pair.
- **Materials:** maps, compasses, control markers, stopwatches. Safety: pairs only; meeting point and time agreed.
- **Joy:** the orienteering race.

### Lesson 68 — Basic first aid (1)
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can assess a situation for danger and know how to call emergency services." (UK RSHE — basic first aid)
- **Hook:** "Someone is lying on the ground and not moving. What's the very first thing you do?"
- **Key activity:** Teach the primary survey simply (Danger, Response, shout for help, Airway, Breathing); practise calling emergency services (role-play: location, what happened, stay on the line); know the local number (e.g. 112, 911, 999).
- **Check for understanding:** "Why do you check for danger before helping someone?" → So you don't get hurt too and become a second casualty.
- **Differentiation:** support: picture sequence cards / stretch: role-play the full emergency call with details.
- **Materials:** first-aid posters, role-play phones, scenario cards.
- **Joy:** emergency call role-play.

### Lesson 69 — Basic first aid (2)
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can place someone in the recovery position and treat minor injuries such as cuts and bumps." (UK RSHE — basic first aid)
- **Hook:** "If someone is unresponsive but breathing, how do you keep their airway open?"
- **Key activity:** Instructor or trained teacher demonstrates the recovery position; pupils practise in pairs on mats; minor injuries (cleaning a graze, cold compress for a bump, getting an adult).
- **Check for understanding:** "Why is the recovery position used for someone who is breathing but unresponsive?" → It keeps the airway open and lets fluids drain from the mouth.
- **Differentiation:** support: step cards / stretch: explain what to do if the person stops breathing (call for help immediately; follow the call handler's instructions).
- **Materials:** mats, first-aid kit, step cards. Safety: gentle handling; consent from partners.
- **Joy:** recovery-position relay (accuracy, not speed).

### Lesson 70 — Adventure day and year review
- **Duration:** 45 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can combine navigation, teamwork and safety skills in an adventure challenge, and reflect on my progress in PE this year." (UK NC KS2 PE — OAA / SHAPE America Standard 5)
- **Hook:** "The Year 5 Adventure Challenge: navigate, solve, rescue."
- **Key activity:** Teams complete a combined course (orienteering controls with a team problem or first-aid scenario at each); year review of personal bests and favourite activities; set an active goal for the summer.
- **Check for understanding:** "What was your team's biggest challenge today and how did you overcome it?" → A specific challenge and strategy.
- **Differentiation:** support: shorter course with adult / stretch: team leader role.
- **Materials:** maps, compasses, challenge equipment, first-aid scenario cards. Safety: as previous lessons; adults at key points.
- **Joy:** the adventure challenge.

**End-of-unit check:** Observed: the pupil works effectively in a team, orientates a map and completes an orienteering course, and demonstrates the primary survey, an emergency call and the recovery position.

## End-of-year mastery checks

Assessed through observation and simple measurements:

1. **Fitness** — names the components of fitness, leads a safe warm-up, and measures and improves a personal fitness score.
2. **Invasion games** — passes, receives, moves into space and defends in small-sided games, explaining a tactic.
3. **Gymnastics** — performs a controlled partner sequence with balances, rolls, jumps and smooth transitions.
4. **Dance** — performs movements from three traditions and choreographs with canon, unison, levels and formations.
5. **Net games** — sustains a rally and uses placement tactics.
6. **Water safety** — explains Float to Live, rip-current advice and 'call, reach, throw, don't go'; swims 25 m where possible.
7. **Athletics** — sprints, paces, jumps and throws with correct technique, recording and explaining improvement.
8. **Outdoor activities** — navigates with a map and compass and solves problems as a team.
9. **Health** — describes puberty accurately and kindly, plans healthy routines for sleep, food and hygiene, and knows how to look after mental health and get help.
10. **First aid** — carries out the primary survey, calls emergency services and places someone in the recovery position.

## Teacher guidance

**Common misconceptions to watch for**
- *"Stretching before exercise means holding long static stretches."* Warm-ups use dynamic movement; long static stretches belong in the cool-down.
- *"Being fit means being thin."* Fitness has many components and bodies differ.
- *"You should jump in to save someone in the water."* Call, reach, throw — don't go.
- *"Swim against a rip current back to shore."* Don't swim against it; swim parallel if you can, or float and signal.
- *"Puberty happens at the same age for everyone."* There's a wide normal range.
- *"Winning is all that matters."* Emphasise personal bests, teamwork and fair play.

**How to teach it well**
- Maximise activity time: short explanations, clear demonstrations, lots of practice.
- Differentiate using the STEP principle (change Space, Task, Equipment or People).
- Include every pupil: adapt for disabilities and medical needs; celebrate effort and improvement.
- Deliver the health unit with care: follow the school's policy, inform parents, use correct vocabulary, set ground rules and use an anonymous question box; follow safeguarding procedures.
- Encourage pupils to join clubs and be active outside school; aim for 60 minutes of activity a day on average.

**Vertical connections**
- *From Year 4:* fundamental movement skills, simple games, basic gymnastics and dance, swimming basics, and healthy-lifestyle knowledge.
- *Into Year 6:* more formal sports and positions, leadership (young leaders), more complex sequences and choreography, advanced athletics techniques, deeper health education (including relationships and substance misuse) and transition to secondary PE.
- *Across subjects:* science (heart rate, circulation, nutrition, microorganisms and hygiene), maths (measuring, timing, decimals, means and percentage improvement), geography (maps and orienteering), music (drumming and dance), life skills (wellbeing, teamwork, resilience), history (capoeira's origins).
