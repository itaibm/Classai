> Part of **the best international primary school** (see [`../../../SCHOOL-CHARTER.md`](../../../SCHOOL-CHARTER.md)).
> Built to [`../../PLANNING-BRIEF.md`](../../PLANNING-BRIEF.md). Source material:

## Year overview

Life skills in Year 5 helps pupils become **self-aware, resilient, financially sensible and actively
caring citizens** as they approach the end of primary school. Six units cover the four strands:

- **Social–emotional learning and character:** identity, values and self-esteem; friendship,
  assertiveness and resolving conflict; resilience, goal-setting and managing stress and change.
- **Money sense:** budgeting, earning, saving and interest, borrowing and credit, advertising, and a
  mini-enterprise project (maths link: percentages and decimals).
- **Sustainability:** understanding consumption and waste, auditing the school, and leading an action
  project linked to the UN Sustainable Development Goals.
- **Global citizenship:** children's rights under the UN Convention on the Rights of the Child,
  stereotypes and inclusion, democracy, and a service project in the community.

Lessons are discussion-rich and practical: role-plays, scenarios, circle time, real budgets, audits
and projects that make a real difference. Pupils keep a reflective journal. (Physical changes at
puberty and personal health are taught in PE & Health; this subject covers the emotional side of
growing up and change.)

**What this year assumes (from Year 4):** naming and managing a wide range of emotions; knowing what
makes a good friend; basic growth-mindset language; understanding needs vs wants and saving; knowing how
to get help from trusted adults; awareness of diversity.

**What Year 6 relies on:** the ability to reflect on values and goals; strategies for conflict and
peer pressure; understanding of interest, credit and budgets; experience of planning and completing an
action project; and knowledge of rights and responsibilities — ready for transition to secondary school,
more complex decisions and leadership roles.

## Unit 1 — Who I am: identity and values

**Essential question:** What makes me who I am, and what do I value?

**Key vocabulary:** identity, value, character strength, self-esteem, self-talk, comparison, belonging, heritage, integrity, empathy

### Lesson 1 — My identity map
- **Duration:** 35 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can describe the different parts of my identity and how they connect." (SEL — self-awareness)
- **Hook:** "If your identity were a map, what would its countries be?"
- **Key activity:** Create an identity map (family, heritage, languages, interests, beliefs, roles, places); share in pairs; discuss which parts are chosen and which are given.
- **Check for understanding:** "Name one part of your identity you chose and one you were born with. How do they both shape you?" → Two named parts and a reflection on their influence.
- **Differentiation:** support: identity map template with prompts / stretch: explain how one part of your identity might change over time.
- **Materials:** large paper, coloured pens, reflective journals.
- **Joy:** identity maps displayed as a class 'atlas'.

### Lesson 2 — Values: what matters to me
- **Duration:** 35 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can identify my top values and explain how they guide my choices." (SEL — values)
- **Hook:** "You find a wallet with money in it. What do you do — and what does that say about you?"
- **Key activity:** Values card sort (honesty, kindness, fairness, courage, loyalty, curiosity, respect…); choose top three; link to a dilemma; discuss how values can conflict (loyalty vs honesty).
- **Check for understanding:** "Describe a situation where two of your values might pull in different directions. What would you do?" → A realistic conflict and a reasoned choice.
- **Differentiation:** support: fewer values cards with pictures / stretch: explain a value you think is underrated and why.
- **Materials:** values cards, dilemma scenarios, journals.
- **Joy:** "Values auction" — spend limited 'money' on the values you'd keep.

### Lesson 3 — Character strengths
- **Duration:** 35 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can name my character strengths and spot them in others." (SEL — self-awareness)
- **Hook:** "Everyone has strengths. What are yours — beyond being good at things?"
- **Key activity:** Explore character strengths (perseverance, humour, fairness, creativity, gratitude, leadership…); find evidence from your own life; 'strength spotting' for classmates on sticky notes.
- **Check for understanding:** "Give an example of a time you used one of your strengths." → A specific example linked to a named strength.
- **Differentiation:** support: strengths word bank with examples / stretch: identify a strength you want to grow and plan how.
- **Materials:** strengths cards, sticky notes, journals.
- **Joy:** receiving strength-spotting notes from classmates.

### Lesson 4 — The comparison trap and self-talk
- **Duration:** 35 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can recognise unhelpful comparison and negative self-talk and replace them with helpful thoughts." (SEL — self-esteem)
- **Hook:** "Would you speak to a friend the way you sometimes speak to yourself?"
- **Key activity:** Identify comparison triggers (including social media images that are edited); practise reframing negative self-talk ("I'm useless at maths" → "I haven't mastered this yet; I'll ask for help").
- **Check for understanding:** "Reframe: 'Everyone else is better than me.'" → e.g. "Other people have different strengths; I'm improving at my own pace."
- **Differentiation:** support: reframing sentence stems / stretch: explain why social media can make comparison worse.
- **Materials:** self-talk cards, examples of edited images, journals.
- **Joy:** "Inner coach" — write a note from your kind inner coach.

### Lesson 5 — Belonging: where I fit
- **Duration:** 35 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can explain why belonging matters and how I can help others feel they belong." (SEL — social awareness)
- **Hook:** "Think of a time you felt you didn't belong. What helped?"
- **Key activity:** Circle time on belonging; read a short story about a newcomer (link to *The Arrival* in English); create a class 'belonging charter' with specific actions.
- **Check for understanding:** "Name two specific things you could do to help a new pupil feel they belong." → Two concrete actions (e.g. invite them to play, learn how to say their name correctly).
- **Differentiation:** support: action cards to choose from / stretch: plan a welcome pack for new pupils.
- **Materials:** story extract, charter paper, journals.
- **Joy:** signing the class belonging charter.

**End-of-unit check:** Pupil explains their top values and character strengths with examples, reframes a negative thought, and suggests concrete ways to help others belong.

## Unit 2 — Friendship, conflict and communication

**Essential question:** How can I build strong friendships and handle disagreements well?

**Key vocabulary:** healthy relationship, trust, boundary, assertive, passive, aggressive, 'I' statement, compromise, mediation, peer pressure, bullying, bystander, upstander

### Lesson 6 — Healthy and unhealthy friendships
- **Duration:** 35 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can recognise signs of healthy and unhealthy friendships." (SEL — relationships)
- **Hook:** "A friend who is fun but always puts you down. Is that a good friendship?"
- **Key activity:** Sort friendship behaviours on a continuum (healthy → unhealthy); discuss scenarios; know when and how to seek help.
- **Check for understanding:** "What is one sign that a friendship might be unhealthy, and what could you do?" → e.g. being controlled or put down; talk to the friend, set a boundary, or seek help from a trusted adult.
- **Differentiation:** support: behaviour cards with pictures / stretch: discuss how a healthy friendship handles disagreement.
- **Materials:** behaviour cards, scenario cards.
- **Joy:** "Friendship recipe" — ingredients of a great friendship.

### Lesson 7 — Passive, aggressive, assertive
- **Duration:** 35 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can respond assertively rather than passively or aggressively." (SEL — communication)
- **Hook:** "Someone pushes in front of you in a queue. Three possible responses — which is best?"
- **Key activity:** Role-play passive, aggressive and assertive responses; teach assertive body language and 'I' statements ("I feel… when… I would like…").
- **Check for understanding:** "Turn this into an assertive 'I' statement: 'You always ruin the game!'" → e.g. "I feel frustrated when the rules change mid-game. I'd like us to agree the rules first."
- **Differentiation:** support: 'I' statement frame / stretch: role-play staying assertive when the other person reacts badly.
- **Materials:** scenario cards, 'I' statement frames.
- **Joy:** role-play freeze-frames.

### Lesson 8 — Resolving conflict
- **Duration:** 35 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can use a step-by-step process to resolve a conflict fairly." (SEL — relationships)
- **Hook:** "Two friends both want to be captain. Can both win?"
- **Key activity:** Teach a conflict-resolution process (calm down → each side speaks without interruption → identify the problem → brainstorm solutions → agree and try); practise with scenarios; introduce peer mediation.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why is it important that both people speak without interruption?" → So each feels heard and understands the other's view before solving the problem.
- **Differentiation:** support: step cards to follow / stretch: act as a peer mediator.
- **Materials:** conflict-resolution step cards, scenarios.
- **Joy:** "Win–win" challenge.

### Lesson 9 — Peer pressure
- **Duration:** 35 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can recognise peer pressure and use strategies to make my own choices." (SEL — responsible decision-making)
- **Hook:** "Everyone in the group is doing it. Do you?"
- **Key activity:** Identify direct and indirect pressure; practise refusal strategies (say no clearly, give a reason, suggest an alternative, walk away, use humour); link to values from Unit 1.
- **Check for understanding:** "Give two strategies for saying no to something you don't want to do." → Two named strategies used appropriately.
- **Differentiation:** support: refusal phrases card / stretch: discuss positive peer pressure and how to use it.
- **Materials:** scenario cards, strategy posters.
- **Joy:** "Say no with style" role-play.

### Lesson 10 — Bullying and being an upstander
- **Duration:** 35 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can explain what bullying is, including online bullying, and how to be an upstander safely." (SEL — safety and relationships)
- **Hook:** "Is a single argument bullying? What makes something bullying?"
- **Key activity:** Define bullying (repeated, intentional, with a power imbalance); distinguish from conflict; roles (target, bully, bystander, upstander); safe upstander actions (support the target, don't join in, report); online bullying and reporting tools.
- **Check for understanding:** "What is the difference between a bystander and an upstander?" → A bystander sees but does nothing; an upstander takes safe action to help (supporting, reporting).
- **Differentiation:** support: scenario sort (bullying / not bullying) / stretch: design a campaign against online bullying.
- **Materials:** scenario cards, school anti-bullying policy (child-friendly version).
- **Joy:** "Upstander pledge" wall.

### Lesson 11 — Unit 2 reflection: my communication toolkit
- **Duration:** 35 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can choose the right communication tool for different friendship situations." (SEL — review)
- **Hook:** "Your toolkit has five tools. Which one fixes this?"
- **Key activity:** Scenario carousel: pupils choose and demonstrate a tool (assertive 'I' statement, conflict steps, refusal, getting help, upstanding); journal reflection.
- **Check for understanding:** "Which tool would you use if a friend keeps borrowing your things without asking, and why?" → An assertive 'I' statement (or setting a boundary) with a reason.
- **Differentiation:** support: toolkit reference card / stretch: combine two tools in one role-play.
- **Materials:** scenario cards, toolkit cards, journals.
- **Joy:** toolkit role-play showcase.

**End-of-unit check:** Pupil distinguishes healthy from unhealthy friendship behaviours, uses assertive 'I' statements and conflict-resolution steps in role-play, and explains how to be a safe upstander.

## Unit 3 — Resilience, goals and change

**Essential question:** How can I bounce back, keep growing and cope with change?

**Key vocabulary:** resilience, growth mindset, fixed mindset, setback, perseverance, goal, SMART goal, strategy, stress, calm, coping strategy, change, transition, support network

### Lesson 12 — Growth mindset and the brain
- **Duration:** 35 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can explain how practice strengthens connections in the brain and why mistakes help learning." (SEL — growth mindset)
- **Hook:** "Your brain physically changes when you learn something hard. How?"
- **Key activity:** Simple explanation of neurons and connections strengthening with practice (model with string); fixed vs growth mindset statements; famous 'failures' before success; link to Carol Dweck's research.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why is 'I can't do it yet' more helpful than 'I can't do it'?" → 'Yet' shows ability can grow with effort and strategies.
- **Differentiation:** support: mindset statement sort / stretch: explain why effort alone isn't enough — you also need good strategies and help.
- **Materials:** string or wool for the neuron model, statement cards.
- **Joy:** "Mistake of the week" celebration.

### Lesson 13 — SMART goals
- **Duration:** 35 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can set a SMART goal and plan steps to reach it." (SEL — self-management)
- **Hook:** "'I want to be better at football.' Is that a good goal?"
- **Key activity:** Teach SMART (Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant, Time-bound); rewrite vague goals; pupils set a personal goal with steps, obstacles and supports; check-ins planned for later in the term.
- **Check for understanding:** "Make this goal SMART: 'I want to read more.'" → e.g. "I will read for 20 minutes every evening for the next four weeks and record it in my reading journal."
- **Differentiation:** support: SMART template with examples / stretch: plan for a likely obstacle and how to overcome it.
- **Materials:** SMART goal templates, journals.
- **Joy:** "Goal buddies" who check in with each other.

### Lesson 14 — Stress and calm
- **Duration:** 35 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can recognise signs of stress in my body and use calming strategies." (SEL — self-management / wellbeing)
- **Hook:** "Where in your body do you feel worry?"
- **Key activity:** Body map of stress signs; practise strategies (box breathing, grounding with the five senses, movement, talking to someone, writing worries down); discuss that some stress is normal and helpful.
- **Check for understanding:** "Describe how box breathing works and when you might use it." → Breathe in for 4, hold for 4, out for 4, hold for 4; use it when feeling anxious or before something stressful.
- **Differentiation:** support: strategy picture cards / stretch: create a personal calm plan with three strategies for different situations.
- **Materials:** body map outlines, strategy cards, calm music.
- **Joy:** a class calm-down corner designed by pupils.

### Lesson 15 — Coping with change and loss
- **Duration:** 35 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can describe feelings that come with change and ways to cope, including where to get support." (SEL — resilience)
- **Hook:** "Moving house, a new sibling, a friend leaving, a loss. How does change feel?"
- **Key activity:** Discuss types of change (link to *Skellig* and *A Long Walk to Water*); normal feelings (including mixed feelings); coping strategies; map a personal support network.
- **Check for understanding:** "Who is in your support network, and what could each person help with?" → Named trusted people and appropriate kinds of help.
- **Differentiation:** support: support-network template / stretch: write advice to a younger pupil facing a big change.
- **Materials:** support-network templates, story extracts. Handle sensitively; be aware of pupils' circumstances.
- **Joy:** "Circles of support" artwork.

### Lesson 16 — Looking ahead to Year 6
- **Duration:** 35 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can reflect on my growth this year and set hopes for my final primary year." (SEL — transition)
- **Hook:** "Next year you'll be the oldest in school. What kind of role model will you be?"
- **Key activity:** Review goals from Lesson 13; celebrate progress; write a letter to your future self; discuss leadership roles in Year 6.
- **Check for understanding:** "What progress did you make towards your SMART goal, and what helped?" → An honest account with a named strategy.
- **Differentiation:** support: reflection frame / stretch: plan how you'll support younger pupils next year.
- **Materials:** journals, letter paper, envelopes.
- **Joy:** sealed letters to be opened at the start of Year 6.

**End-of-unit check:** Pupil explains growth mindset with reference to the brain, writes a SMART goal and reviews progress, and describes at least two calming strategies and their support network.

## Unit 4 — Money sense

**Essential question:** How can I make wise choices with money — earning, spending, saving and borrowing?

**Key vocabulary:** income, expense, budget, needs, wants, saving, interest, interest rate, compound interest, loan, borrowing, credit, debt, profit, loss, cost price, selling price, advertising, value for money, enterprise

### Lesson 17 — Earning and income
- **Duration:** 35 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can explain different ways people earn money and why jobs are paid differently." (Money sense)
- **Hook:** "Why does a footballer earn more than a nurse? Is that fair?"
- **Key activity:** Explore jobs, skills and pay; discuss factors (training, demand, risk, responsibility); introduce gross vs net pay simply (tax pays for schools, hospitals and roads).
- **Check for understanding:** "Give two reasons why some jobs are paid more than others." → e.g. long training, rare skills, high demand, big responsibility.
- **Differentiation:** support: job cards with salary ranges to order / stretch: debate whether pay reflects value to society.
- **Materials:** job cards, discussion prompts.
- **Joy:** "Dream job" research.

### Lesson 18 — Budgeting
- **Duration:** 35 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can plan a simple budget balancing income and expenses." (Money sense / maths link)
- **Hook:** "You have 50 a month. Can you afford a phone contract, hobbies and savings?"
- **Key activity:** Budget simulation: income, fixed and variable expenses, needs vs wants; adjust to balance; unexpected events cards (a broken bike).
- **Check for understanding:** "Your budget is 10 short this month. What are two sensible ways to fix it?" → e.g. cut a want, earn a little more, delay a purchase — not borrowing without a plan.
- **Differentiation:** support: budget template with fewer items / stretch: add a savings goal and plan to reach it.
- **Materials:** budget templates, event cards, calculators.
- **Joy:** "Budget survival" game.

### Lesson 19 — Saving and interest
- **Duration:** 35 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can calculate simple interest and explain how compound interest helps savings grow." (Money sense / maths link: percentages)
- **Hook:** "Would you rather have 100 today or 10% more each year?"
- **Key activity:** Calculate 10% interest on 100 (110 after one year); compound: 121 after two years (10% of 110 = 11); compare with keeping money in a jar; use a spreadsheet to model over 10 years (computing link).
- **Check for understanding:** "Why does compound interest grow faster than simple interest?" → Interest is earned on previous interest as well as the original amount.
- **Differentiation:** support: calculate one year of interest with a bar model / stretch: model different interest rates in a spreadsheet and compare after 10 years.
- **Materials:** calculators, spreadsheet, bar-model templates.
- **Joy:** watching savings 'snowball' on a chart.

### Lesson 20 — Borrowing and credit
- **Duration:** 35 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can explain how borrowing works and why debt can become a problem." (Money sense)
- **Hook:** "Buy now, pay later. What's the catch?"
- **Key activity:** Borrowing scenarios: a loan with interest costs more than the original price; 'buy now pay later' and credit cards; when borrowing can be sensible (a home, education) and risky.
- **Check for understanding:** "If you borrow 200 at 10% interest for a year, how much do you pay back, and why is it more than 200?" → 220: the interest is the cost of borrowing someone else's money.
- **Differentiation:** support: bar models for simple loans / stretch: compare two loan offers and choose the better one.
- **Materials:** scenario cards, calculators.
- **Joy:** "Spot the catch" advert challenge.

### Lesson 21 — Advertising and smart shopping
- **Duration:** 35 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can identify persuasive techniques in adverts and make value-for-money decisions." (Money sense / media literacy)
- **Hook:** "Why do you suddenly want something after seeing an advert?"
- **Key activity:** Analyse adverts (celebrity endorsement, limited offers, influencer posts, in-game purchases); compare unit prices (maths link); discuss marketing to children.
- **Check for understanding:** "Name two advertising techniques and explain how each tries to persuade you." → Two techniques with explanations (e.g. 'limited time only' creates urgency).
- **Differentiation:** support: technique matching cards / stretch: design an honest advert.
- **Materials:** advert examples, packaging with prices.
- **Joy:** "Advert detectives".

### Lesson 22 — Mini-enterprise: planning
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can plan a small business, calculating costs, prices and profit." (Money sense / enterprise)
- **Hook:** "You have 20 to start a business for the school fair. What will you make or sell?"
- **Key activity:** Teams choose a product, research costs, set a price, estimate profit, plan marketing; decide what to do with profits (e.g. donate to a chosen charity).
- **Check for understanding:** "If each item costs 0.80 to make and you sell it for 2.00, what is your profit on 25 items?" → 25 × 1.20 = 30.
- **Differentiation:** support: a costing template / stretch: calculate the break-even point.
- **Materials:** business plan templates, calculators, material price lists.
- **Joy:** "Dragons' Den" pitch.

### Lesson 23 — Mini-enterprise: running and reviewing
- **Duration:** 45 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can run a small enterprise and evaluate its success using real figures." (Money sense / enterprise)
- **Hook:** "The school fair is open for business!"
- **Key activity:** Sell at the fair (or class market); record sales; calculate profit or loss; evaluate what worked; donate profits as agreed.
- **Check for understanding:** "Did you make a profit? Show how you calculated it and one thing you'd change." → Correct calculation and a sensible improvement.
- **Differentiation:** support: a sales record sheet with prompts / stretch: present a financial report with a chart.
- **Materials:** products, cash box and float (with adult), record sheets.
- **Joy:** the enterprise fair.

**End-of-unit check:** Pupil balances a budget, calculates interest and profit correctly, explains the risks of borrowing, and evaluates an enterprise with real figures.

## Unit 5 — Sustainability in action

**Essential question:** How can we use the planet's resources fairly — and what can we actually do about it?

**Key vocabulary:** sustainability, resource, consumption, waste, reduce, reuse, repair, recycle, carbon footprint, renewable, audit, Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs), action plan, campaign

### Lesson 24 — The Sustainable Development Goals
- **Duration:** 35 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can describe the UN Sustainable Development Goals and connect them to my life." (Sustainability / global citizenship)
- **Hook:** "In 2015, the world's countries agreed 17 goals for a fairer, greener world by 2030. Which matters most to you?"
- **Key activity:** Explore the 17 SDGs with icon cards; groups rank them and justify; link goals to topics studied (water — SDG 6; climate — SDG 13; quality education — SDG 4).
- **Check for understanding:** "Choose one SDG and explain how it connects to something we studied this year." → A named goal linked to a specific topic (e.g. SDG 6 and *A Long Walk to Water*).
- **Differentiation:** support: a smaller set of goals with pictures / stretch: explain how two goals depend on each other.
- **Materials:** SDG icon cards, world map.
- **Joy:** "Diamond ranking" debate.

### Lesson 25 — Where does our stuff go? Waste
- **Duration:** 35 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can explain the waste hierarchy and why reducing and reusing beat recycling." (Sustainability)
- **Hook:** Tip out (clean) classroom waste from one day onto a sheet.
- **Key activity:** Sort the waste; learn the waste hierarchy (refuse, reduce, reuse, repair, recycle, dispose); discuss why recycling still uses energy.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why is reusing a bottle better than recycling it?" → Reusing needs no energy for processing; recycling still uses energy and resources.
- **Differentiation:** support: waste sorting with picture bins / stretch: research what happens to recycling in your area.
- **Materials:** a day's clean waste, gloves, sorting sheets.
- **Joy:** "Trash detectives".

### Lesson 26 — School sustainability audit
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can carry out an audit of energy, water or waste in school and record data accurately." (Sustainability / maths link)
- **Hook:** "Is our school a sustainability hero or villain?"
- **Key activity:** Groups audit one area (lights left on, taps, waste bins, paper use) using tally charts at set times; compile data.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why do we need to collect data at several times, not just once?" → To get a fair picture; one moment might not be typical.
- **Differentiation:** support: a structured audit sheet / stretch: calculate the percentage of rooms with lights on when empty.
- **Materials:** clipboards, audit sheets, tally counters.
- **Joy:** the audit expedition around school.

### Lesson 27 — Planning an action project
- **Duration:** 35 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can plan an action project with clear aims, steps and measures of success." (Sustainability / active citizenship)
- **Hook:** "Data is only useful if we act on it."
- **Key activity:** Choose one issue from the audit; write an action plan (aim, actions, who, when, how we'll measure success); present to the headteacher or school council.
- **Check for understanding:** "How will you know if your project worked?" → A measurable indicator (e.g. fewer lights on in the next audit).
- **Differentiation:** support: action plan template / stretch: anticipate obstacles and plan responses.
- **Materials:** action plan templates, audit data.
- **Joy:** pitching to the school leadership.

### Lesson 28 — Taking action
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can carry out a planned action and persuade others to join in." (Sustainability / communication)
- **Hook:** "Campaign day!"
- **Key activity:** Groups carry out actions (switch-off stickers, water-saving posters, a repair café, a litter pick, assembly presentation — link to art Unit 6 posters and English speeches).
- **Check for understanding:** "What persuasive technique did your group use to encourage others, and did it work?" → A named technique and an observation.
- **Differentiation:** support: a defined role in the group / stretch: lead the campaign or present at assembly.
- **Materials:** campaign materials, litter pickers, gloves.
- **Joy:** visible change around school.

### Lesson 29 — Measuring impact
- **Duration:** 35 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can evaluate the impact of our project using before-and-after data." (Sustainability / evaluation)
- **Hook:** "Did we make a difference?"
- **Key activity:** Repeat the audit; compare data (chart); reflect on what worked and what didn't; plan how to keep the change going.
- **Check for understanding:** "What does the before-and-after data show, and could anything else explain the change?" → A comparison and a thoughtful consideration of other factors.
- **Differentiation:** support: comparison chart template / stretch: write a short report to the school council.
- **Materials:** audit sheets, charts.
- **Joy:** "Impact reveal" with a class celebration.

**End-of-unit check:** Pupil explains the waste hierarchy and one SDG, completes an audit with accurate data, and evaluates the impact of an action using before-and-after evidence.

## Unit 6 — Rights, fairness and global citizenship

**Essential question:** What rights does every child have, and how can I help make the world fairer?

**Key vocabulary:** human rights, children's rights, UN Convention on the Rights of the Child (UNCRC), responsibility, equality, discrimination, stereotype, prejudice, inclusion, democracy, vote, representation, charity, volunteer, service

### Lesson 30 — Every child's rights
- **Duration:** 35 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can explain what the UN Convention on the Rights of the Child is and give examples of children's rights." (Global citizenship)
- **Hook:** "In 1989, world leaders agreed a list of rights for every child. What would you put on it?"
- **Key activity:** Pupils brainstorm rights, then compare with a child-friendly version of the UNCRC (e.g. the right to education, to play, to be safe, to be heard); discuss rights and responsibilities.
- **Check for understanding:** "What is the difference between a right and a want?" → A right is something every child needs to be safe, healthy and develop (e.g. education); a want is nice to have (e.g. a games console).
- **Differentiation:** support: rights and wants sorting cards / stretch: identify a right that is not yet met for all children and suggest why.
- **Materials:** child-friendly UNCRC summary (from UNICEF), sorting cards.
- **Joy:** "Rights and wants" sorting race.

### Lesson 31 — Stereotypes and prejudice
- **Duration:** 35 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can identify stereotypes and explain how they can lead to unfair treatment." (Global citizenship / SEL)
- **Hook:** "Draw a scientist. Now look around — who did we all draw?"
- **Key activity:** Discuss the 'draw a scientist' results; identify stereotypes in media and toys; link stereotype → prejudice → discrimination; challenge stereotypes with real examples (scientists and leaders from many backgrounds).
- **Check for understanding:** "How can a stereotype lead to discrimination? Give an example." → A stereotype (a fixed idea about a group) can shape how people are treated unfairly, e.g. assuming girls aren't good at football and leaving them out.
- **Differentiation:** support: stereotype-spotting cards / stretch: analyse a toy advert for stereotypes.
- **Materials:** drawing paper, advert examples, images of diverse role models.
- **Joy:** "Stereotype busters" gallery.

### Lesson 32 — Inclusion and disability
- **Duration:** 35 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can explain how environments and attitudes can include or exclude people with disabilities." (Global citizenship)
- **Hook:** "Is a person disabled by their impairment or by stairs with no ramp?"
- **Key activity:** Explore the social model of disability; accessibility audit of part of the school (ramps, signs, noise, lighting); meet or learn about Paralympians and disabled leaders; sign a simple phrase in the local sign language.
- **Check for understanding:** "Give one example of a change that would make our school more inclusive." → A specific, realistic change (e.g. visual timetables, a quiet space, a ramp).
- **Differentiation:** support: accessibility checklist with pictures / stretch: write recommendations to the school.
- **Materials:** accessibility checklists, Paralympian profiles, sign-language video.
- **Joy:** learning to sign a greeting.

### Lesson 33 — Democracy and having a say
- **Duration:** 35 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can explain how democracy works and take part in a fair class election." (Global citizenship / history link)
- **Hook:** "Who decides the rules in our school — and how could you have a say?"
- **Key activity:** Link to suffrage history (history Unit 6); features of democracy (free elections, secret ballot, representation, rule of law); hold a class election for a real role with manifestos and a secret ballot.
- **Check for understanding:** "Why is voting in secret important?" → So people can vote freely without pressure or fear of what others think.
- **Differentiation:** support: manifesto template / stretch: compare two voting systems and explain which is fairer.
- **Materials:** ballot papers, ballot box, manifesto templates.
- **Joy:** election day.

### Lesson 34 — Service: planning a community project
- **Duration:** 40 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can plan a service project that meets a real need in our community." (Global citizenship / service)
- **Hook:** "What does our community need that we could help with?"
- **Key activity:** Identify needs (a care home visit, a food bank collection, reading with younger pupils, a litter pick, letters to isolated people); plan the project responsibly with adults.
- **Check for understanding:** "How do we know our project meets a real need rather than what we assume people want?" → We asked the people or organisation involved and listened to what would help.
- **Differentiation:** support: project menu with guided plans / stretch: lead a team and create a timeline.
- **Materials:** project planning sheets, contact with local organisations (via school).
- **Joy:** choosing the project as a class.

### Lesson 35 — Service in action and year reflection
- **Duration:** 45 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can carry out a service project and reflect on what I learned about myself and others this year." (Global citizenship / SEL)
- **Hook:** "Today we help."
- **Key activity:** Carry out the service project (or its key event); reflect in journals: what we did, who it helped, what we learned; year review of the whole life-skills course.
- **Check for understanding:** "What did the service project teach you about your community — and about yourself?" → A thoughtful reflection with a specific example.
- **Differentiation:** support: reflection prompts / stretch: write a short article for the school newsletter.
- **Materials:** project materials, journals.
- **Joy:** the service day and a year-end celebration.

**End-of-unit check:** Pupil explains children's rights and responsibilities, challenges a stereotype with evidence, describes how democracy works, and reflects on a completed service project.

## End-of-year mastery checks

1. **Self-awareness** — describes their values and character strengths with examples.
2. **Self-management** — uses at least two calming strategies and sets and reviews a SMART goal.
3. **Relationships** — resolves a conflict using assertive communication and a clear process (seen in role-play).
4. **Safety** — explains bullying (including online) and how to be a safe upstander and get help.
5. **Money** — balances a budget and calculates interest and profit correctly; explains the risks of debt.
6. **Sustainability** — explains the waste hierarchy and completes an evidence-based action project.
7. **Rights and fairness** — explains children's rights and how stereotypes lead to discrimination.
8. **Active citizenship** — takes part in a democratic process and a community service project, reflecting on its impact.

## Teacher guidance

**Common misconceptions to watch for**
- *"Growth mindset means just try harder."* Effort plus good strategies plus asking for help.
- *"Being assertive is being bossy."* Assertiveness respects both your own needs and others'.
- *"Any unkindness is bullying."* Distinguish conflict, one-off unkindness and bullying — all matter, but responses differ.
- *"Borrowing is free money."* Always compare total cost with interest.
- *"Recycling solves waste."* Reduce and reuse first.
- *"Rights are things I want."* Rights are universal needs, balanced with responsibilities.

**How to teach it well**
- Establish ground rules for discussion (listen, respect, right to pass, no names in examples).
- Use distancing techniques (stories, characters, scenarios) so pupils don't have to disclose personal experiences.
- Follow safeguarding procedures if a pupil discloses something of concern.
- Make projects real: real money at the fair, real audits, real service — real impact motivates.
- Link to the English texts (*Journey to Jo'burg*, *A Long Walk to Water*, *The Arrival*, *Skellig*) for empathy and discussion.

**Vertical connections**
- *From Year 4:* emotional vocabulary, friendship basics, early growth mindset, saving and needs vs wants.
- *Into Year 6:* transition to secondary school, more complex relationships and online life, personal finance (bank accounts, payslips, taxes), leadership and a larger independent social-action project.
- *Across subjects:* maths (budgets, interest, percentages), computing (spreadsheets, online safety), history (suffrage, freedom movements), geography (trade, water, SDGs), PE & Health (wellbeing, growing up), English (speeches, persuasive writing).
