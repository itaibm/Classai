## The year in one paragraph

Year 5 is the year pupils become **independent thinkers who can explain, argue, investigate and
make**. In maths they master fractions, decimals and percentages, meet ratio and algebra, and divide by
two-digit numbers; in English they read eight whole texts (from *Wonder* to *The Tempest*), write a
literary essay, a balanced argument and a researched report; in science they design their own
investigation for a science fair; in the humanities they study how connected the medieval world was
and how people changed unfair laws; they write their first Python programs, strum ukulele chords and
play keyboard, compose a film soundtrack, run a mini-enterprise, carry out a sustainability project,
learn to float to live and navigate with a map and compass. Everything prepares them for Year 6 —
the final primary year — which relies on fluent number, early algebra and ratio, and the habits of
independent research and extended writing.

## Sample weekly timetable

22 lessons a week, following the §2 budget (maths 5, English 5, science 2, languages 2, PE & Health 2,
and one each of history, geography, art, music, computing and life skills). Lessons are 30–45 minutes.
Around them sit a daily 5-minute maths retrieval warm-up, 20–30 minutes of independent reading, a class
read-aloud, spelling practice, breaks and outdoor play.

| | Session 1 | Session 2 | Session 3 | Session 4 | Session 5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Monday** | English | Maths | Science | Languages | — |
| **Tuesday** | English | Maths | PE & Health | History | — |
| **Wednesday** | English | Maths | Science | Art & Design | — |
| **Thursday** | English | Maths | PE & Health | Languages | Music |
| **Friday** | English | Maths | Geography | Computing | Life Skills |

Tips: keep maths and English in the mornings when attention is highest; place practical subjects
(science, art, PE) where double sessions are possible; the timetable's 36 weeks leave a few spare
slots in every subject for trips, assessment conferences and events.

## Cross-curricular threads

- **Journeys and belonging** — *The Breadwinner*, *A Long Walk to Water* and *The Arrival* (English)
  link to rights and freedom movements (history), water scarcity and migration (geography), and
  belonging and rights (life skills).
- **Maths everywhere** — ratio and map scale (geography), means and line graphs (science, PE), angles
  in turtle graphics (computing), time signatures as fractions (music), budgets and interest (life
  skills), Maya and Babylonian numerals (history).
- **Sustainability** — climate and the carbon cycle (science), fair trade and sustainable cities
  (geography), art for a cause (art), the SDGs action project (life skills).
- **A connected world** — Baghdad, the Maya, Mali, Benin and the Vikings on one timeline (history);
  Islamic geometric art and Benin plaques (art); griots and the kora (music); the Spanish-speaking
  world (languages).

## How Year 5 builds on Year 4 and prepares for Year 6

- **From Year 4** (ages 9–10, UK Year 5 content): place value to 1,000,000, long multiplication, short
  division, related-denominator fractions and decimals to three places; relative clauses, modal verbs,
  parenthesis and persuasive writing; forces, materials, Earth and space; ancient civilisations;
  climate zones and the water cycle; Scratch with selection and variables.
- **For Year 6** (ages 11–12, UK Year 7 / US Grade 6 content): Year 5 secures instant
  fraction–decimal–percentage conversion, dividing by fractions (introduced), ratio and unit rate,
  order of operations, substitution and two-step equations, negative numbers and four-quadrant
  coordinates, area of triangles and parallelograms, and the mean. In English it secures literary
  analysis with embedded quotations, balanced argument, précis, independent research with a
  bibliography, and control of register and punctuation. In science and the humanities it secures
  the particle model, fair testing with repeats, source evaluation and fieldwork; in computing, first
  Python programs.

## Assessment in Year 5

Assessment is **low-stakes, frequent and mastery-oriented**: daily checks for understanding in every
lesson, an end-of-unit check for each unit, and end-of-year mastery checks in each subject file.
There are no practice-test drills: grammar is taught and assessed through pupils' own writing, and
art, music and PE are assessed by looking at, listening to and watching the work. Portfolios (writing,
sketchbooks, recordings, fieldwork and investigation reports) and one-to-one conferences give each
child and family a clear picture of progress.
