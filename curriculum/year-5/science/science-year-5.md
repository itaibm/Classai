# Year 5 Science (ages 9–10, KS2) — Lesson Outlines

> Part of **the best international primary school** (see [`../../../SCHOOL-CHARTER.md`](../../../SCHOOL-CHARTER.md)).
> Source of truth: [`knowledge-base/subjects/science/year-5.md`](../../../knowledge-base/subjects/science/year-5.md).

## Year overview

Year 5 is the year of **life cycles, change and the wider universe**. A child learns the different **life cycles** of mammals, birds, amphibians and insects (and the drama of metamorphosis); how plants and animals **reproduce**, sexually and asexually; how humans **grow up** from baby to old age; how to **compare and separate materials** by their properties, including dissolving and recovering a dissolved solid; the difference between **reversible and irreversible changes**; the structure of the **Solar System** and why we have day and night; and the **forces** of gravity and resistance, and the mechanisms that multiply force. The teaching keeps inquiry at its heart, but the **working-scientifically thread** now reaches its KS2 peak in *planning*: this year the child **chooses the right type of enquiry, controls variables** (changes one thing while keeping all others the same), and **reports findings with conclusions and explanations**. This builds on Year 4's accurate measurement and charting — the child now owns the *whole* method, from question to controlled test to explained conclusion. Lessons run mostly 30–35 min; investigation lessons (separating mixtures, reversible changes, Earth–space modelling, parachutes) use the upper band.

## Unit 1 — Life cycles and reproduction

**Essential question:** How do different living things reproduce and complete their life cycles?

**Key vocabulary:** life cycle, mammal, bird, amphibian, insect, metamorphosis, larva, pupa, reproduction, sexual, asexual, stamen, pollen, stigma, ovary, fertilisation, runner, bulb, cutting.

### Lesson 1 — Life cycles of animals
- **Duration:** 35 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can describe how mammal, bird, amphibian and insect life cycles differ."
- **Hook:** "A caterpillar and a butterfly — same animal, or two different ones?"
- **Key activity:** Explore — compare cycles: **mammal** (live young, milk), **bird** (egg → chick), **amphibian** (egg → tadpole → froglet → frog), **insect** (egg → larva → pupa → adult). Name **metamorphosis** (a dramatic body change). Order a frog and a butterfly cycle. Fix "a caterpillar and butterfly are different animals" and "all animals lay eggs."
- **Check for understanding:** "What is metamorphosis?" → A big change in body shape as an animal grows (e.g. tadpole → frog).
- **Differentiation:** support: order one cycle with cards; stretch: compare two cycles and say what's the same/different.
- **Materials:** life-cycle cards for frog, butterfly, bird, mammal.
- **Joy:** ordering the frog's stunning tadpole-to-frog transformation.

### Lesson 2 — Reproduction in plants: sexual and asexual
- **Duration:** 30 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can describe two ways plants reproduce and name the flower's reproductive parts."
- **Hook:** "A strawberry plant can make babies two completely different ways — what are they?"
- **Key activity:** Explore — **sexual**: the **stamen** makes **pollen** → carried to a **stigma** → **fertilises** an egg in the **ovary** → **seed** forms (offspring vary). **Asexual**: **runners** (strawberry), **bulbs** (daffodil), **cuttings** (mint) make copies from one parent. Fix "plants only grow from seeds" and "pollination = fertilisation."
- **Check for understanding:** "Give one way a plant reproduces asexually." → Runners / bulbs / cuttings.
- **Differentiation:** support: sort examples sexual/asexual; stretch: explain the difference between pollination and fertilisation.
- **Materials:** flower diagram, strawberry-runner picture, a mint/spider-plant cutting in water.
- **Joy:** starting a cutting in water to watch roots grow over the unit.

### Lesson 3 — Growing up: humans from birth to old age
- **Duration:** 30 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can order human life stages and explain that growth comes in bursts."
- **Hook:** "Do you grow the same amount every single year of your life?"
- **Key activity:** Explore — order **baby → child → teenager → adult → old age**, with one ability gained at each stage. Notice — growth is **fastest in babyhood and the teenage years**, not steady. Compare **gestation** lengths (mouse weeks, human ~9 months, elephant ~22 months). Fix "people grow at a steady rate."
- **Check for understanding:** "During which stages is human growth fastest?" → Babyhood and the teenage years.
- **Differentiation:** support: order the five stages; stretch: explain why bigger animals often have longer gestation.
- **Materials:** life-stage cards, gestation-matching cards.
- **Joy:** making a personal "life timeline" with what they can do now vs as a baby.

**End-of-unit 1 check:** Describe how two animal life cycles differ and define metamorphosis; name two ways plants reproduce; order human life stages and say when growth is fastest.

## Unit 2 — Properties and changes of materials

**Essential question:** How do we group and separate materials, and which changes can be undone?

**Key vocabulary:** property, hardness, transparency, conductivity, magnetism, solubility, dissolve, solution, mixture, sieve, filter, evaporate, reversible, irreversible, new material.

### Lesson 4 — Comparing and grouping materials by properties
- **Duration:** 30 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can compare and group materials using several properties."
- **Hook:** "Which of these materials would you trust to make a window? A pan? A magnet's friend?"
- **Key activity:** Explore — test materials for **hardness, transparency, electrical/thermal conductivity, magnetism, solubility**. Notice — different jobs need different properties. Group materials and justify each choice from its properties.
- **Check for understanding:** "Name two properties you could use to compare materials." → Any two: hardness, transparency, conductivity, magnetism, solubility.
- **Differentiation:** support: group by one property; stretch: choose the best material for a job from its properties.
- **Materials:** assorted materials, magnet, water, (optional) circuit tester.
- **Joy:** running a "material Olympics" — which wins on each property.

### Lesson 5 — Dissolving and separating mixtures (investigation)
- **Duration:** 35 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can choose the right method to separate a mixture and recover a dissolved solid."
- **Hook:** "If you stir salt into water and it 'vanishes,' is it gone forever?"
- **Key activity:** Investigate — dissolve salt in warm water to make a **solution** (it's still there — taste/observe). Match methods to mixtures: **sieve** (big from small), **filter** (undissolved solid from liquid, e.g. sand from water), **evaporate** (recover a dissolved solid). Leave salty water to evaporate and watch crystals appear. Fix "dissolved sugar disappears" and "you can filter salt out of salty water."
- **Check for understanding:** "How would you get dissolved salt back from salty water?" → Evaporate the water (the salt is left behind).
- **Differentiation:** support: match two mixtures to methods; stretch: explain why filtering won't recover dissolved salt.
- **Materials:** salt, warm water, sand, filter paper, sieve, saucer, warm spot.
- **Joy:** finding real salt crystals left behind after the water evaporates.

### Lesson 6 — Reversible and irreversible changes (investigation)
- **Duration:** 35 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can sort changes as reversible or irreversible and spot when a new material forms."
- **Hook:** "Can you un-cook a fried egg? Can you un-melt an ice cube?"
- **Key activity:** Investigate — (1) melt then refreeze ice (**reversible** — same water); (2) mix vinegar + bicarbonate of soda → it **fizzes** (gas = clue a **new material** formed → **irreversible**). Notice clues of a new material: **gas, heat, light, colour change**. Sort changes (burning, cooking, rusting = irreversible). Fix "all changes can be undone" and "mixing always makes a new material."
- **Check for understanding:** "What clue tells you a new material may have formed?" → Gas/bubbles, heat, light, or a colour change.
- **Differentiation:** support: sort three changes; stretch: explain why the fizzing change can't be reversed.
- **Materials:** ice, vinegar, bicarbonate of soda, cups, change-sorting cards.
- **Joy:** the dramatic safe "fizzing volcano."

**End-of-unit 2 check:** Group materials by two properties; choose the right method to separate a mixture and recover dissolved salt; sort changes reversible/irreversible and name a clue of a new material.

## Unit 3 — Earth and Space

**Essential question:** Why do we have day and night, and how does our Solar System fit together?

**Key vocabulary:** Solar System, Sun, star, planet, orbit, spherical, Moon, axis, rotate, spin, day, night, 24 hours.

### Lesson 7 — The Solar System
- **Duration:** 30 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can describe the Solar System and say the Sun is a star at its centre."
- **Hook:** "The Sun isn't special-looking up close — it's just a star. So why does it rule the day?"
- **Key activity:** Explore — the **Sun (a star)** at the centre, **eight planets** in **orbits** around it, including Earth; the **Moon orbits the Earth**. Notice — the Earth, Sun and Moon are roughly **spherical**, even though the ground looks flat. Place planets in order from the Sun.
- **Check for understanding:** "What is at the centre of our Solar System?" → The Sun (a star).
- **Differentiation:** support: order the inner planets; stretch: explain why the ground looks flat though Earth is a sphere.
- **Materials:** Solar System diagram, planet cards, a ball to represent a sphere.
- **Joy:** building a scale-ish model of the planets around the Sun.

### Lesson 8 — Day and night (investigation/model)
- **Duration:** 35 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can explain that day and night come from the Earth spinning."
- **Hook:** "When it's morning here, it's the middle of the night across the world — same Sun. How?"
- **Key activity:** Investigate with a model — a torch (the Sun) and a globe with a sticker on your town. Shine the torch: the lit side has **day**, the dark side **night**. Spin the globe slowly: the sticker turns into night and back. Name — the Earth **rotates** once every **24 hours**; the Sun only *appears* to move. Fix "the Sun moves across the sky" and "night happens because the Sun goes away/switches off."
- **Check for understanding:** "What causes day and night?" → The Earth spinning (rotating) once every 24 hours.
- **Differentiation:** support: show day and night on the model; stretch: explain why shadows swing and change length through the day.
- **Materials:** globe, torch, sticker; (optional) a shadow stick to track outside.
- **Joy:** spinning "their town" from day into night with the torch-Sun.

**End-of-unit 3 check:** Describe the Solar System with the Sun at the centre; explain that day and night come from the Earth's 24-hour spin, not the Sun moving.

## Unit 4 — Forces

**Essential question:** What is gravity, what slows moving things, and how do machines multiply force?

**Key vocabulary:** gravity, weight, fall, air resistance, water resistance, friction, drag, opposite direction, mechanism, lever, pulley, gear, streamlined.

### Lesson 9 — Gravity and resistance (investigation)
- **Duration:** 35 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can explain gravity and show how air resistance slows a fall."
- **Hook:** "Drop a flat sheet of paper and a scrunched ball at once — which lands first, and why?"
- **Key activity:** Investigate — drop a flat sheet and an identical scrunched ball; the ball falls fast, the sheet floats (more **air resistance**). Control variables: same paper, same height — only shape changes. Name **gravity** (pulls things down, gives weight) and resistive forces (**air, water resistance, friction**) that act **opposite to movement**. Fix "heavier always falls faster" and "no gravity in space."
- **Check for understanding:** "Why does a flat sheet of paper fall more slowly than a scrunched one?" → It has more air resistance.
- **Differentiation:** support: observe and describe the difference; stretch: explain why "heavier falls faster" is wrong.
- **Materials:** two identical paper sheets, (optional) a feather and a book.
- **Joy:** the surprise that two identical papers fall at totally different speeds.

### Lesson 10 — Mechanisms and a parachute fair test (investigation)
- **Duration:** 35 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can name mechanisms that multiply force, and run a controlled parachute test."
- **Hook:** "How can a small child lift a heavy friend on a see-saw?"
- **Key activity:** Explore **mechanisms** — **levers** (see-saw, crowbar), **pulleys** (lift by pulling down), **gears** (bike) let a small force do a big job. Then investigate: build a parachute and **time its fall**, changing **one variable** (canopy size) while keeping the rest the same. Record times in a table and conclude (bigger canopy → more air resistance → slower fall).
- **Check for understanding:** "Name a mechanism that helps lift a heavy load with less force." → A lever (or pulley, or gears).
- **Differentiation:** support: time one parachute; stretch: design the fair test and explain the controlled variable.
- **Materials:** plastic/cloth for canopies, string, a small weight, timer, results table; lever/pulley/gear examples or pictures.
- **Joy:** dropping homemade parachutes and timing the slowest.

**End-of-unit 4 check:** Explain gravity and why a scrunched sheet falls faster than a flat one; name a mechanism that multiplies force; run a controlled parachute test changing only one variable and state the conclusion.

## Unit 5 — Working scientifically (year wrap-up)

**Essential question:** How do I plan a fair, controlled investigation and explain my results?

**Key vocabulary:** enquiry type, variable, control, change, keep the same, prediction, evidence, conclusion, explanation.

### Lesson 11 — Planning a controlled investigation (year review)
- **Duration:** 35 min
- **Objective:** "By the end, I can plan a controlled investigation and report a conclusion with an explanation."
- **Hook:** "If we wanted to find out what makes salt dissolve fastest, how do we keep it fair?"
- **Key activity:** Review the year's investigations. Choose a question (e.g. "does warm water dissolve salt faster than cold?"). Identify the **one variable to change** (water temperature) and the variables to **keep the same** (amount of salt, amount of water, stirring). Predict, run a quick version, **record data in a table**, and write a **conclusion with an explanation**. Name the enquiry type (comparative/fair test).
- **Check for understanding:** "In a 'warm vs cold water' dissolving test, what must you keep the same?" → Amount of salt, amount of water, and how you stir — only the temperature changes.
- **Differentiation:** support: identify the variable to change with a prompt; stretch: plan, run and explain the whole investigation independently.
- **Materials:** salt, warm and cold water, timer, stirrers, results table.
- **Joy:** running their own controlled experiment from start to finish.

**End-of-unit 5 check:** Choose what to change and what to keep the same in an investigation; record results in a table; state a conclusion with a scientific explanation.

## End-of-year mastery checks

1. **Life cycles:** Describe how two animal life cycles differ and define metamorphosis.
2. **Reproduction:** Name two ways plants reproduce and the difference between pollination and fertilisation.
3. **Growing up:** Order human life stages and say when growth is fastest.
4. **Materials:** Group materials by two properties and choose the right method to recover dissolved salt.
5. **Changes:** Sort changes as reversible or irreversible and name a clue that a new material has formed.
6. **Earth & space:** Explain that day and night come from the Earth's 24-hour spin, not the Sun moving.
7. **Forces:** Explain gravity and why a scrunched sheet falls faster than a flat one; name a force-multiplying mechanism.
8. **Investigation:** Plan a controlled test (change one variable, keep the rest the same), record results, and state a conclusion with an explanation.
