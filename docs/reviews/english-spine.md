## 1. The main problem: the same books taught in several years

Because the planners worked blind, whole class texts recurred: *The Wild Robot* was a unit anchor in
Years 2, 3 **and** 4; *The Iron Man* in 2 and 3; *Holes* in 5 and 6; *A Long Walk to Water* in 5 and
6; *Journey to Jo'burg* and "The Highwayman" in 4 and 5; *Where the Mountain Meets the Moon* in 3 and
4; *The Arrival* in 3 and 5; *The Owl Who Was Afraid of the Dark*, *Mufaro's Beautiful Daughters* and
*Please Mrs Butler* in 1 and 2. Single poems repeated even more: "Talking Turkeys!!" was taught in
Years 2, 3, 4 **and** 5, Langston Hughes's "Dreams" in 3, 4, 5 **and** 6, Bashō's frog haiku in 3, 4
and 6, and "The Tyger", "Where I'm From", "Jabberwocky", "The Road Not Taken", "Paper Boats",
"Give Yourself a Hug" and Malala's UN speech in two years each. Optional reading shelves also listed
other years' class texts.

**Rule applied:** each whole book (and each anchor poem) has one owning year, chosen by age and
reading level and by which year had built the most lessons on it; the other year got a different,
real book and the unit was rewritten lesson by lesson (titles, hooks, activities, checks,
vocabulary, materials, end-of-unit check). Other years may *refer back* to a text (retrieval,
"compare with … from Year 3") but never re-teach it. Replacements were chosen for diversity of
author and setting and a classic/modern balance, and only titles whose existence and content are
well established were used; where plot details were uncertain the lessons stay at the level of
widely known events.

## 4. Progression fixes at year boundaries

- **Phonics → spelling.** Year 1 (Phase 5 alternatives → Phase 6 suffixes, with decodable texts) and
  Year 2 (phonics completion + Years 3–4 appendix) are sound. But Year 2 raced through most of the
  Years 3–4 appendix, and Year 3 then re-taught the same lessons almost word for word. Year 3 lessons
  were reframed as *retrieval + new content*: prefixes (L25: add non-, over-, trans-); -ous (L14:
  -eous/-ious — courageous keeps its e for a soft g); homophones (L75: the remaining Y3–4 pairs —
  accept/except, rain/rein/reign, missed/mist…); /shun/ (L95: root changes and the attend →
  attention exception); French spellings (L118: -eau, -et, ou); ei/eigh/ey (L144, identical to Y2 L8)
  replaced by **its/it's**; statutory word-list lessons (L18, L135) no longer re-teach Year 2's words.
- Year 5 repeated Year 4's Years 5–6 appendix lessons: -cious/-tious + -cial/-tial (L8 → suffix
  exceptions: argument, truly, duly, wholly, awful, judgement/judgment), 'ough' (L65 → unstressed
  vowels/schwa with related-word strategy), hyphens (L47 → compound adjectives before vs after the
  noun). Inverted commas in Y3 L39 now explicitly extend Y2's rules.
- Poetry forms now build: haiku/tanka (Y3) → pantoum (Y4) → renga linked verse (Y6); narrative
  poems: Causley/Eliot (Y3) → "The Highwayman" (Y4) → *The Lady of Shalott* (Y5); Hughes grows from
  "Dreams" (Y3) → "April Rain Song" (Y4) → "I, Too" (Y5) → "Mother to Son"/"Harlem" (Y6).
- Handwriting (first joins Y1 → fully joined from Y2 → speed/legibility Y3–4) and genre progression
  (recount/instructions/letters → reports/explanations/news → persuasion/biography → balanced
  argument/memoir/analysis → essay/research/rhetoric) were already coherent and are unchanged.

## 5. Rule and fact corrections

- Y1 L8: standard code said "/ai/ spelled -y at end of words" — corrected to /igh/ (my, try, fly).
- Y1 L13: hook claimed "blue, grew, moon and flute all rhyme" (they don't) — now "share the same /oo/ sound".
- Y1 L59: "one morning… falls on Stanley in the night" tidied to match the book.
- Y2 teacher guidance: the doubling rule now states the stressed-final-syllable condition
  (begin → beginning, but visit → visited, garden → gardening).
- Y3 teacher guidance: y → i now also says y stays before -ing (cry → crying). Y3 L61
  multiply → multiplication explained as y becoming ic.
- Y3 L135: "rehearse" is **not** on the Years 3–4 statutory list — hook rewritten.
- Checked and left as correct: y → i only after a consonant (Y1 L64, Y2 L4); plurals -s/-es/-ies
  and vowel + y (keys, monkeys); -dge/-ge; -le/-el/-al/-il guidance; a/an by sound; -able/-ible with
  soft c/g (noticeable); -ant/-ent clues; -cial/-tial exceptions (initial, financial, commercial,
  provincial); -cious exception anxious; -fer stress rule; i before e only for /ee/ (seize, protein);
  semicolon/colon/dash tests; subjunctive base form; relative-clause comma 'lift-out' test; reported
  speech back-shift; "The Highwayman" rhyme scheme AABCCB. Literary facts spot-checked for every
  class text, including the new ones.
- Checks for understanding in rewritten lessons ask *why/how/which is wrong* rather than bare
  recall (e.g. "Why does 'courageous' keep the e when 'famous' drops it?", "Why does the council
  blame the mosquito, not the iguana?").
