# SCHOOL-CHARTER.md — The Best School (Ages 6–12)

> **This is the north star for the whole school project.** Every chat, question, file,
> lesson, and decision in this project exists to serve one goal: **build the best
> international primary school in the world — a complete, year-by-year curriculum
> (ages 6–12) with the best lessons, the best books and materials, taught by the best
> methods, so that every child becomes the most capable, curious, and successful
> version of themselves.**
>
> If a piece of work doesn't move us toward that goal, it doesn't belong here.

---

## 1. Vision & mission

**Mission.** Design and produce a full, ready-to-teach curriculum for an international
school covering ages 6 to 12 (six year-levels), across every subject a well-rounded
child needs — so good that a strong teacher can pick up any year, any subject, any
lesson, and deliver a world-class education from day one.

**Vision of the graduate.** A 12-year-old leaving this school is:

- **Literate and numerate to a high standard** — reads fluently and critically, writes
  clearly, reasons mathematically, and is comfortable with numbers, data, and logic.
- **A confident communicator** in English plus at least one additional language.
- **A curious inquirer** who asks good questions, investigates, and learns independently.
- **A creative maker** in art, music, design, and technology.
- **Healthy and active** in body and mind, with strong habits and emotional regulation.
- **A kind, globally-minded citizen** — empathetic, collaborative, ethical, and aware of
  the wider world.
- **Resilient and self-directed** — knows how to set goals, persist, and bounce back.

Everything we build is measured against whether it produces this graduate.

## 2. What "the most successful kid" means here

Success is **not** just test scores. We aim for the whole child:

1. **Mastery** — deep, durable understanding of core knowledge and skills (not surface
   coverage). A child should *own* what they learn.
2. **Love of learning** — intrinsic motivation that outlasts the lesson. Joy and wonder
   are design requirements, not extras.
3. **Character & wellbeing** — kindness, integrity, perseverance, curiosity, confidence.
4. **Agency** — the ability to think, create, and act independently.

When these pull in tension, we favor **deep mastery + love of learning** over coverage
and grades. A child who loves to learn and understands deeply will outrun one who was
merely drilled.

## 3. The "best method" — our teaching philosophy

We are not copying one system. We synthesize the **best of the best** into one coherent
method:

- **Inquiry & conceptual understanding (from IB PYP):** organize learning around big
  transferable concepts and real questions, not disconnected facts. Units are driven by
  inquiry and connect across subjects where it's natural.
- **Rigor, clear progression & mastery (from Cambridge Primary & Singapore):** every
  subject has explicit, sequenced learning objectives per year. Math uses the
  **concrete → pictorial → abstract** progression, mastery before moving on, and
  rich problem-solving. No gaps, no rushing.
- **Wellbeing, play, and trust (from Finland):** less is more. Protect childhood — play,
  movement, rest, and outdoor time are part of learning, not a reward for finishing it.
  Low-stakes, high-trust environment; assessment informs teaching rather than ranking kids.
- **Evidence-based instruction:** systematic phonics for early reading, spaced and
  retrieval practice for retention, explicit teaching of new material followed by guided
  then independent practice, and frequent low-stakes feedback.
- **Differentiation:** every lesson supports the child who needs more time AND stretches
  the child who's ready for more. No child bored, no child lost.
- **Knowledge-rich + skills-rich:** strong content knowledge is the foundation on which
  critical thinking and creativity are built — we reject the false choice between them.

**Design principles for any lesson we create:**

1. Clear learning objective tied to the year's standards.
2. Hook / question that creates curiosity.
3. Explicit teaching of the new idea (concrete first for young children).
4. Guided practice → independent practice.
5. A check for understanding (was it actually learned?).
6. Built-in differentiation (support + stretch).
7. Connection to the real world or other subjects.
8. Joy — at least one element a child would be excited to do.

## 4. Structure & scope

### Year levels (the "years")

Six year-levels, one per age. We use neutral labels so the curriculum stays international:

| Our label | Typical age | UK equiv. | US equiv. |
|-----------|-------------|-----------|-----------|
| **Year 1** | 6–7  | Year 2      | Grade 1 |
| **Year 2** | 7–8  | Year 3      | Grade 2 |
| **Year 3** | 8–9  | Year 4      | Grade 3 |
| **Year 4** | 9–10 | Year 5      | Grade 4 |
| **Year 5** | 10–11| Year 6      | Grade 5 |
| **Year 6** | 11–12| Year 7      | Grade 6 |

Each year is a complete package. The six years form one continuous, gap-free spine —
each year builds deliberately on the last (vertical alignment).

### Subjects (every year)

**Core:**

- **English / Language & Literacy** — reading (phonics → fluency → comprehension &
  critical reading), writing (handwriting → composition → genre & craft), speaking &
  listening, vocabulary, grammar.
- **Mathematics** — number, operations, fractions/decimals, measurement, geometry, data,
  early algebra & reasoning; mastery + problem-solving.
- **Science** — biology, chemistry, physics, earth & space; taught through inquiry and
  hands-on investigation; scientific method.
- **Humanities** — history, geography, and global/cultural studies; civics and our place
  in the world.

**Languages:**

- **Additional language(s)** — at least one world language with a clear progression
  (international school = multilingual by design).

**Creative & physical:**

- **Art & Design** — visual art, craft, design thinking.
- **Music** — listening, singing, rhythm, an instrument pathway.
- **Physical Education & Health** — movement, sport, motor skills, healthy habits.

**Thinking & character:**

- **Computing / Digital Literacy** — computational thinking, safe & creative tech use,
  age-appropriate coding.
- **Social-Emotional Learning (SEL) & Character** — emotions, relationships, ethics,
  growth mindset, mindfulness.
- **Life skills / Global citizenship** — sustainability, money sense, collaboration,
  service.

(Exact depth scales with age; not every minor strand appears with equal weight in every
year, but the spine is consistent.)

### What a complete "Year package" must contain

A year is **done** only when, for each subject, we have produced:

1. **Year overview** — the big picture, themes, and how units sequence across the year.
2. **Scope & sequence** — the explicit, ordered list of learning objectives/standards.
3. **Units of work** — broken into teachable topics with objectives, key vocabulary, and
   essential questions.
4. **Lesson plans** — ready-to-teach, following the 8 design principles in §3.
5. **Materials & resources** — worksheets, activities, projects, manipulatives lists.
6. **The "best books"** — a curated reading list and recommended texts/resources per
   subject and year (see §5).
7. **Assessment** — checks for understanding, end-of-unit and end-of-year assessments
   that inform teaching (low-stakes, mastery-oriented).
8. **Teacher guidance** — notes on how to teach it well, common misconceptions,
   differentiation, and extension.

## 5. Quality & method standards ("best")

These are the bars every artifact must clear. "Good enough" is not the standard — **best**
is.

**Lessons must be:**

- Aligned to a specific year objective and the graduate vision.
- Built on the 8 design principles (§3) — including a real check for understanding.
- Age-appropriate in language, length, and cognitive load.
- Differentiated (support + stretch) by default.
- Engaging enough that a child *wants* to do them.
- Teacher-ready: another teacher could deliver it without guessing our intent.

**Books & materials must be:**

- The **best available** for the purpose — chosen on quality, not convenience.
- Age-appropriate, inclusive, and internationally minded (diverse authors, cultures,
  perspectives).
- A balance of classic/canonical and modern/excellent.
- Justified — when we recommend a book or resource, we say *why* it's the best choice.

**Assessment must:**

- Measure deep understanding, not recall tricks.
- Be low-stakes and frequent (for learning), with clear end-of-year mastery checks.
- Feed back into teaching and into each child's path.

**Pedagogy must:**

- Reflect evidence (phonics, retrieval/spaced practice, explicit instruction, mastery).
- Protect wellbeing, play, and curiosity.
- Be globally minded and inclusive of all learners.

## 6. Working rules — how we operate in every chat

So that **every** interaction stays anchored to this vision:

1. **Frame everything against the goal.** Before producing anything, ask: *does this help
   build the best school / best curriculum / best lesson for the most successful kid?*
   If unclear, clarify scope against this charter.
2. **Always know the coordinates.** State which **year**, **subject**, and **unit/lesson**
   a piece of work belongs to, so it slots into the larger structure.
3. **Build the spine, gap-free.** New work must align vertically (with the year before and
   after) and horizontally (with other subjects in the same year).
4. **Best, not fast.** Default to the highest-quality version. Note trade-offs explicitly
   rather than silently lowering the bar.
5. **Make it teacher-ready.** Output should be usable by a real teacher in a real
   classroom without needing to reverse-engineer our intent.
6. **Justify "best" claims.** When we pick a method, book, or sequence, briefly say why
   it's the best choice.
7. **Keep the child at the center.** Mastery + love of learning + character + agency —
   in that spirit — win over coverage and grades.
8. **Organize files clearly.** Curriculum work lives under a predictable structure
   (e.g. `curriculum/year-N/<subject>/...`) so the school is navigable as it grows.
